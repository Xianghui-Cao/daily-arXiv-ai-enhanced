### [1] [Thermal axion production in QCD from the lattice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01925)
*Luis Neubauer,Guy D. Moore*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses a method for computing thermal axion production in the early Universe using Euclidean lattice QCD, which allows for the extraction of weighted k-integrals of the k-dependent production rate, potentially applicable at temperatures around and above the crossover temperature.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a reliable method for computing thermal axion production rates in the early Universe, particularly at temperatures where other methods are not dependable, by using the QCD topological-charge-density spectral function without requiring analytic continuation.

Method: The authors use a technique similar to that used by Harvey Meyer and collaborators for photon production rate, applying it to pure-glue QCD at 1.5 times the critical temperature (Tc) to compute weighted integrals of the QCD topological-charge-density spectral function using Euclidean lattice QCD.

Result: The approach tested in the study was found to be feasible for extracting one or possibly two finite weighted k-integrals of the momentum (k)-dependent axion production rate, suggesting potential for application in full QCD at relevant temperatures.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the proposed method opens up new possibilities for extracting thermal axion production rates in full QCD at temperatures near and above the crossover temperature, where previous methods have been unreliable.

Abstract: Thermal axion production in the early Universe is controlled by the QCD topological-charge-density spectral function evaluated on the light-cone as a function of momentum k. We show how weighted integrals of this quantity can be computed using Euclidean lattice QCD without any analytic continuation, similar to what Harvey Meyer and collaborators have recently done for the photon production rate. We then test the approach in pure-glue QCD at T=1.5 Tc, finding that it is feasible to extract at least one and possibly two finite weighted k-integrals of the k-dependent production rate. This opens the possibility for extractions in full QCD at temperatures around and somewhat above the crossover temperature, where other methods are not reliable.
