### [1] [The 2d Ising Model on Hyperbolic Lattices and the Role of Boundary Conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07045)
*Pallabi Dey,Debasish Banerjee,Arnab Kundu,Ritam Sinha*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study explores the impact of boundary conditions on the critical behavior of the ferromagnetic Ising model in hyperbolic tessellations, using simulations. It finds that open boundaries lead to non-mean-field scaling, while wired boundaries show mean-field behavior. The research underscores the significance of boundary conditions and the difference between interior and system-wide observables in understanding critical phenomena on complex lattices.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the role of boundary conditions in the critical behavior of the ferromagnetic Ising model on regular hyperbolic tessellations, as well as to explore the finite-size effects and the potential for a common universality class across different tessellations.

Method: Worm and Metropolis simulations were used to compare the critical behavior of the Ising model under open and wired boundary conditions. For open boundaries, volume-based finite-size scaling was analyzed, whereas for wired boundaries, the focus was on radial scaling and the central-to-auxiliary spin correlator. Additional tests included layer-resolved responses to weak boundary fields and the response of the central spin to a uniform magnetic field.

Result: Open boundaries led to non-mean-field scaling exponents, which were consistent across four tessellations, suggesting a possible common universality class. Wired boundaries showed mean-field magnetization exponent with radial scaling. There was also an enhancement of induced magnetization towards the interior when a weak boundary field was applied. Significant finite-size effects were observed, especially in comparisons of Binder crossings and susceptibility peaks.

Conclusion: The findings highlight the importance of boundary conditions in determining the critical behavior of the Ising model on hyperbolic tessellations. They also point out the distinction between the properties of interior and whole-system observables, which is crucial for characterizing critical phenomena on non-amenable lattices.

Abstract: We investigate the role of boundary conditions in the critical behaviour of the ferromagnetic Ising model on regular hyperbolic tessellations. Using worm and Metropolis simulations, we compare open boundaries with wired boundaries implemented through a common auxiliary spin. For open boundaries, the susceptibility exhibits volume-based finite-size scaling with non-mean-field scaling exponents that are approximately stable across four tessellations, supporting the possibility of a common universality class. Comparisons with Binder crossings and susceptibility peaks reveal substantial finite-size effects. For wired boundaries, the central-to-auxiliary spin correlator exhibits radial scaling consistent with a mean-field magnetization exponent. The central-spin response to a uniform magnetic field provides a further consistency test of $δ=3$. Layer-resolved responses to a weak boundary field also reveal an enhancement of the induced magnetization towards the interior. Our results highlight the importance of boundary conditions and the distinction between interior and whole-system observables in characterizing critical phenomena on non-amenable lattices.

### [2] [Exact and Near-Exact Inverse Renormalization Group with Constrained Monte Carlo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07641)
*Dimitrios Bachtis*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents conditions for the exactness of the inverse renormalization group using constrained Markov processes, which are a type of Monte Carlo simulation. It shows that at the fixed point action, this method exactly inverts the renormalization group, and it is nearly exact around the fixed point. The method is used to generate configurations for high-dimensional systems, like 3D φ⁴ quantum field theory, without critical slowing down, as evidenced by a dynamic critical exponent close to zero.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a technique for the exact inversion of the renormalization group, which is important for simulating and understanding the behavior of complex systems, especially near critical points, where traditional methods suffer from critical slowing down.

Method: The authors use constrained Markov processes, a form of Monte Carlo simulations, to sample degrees of freedom on a fine lattice while maintaining consistency with the coarse lattice. They optimize the renormalization group transformations on these inversely renormalized configurations to map the system's action onto itself, ensuring stability during the generation of configurations for larger lattice volumes.

Result: The results demonstrate that constrained Markov processes can invert the renormalization group exactly at the fixed point and nearly exactly nearby. Using this approach, the researchers generated uncorrelated, near-equilibrium configurations for a 3D φ⁴ quantum field theory at a significantly larger lattice volume, with no critical slowing down indicated by a very low dynamic critical exponent (z=0.03(2)).

Conclusion: The study concludes that the proposed method of using constrained Markov processes for inverse renormalization group transformations is effective and efficient, particularly for high-dimensional systems, as it overcomes the issue of critical slowing down and allows for the accurate extraction of critical exponents.

Abstract: We establish conditions for exactness of the inverse renormalization group via constrained Markov processes, a class of Monte Carlo simulations which sample the degrees of freedom within a block on a fine lattice under the constraint that they remain consistent with the corresponding degree of freedom on the coarse lattice according to a forward renormalization group transformation. We show analytically, for an arbitrary transformation, that constrained Markov processes correspond to an exact inversion of the renormalization group at the fixed point action, and to a near-exact inversion in the vicinity of the fixed point. We then optimize renormalization group transformations directly on inversely renormalized configurations to effectively map the action onto itself and ensure computational stability in the generation of configurations for high-dimensional systems. Starting from a set of uncorrelated configurations for the three-dimensional $φ^{4}$ quantum field theory at lattice volume $V=16^{3}$, we invert the renormalization group with constrained Monte Carlo to construct a set of uncorrelated, near-equilibrium configurations for $V=128^{3}$ and extract two critical exponents of the system. The inversion exhibits no critical slowing down, with dynamic critical exponent $z=0.03(2)$.

### [3] [First Overlap Formulation and Perturbative Results for Supersymmetric QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07745)
*Marios Costa,Elias Antoniou,Herodotos Herodotou,Eleni Ioannou,Constantinos Kartoudes,Gregoris Spanoudes,Haralambos Panagopoulos*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper introduces a lattice formulation for N=1 supersymmetric QCD (SQCD) using overlap fermions, ensuring consistency with the Ginsparg-Wilson relation. It focuses on constructing Yukawa terms that preserve a modified chiral symmetry, and includes one-loop perturbative calculations to determine field and mass counterterms, which sets the stage for future fine-tuning of couplings.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a consistent discretization of N=1 SQCD that respects the Ginsparg-Wilson relation, specifically addressing the construction of Yukawa terms with a lattice-modified chiral symmetry, and to facilitate future perturbative studies of coupling constants.

Method: The method involves using overlap fermions in the lattice formulation and implementing Lüscher's strategy to introduce auxiliary Dirac and Majorana fermionic fields. The authors also perform one-loop perturbative calculations to compute self-energies and determine the necessary counterterms.

Result: The results include the successful construction of the lattice action with ultralocal interaction terms, as well as the computation of quark, gluon, gluino, and squark self-energies, leading to a clear identification of the corresponding counterterms. The study indicates a reduced number of required counterterms compared to Wilson-type fermions.

Conclusion: The work establishes a solid basis for future perturbative studies and the fine-tuning of gauge, Yukawa, and quartic couplings, demonstrating an expected reduction in the number of needed counterterms, which is advantageous for lattice simulations.

Abstract: We present a lattice formulation of N=1 supersymmetric QCD (SQCD) based on overlap fermions, providing a discretization consistent with the Ginsparg-Wilson relation. Particular emphasis is placed on the construction of Yukawa terms that preserve a lattice-modified chiral symmetry involving overlap quarks and gluinos. To realize this modified chiral symmetry in the Yukawa sector, we follow Lüscher's strategy and introduce auxiliary Dirac and Majorana fermionic fields for the quark and gluino fields, respectively. After functional integration over these auxiliary fields, ultralocal interaction terms arise as additional contributions to the lattice action. Within this framework, and using the Wilson gauge action for gluons, we also carry out one-loop perturbative calculations. In particular, we compute the self-energies of the quark, gluon, gluino, and squark fields in the presence of quark and squark masses, enabling an unambiguous determination of the corresponding field and mass counterterms on the lattice. This work provides a robust foundation for future perturbative determinations of the fine-tunings of the gauge, Yukawa, and quartic couplings, and already shows, as expected, a reduction in the number of required counterterms relative to Wilson-type fermions.

### [4] [Quark-mass dependence of two-nucleon scattering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07864)

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores the quark-mass dependence of low-energy nucleon-nucleon scattering in the deuteron and dineutron channels using lattice-QCD calculations, with a focus on pion-mass dependence. The results suggest that the deuteron unbinds near a pion mass of 220 MeV.

Motivation: The motivation is to understand how the properties of the visible universe are influenced by the parameters of the Standard Model of particle physics, specifically focusing on the quark-mass dependence of nucleon-nucleon interactions at different pion masses.

Method: Lattice-QCD calculations were performed at pion masses of approximately 200 and 280 MeV, and the outcomes were compared with data at the physical point and a previous study at 710 MeV. The researchers used quantization conditions that account for one-pion exchange effects to determine scattering amplitudes, phase shifts, and threshold parameters from finite-volume spectra. Chiral effective field theory was applied to analyze the pion-mass dependence of scattering lengths and effective ranges.

Result: The analysis provided estimates of the pion-mass derivatives of inverse scattering lengths and binding momentum at the physical point. It was found that both power countings (Weinberg and Kaplan-Savage-Wise) indicate the deuteron becomes unbound near a pion mass of 220 MeV.

Conclusion: The research concludes with an estimation of the pion-mass derivatives related to the binding characteristics of the deuteron, indicating a critical pion mass around 220 MeV where the deuteron no longer remains bound, which contributes to our understanding of the quark-mass dependence of nuclear forces.

Abstract: Understanding how the properties of the visible Universe depend on the parameters of the Standard Model of particle physics requires \emph{ab initio} theoretical computations. To that end, we investigate the quark-mass dependence of low-energy nucleon-nucleon scattering in the deuteron and dineutron channels. We perform lattice-QCD calculations at pion masses $M_π\approx 200$ and $280$ MeV and compare their results to phenomenology at the physical point and to a previous calculation at $M_π\approx 710$ MeV. Scattering amplitudes, phase shifts, and threshold parameters are determined from finite-volume spectra using quantization conditions that explicitly incorporate long-distance effects from one-pion exchange. We analyze the pion-mass dependence of scattering lengths and effective ranges using chiral effective field theory with Weinberg and Kaplan-Savage-Wise power countings. From this, we estimate the pion-mass derivatives of the inverse scattering lengths and binding momentum, at the physical point. Both power countings indicate that the deuteron unbinds near $M_π\approx 220$ MeV.

### [5] [Primitive Quantum Gates for an $SU(3)$ Discrete Subgroup: $Σ(216\times3)$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08161)
*Erik J. Gustafson,Henry Lamm,Edison Murairi,Sebastian Osorio Perez*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper constructs a primitive gate set for simulating a 648-element discrete subgroup of SU(3) and provides cost estimates for qubit and qutrit-qubit architectures in a shear-viscosity calculation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a fundamental set of quantum gates necessary for the digital quantum simulation of a specific discrete subgroup of SU(3), which can be used for complex calculations such as shear viscosity, while considering the costs associated with different quantum computing architectures.

Method: The method involves developing five algorithmic primitives (inversion, group multiplication, trace, group Fourier transform, and kinetic-phase gates) and decomposing them into qubit and qutrit-qubit operations. The researchers then estimate the fault-tolerant costs for a fiducial shear-viscosity calculation using these primitives on a 10^3 spatial lattice with 50 time steps, under a total synthesis-error budget.

Result: The result is an estimated cost of 2.3-6.8x10^10 T gates for the qubit architecture and 1.3-5.9x10^12 R gates for the qutrit-qubit architecture, for a shear-viscosity calculation within the specified parameters.

Conclusion: The conclusion would likely emphasize the practicality of the proposed primitive gate set for performing complex quantum simulations, and it suggests that the qubit architecture may be more efficient for this type of computation compared to the qutrit-qubit architecture, based on the lower estimated gate count.

Abstract: We construct a primitive gate set for the digital quantum simulation of the $648$-element discrete subgroup of $SU(3)$, $\dsb$. The five algorithmic primitives are the inversion, group multiplication, trace, group Fourier transform, and kinetic-phase gates, for which we provide qubit and qutrit--qubit decompositions. For a fiducial shear-viscosity calculation on a $10^3$ spatial lattice with $50$ time steps and a total synthesis-error budget of $10^{-8}$, the estimated fault-tolerant costs are $2.3$--$6.8\times10^{10}$ $T$ gates in the qubit architecture and $1.3$--$5.9\times10^{12}$ $R$ gates in the qutrit--qubit architecture.
