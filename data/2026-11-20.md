### [1] [Testing the D234 Dirac operator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25241)
*Jorge Baeza-Ballesteros,Andreas Risch,Mattis Roost,Stefan Schaefer,Rainer Sommer*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper evaluates a version of the D234 fermion action, which is improved to reduce discretization effects, improve rotational invariance, and decrease small eigenvalues in the Dirac operator spectrum, outperforming the clover-improved Wilson action.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to assess an enhanced D234 fermion action that has been non-perturbatively improved to first order and tree-level improved to second order, with additional features such as stout-smeared gauge links and an exponentiated clover term, in comparison to the established clover-improved Wilson action.

Method: The method involves a quenched approximation study where the D234 fermion action, with its improvements, is compared against the clover-improved Wilson action, focusing on the reduction of discretization artifacts, enhancement of rotational symmetry, and minimization of small eigenvalues in the Dirac operator.

Result: The results show that the D234 fermion action significantly reduces discretization effects, improves the adherence to rotational invariance, and decreases the occurrence of exceptionally small eigenvalues in the Dirac operator's spectrum, indicating superior performance over the clover-improved Wilson action.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the proposed D234 fermion action, with its higher-order improvements and stout smearing, offers a notable advantage by diminishing lattice artifacts and enhancing symmetry properties, making it a promising choice for future studies in lattice QCD.

Abstract: We study a version of the so-called D234 fermion action. On top of non-perturbative improvement to $\mathcal{O}(a)$, this action is also tree-level improved at $\mathcal{O}(a^2)$. The version we consider also has gauge links smoothed with one level of stout smearing and the clover term in its exponentiated form. Working in the quenched approximation, we compare this action to the clover-improved Wilson action. The proposed action reduces the size of discretization effects and the violation of rotational invariance to a remarkable degree. The presence of exceptionally small eigenvalues in the spectrum of the Dirac operator is also reduced.

### [2] [Equation of state of hot and dense QCD at the physical point via the complex Langevin equation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25943)
*Michael Mandl,Dénes Sexty,Daniel Unterhuber*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents the first results for the equation of state in 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, using complex Langevin equations to calculate thermodynamic quantities at high baryon chemical potentials and above the crossover temperature.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To explore the equation of state of 2+1+1-flavor QCD under conditions that have not been extensively studied, specifically at the physical point and with large baryon chemical potentials, providing new insights into the thermodynamics of quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

Method: The authors use the complex Langevin equation, a numerical method for simulating lattice QCD, to perform calculations. The simulations are set in an isospin-symmetric, strangeness- and charm-neutral environment, and they extrapolate to the continuum limit to obtain the thermodynamic properties like baryon density, pressure, speed of sound, and adiabatic compressibility.

Result: The study successfully provides the first continuum-extrapolated results for the equation of state of 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, including the calculation of key thermodynamic quantities such as baryon density, pressure, and others, at temperatures above the crossover and at very high baryon chemical potentials.

Conclusion: The research contributes significantly to the understanding of the QCD phase diagram by presenting novel data on the equation of state for 2+1+1-flavor QCD, which could help in the interpretation of heavy-ion collision experiments and in the theoretical modeling of dense nuclear matter.

Abstract: This study presents the first results for the equation of state of 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, obtained using the complex Langevin equation. We calculate continuum-extrapolated thermodynamic quantities, such as the baryon density, pressure, the speed of sound and the adiabatic compressibility. The simulations are performed in the isospin-symmetric, strangeness- and charm-neutral setting above the crossover temperature and at unprecedentedly large baryon chemical potentials.

### [3] [Half-strings across the deconfinement transition of a $U(1)$ quantum link model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26415)
*Indrajit Sau,Arnab Sen,Debasish Banerjee*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper examines the finite temperature phase transition in a spin-1/2 U(1) quantum link model, with and without annealed charges, by extracting critical temperatures and exponents. It compares (effective) string tensions at zero and finite temperatures, finding that the ratio of the critical temperature to the square root of the string tension for the model with annealed charges is similar to the Z_2 gauge theory, while the model without charges offers a new universal value.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the phenomenon of confinement, a key feature of gauge theories, through the study of simpler lattice gauge theories, specifically focusing on how the addition of annealed charges impacts the confinement-deconfinement transition in a quantum link model.

Method: The method involves detailed extraction of critical temperature and exponents, as well as comparing the (effective) string tension at both zero and finite temperatures for the quantum link model with and without annealed charges.

Result: The result shows that the T_c/√σ ratio for the quantum link model with annealed charges is similar to that of the usual Z_2 gauge theory, whereas the model without these charges provides a new universal value for this ratio, indicating different behavior in the presence or absence of annealed charges.

Conclusion: The conclusion suggests that the inclusion of annealed charges in the quantum link model significantly affects the characteristics of the confinement-deconfinement transition, leading to a distinct universal value for the T_c/√σ ratio compared to when such charges are absent.

Abstract: The phenomenon of confinement is arguably the key feature of gauge theories regularly studied in high-energy and condensed matter physics. This causes hadrons to exist as stable particles in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) as bound states of quarks (and gluons), and strings in pure gauge theories. The promise of realizing such strings in quantum simulator setups have fueled extensive studies of a plethora of simpler lattice gauge theories (LGTs). Quantum link models (QLMs) generalize Wilsonian LGTs and yield richer scenarios of confinement. For example, the string joining two static charges in the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ $U(1)$ QLM fractionalize, giving rise to confining phases that break the point group and internal symmetries spontaneously. It was further shown in PRL 130, 071901 (2023) that adding annealed $Q = \pm 2$ charges to this model qualitatively changes the confinement-deconfinement transition. In this paper, we examine the finite temperature phase transition both in the presence and the absence of annealed charges through detailed extraction of critical temperature, $T_c$ , and exponents. The (effective) string tension $σ$ at zero and finite- temperatures are compared between the theory with and without annealed charges. The quantity $T_c/\sqrtσ$ for the QLM with annealed charges is similar to the usual $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory, while the theory without the charges provides a new universal value.

### [4] [Review of Pion and Nucleon Gluon Parton Distribution Functions from Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26443)
*William Good,Huey-Wen Lin*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper reviews recent advancements in calculating gluon parton distribution functions (PDFs) using lattice QCD, particularly with the LaMET and pseudo-PDF methods, discussing current achievements, challenges, and future prospects.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to address the limited understanding of gluon PDFs, which are crucial for describing high-energy scattering processes involving hadrons, due to experimental difficulties. It aims to highlight how recent breakthroughs in lattice QCD calculations, especially through LaMET and pseudo-PDF approaches, have enabled a better comprehension of the x-dependent structure of hadrons, including nucleons and pseudoscalar mesons.

Method: The method involves summarizing and analyzing the recent progress made in the calculation of the Bjorken-x dependence of gluon PDFs directly from lattice Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), focusing on the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET) and pseudo-PDF frameworks. The review discusses these methodologies' application, their current status, and the impact of the findings on the field.

Result: The result is an overview of the latest developments in lattice QCD determinations of gluon PDFs, showcasing how the LaMET and pseudo-PDF techniques have led to significant advances in first-principles studies of the x-dependent structure of hadrons. It also highlights the ongoing challenges and suggests potential areas for future research.

Conclusion: In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the importance of continued research into gluon PDFs, noting that while substantial progress has been made through the use of LaMET and pseudo-PDF methods, there remain critical challenges to overcome. It underscores the need for further investigation to deepen our understanding of hadron structure and the dynamics of QCD.

Abstract: Gluons play an essential role in the strong interaction that binds quarks into hadrons, as described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD). Understanding how the fundamental dynamics of QCD give rise to hadron structure remains one of the outstanding challenges in nuclear and particle physics. Parton distribution functions (PDFs) provide a successful framework for describing high-energy scattering processes involving hadrons. However, our knowledge of gluon PDFs remains comparatively limited because of the experimental challenges associated with directly constraining the gluon structure of hadrons. In recent years, a major breakthrough has enabled the calculation of the Bjorken-$x$ dependence of PDFs directly from lattice QCD. Among the available approaches, the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET) and pseudo-PDF frameworks have emerged as the most widely used methods and have produced the majority of lattice determinations of parton distributions. These developments have sparked rapid progress in first-principles studies of the $x$-dependent structure of hadrons, including gluon PDFs of the nucleon and pseudoscalar mesons. In this review, we summarize recent advances in lattice QCD calculations of gluon PDFs {using the LaMET and pseudo-PDF methods}, discuss the current status and impacts of these developments, and highlight the remaining challenges and future opportunities.
