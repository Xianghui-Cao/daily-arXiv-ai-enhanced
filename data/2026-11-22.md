### [1] [Testing the D234 Dirac operator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25241)
*Jorge Baeza-Ballesteros,Andreas Risch,Mattis Roost,Stefan Schaefer,Rainer Sommer*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper examines a version of the D234 fermion action that is improved to O(a) non-perturbatively and O(a^2) at tree level, with stout-smeared gauge links and an exponentiated clover term. It shows significant reduction in discretization effects, violation of rotational invariance, and small eigenvalues in the Dirac operator spectrum, compared to the clover-improved Wilson action, within the quenched approximation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation for this study is to improve upon existing fermion actions by reducing discretization errors, enhancing rotational invariance, and minimizing the presence of very small eigenvalues in the Dirac operator's spectrum, which are important for achieving more accurate lattice QCD simulations.

Method: The method involves the use of a modified D234 fermion action that includes non-perturbative improvement to first order, tree-level improvement to second order, stout smearing of the gauge links, and an exponentiated clover term. The performance of this action is then evaluated against the clover-improved Wilson action using quenched QCD simulations.

Result: The results show that the proposed action leads to a substantial decrease in discretization effects and the violation of rotational symmetry, as well as a reduction in the occurrence of exceptionally small eigenvalues of the Dirac operator, indicating an overall improvement over the clover-improved Wilson action.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the study is that the examined D234 fermion action, with its additional improvements, represents a promising approach for lattice QCD calculations, as it provides better control over systematic errors associated with the discretization of space-time.

Abstract: We study a version of the so-called D234 fermion action. On top of non-perturbative improvement to $\mathcal{O}(a)$, this action is also tree-level improved at $\mathcal{O}(a^2)$. The version we consider also has gauge links smoothed with one level of stout smearing and the clover term in its exponentiated form. Working in the quenched approximation, we compare this action to the clover-improved Wilson action. The proposed action reduces the size of discretization effects and the violation of rotational invariance to a remarkable degree. The presence of exceptionally small eigenvalues in the spectrum of the Dirac operator is also reduced.

### [2] [Equation of state of hot and dense QCD at the physical point via the complex Langevin equation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25943)
*Michael Mandl,Dénes Sexty,Daniel Unterhuber*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents the first results for the equation of state in 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point using complex Langevin equations, with calculations of thermodynamic quantities at high baryon chemical potentials.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To explore the equation of state for 2+1+1-flavor QCD under conditions not previously achievable, specifically at the physical quark masses and large baryon chemical potentials, providing new insights into the thermodynamics of quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

Method: Utilized the complex Langevin equation to perform simulations in an isospin-symmetric, strangeness- and charm-neutral setting, calculating continuum-extrapolated thermodynamic quantities such as baryon density, pressure, speed of sound, and adiabatic compressibility above the crossover temperature.

Result: Obtained the first set of results for the equation of state in 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, with data on thermodynamic properties at unprecedentedly large baryon chemical potentials, contributing to a better understanding of QCD matter under extreme conditions.

Conclusion: The study successfully provides novel data on the equation of state for 2+1+1-flavor QCD, extending the knowledge of QCD thermodynamics to regions of the phase diagram that were previously inaccessible, particularly at the physical point and large baryon chemical potentials.

Abstract: This study presents the first results for the equation of state of 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, obtained using the complex Langevin equation. We calculate continuum-extrapolated thermodynamic quantities, such as the baryon density, pressure, the speed of sound and the adiabatic compressibility. The simulations are performed in the isospin-symmetric, strangeness- and charm-neutral setting above the crossover temperature and at unprecedentedly large baryon chemical potentials.

### [3] [Half-strings across the deconfinement transition of a $U(1)$ quantum link model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26415)
*Indrajit Sau,Arnab Sen,Debasish Banerjee*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper examines the finite temperature phase transition in a spin-1/2 U(1) quantum link model (QLM), both with and without annealed charges, comparing critical temperatures and string tensions, and finding that the QLM with annealed charges has a similar T_c/√σ value to the standard Z_2 gauge theory, while the model without charges presents a new universal value.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this study is to further understand the phenomenon of confinement in gauge theories, particularly through the examination of simpler lattice gauge theories such as quantum link models. The researchers aim to investigate how the addition of annealed charges affects the confinement-deconfinement transition, which was previously shown to qualitatively change when these charges are added to the spin-1/2 U(1) QLM.

Method: The method involves a detailed analysis of the finite temperature phase transition within the spin-1/2 U(1) quantum link model, both with and without the presence of annealed charges. This includes the extraction of critical temperatures (T_c) and exponents, as well as the comparison of effective string tensions (σ) at zero and finite temperatures for the two scenarios.

Result: The results show that the ratio of the critical temperature to the square root of the string tension (T_c/√σ) for the QLM with annealed charges is comparable to that of the usual Z_2 gauge theory. In contrast, the QLM without annealed charges exhibits a distinct, new universal value for T_c/√σ, indicating a different behavior in the confinement-deconfinement transition.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the study is that the presence of annealed charges in the spin-1/2 U(1) quantum link model significantly influences the characteristics of the confinement-deconfinement transition, aligning the behavior of the model with that of the Z_2 gauge theory, whereas the absence of these charges leads to a unique phase transition with a new universal value for T_c/√σ.

Abstract: The phenomenon of confinement is arguably the key feature of gauge theories regularly studied in high-energy and condensed matter physics. This causes hadrons to exist as stable particles in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) as bound states of quarks (and gluons), and strings in pure gauge theories. The promise of realizing such strings in quantum simulator setups have fueled extensive studies of a plethora of simpler lattice gauge theories (LGTs). Quantum link models (QLMs) generalize Wilsonian LGTs and yield richer scenarios of confinement. For example, the string joining two static charges in the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ $U(1)$ QLM fractionalize, giving rise to confining phases that break the point group and internal symmetries spontaneously. It was further shown in PRL 130, 071901 (2023) that adding annealed $Q = \pm 2$ charges to this model qualitatively changes the confinement-deconfinement transition. In this paper, we examine the finite temperature phase transition both in the presence and the absence of annealed charges through detailed extraction of critical temperature, $T_c$ , and exponents. The (effective) string tension $σ$ at zero and finite- temperatures are compared between the theory with and without annealed charges. The quantity $T_c/\sqrtσ$ for the QLM with annealed charges is similar to the usual $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory, while the theory without the charges provides a new universal value.

### [4] [Review of Pion and Nucleon Gluon Parton Distribution Functions from Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26443)
*William Good,Huey-Wen Lin*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper reviews recent advances in the calculation of gluon parton distribution functions (PDFs) using lattice QCD, focusing on the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET) and pseudo-PDF methods. It discusses the current state, impacts, and future directions of these studies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the limited understanding of gluon PDFs, a key component in hadron structure, due to experimental challenges. The breakthrough in calculating the Bjorken-$x$ dependence of PDFs directly from lattice QCD, especially through LaMET and pseudo-PDF methods, has driven this review.

Method: The method involves summarizing and discussing recent advances in lattice QCD calculations, particularly those employing the LaMET and pseudo-PDF frameworks, for determining the $x$-dependent structure of hadrons, with an emphasis on nucleons and pseudoscalar mesons.

Result: The result is a comprehensive overview of the current status of lattice QCD determinations of gluon PDFs, including the successes and limitations of the LaMET and pseudo-PDF approaches, as well as their impact on our understanding of hadron structure.

Conclusion: The conclusion highlights the significant progress made in first-principles studies of gluon PDFs, the remaining challenges, and the promising opportunities for future research in this area.

Abstract: Gluons play an essential role in the strong interaction that binds quarks into hadrons, as described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD). Understanding how the fundamental dynamics of QCD give rise to hadron structure remains one of the outstanding challenges in nuclear and particle physics. Parton distribution functions (PDFs) provide a successful framework for describing high-energy scattering processes involving hadrons. However, our knowledge of gluon PDFs remains comparatively limited because of the experimental challenges associated with directly constraining the gluon structure of hadrons. In recent years, a major breakthrough has enabled the calculation of the Bjorken-$x$ dependence of PDFs directly from lattice QCD. Among the available approaches, the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET) and pseudo-PDF frameworks have emerged as the most widely used methods and have produced the majority of lattice determinations of parton distributions. These developments have sparked rapid progress in first-principles studies of the $x$-dependent structure of hadrons, including gluon PDFs of the nucleon and pseudoscalar mesons. In this review, we summarize recent advances in lattice QCD calculations of gluon PDFs {using the LaMET and pseudo-PDF methods}, discuss the current status and impacts of these developments, and highlight the remaining challenges and future opportunities.
