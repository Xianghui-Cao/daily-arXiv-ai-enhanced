### [1] [Blind Spots of the Zwanziger Horizon Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12413)
*Daniel G. Tedesco*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the relationship between the first Gribov horizon and Zwanziger's horizon function, focusing on their spectral directions and accessibility for SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in 3D and 4D Euclidean spaces, using variational estimates and finite-volume calculations, with a separate analysis for the BPST background due to domain issues.

Motivation: The motivation is to understand if the spectral directions that signify the start of the Gribov horizon can be accessed by the sources in Zwanziger's horizon function, particularly when considering degenerate critical subspaces, without addressing the statistical relevance of these configurations in the Yang-Mills functional integral.

Method: The method involves the use of variational estimates and finite-volume calculations to characterize the threshold structure for smooth radial profiles of SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in three and four-dimensional Euclidean spaces. The regular-gauge BPST background is analyzed separately due to specific domain-related challenges.

Result: The results provide insights into the configurationwise spectral properties of the Faddeev-Popov operator and its thresholds, as well as the accessibility of the critical subspace to the sources of Zwanziger's horizon function, under the constraints of angular symmetry and radial profile control in the hedgehog backgrounds.

Conclusion: The conclusion would likely summarize the findings regarding the interplay between the Gribov horizon and Zwanziger's horizon function, the role of the radial profile in controlling Faddeev-Popov thresholds, and the implications for the understanding of the gauge-fixing procedure in non-Abelian gauge theories, while noting the exclusion of statistical weighting considerations.

Abstract: We examine the configurationwise relation between the first Gribov horizon, defined by loss of positivity of the Faddeev-Popov operator, and Zwanziger's horizon function, which probes the inverse operator through background-dependent sources. The analysis focuses on whether the spectral directions associated with the onset of the Gribov horizon are necessarily accessible to the sources entering the horizon function, including situations in which the critical subspace is degenerate. This question is studied for radial SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in three and four Euclidean dimensions, where angular symmetry constrains the source sector while the radial profile controls the ordering of Faddeev-Popov thresholds. Variational estimates and finite-volume calculations are used to characterize the threshold structure for smooth radial profiles. The regular-gauge BPST background is treated separately because of domain issues associated with zero-energy behavior and the horizon source in the full-space setting. The discussion is restricted to configurationwise spectral properties and does not address the statistical weighting of these backgrounds in the Yang-Mills functional integral.

### [2] [Topological properties around the Roberge-Weiss transition in $N_f = 2 + 1 + 1$ QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12883)
*Massimo D'Elia,Fabio Siliberto,Kevin Zambello*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study examines the topological properties of QCD with $N_f = 2+1+1$ at the Roberge-Weiss transition, using stout improved staggered fermions. It finds a rapid decay of the topological susceptibility above the transition temperature and a sharp transition in the fourth-order cumulant, which is more akin to pure gauge theories than full QCD.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To explore how the topological properties of QCD change across the Roberge-Weiss transition for specific values of the imaginary baryon chemical potential, particularly in the context of $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD with physical quark masses, and to compare these findings with the $N_f = 2 + 1$ case and with full QCD along the standard thermal line.

Method: The researchers used lattice QCD simulations with stout improved staggered fermions, focusing on two different values of the compactified dimension, $N_t = 8$ and $N_t = 10$. They analyzed the topological susceptibility and the fourth-order cumulant of the topological charge distribution, $b_2$, to investigate the nature of the phase transition and its universality class.

Result: The results show that the topological susceptibility remains nearly constant for temperatures below the Roberge-Weiss transition temperature, $T_{RW}$, and then decays rapidly for higher temperatures. The fourth-order cumulant, $b_2$, exhibits a sharp transition immediately after $T_{RW}$, which is consistent with the Dilute Instanton Gas Approximation and similar to transitions in pure gauge theories rather than the slower transitions observed in full QCD along the standard thermal line.

Conclusion: The Roberge-Weiss transition in $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD with physical quark masses displays a sharp change in topological properties, as evidenced by the behavior of the topological susceptibility and the fourth-order cumulant, indicating a closer resemblance to the phase transition in pure gauge theories than to the one found in full QCD without an imaginary chemical potential.

Abstract: We investigate the topological properties of QCD across the finite temperature Roberge-Weiss transition, which is found for particular values of the imaginary baryon chemical potential. Our study is conducted for $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD with physical quark masses, discretized via stout improved staggered fermions and considering mostly two different values of the compactifed dimension, $N_t = 8$ and $N_t = 10$. Results for $T_{RW}$ and for the associated universality class are consistent with those found in the $N_f = 2 + 1$ case with a slightly different discretization. The topological susceptibility appears to be practically constant for $T \lesssim T_{RW}$, then rapidly decaying for higher temperatures. The analysis of the fourth order cumulant of the topological charge distribution, $b_2$, reveals that it is compatible with the prediction of the Dilute Instanton Gas Approximation right after $T_{RW}$, showing thus a sharp transition, which is more similar to what observed in pure gauge theories rather than to full QCD along the standard thermal line, where instead a slower transition was observed in previous studies.
