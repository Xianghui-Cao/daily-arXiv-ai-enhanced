### [1] [TempLat: a versatile C++ engine for lattice field theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24908)
*Adrien Florio,Franz R. Sattler*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: TempLat is a C++ framework for lattice field theory simulations, optimized for performance and portability across different hardware, including CPUs and GPUs. It includes ParaFaFT, a library for parallel discrete Fourier transforms. Both are open-source and enhance the capabilities of CosmoLattice, an early-universe simulation library.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation behind this paper is to introduce a versatile and efficient tool (TempLat) for performing lattice field theory simulations that can be adapted to various hardware configurations, from classical-statistical to Monte Carlo contexts, ensuring high performance and future-proof extensibility. Additionally, there's a need for a scalable, parallel discrete Fourier transform library (ParaFaFT) that supports these simulations on diverse computing architectures.

Method: The authors developed TempLat using C++, leveraging expression templates for a symbolic language that closely mirrors mathematical expressions while generating highly optimized code. The framework utilizes Kokkos for performance portability, enabling it to run efficiently on large CPU clusters as well as NVIDIA and AMD GPUs. Furthermore, they created ParaFaFT, which is designed to perform parallel discrete Fourier transforms in any dimension, supporting the same range of hardware as TempLat.

Result: The results show that TempLat demonstrates strong and weak scaling on both CPU and GPU platforms, indicating its efficiency and scalability. ParaFaFT also exhibits good scaling properties, making it suitable for use in high-performance computing environments. These tools have been integrated into a new version of CosmoLattice, expanding its computational reach to include GPU support.

Conclusion: In conclusion, the introduction of TempLat and ParaFaFT offers researchers in the field of lattice field theory and beyond, powerful, flexible, and portable tools for conducting simulations. Their integration into CosmoLattice enhances the software's utility by extending its compatibility with modern computing architectures, thereby facilitating more complex and detailed studies of the early universe.

Abstract: We present TempLat, a C++ framework for lattice field theory simulations in arbitrary dimensions. Its symbolic language, built on expression templates, translates mathematical expressions almost verbatim into C++ while compiling to highly optimized kernels, in contexts ranging from classical-statistical to Monte Carlo simulations. TempLat is performance-portable: building on Kokkos, it supports large CPU clusters, as well as NVIDIA and AMD GPUs. Its abstraction of hardware into a device concept makes TempLat extensible to future architectures. We demonstrate excellent strong and weak scaling on both CPUs and GPUs. We also present ParaFaFT, a standalone parallel discrete Fourier transform library supporting arbitrary dimensions on all of the above hardware, and benchmark its scaling. Both are open source and available on GitHub, and power a new release of CosmoLattice, a widely used early-universe simulation library now available on GPUs.

Method: The method involves the use of a normalizing-flow approach, which integrates Gaussian Process priors with invertible neural networks. Result: The results show that the proposed framework successfully reconstructs the parton distribution functions from the synthetic data, adhering to the required physical constraints and demonstrating good extrapolation performance, even when dealing with limited Ioffe-time data.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn is that the normalizing-flow approach, utilizing Gaussian Process priors and invertible neural networks, is effective for PDF reconstruction and offers a promising technique for handling the challenge of limited experimental data in particle physics, ensuring both accuracy and physical consistency.

Abstract: We investigate a normalizing-flow approach for reconstructing parton distribution functions (PDFs) from synthetic matrix-element data. Our framework combines Gaussian Process priors with invertible neural networks to learn a posterior distribution over PDFs consistent with limited Ioffe-time data. We demonstrate that the architecture preserves physical constraints and extrapolation properties.

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper investigates the duality structure of lattice Maxwell theory with a theta term, focusing on the bosonic theory and the transformations that realize the theta subgroup of SL(2, Z). It also examines how these transformations affect electric and magnetic charges, and discusses a non-invertible defect with a specific fusion rule.

Motivation: The motivation is to explore the duality structure of lattice Maxwell theory, particularly in the context of the Hamiltonian Villain formulation, and to understand the role of the theta subgroup of SL(2, Z) within this theory. The study aims to provide insights into the behavior of electric and magnetic charges under these transformations, as well as the properties of non-invertible defects.

Method: The authors use a theoretical approach, constructing the S and T^2 transformations at the operator level. They analyze the effects of these transformations on the sectors with electric and magnetic charges, which are introduced through violations of the Gauss-law constraint and the Bianchi identity. Additionally, they examine a non-invertible defect by gauging a Z_N subgroup of the global U(1) 1-form symmetry.

Result: The results show that the constructed transformations indeed realize the theta subgroup structure of SL(2, Z). The S transformation exchanges electric and magnetic charges, while the T^2 transformation demonstrates the Witten effect. Furthermore, the fusion rule of the non-invertible defect is found to match the expected Tambara-Yamagami structure.

Conclusion: The paper concludes that the bosonic sector of lattice Maxwell theory with a theta term exhibits a rich duality structure, including the realization of the theta subgroup of SL(2, Z) and the associated charge exchange phenomena. The findings contribute to a deeper understanding of topological aspects and symmetries in gauge theories.

Abstract: We study the duality structure of lattice Maxwell theory with a theta term in the Hamiltonian Villain formulation. Reflecting the fact that odd-level Chern--Simons theory depends on a choice of spin structure, a complete realization of the full $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ structure would require fermionic degrees of freedom.

We construct these transformations at the operator level and show that they realize the theta subgroup structure of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb Z)$. We also extend the analysis to sectors with electric and magnetic charges, introduced as violations of the Gauss-law constraint and the Bianchi identity, respectively. We show that the $\mathcal S$ transformation exchanges electric and magnetic charges, while the $\mathcal T^2$ transformation realizes the Witten effect. Finally, we discuss a related non-invertible defect obtained by gauging a $\mathbb Z_N$ subgroup of the global $\mathrm{U}(1)$ $1$-form symmetry, and show that its fusion rule reproduces the expected Tambara--Yamagami structure.

### [5] [Radiative decays of the $h_c$ meson from lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25707)
*Mischa Batelaan,Jozef J. Dudek,Robert G. Edwards*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper investigates the radiative decays of the $h_c$ charmonium state to $γη$ and $γη'$ for the first time in lattice QCD, revealing a suppression in decay rates compared to experimental data, which is consistent with previous findings for $J/ψ$ decays. It also explores the implications for unmeasured Dalitz decays.

Motivation: The motivation is to expand on previous work regarding $J/ψ$ decays by studying the radiative decays of the $h_c$ state, which has not been done before in lattice QCD, and to understand the form-factors that influence both radiative and Dalitz decays.

Method: The authors use lattice QCD simulations with three-flavor lattices at a pion mass of about 391 MeV, implementing new techniques to study the $h_c$ as an excited state and to distinguish between the electric dipole and longitudinal form-factors.

Result: The results show that the radiative decay rates are suppressed relative to experimental values, similar to the suppression found in $J/ψ 	o γη^{(′)}$. The form-factors' $Q^2$ dependence is also studied, providing insights into the unmeasured Dalitz decays of the $h_c$ state.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the observed suppression in the radiative decay rates of $h_c$ to $γη$ and $γη'$ is consistent with earlier findings for $J/ψ$ decays, and the form-factor analysis suggests a specific behavior for the Dalitz decays, which could be tested in future experiments.

Abstract: We explore, for the first time in lattice QCD, the radiative decays to $γη$ and $γη'$ of the $J^{PC}=1^{+-}$ charmonium state, $h_c$. This work expands upon a previous calculation of $J/ψ$ decays to the same final states, incorporating novel technology to access the $h_c$ where it is an excited state and to separate the independent electric dipole and longitudinal form--factors. Results for the radiative decay rates, computed on three-flavor lattices with $m_π\sim $ 391 MeV show a suppression relative to experiment that is similar to that observed in the prior calculation of $J/ψ\to γη^{(\prime)}$. The timelike $Q^2$ dependence of both form--factors together influence the Dalitz decays, $h_c \to \ell^+ \ell^- η^{(\prime)}$, which are as yet unmeasured experimentally.
