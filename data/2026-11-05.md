### [1] [A VQE simulation of the transverse field Ising model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05379)
*Luke Bennett*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper describes the application of a variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) to estimate the ground state energy of a 4-spin transverse field Ising model (TFIM), demonstrating its performance in both simulated and real quantum environments, and comparing results with exact-diagonalisation benchmarks.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to showcase the capability of hybrid quantum-classical algorithms, specifically VQE, in simulating quantum systems and accurately estimating the ground-state energy, which is crucial for understanding condensed matter physics, including phenomena such as quantum phase transitions (QPT).

Method: The method employed is the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE), a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm. The researchers apply VQE to a 4-spin TFIM, using both a local noise model and actual IBM quantum hardware, to calculate the ground-state energy and to observe the quantum phase transition (QPT).

Result: The results show that the VQE algorithm can effectively estimate the ground-state energy of the 4-spin TFIM and reproduce the QPT. The performance of the VQE was assessed by comparing the ground-state energy estimates and the QPT behavior with those obtained from exact diagonalization, providing a benchmark for accuracy.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the study is that the VQE is a viable approach for simulating quantum systems and estimating their ground-state energies, even when using noisy quantum hardware. The comparison with exact-diagonalisation benchmarks validates the VQE's effectiveness and highlights its potential for further research in condensed matter physics.

Abstract: One of the most promising applications of quantum computing is the ability to simulate quantum systems. A quantity of particular importance in condensed matter physics is the ground-state energy of a system. Here, we present an implementation of a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm known as the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) to estimate the ground state energy of a prototypical quantum spin system. For this spin system, we use the transverse field Ising model (TFIM) which is known to exhibit a quantum phase transition (QPT) at a critical point. We apply the VQE to a $4$-spin TFIM and evaluate its performance using both a local noise model and IBM quantum hardware to estimate the ground-state energy and reproduce the QPT. The resulting ground-state energy estimates and QPT behaviour are compared with exact-diagonalisation benchmarks.

### [2] [Exact chiral symmetry, non-Hermiticity, and the axial anomaly on the lattice: A symmetry-class criterion for disorder-induced undoubling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05395)
*Laurence A. Jacobs*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper re-examines the chiral disordered fermions (CDF) operator, showing it preserves exact chiral symmetry but cannot reproduce the axial anomaly. It classifies similar constructions by the Dirac channel carrying lattice structure, unifying lattice-QCD undoubling with the classification of disordered Dirac fermions and resolving the disorder route to chiral lattice fermions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to revisit and analyze the CDF operator using contemporary methods, aiming to understand its behavior in preserving chiral symmetry and its limitations, especially regarding the axial anomaly, while also placing it within a broader context of disordered Dirac fermions and their classification.

Method: The method involves a theoretical analysis of the CDF operator, including an examination of the disorder-averaged operator's properties, such as non-Hermiticity, the impact on doublers, and the vanishing axial index. The authors also classify related operators based on the Dirac channel they use, comparing their characteristics and implications for chiral symmetry and the axial anomaly.

Result: Results show that the CDF operator maintains exact chiral symmetry but fails to produce the axial anomaly due to this very symmetry. Doublers are removed through non-Hermiticity rather than a Ginsparg-Wilson relation, and no formulation allows for a non-zero axial index. The study distinguishes between operators based on the type of disorder, leading to different physical outcomes, with the CDF and Kaplan's domain wall representing opposite ends of a spectrum.

Conclusion: Conclusions highlight that the CDF operator, despite preserving chiral symmetry, cannot replicate the axial anomaly. The work provides a unified view of lattice QCD undoubling and the classification of disordered Dirac fermions, clarifying the role of disorder in achieving chiral lattice fermions and setting the stage for future research into these areas.

Abstract: A lattice Dirac operator designed to preserve exact chiral symmetry by weighting the derivative with a quenched random field---the \emph{chiral disordered fermions} (\CDF) of [L.\ Jacobs, Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.\ \textbf{51}, 172 (1983)]---is reexamined with modern tools. We show that the disorder-averaged operator satisfies $\anticomm{\Deff}{\gfive}=0$ exactly, to all orders in disorder strength. Disorder lifts the doubler degeneracy, driving doublers off the real axis into a complex mass while the physical mode remains protected, but does so through non-Hermiticity rather than a Ginsparg--Wilson relation. Consequently the axial index vanishes in every formulation: the Hermitian trace $\Tr(\gfive D)$, the bi-orthogonal index for non-Hermitian operators, and the algebraic Riesz-projector index, including at exceptional points. The \CDF operator therefore cannot reproduce the axial anomaly: its failure is enforced by the exact chiral symmetry it preserves. We classify neighboring constructions by the Dirac channel carrying the lattice structure. Disorder in the $\g_μ$ channel preserves chirality, realizes an Altland--Zirnbauer chiral class, and cannot carry an index; disorder in the $\mathbb{1}$ mass channel breaks chiral symmetry and yields Callan--Harvey/domain-wall physics. A one-parameter family interpolates between them, with the 1983 operator and Kaplan's domain wall as opposite limits, distinguished not by disorder itself but by the channel it occupies. This unifies lattice-QCD undoubling with the tenfold-way classification of disordered Dirac fermions and settles the disorder route to chiral lattice fermions.

Abstract: This thesis studies the Schwinger model in (1 + 1) dimensions using a lattice formulation and its dual representation. We implement Monte Carlo algorithms adapted to the dual representation, including local and non-local updates that allow admissible configurations to be sampled. For the massive model, we also analyse the emergence of the sign problem and its effect on the estimation of observables through reweighting.

These observables characterise the dependence on the mass, coupling and topological angle ($θ$), and identify the regions in which the sign problem becomes most significant. Finally, we discuss the numerical results and the usefulness of the dual formulation for investigating gauge theories in non-perturbative regimes.

TL;DR: The paper investigates the on-shell subtraction process in finite-volume analyses, focusing on how the correction changes as the mass parameter decreases. Method: The authors use a dimensional-regularization prescription to derive analytic expressions for the finite volume corrections associated with on-shell subtraction for various momentum structures. They also perform a numerical examination of these corrections' behavior as the mass parameter varies, particularly looking at the transition from nonzero to zero mass.

Result: For a nonzero mass parameter, the finite volume corrections due to on-shell subtraction are exponentially suppressed at large volumes. Abstract: On-shell subtraction is a relevant step in finite-volume analyses, which is to isolate an on-shell singularity and evaluate regular momentum-dependent factors at their on-shell values. For short-range interactions with a finite mass gap, the correction associated with this on-shell subtraction is exponentially suppressed at sufficiently large volume and can therefore be neglected at the corresponding accuracy. We examine how this correction changes as the relevant mass parameter decreases. Using a common dimensional-regularization prescription, we derive analytic expressions for the resulting finite volume correction for representative momentum structures and examine their behavior numerically. For a nonzero mass parameter, the corrections are indeed exponentially suppressed at large $μL$, whereas in the massless limit this suppression is replaced by an enhanced finite-volume dependence. More importantly, the $S$-wave contribution can develop an infrared divergence of finite volume correction in the massless limit. These results clarify the conditions under which on-shell subtraction can be applied and provide guidance for assessing such corrections in finite-volume hadronic calculations involving long-range effects, including QCD+QED.

### [6] [Simulating a chiral gauge theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06880)
*Laurens Lootens,Hersh Singh*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper introduces a novel method for simulating 1+1-dimensional abelian chiral gauge theories using a lattice regularization with clock-and-shift qudits and Z_N gauge fields, demonstrating that it can reproduce the expected infrared physics of the continuum model. The approach is practical for both classical and quantum computing platforms.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to address the challenge of nonperturbatively simulating chiral gauge theories, which are essential to the Standard Model, while overcoming the Nielsen-Ninomiya no-go theorem. The authors aim to provide an efficient and practical solution for such simulations in 1+1 dimensions that could work on both classical and quantum computers.

Method: The authors develop a gauge-invariant Hamiltonian lattice regularization scheme using clock-and-shift qudits and Z_N gauge fields. They ensure that the global chiral symmetry and its 't Hooft anomaly are preserved exactly modulo N, and they use infinite matrix product states to simulate the 34-50 model, a canonical 1+1-dimensional chiral gauge theory.

Result: The simulation results show excellent agreement with analytical predictions, indicating that even with a finite-dimensional local Hilbert space at N=6, the expected infrared physics of the 34-50 model is accurately reproduced. This suggests that the proposed method effectively captures the desired physical behavior of the system.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the research is that the new lattice regularization technique provides a viable and practical way to simulate general abelian chiral gauge theories in 1+1 dimensions. It offers a promising avenue for further study and application in both classical and quantum computational contexts.

Abstract: Chiral gauge theories underlie the Standard Model, yet none has been simulated nonperturbatively. Lattice constructions that evade the Nielsen-Ninomiya obstruction entail complex fermion measures or infinite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces. Here we provide a gauge-invariant Hamiltonian lattice regularization of 1+1-dimensional abelian chiral gauge theories built from clock-and-shift qudits with $\mathbb{Z}_N$ gauge fields, in which the global chiral symmetry and its 't Hooft anomaly survive exactly mod $N$. Anomaly cancellation on the lattice lets the Gauss laws be imposed and solved exactly, reducing the theory to an unconstrained qudit chain. Using infinite matrix product states, we simulate the canonical 1+1-dimensional chiral gauge theory, the 34-50 model, and find excellent agreement with analytic predictions, showing that a finite-dimensional local Hilbert space at $N=6$ reproduces the expected infrared physics of the continuum 34-50 model. This provides an efficient and practical solution to the problem of simulating general abelian chiral gauge theories in 1+1 dimensions, both on classical and quantum computers.
