### [1] [Thermal modifications of the B-meson spectrum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14440)
*Rachel Horohan D'Arcy,Benjamin Jäger,Seyong Kim,Jon-Ivar Skullerud*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study uses anisotropic lattice ensembles to investigate the behavior of B and B_s mesons at non-zero temperatures, revealing that thermal effects are more pronounced on B meson states than on B_s states, with indications of B-meson bound state dissolution above T_c.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a first-principles understanding of heavy-light meson behavior in hot QCD matter, which is necessary for interpreting experimental results from open heavy flavor production in high-energy heavy-ion collisions.

Method: Anisotropic lattice ensembles from the FASTSUM collaboration with Nf=2+1 dynamical Wilson-clover fermions are used. The researchers employ relativistic light quark propagators and NRQCD for b quark propagators to study the B and B_s spectra at elevated temperatures.

Result: Above the critical temperature (T_c), the thermal effects on B meson states are found to be more significant compared to those on B_s mesons. There is evidence suggesting the dissolution of B-meson bound states above T_c.

Conclusion: The research concludes that the thermal environment has a differential impact on B and B_s mesons, with B mesons showing greater sensitivity to the thermal conditions, indicating their potential to dissolve into the quark-gluon plasma phase.

Abstract: A first-principles investigation of heavy-light meson behaviour in hot QCD matter is essential for the interpretation of experimental results associated with open heavy flavour productions in relativistic heavy-ion collision experiments. Using anisotropic lattice ensembles from the FASTSUM collaboration with Nf=2+1 dynamical Wilson-clover fermions at non-zero temperatures, we study the B and B_s spectra at non-zero temperature. We use relativistic light quark propagators, while the b quark propagators are computed with a non-relativistic effective theory (NRQCD). We find that above T_c, thermal effects on the B meson states are more significant than those on the B_s states. Also, our results support the dissolution of the B-meson bound states above T_c.

### [2] [Fermionic quantum field theories as probabilistic cellular automata in three dimensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14749)
*Piotr Stanisław Topa*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a 3D construction of a layered probabilistic cellular automaton, named 'coincube', which is equivalent to fermionic quantum field theories. It demonstrates the automaton's unitary step operator, local Grassmann action, and single-carrier excitation as a Weyl fermion. The research also explores the interaction, transport properties, and limitations within this model, including a dichotomy for quaternionic event classes.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation for this paper stems from Wetterich's previous work, which established the equivalence between certain 1+1 dimensional probabilistic cellular automata and fermionic quantum field theories (QFTs). The open challenge was to extend this concept into three dimensions, which the authors of this paper have addressed by constructing a 3D layered automaton that could serve as an equivalent to a fermionic QFT in 3D space.

Method: The method employed in this paper involves the creation of a 3D layered probabilistic cellular automaton called 'coincube'. This automaton uses conversion blocks that form the real quaternion representation of Cl(0,3) and is controlled by binary environment fields. The researchers derive the unitarity of the step operator, extract the local Grassmann action, and analyze the single-carrier excitation, among other characteristics. They also investigate the interaction mediated by the medium, the constraints on ballistic transport, and the behavior of Abelian sign gauges.

Result: The results include the demonstration of the coincube's unitary step operator, the mechanical extraction of the local Grassmann action, and the identification of the single-carrier excitation as a Weyl fermion with an isotropic leading cone. The study reveals that the Dirac branch has a gap determined by the annealed Bloch operator, and the media-mediated interaction follows a specific formula. The research further uncovers obstructions, such as the finite velocity sets for ballistic transport and the inability to sustain a protected two-component node. Additionally, the paper establishes a dichotomy for quaternionic events regarding isotropic cones and in-state unitarity.

Conclusion: In conclusion, the paper successfully constructs a 3D layered probabilistic cellular automaton, 'coincube', that is equivalent to a fermionic QFT. It provides exact results for the automaton's properties and interactions, while also highlighting the inherent limitations and trade-offs, such as the dichotomy between isotropic cones and factorized transport. These findings contribute to the understanding of how to simulate fermionic systems using cellular automata in higher dimensions.

Abstract: Wetterich showed that certain 1+1D probabilistic cellular automata are equivalent to fermionic QFTs and posed the 3D construction as an open problem. We construct one: a layered automaton ("coincube") whose conversion blocks form the real quaternion representation of Cl(0,3), controlled by binary environment fields whose streaming outruns the carrier ("fresh tape") -- no bit is consulted twice, and the single-particle ensemble propagator equals the annealed Bloch operator at every time. Exact results: the step operator is unitary for an explicit complex structure; the local Grassmann action is extracted mechanically; the single-carrier excitation is a Weyl fermion with an isotropic leading cone (slope ratios 1 within errors, factorized transport excluded by >=10 error bars); at finite momentum the node carries a parity-odd anisotropy c(q)kx ky kz/|k|, cancelled on the Dirac branch whose gap is 2 arctan[qm/(1-qm)], while off-resonant spectators stay massless; media-mediated interaction obeys Ug = (1-2gq^2) U. Obstructions: ballistic transport admits only finite velocity sets; Abelian sign gauges leave the factorized surface; no protected 2-component node survives an exhaustive search. Finally, a dichotomy for the quaternionic event class: an isotropic cone forces overdamping -- Q = w_0/Gamma <= pi/(2 ln 2) at all densities, 0.66 at working density -- while in-state unitarity forces factorized transport. Postselected two-boundary amplitudes evade this, exactly unitary at all times under the fresh tape, with induced weight positivity open: the relativistic object here is an amplitude whose probabilistic reading is unsettled.

### [3] [Renormalized Polyakov loop in accelerated gluodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15699)
*Victor V. Braguta,Vladimir A. Goy,Jayanta Dey,Artem A. Roenko*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores accelerated gluodynamics over a wide range of temperatures and accelerations using lattice simulations in the Rindler coordinates, comparing it with non-accelerated gluodynamics adjusted by the Tolman-Ehrenfest law, and finds agreement near the critical temperature but disagreement at higher temperatures.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate the behavior of accelerated gluodynamics across different temperatures and accelerations, and to compare the results obtained from the Rindler coordinate system with those from a non-accelerated system that has been modified according to the Tolman-Ehrenfest law.

Method: Lattice simulation in the co-moving reference frame defined by Rindler coordinates, development of a renormalization prescription for calculating the renormalized Polyakov loop, and calculation of the static quark free energy and effective mass. The comparison is made between the accelerated system and an approximation using non-accelerated gluodynamics with a temperature gradient.

Result: The study found that the observables agree well near the critical temperature when comparing the two approaches, but there is a discrepancy at higher temperatures, which is suggested to be due to corrections to the Tolman-Ehrenfest law in the context of Rindler gluodynamics.

Conclusion: The research concludes that while the Tolman-Ehrenfest law can provide a good approximation for accelerated gluodynamics near the critical temperature, at higher temperatures, the effects of acceleration on the gluodynamics are not fully captured by this law, indicating the need for further investigation into the corrections to the Tolman-Ehrenfest law in the context of Rindler spacetime.

Abstract: In this paper we investigate accelerated gluodynamics for a broad intervals of temperature and acceleration. Our study is carried out within lattice simulation in the co-moving reference frame parameterized by the Rindler coordinates. We developed the renormalization prescription that allowed us to calculate renormalized Polyakov loop as a function of coordinate in the Rindler spacetime. Using the data for the renormalized local Polyakov loop, we calculated spatial dependence of the static quark free energy and effective mass of static quark. Besides the Rindler coordinates, it is believed that accelerated gluodynamics can be approximated utilizing non-accelerated gluodynamics with a properly adjusted temperature gradient in accordance with the Tolman-Ehrenfest law. We compared these approaches for the observables under study. It was found that they agree quite well close to the critical temperature and demonstrate disagreement at higher temperatures. We believe that this disagreement might be attributed to the Tolman-Ehrenfest law corrections which appear in the Rindler gluodynamics.

### [4] [Continuous Hasenbusch transport towards gauge diffusion with fermions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15959)
*Akio Tomiya*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces an analytic pseudofermion sampler for lattice gauge theories, which uses continuous Hasenbusch transport and does not require explicit fermion determinant evaluation or a learned model. It demonstrates compatibility with physical observables in the two-flavour Schwinger model and provides a method to reduce log-weight variance, improving upon existing methods without altering generated fields or increasing computational demands.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the challenge of incorporating dynamical fermions into diffusion samplers for lattice gauge theories, aiming to improve the efficiency and accuracy of these samplers without relying on computationally expensive operations or neural networks.

Method: The method involves developing an analytic pseudofermion sampler that utilizes continuous Hasenbusch transport, shifted linear solves, and a finite-path correction. This approach avoids the need for explicit fermion determinant evaluations and learned pseudofermion models, and includes a novel correction based on the Wilson operator trace to control weight fluctuations.

Result: The proposed method was successfully demonstrated in the two-flavour Schwinger model, showing corrected physical observables consistent with independent references. Additionally, the introduced correction based on the Wilson operator trace effectively reduced log-weight variance, as verified on new gauge backgrounds, without changing the generated fields or requiring additional Dirac operator applications.

Conclusion: The research offers a viable and efficient way to incorporate fermions into gauge diffusion processes, providing both an analytic option for pseudofermion sampling and a strategy to manage correction weights, thereby contributing to the advancement of lattice gauge theory simulations.

Abstract: Incorporating dynamical fermions is a central challenge for diffusion samplers of lattice gauge theories. We propose an analytic pseudofermion sampler based on continuous Hasenbusch transport as a component for gauge diffusion. The construction uses shifted linear solves and a finite-path correction, requiring neither explicit fermion determinant evaluation nor a learned pseudofermion model. We demonstrate the coupling in the two-flavour Schwinger model without neural networks, obtaining corrected physical observables compatible with independent references. We also show why accurate covariance transport can leave large weight fluctuations, tracing them to the backward transition density. This analysis leads to a correction based on the Wilson operator trace that reduces log-weight variance without changing the generated fields or increasing the number of Dirac operator applications. The predicted improvement is verified on previously unused gauge backgrounds. These results provide an analytic option for incorporating fermions in gauge diffusion and a guide to controlling its correction weights.
