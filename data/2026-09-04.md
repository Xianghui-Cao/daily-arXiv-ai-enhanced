### [1] [Real-Time String Dynamics in $3+1$D Lattice Quantum Electrodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04114)
*Helen Zwölfer,Giovanni Cataldi,Umberto Borla,Jad C. Halimeh*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first real-time simulations of string breaking in 3+1D lattice quantum electrodynamics using tree tensor networks, revealing dynamics at strong coupling and classifying competing channels off resonance. The findings provide diagnostics and benchmarks for future quantum simulators of higher-dimensional LGTs.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand real-time string dynamics in three spatial dimensions, which is crucial for relating quantum simulations of lattice gauge theories (LGTs) to the physical dimensionality of QED and QCD, including the complexities introduced by transverse fluctuations and local processes.

Method: The method employed involves the use of tree tensor networks to simulate the quench dynamics of electric flux strings within a 3+1D U(1) LGT framework that includes dynamical matter. The researchers analyze both on-resonance and off-resonance conditions, with a detailed classification of competing processes and the development of a channel-resolved perturbation theory to explain the observed phenomena.

Result: At strong coupling, the study finds that string breaking occurs resonantly under specific conditions related to mass and gauge couplings, leading to the conversion of electric energy into matter-antimatter pairs. Off resonance, the analysis identifies multiple competing channels, such as pair production, string deformations, and flux loops, and shows how these processes can deplete the string sector even when not at resonance. A channel-resolved perturbation theory is developed that successfully reproduces the dynamics and their Fourier spectrum.

Conclusion: The research establishes key diagnostics and benchmarks for the simulation of higher-dimensional LGTs, providing a deeper understanding of the complex dynamics involved in string breaking and the transition of energy between different forms in 3+1D lattice quantum electrodynamics.

Abstract: Understanding real-time string dynamics in three spatial dimensions is essential for connecting quantum simulations of lattice gauge theories (LGTs) to the physical dimensionality of QED and QCD, where transverse fluctuations and competing local processes proliferate. We present the first real-time simulations of string breaking in $3+1$D lattice quantum electrodynamics. Using tree tensor networks, we simulate the quench dynamics of electric flux strings in a $3\!+\!1$D U(1) LGT with dynamical matter. At strong coupling, the string breaks resonantly at a sharp resonance condition of mass and gauge couplings, converting electric energy into matter--antimatter pairs that screen the static charges. Off resonance, we classify all competing channels---pair production, string deformations and extensions, and flux loops---whose multiplicity, extensive for pair production and flux loops, depletes the string sector even far from resonance. A channel-resolved perturbation theory quantitatively reproduces these dynamics and their Fourier spectrum. Our results establish diagnostics and benchmarks for upcoming quantum simulators of higher-dimensional LGTs.

### [2] [Non-Abelian string melting and thermalization in an open lattice gauge theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04117)
*Erick Parra Verde,Giovanni Cataldi,Stefan Kühn,Giuseppe Magnifico,Jad C. Halimeh*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper investigates the impact of a thermal environment on the dynamics of a 1+1D SU(2) Yang-Mills lattice gauge theory with dynamical matter, showing that the thermal medium can melt the chromoelectric flux string between a quark-antiquark pair and that the thermalization time is non-monotonic with respect to the coupling strength. The study also reveals a mesonic bias in the steady state due to non-Abelian matter structure, with thermalization times decreasing as temperature increases, which contrasts with Abelian models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation for this paper is to understand how genuinely non-Abelian gauge structures affect the dissipative real-time dynamics in an open-system lattice gauge theory, particularly when coupled to a thermal environment, as most previous work has focused on Abelian settings.

Method: The authors use a gauge-preserving Lindblad evolution to couple the 1+1D SU(2) Yang-Mills LGT with dynamical matter to a thermal scalar environment. They solve the resulting equations using tensor networks, and in the strong-dephasing limit, they apply a Schrieffer-Wolff expansion to map the dynamics to a classical exclusion process.

Result: The results show that the thermal medium melts the chromoelectric flux string by delocalizing color charges and screening the flux, and the thermalization time exhibits non-monotonic behavior with the environment coupling. Additionally, there is a mesonic bias in the steady state, and the thermalization time decreases with increasing temperature, which is contrary to what is observed in Abelian models like the Schwinger model.

Conclusion: The study establishes a framework for analyzing thermalization and string dynamics in open non-Abelian lattice gauge theories, demonstrating that non-Abelian matter structure leads to distinct behaviors compared to Abelian systems, such as a temperature-dependent thermalization time and a mesonic bias in the steady state.

Abstract: Open-system lattice gauge theory (LGT) has so far been developed predominantly in Abelian settings, leaving open how genuinely non-Abelian gauge structure reshapes dissipative real-time dynamics. Here, we study a $1+1$D SU(2) Yang--Mills LGT with dynamical matter coupled to a thermal scalar environment through a gauge-preserving Lindblad evolution, which we solve using tensor networks. Starting from a quark--antiquark pair connected by a chromoelectric flux string, we find that the thermal medium melts the string by delocalizing the color charges and screening the flux; on resonance, this dissipative melting competes with and delays coherent string breaking. The thermalization time is non-monotonic in the environment coupling, decreasing through environment-assisted transport at weak dissipation before increasing in a quantum-Zeno regime. In the strong-dephasing limit, a Schrieffer--Wolff expansion maps the dynamics to a classical exclusion process and yields the Liouvillian thermalization time analytically. Beyond these generic open-system effects, the non-Abelian matter structure produces a systematic mesonic bias in the steady state, while the thermalization time decreases with temperature, in contrast to the Abelian Schwinger model trend and in qualitative agreement with pNRQCD studies of the quark--gluon plasma. These results establish a gauge-preserving framework for thermalization and string dynamics in open non-Abelian lattice gauge theories.

### [3] [Lattice QCD calculation of the pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $Θ$-term](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04129)
*Fangcheng He,Tanmoy Bhattacharya,Rajan Gupta,Emanuele Mereghetti*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents lattice QCD results for the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $\overlineΘ$ term, using three 2+1+1-flavor ensembles. The coupling is extracted in two ways and the more precise result is obtained using low energy effective field theory methods or the AWI. Contamination from the $Nπ$ excited states is controlled using chiral perturbation theory and the axial Ward identity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to provide a quantitative measure of the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ that arises due to the QCD $\overlineΘ$ term, which is important for understanding the fundamental symmetries of the strong interaction and has implications for the matter-antimatter asymmetry in the universe. The work also aims to address and control the contamination from the $Nπ$ excited state in the calculation of nucleon matrix elements.

Method: The method employed in this study involves the use of lattice QCD with 2+1+1-flavor HISQ ensembles at a lattice spacing of approximately 0.09 fm and pion masses of 313, 226, and 138 MeV. The researchers extract the coupling $\bar{g}_0$ through two approaches: (i) from the matrix element of the correlation between the pseudoscalar current and the topological charge, and (ii) using low energy effective field theory methods or the axial Ward identity. They utilize chiral perturbation theory to manage the $Nπ$ excited state contamination.

Result: The first extraction method yields a noisy result after removing the $Nπ$ contamination and extrapolating to the physical pion mass: $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=-7(63)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$. A more precise result, $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=17.4(1.9)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$, is obtained using the second method. The paper discusses in detail the $Nπ$ excited state contamination and how it can be controlled using the axial Ward identity.

Conclusion: The research concludes with a more accurate determination of the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ using low energy effective field theory or the axial Ward identity, providing an improved value over previous estimates. It also highlights the importance of controlling the $Nπ$ excited state contamination in such calculations and provides a framework for doing so.

Abstract: We present lattice QCD results for the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $\overlineΘ$ term from the analysis of three 2+1+1-flavor ensembles generated with highly improved staggered quarks (HISQ) by the MILC collaboration. These ensembles are at lattice spacing $a\approx 0.09~\text{fm}$ and pion masses of 313, 226 and 138 MeV, respectively. The coupling $\bar{g}_0$ is extracted in two ways. First, from the matrix element of the correlation between the pesudoscalar current and the topological charge evaluated between the nucleon ground state. The data for correlation functions with both the pseudoscalar and axial vector exhibit large contamination from the $Nπ$ excited state. We show that these can be controlled at the leading order using chiral perturbation theory ($χ$PT) and the axial Ward identity (AWI, also called the partially conserved axial current (PCAC) relation). The result after removing the $Nπ$ contamination and extrapolating to the physical pion mass is, however, noisy: $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=-7(63)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$. The more precise result $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=17.4(1.9)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$ is obtained using low energy effective field theory methods or equivalently the AWI. Since contamination from the $Nπ$ excited states arises in the calculation of many nucleon matrix elements, we give an extended discussion on them and the use of the AWI for controlling them in the calculation of $\bar g_0$.

### [4] [First Two-Hadron Form Factor from QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04142)
*Felipe G. Ortega-Gama,Raúl A. Briceño,Ivan M. Burbano,Robert G. Edwards*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first QCD determination of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state, specifically the forward electromagnetic π⁺π⁺ + γ → π⁺π⁺ amplitude at m_π ≈ 400 MeV. The study uses lattice QCD and a finite-volume formalism to calculate the required matrix elements and spectrum, leading to a single real-valued form factor that is consistent with fundamental physical principles, thus validating the approach for future studies on resonances and multi-hadron bound states.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a first-principles QCD calculation of the electromagnetic form factor for a two-hadron system, which cannot be directly accessed in lattice calculations due to the absence of asymptotic scattering states. This work aims to open up new possibilities for studying the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of hadronic systems, including resonances and bound states, using a nonperturbative finite-volume framework.

Method: The method involves using lattice QCD to compute three-point correlation functions that couple π⁺π⁺ finite-volume states, from which the electromagnetic matrix elements are extracted. By combining these results with the known finite-volume spectrum, the researchers constrain the infinite-volume π⁺π⁺ + γ → π⁺π⁺ amplitude. They also apply constraints from Lorentz symmetry, unitarity, and analyticity to describe the amplitude in terms of a single real-valued form factor.

Result: The result is the successful derivation of a single real-valued, energy-dependent form factor for the π⁺π⁺ + γ → π⁺π⁺ process, which satisfies the Ward-Takahashi identity. This finding provides a validation of the finite-volume approach used and sets the stage for further investigations into the electromagnetic properties of hadronic systems.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the derived form factor, which is consistent with key physical principles, represents the first QCD-based confirmation of the finite-volume methodology. This opens the door to future research on the electromagnetic structure and electroweak interactions of complex hadronic systems, such as resonances and multi-hadron bound states.

Abstract: We present the first QCD determination of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state. In particular, we calculate the QCD contribution to the forward electromagnetic $π^+π^+ + γ\toπ^+π^+$ amplitude at $m_π\approx 400$~MeV using lattice QCD. Because lattice calculations are performed in a finite Euclidean spacetime, where asymptotic scattering states are absent, this amplitude cannot be accessed directly from correlation functions. Instead, we can constrain this and related amplitudes nonperturbatively using a finite-volume formalism that requires two ingredients: the discrete finite-volume spectrum and finite-volume matrix elements of the electromagnetic current. We calculate three-point correlation functions coupling $π^+π^+$ finite-volume states and extract the corresponding electromagnetic matrix elements. Combining these results with the previously determined spectrum, we constrain the infinite-volume $π^+π^+ + γ\toπ^+π^+$ amplitude in the forward limit. Using constraints from Lorentz symmetry, unitarity, and analyticity, we describe this amplitude in terms of a single real-valued energy-dependent two-hadron form factor. The resulting amplitude and form factor agree with the Ward-Takahashi identity across all energies and moving frames considered, providing the first QCD validation of this finite-volume approach and a pathway toward first-principles studies of the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of resonances and multi-hadron bound states.
