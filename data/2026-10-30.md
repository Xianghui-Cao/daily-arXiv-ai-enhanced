### [1] [Complex Langevin kernels for real-time SU(N) lattice gauge theory in 3+1 dimensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37800)
*Kirill Boguslavski,Paul Hotzy,David I. Müller*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces and applies new kernel modifications to complex Langevin dynamics for real-time SU(N) gauge theories in 3+1 dimensions, enabling stable simulations up to a real-time extent of 3.5β. The authors also provide a generalized correctness criterion for the modified dynamics, which advances the use of complex Langevin methods in Minkowski space for non-Abelian gauge theories.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to extend the applicability of complex Langevin (CL) dynamics to real-time simulations of SU(N) gauge theories in 3+1 dimensions, specifically to achieve longer real-time extents and improve stability in lattice gauge theory (LGT).

Method: The method involves the introduction of new kernel modifications, including a contour-dependent phase kernel and a local, field-dependent kernel based on traced plaquettes, applied to anisotropic CL dynamics on a rectangular regularized Schwinger-Keldysh time contour. Additionally, the authors generalize the CL correctness criterion to validate the convergence of the simulations with these kernels.

Result: The results show that the new kernels allow for stable simulations of pure SU(2) gauge theory up to a real-time extent of 3.5β at a bare coupling g=0.5, which is an improvement over the baseline anisotropic kernel. The generalized correctness criterion provides a tool to assess the proper convergence of the simulations using the modified dynamics.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the new kernel modifications significantly enhance the capability of complex Langevin dynamics for real-time LGT simulations, providing a robust framework for studying non-Abelian gauge theories at extended time scales, and setting the stage for future work on unequal-time correlation functions.

Abstract: We investigate complex Langevin (CL) dynamics for real-time SU($N$) gauge theories in 3+1 dimensions and introduce new kernel modifications tailored for lattice gauge theory (LGT). Using the anisotropic kernel as a baseline, we demonstrate that on a rectangular regularized Schwinger-Keldysh time contour, stable simulations are possible for pure SU(2) gauge theory up to a real-time extent of $2β$ at a bare coupling $g=0.5$, where $β$ denotes the inverse temperature. We show that a contour-dependent phase kernel extends this reach to $3β$, while a local, field-dependent kernel based on traced plaquettes achieves $3.5β$. These constructions represent the first application of such kernels to LGTs, especially in 3+1 dimensions. To validate the approach, we generalize the CL correctness criterion based on the drift magnitude to kernelled dynamics, providing a practical tool to assess correct convergence. Our results substantially advance the applicability of CL to real-time simulations in Minkowski space, establishing a framework for controlled studies of non-Abelian gauge theories at unprecedented time scales. A forthcoming paper applies these developments to the computation of unequal-time correlation functions.

### [2] [Agentic AI for QCD perturbation theory or: how I learned to stop worrying and love the bot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37960)
*Erik Lundstrum*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses the use of AI coding agents for perturbative QCD calculations, highlighting their efficiency and the transferability of instructions across similar calculations, which can be completed with minimal user effort after initial setup.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to leverage the capabilities of AI coding agents in performing complex yet modular and verifiable calculations, specifically in the domain of perturbative Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD).

Method: The method involves developing and applying specific instructions for AI agents to conduct one- and two-loop perturbative QCD calculations, with a strong emphasis on preventing common errors and ensuring the accuracy of each step through verification processes.

Result: Results indicate that once an AI agent successfully completes a calculation, its learned approach can be effectively applied to other similar problems, allowing for rapid and reliable completion of further perturbative QCD calculations with minimal additional input from human users.

Conclusion: The conclusion suggests that agentic AI systems are well-suited for perturbative QCD calculations due to their ability to follow detailed instructions, perform verifiable steps, and transfer learned procedures, significantly reducing the time and effort required for subsequent similar calculations.

Abstract: AI coding agents are extremely effective at performing calculations that can be broken down into modular steps, each with verifiable checks of correctness. Perturbative QCD calculations satisfy both of these properties and we find that they are well-suited to solution by agentic AI systems. We discuss our methods for instructing and verifying the work of agents in one- and two-loop perturbative calculations, with a focus on pre-empting common mistakes. We find that once an agent has successfully completed one perturbative calculation, its instructions transfer very well to other similar calculations which can then be completed and verified in a matter of hours with little effort from the user.

### [3] [Hot and dense QCD equation of state from the complex Langevin equation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38466)
*Michael Mandl,Dénes Sexty,Daniel Unterhuber*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents the QCD equation of state at high temperatures and baryon chemical potentials using complex Langevin simulations, with continuum-extrapolated results for energy, entropy, and enthalpy densities, as well as specific heat capacity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To explore the QCD equation of state under conditions of high temperature and baryon chemical potential, which is important for understanding the properties of matter in extreme conditions, such as those found in the early universe or in heavy-ion collisions.

Method: The authors utilized complex Langevin simulations, a numerical method that allows for the exploration of the theory's phase space even when there is a sign problem, and they performed these simulations at the physical pion mass. They also extrapolated their results to the continuum limit to remove lattice artifacts.

Result: The study produced continuum-extrapolated results for the energy, entropy, and enthalpy densities, as well as the specific heat capacity, as functions of both baryon chemical potential and temperature, providing a more accurate description of the QCD matter under these conditions.

Conclusion: The research provides new insights into the thermodynamic properties of QCD matter at high baryon density and temperature, contributing to the understanding of the phase structure of QCD and its implications for astrophysics and heavy-ion collision experiments.

Abstract: We discuss the QCD equation of state at temperatures above the crossover and for high baryon chemical potentials, obtained via the first ever complex Langevin simulations at the physical pion mass. In particular, we present continuum-extrapolated results for the energy, entropy, and enthalpy densities, as well as for the specific heat capacity as a function of baryon chemical potential and temperature.

### [4] [Enhanced Signal and Excited-State Control in Nonlocal Lattice Correlators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39944)
*Shi-Yi Zhong,Jian-Hui Zhang,Dian-Jun Zhao*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A new fitting strategy is proposed for lattice calculations of parton physics, which enhances the signal-to-noise ratio and reduces excited-state contamination. This method, involving the ratio of nonlocal to local correlators, has been successfully applied to quark and gluon PDF, quark GPD, and TMDPDF, showing improved precision, stability, and an extended accessible range. It simplifies the analysis of nonforward matrix elements and is applicable to a wide range of structures and contributions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation stems from the limitations in current methods for extracting nonlocal correlation functions in lattice calculations, such as poor signal-to-noise ratios at large Euclidean separations and high hadron momenta, and significant excited-state contamination. There's a need for a more robust approach that can improve the precision and reliability of these calculations, particularly for accessing larger hadron momenta and simplifying the analysis of nonforward matrix elements like GPDs.

Method: The paper introduces a novel fitting strategy where the ratio of nonlocal to local correlation functions is utilized. This method projects out the ground-state dynamics with a cleaner signal, significantly less sensitive to excited states compared to traditional approaches. The technique was demonstrated on various parton distribution functions (quark and gluon PDF, quark GPD, and TMDPDF) to showcase its effectiveness and versatility across different scenarios, including connected and disconnected contributions, and for both Dirac and momentum structures.

Result: The results indicate that this new fitting strategy provides consistent outcomes with conventional three-point/two-point ratio fits but with greatly enhanced precision, stability, and an ability to access a broader range of hadron momenta. The method notably improves the analysis of nonforward matrix elements, such as GPDs, by simplifying the procedure and extending the applicability to other nonlocal correlators, thereby offering immediate benefits for existing lattice programs focused on hadron structure.

Conclusion: The proposed fitting strategy effectively addresses key challenges in lattice QCD calculations related to parton physics, specifically enhancing the signal-to-noise ratio and reducing sensitivity to excited states. It is a versatile and powerful tool, capable of being applied to a variety of physical scenarios, and represents a significant advancement toward achieving precision in the study of hadron structure through lattice calculations.

Abstract: Nonlocal correlation functions underpin lattice calculations of parton physics. However, their extraction is limited by a rapidly deteriorating signal-to-noise ratio with increasing Euclidean separation and hadron momentum, and by excited-state contamination. We propose a new fitting strategy that enhances control over both: by taking the ratio of nonlocal to local correlators, we can project out the dominant ground-state dynamics with a signal that is much cleaner and far less sensitive to excited states than the conventional approach. We demonstrate the method on the quark and gluon PDF, the quark GPD and TMDPDF. The results are consistent with those from the conventional three-point/two-point ratio fit but exhibit markedly improved precision and stability, and extend the accessible range to larger hadron momenta. For nonforward matrix elements such as GPDs, our strategy greatly simplifies the analysis procedure. Our method applies to general Dirac and momentum structures, for connected and disconnected contributions, and extends naturally to other nonlocal correlators, making it immediately deployable in existing lattice programs toward precision hadron structure.

### [6] [Complex potential and the thermal width of quarkonium in quark-gluon plasma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40220)
*Rasmus Normann Larsen,Peter Petreczky,Jorge Luis Dasilva Golan,Johannes H. Weber*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study examines the complex heavy quark-antiquark potential in 2+1 flavor QCD at non-zero temperature and its relation to the thermal quarkonium width, providing estimates for charmonia and bottomonia thermal widths that are compared with T-matrix analysis results.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the behavior of quarkonia, specifically charmonia and bottomonia, in a high-temperature environment, which is relevant for heavy-ion collision experiments. The work aims to provide an alternative method for estimating the thermal width of quarkonia, as direct lattice QCD calculations are not yet possible for these states at high temperatures.

Method: The researchers analyze the complex heavy quark-antiquark potential obtained from lattice 2+1 flavor QCD simulations. They establish a connection between the imaginary part of the potential and the thermal width of quarkonium states. This relationship is then used to estimate the thermal widths of charmonia and bottomonia at high temperatures.

Result: The estimated thermal widths for some excited quarkonia states are found to be smaller than those obtained from the T-matrix analysis. This suggests a discrepancy between the two approaches and may indicate a need for further investigation into the dynamics of quarkonia in a thermal medium.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn is that there is a significant relation between the imaginary part of the heavy quark-antiquark potential and the thermal width of quarkonium, which can be used to estimate the thermal widths of charmonia and bottomonia. The comparison with T-matrix analysis indicates that the thermal widths for some excited states might be overestimated by the latter approach.

Abstract: We study the complex heavy quark anti-quark potential in lattice 2+1 flavor QCD at non-zero temperature. We point out a close relation between the imaginary part of the potential and the thermal quarkonium width. Using this relation, we estimate the thermal width of charmonia and bottomonia at high temperatures, which are not yet accessible with direct lattice QCD calculations of the quarkonium correlation functions and compare these to the results of the T-matrix analysis. For some of the excited quarkonia states, our estimates of the thermal width are smaller than the ones obtained in the T-matrix analysis.

</details>
