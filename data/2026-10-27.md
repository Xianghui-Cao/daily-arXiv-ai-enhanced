The lengths of irrelevant directions change differently with respect to the mass-term direction, and only two directions relevant to the continuum theory remain when considering long-distance modes. For the full distribution, the regularization differences persist at short distances. A specific deformation's length vanishes in both metrics, aligning with Kutasov's result. Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to understand how the Fisher metric behaves in the continuum limit of a 2D lattice field theory, specifically the Gross-Neveu model. This is important for understanding the number of measurements needed to distinguish nearby actions and for identifying the relevant and irrelevant directions in the parameter space as the lattice spacing goes to zero.

Method: The authors use the Fisher metric, which is derived from the probability distributions defined by the actions of the lattice field theory, to study the continuum limit. They focus on the large-N limit of the 2D lattice Gross-Neveu model with Wilson fermions, and they analyze the renormalization conditions and dimensional transmutation in terms of the expectation coordinates and natural coordinates. The analysis includes the scaling behavior of the Fisher metric for both the long-distance modes and the full auxiliary field distribution P(σ).

Result: The key findings are that in the continuum limit, the Fisher metric of the long-distance modes indicates that irrelevant directions shrink relative to the mass-term direction, while in the full distribution P(σ), the kinetic-term and mass-term directions shrink at the same rate. A particular deformation related to the propagator becomes indistinguishable from a change in the coupling as the lattice spacing approaches zero. The curvature of the coupling plane diverges logarithmically, and the critical point is found to be infinitely far in Fisher distance from any point with finite lattice spacing.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the Fisher metric provides insight into the structure of the theory in the continuum limit, showing a clear distinction between the behavior of long-distance modes and the full distribution. The results suggest that the irrelevant directions become negligible for long-distance physics, while short-distance physics retains some dependence on the lattice regularization. The divergence of the curvature and the infinite Fisher distance to the critical point highlight the non-trivial nature of the continuum limit in this model.

Abstract: When the Boltzmann weight is nonnegative, the actions of a lattice field theory define a family of probability distributions that forms a statistical manifold, and its Fisher metric gives the number of measurements needed to distinguish two nearby actions. An exception is the deformation that only adds a contact term to the propagator: as $a\to0$ its length goes to zero in both metrics, and it becomes indistinguishable from a change of the coupling $1/g^2$ (a lattice version of Kutasov's result). The curvature of the coupling plane at fixed kinetic term diverges as $(1/2)\log^2(1/(aΛ))$, not as the correlation volume often assumed in thermodynamic geometry, and the critical point is infinitely far in Fisher distance from any point at finite $a$.
