### [1] [Exotic $B_s$ mesons in the continuum from a nonperturbatively-tuned heavy quark action](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15377)
*R. J. Hudspith,Daniel Mohler,M. Stolz*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a Lattice QCD study predicting the masses and binding energies of two exotic mesons, $B_{s0}^*$ and $B_{s1}$, using a relativistic heavy-quark action for the b-quark, which is tuned non-perturbatively. The results show that these states are deeply bound in the continuum limit, with specific binding energies reported for both mesons.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to improve the accuracy of the mass and binding energy predictions for the $B_{s0}^*$ and $B_{s1}$ mesons by addressing previous systematic uncertainties, and to confirm if these states remain bound when taking the continuum limit.

Method: A relativistic heavy-quark action for the valence b-quark was used, which was fully non-perturbatively tuned using a neural network. This method allowed the researchers to take the continuum limit and reduce systematic errors from their earlier work with Lattice-NRQCD. The approach was validated by reproducing known experimental values for 1S hyperfine splittings and mass differences between certain mesons.

Result: The final results indicate that the $B_{s0}^*$ and $B_{s1}$ mesons have significant binding energies with respect to the $BK$ and $B^*K$ thresholds, specifically $-65.9(6.0)(3.0)_	ext{Iso}$ MeV and $-60.6(6.6)(3.0)_	ext{Iso}(1.0)_	ext{GEVP}$ MeV respectively, confirming that they are deeply bound states in the continuum limit.

Conclusion: The study concludes that the $B_{s0}^*$ and $B_{s1}$ mesons are indeed deeply bound states, with the new approach providing more accurate and reliable predictions for their binding energies, thereby reducing the systematic uncertainties present in previous determinations.

Abstract: In this work we predict the masses and binding energies of two $B_s$ exotic-meson candidates using Lattice QCD, namely the $B_{s0}^*$ and $B_{s1}$. We use a relativistic heavy-quark action for the valence b-quark in our simulations, tuned fully non-perturbatively by a neural network. This allows us to take the continuum limit and eliminates the largest systematic we attributed to our previous determination of these states using Lattice-NRQCD. This is the first Lattice QCD study to show that these states remain deeply bound in the continuum limit. We thoroughly benchmark our heavy-quark approach by reproducing the experimental values of the 1S hyperfine splittings of $B$ and $B_s$ mesons, as well as the mass splitting between the $B$ and $B_s$ mesons. Our final results yield binding energies with respect to the $BK$ and $B^*K$ thresholds of $-65.9(6.0)(3.0)_\text{Iso}$ MeV and $-60.6(6.6)(3.0)_\text{Iso}(1.0)_\text{GEVP}$ MeV for the $B_{s0}^*$ and $B_{s1}$ respectively.

### [2] [Operator subspace based method for the extraction higher energy levels in Lattice field theoretic systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16012)
*Ken-Ichi Ishikawa,Siddharth Ramesh Pandey*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces a new algorithm for spectral analysis in lattice QFT, using the first three time slices of the correlation function matrix to build the transfer matrix and extract energy eigenvalues. It reduces systematic errors and is efficient in extracting higher-excited-state energies, as demonstrated through simulations of harmonic and anharmonic oscillators, outperforming the standard GEVP method for these states but not for ground-state energy.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation for this work is to improve the techniques for spectral analysis of lattice quantum field theory (QFT) data, particularly for accurately extracting higher-excited-state energies while minimizing systematic errors from operator basis truncation.

Method: The authors propose an algorithm that utilizes the initial three time slices of the correlation function matrix to construct the transfer matrix and determine its energy eigenvalues. They reduce systematic errors by varying the dimension of the operator subspace and applying eigenvalue-variance extrapolation. The method is tested on lattice Monte Carlo simulations of quantum mechanical systems, specifically harmonic and anharmonic oscillators, and compared with the standard Generalized Eigenvalue Problem (GEVP) method.

Result: The results show that the proposed method performs better than the standard GEVP method when it comes to extracting intermediate excited state energies for both the harmonic and anharmonic oscillator systems. However, for ground-state energy extraction, the current form of the proposed method does not outperform the GEVP method, since it only uses the first three time slices.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the study is that the new algorithm is more effective for the extraction of higher-excited-state energies in lattice QFT, offering an improvement over the standard GEVP method, although it is less suitable for ground-state energy extraction.

Abstract: Given the importance of spectral analysis of lattice quantum field theory data, the advancement of techniques for the same remains critically important. Therefore, we propose an algorithm that uses the first three time slices of the correlation function matrix to construct the transfer matrix and extract its energy eigenvalues. Reduction of systematic error arising from truncating the operator basis is achieved by varying the operator subspace dimension and applying eigenvalue-variance extrapolation. Since the correlation functions in early time slices typically exhibit small statistical errors and retain a strong signal of higher-excited states, we expect our proposed method to perform well in extracting higher-excited-state energies. In this paper, we conduct two lattice Monte Carlo simulations for quantum mechanical systems, harmonic and anharmonic oscillators, to evaluate the efficiency of our proposed method for higher-excited-state energies. We compare the energy levels obtained using the standard GEVP method and our proposed method. We find that our method consistently outperforms the standard GEVP method in extracting intermediate excited states for both systems. Because our method has access only to the first three time slices, it is not preferable to the GEVP method for extracting the ground-state energy in its current form.

### [3] [Jump-Diffusion Stochastic Quantization for Euclidean Lattice Field Theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16451)
*Alexander Rothkopf*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces a generalized form of stochastic quantization using jump-diffusion processes, which are effective in exploring action landscapes with barriers and improving ergodicity. It applies these methods to address topological freezing in 2d U(1) gauge theory.

Motivation: The motivation is to improve and restore ergodicity in the exploration of action landscapes, particularly where traditional diffusion approaches are inefficient, such as in the case of topological freezing in 2d U(1) gauge theory.

Method: The method involves the use of jump-diffusion processes, which include non-continuous paths (Lévy flights), to generalize stochastic quantization. The authors also explore various strategies for constructing efficient jump updates.

Result: The result shows that the application of jump-diffusion processes can efficiently explore action landscapes with barriers, thereby addressing the issue of topological freezing in 2d U(1) gauge theory more effectively than traditional diffusion methods.

Conclusion: The conclusion suggests that the generalization of stochastic quantization through jump-diffusion processes provides a promising approach to enhancing ergodicity in complex systems, with a demonstrated application in overcoming topological freezing in 2d U(1) gauge theory.

Abstract: We construct the natural generalization of stochastic quantization (in the Markovian sense) by considering jump-diffusion processes. This class of stochastic processes exhibits non-continuous paths, so-called Lévy flights. In the presence of jumps, action landscapes with barriers can be efficiently explored, improving and even restoring ergodicity where traditional diffusion approaches become inefficient. We explore different strategies for constructing efficient jump updates, which we deploy to address the benchmark problem of topological freezing in 2d U(1) gauge theory.

### [4] [Configurational Temperature in the 3D XY Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16677)
*Kutloano Nkojoana,Anosh Joseph*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study explores the configurational temperature estimator's effectiveness in diagnosing Monte Carlo and Langevin simulations of a 3D XY model with an imaginary chemical potential, finding it to be a useful diagnostic tool for algorithm correctness and thermalization, especially in scenarios with sign problems. The results show agreement with theoretical predictions at small coupling values, but deviations in the ordered phase are attributed to finite-size and discretization effects.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to validate the use of the configurational temperature estimator as a diagnostic tool for ensuring the internal consistency of numerical sampling algorithms in lattice field theory, particularly in the context of simulations that involve an imaginary chemical potential and where traditional validation methods may not be reliable due to sign problems.

Method: Simulations were carried out using real Langevin dynamics and the Metropolis Monte Carlo algorithm on an 8^3 lattice, over a range of coupling values (β=0.2-0.7), to calculate the action density and the configurational temperature estimator, and then compare these with strong-coupling expansion predictions and the expected value of unity in the symmetric phase, respectively.

Result: The action density results matched well with the strong-coupling expansion predictions for small β, validating the simulation approaches. The configurational temperature estimator showed good agreement with its expected value in the symmetric phase, while systematic deviations occurred in the ordered phase, which were linked to finite-size and discretization artifacts.

Conclusion: The configurational temperature estimator is concluded to be a valuable diagnostic for assessing thermalization and the correctness of algorithms in lattice field theory simulations, with particular importance for studies involving a real chemical potential where sign problems can complicate conventional validation techniques.

Abstract: We investigate the configurational temperature estimator as a diagnostic tool for Monte Carlo and Langevin simulations of the three-dimensional XY model with an imaginary chemical potential. This estimator depends only on the field configurations. It provides a stringent internal consistency check for numerical sampling algorithms. We perform simulations using both real Langevin dynamics and the Metropolis Monte Carlo algorithm on an $8^3$ lattice across a range of coupling values, $β= 0.2 - 0.7$. Our results for the action density are in excellent agreement with strong-coupling expansion predictions at small $β$, providing an important validation of both simulation approaches. The measured value of the configurational temperature estimator shows good agreement with its expected value of unity in the symmetric phase. However, systematic deviations appear in the ordered phase. We attribute these deviations primarily to finite-size effects and discretization artifacts associated with the relatively small lattice volume. Our results demonstrate that the configurational temperature estimator provides a valuable diagnostic for assessing thermalization and algorithmic correctness in lattice field theory simulations. Such diagnostics are particularly important in preparation for studies at real chemical potential, where sign problems arise and conventional validation methods become less reliable.

### [5] [Quantum Simulation of QCD in Axial Gauge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16783)
*Xiaojun Yao*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores the quantum simulation of SU(3) non-Abelian gauge theory with fermions in 4D, using a lattice Hamiltonian in axial gauge. It provides a method to solve the temporal component of the gauge field and offers an efficient transformation between local field and momentum bases. The authors also present a quantum algorithm for time evolution and analyze its gate complexity, concluding that the required quantum resources scale polynomially with respect to volume, energy, time, accuracy, and bare Hamiltonian parameters.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a feasible approach for the quantum simulation of SU(3) non-Abelian gauge theory coupled with fermions in 3+1 dimensions, which is significant for understanding the strong force and potentially for applications such as quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

Method: The method involves employing a lattice Hamiltonian in the axial gauge, solving the temporal component of the gauge field, and using local quantum Fourier transforms to switch between field and momentum representations. A quantum algorithm for time evolution is analyzed, including Trotterization, quantum Fourier transform, and Jordan-Wigner transformations, with explicit quantum circuit constructions provided.

Result: The results include a proof on the number of qubits needed for describing states up to a certain energy with given accuracy, and an analysis of the scaling of CNOT and single-qubit rotation gates per Trotter step, showing polynomial scaling with system parameters.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the quantum resources necessary for simulating real-time dynamics of lattice QCD, including the number of qubits and the complexity of the quantum circuits, scale polynomially with the volume, energy, time, accuracy, and the bare coupling constant, making it a promising direction for future quantum simulations.

Abstract: We study quantum simulation of SU(3) non-Abelian gauge theory dynamically coupled with fundamental fermions in $3+1$ dimensions by employing the lattice Hamiltonian in axial gauge that avoids Gauss's law constraints. The temporal component of the gauge field is analytically solved in terms of independent field degrees of freedom and a lattice regulated Green's function. The axial gauge condition is trivially maintained in time evolution, even under Trotterization. The gauge field degrees of freedom are expressed in the local field basis and can be efficiently transformed into the canonical conjugate momentum basis by local quantum Fourier transforms. We prove the number of qubits needed for describing all states up to an energy $E$ with an accuracy $ε$ on a lattice of volume $V$ at bare coupling $g$ is bounded as $16n_A V + 12n_f V$, where $n_A \approx \log_2 (\frac{64 E' V^{4/3}}{π^2ε} + \frac{32\sqrt{2}g n_f E'^{1/2} V^{7/6}}{\sqrt{3}π^3ε^{1/2}} ) $ is the number of qubits needed for each independent gauge field per site with a shifted energy $E'$, and $n_f$ denotes the number of fermion flavors. We then analyze a quantum algorithm for time evolution that is based on Trotterization, quantum Fourier transform, and Jordan-Wigner transformation, for which quantum circuits can be explicitly constructed under arbitrary gauge field truncation and digitization. We find the numbers of CNOT and single-qubit rotation gates both scale as $O(n_A^4 V^{4/3}) + O(V^{5/3})$ per Trotter step for fixed $n_f\leq 6$. We conclude that quantum resources needed for simulating real-time dynamics of lattice QCD scale polynomially with volume, energy, time, accuracy, and bare Hamiltonian parameters.
