### [1] [Confinement and String Breaking in the Compact Abelian Higgs Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27550)
*Blake Senseman,Zane Ozzello,Yannick Meurice,Stephen Mrenna*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper introduces a simplified model, a spin-1 truncation of the Compact Abelian Higgs Model, to study QCD phenomenology, particularly confinement and string breaking, using current simulation methods. It employs DMRG methods on around 100 sites to investigate the spectrum of string-like excitations, and by adding a local chemical potential, it enables the measurement of physical features like string tension and effective meson mass, as well as an assessment of string stability not covered in previous studies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a simple and accessible model for studying key aspects of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) such as confinement and string breaking, which are typically beyond the reach of real-time simulations due to technological limitations. The work aims to use this model to explore the low-energy regime of 1+1D scalar electrodynamics, where heavy modes are excluded, and to provide new insights into the behavior of string-like excitations under varying conditions.

Method: The method involves creating a spin-1 truncation of the Compact Abelian Higgs Model, simulated on qutrit sites, and applying DMRG (Density Matrix Renormalization Group) techniques to a system of approximately 100 sites. A local chemical potential is introduced to simulate external charges, allowing for the investigation of string tension, effective meson mass, and the stability of strings under different parameters.

Result: The results show that the model can be used to effectively measure the string tension and effective meson mass, with the ability to characterize the stability of strings by varying the chemical potential. This approach offers a way to examine the physical features of interest in a more controlled and measurable environment compared to previous models.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the research is that the proposed simplified model, when combined with DMRG methods and the introduction of a local chemical potential, provides a viable framework for investigating QCD-related phenomena, including confinement and string breaking. This allows for a detailed analysis of string-like excitations and their properties, contributing to a deeper understanding of QCD dynamics.

Abstract: While real-time simulation of Quantum Chromodynamics remains technologically out of reach, simplified models for studying elements of QCD phenomenology abound. This work presents a simple model, a spin-1 truncation of the Compact Abelian Higgs Model simulated on qutrit sites, in which confinement and string breaking is accessible to current simulation methods. In the low-energy regime of 1+1D scalar electrodynamics, the heavy modes are integrated out, producing a spin chain effective Hamiltonian in which Gauss' law is implicitly satisfied. We study the spectrum of string-like excitations using DMRG methods on the order of 100 sites. We demonstrate that an added, local chemical potential, playing a role analogous to external charges, permits parameter-dependent measurements of physical features of interest like the string tension and effective meson mass. Varying the chemical potential also permits a characterization of string stability not assessed in prior studies of confining lattice models.

### [2] [Pion Transition Form Factor in Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27868)
*Shihao Su,Liuming Liu,Peng Sun*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper examines the neutral pion transition form factor using lattice QCD, finding that connected and disconnected contributions have the same sign, with the disconnected part being about 1% of the connected part, allowing for constructive interference.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to investigate the neutral pion transition form factor in lattice QCD and to confirm the nature of the contributions from connected and disconnected parts, which are important for understanding the structure of the pion and its interactions with photons.

Method: The method involves the use of a recently proposed blending technique to estimate all-to-all propagators, employing the distillation framework for external pion states, and evaluating electromagnetic currents in the full blending space on an N_f=2+1 lattice ensemble.

Result: The results show that the disconnected contribution to the neutral pion transition form factor is around 1% of the connected contribution and that both contributions have the same sign, leading to constructive interference of probability amplitudes.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the blending method provides an efficient way to calculate the neutral pion transition form factor, and the confirmation of the sign and relative magnitude of the disconnected contribution is significant for theoretical predictions and experimental comparisons.

Abstract: We investigate the neutral pion transition form factor $F_{π^0γ^\astγ^\ast}(q_1^2,q_2^2)$ in lattice QCD and confirm that the connected and disconnected contributions have the same sign. We employ the recently proposed blending method, which supplies an unbiased and cheap estimators for the required all-to-all propagators. The external pion states are treated within the distillation framework, while the electromagnetic currents are evaluated in the full blending space. Numerical tests are performed on an $N_f=2+1$ lattice ensemble. Our result shows that the contribution of the disconnected part is approximately $1\%$ of that of the connected part and enables constructive interference of probability amplitudes.

Method: The method employed in this paper is a documented case study from the field of lattice QCD. It presents a factual narrative of the case, which serves as a basis for reflection and discussion on the application of current practices and guidelines in realistic collaborative environments, pointing out the tensions between different aspects of research practice.

Abstract: Questions of authorship, credit attribution, and the recognition of research software contributions have become increasingly prominent in those constantly growing research areas for which software constitutes an essential part of the research process. While general guidelines and best practices exist, their application in concrete situations often raises nontrivial interpretative and procedural issues. This article presents a documented case study from lattice QCD research, illustrating how the development of numerical strategies, long-term software infrastructure, and conceptual extensions of existing work can give rise to complex questions of authorship, priority, and credit attribution. Rather than discussing the underlying scientific results, the focus is on the sequence of events, the role of software as a long-term research infrastructure, and the interaction with established mechanisms for credit attribution and research integrity assessment. The aim of this work is to contribute to transparency and discussion on how current practices and guidelines are applied in realistic collaborative environments, and to highlight structural tensions that may arise between open scientific collaboration, software sustainability, and traditional notions of authorship. The article is intended as a factual account and reflection on research practice, and aims to stimulate discussion on whether additional community standards for recognising long-term research software contributions may be beneficial within lattice QCD.

### [4] [Exact chiral symmetry with quantum signal processing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28524)
*Henry Lamm,Alessandro Roggero,Hersh Singh,Luca Spagnoli*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A QSP algorithm for the overlap fermion Hamiltonian is presented, which maintains the Ginsparg-Wilson relation with a controllable error. This allows for efficient quantum simulations of Dirac fermions with near-exact chiral symmetry, and demonstrates an analogy to the construction of an extra dimension in domain-wall fermions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a more efficient method for simulating Dirac fermions on a quantum computer while preserving chiral symmetry, which is crucial for many physical theories. The goal is to achieve this with a computational cost that scales favorably and to explore the connection between the physics of overlap fermions and the efficiency of quantum algorithms.

Method: The authors introduce a Quantum Signal Processing (QSP) algorithm designed for the overlap fermion Hamiltonian. This algorithm ensures the preservation of the Ginsparg-Wilson relation, which is key for maintaining chiral symmetry, up to a small, adjustable error. The method leverages the logarithmic relationship between the precision of the simulation and the computational cost, making it more efficient than other methods such as domain-wall fermions, particularly in terms of qubit usage.

Result: The result is that the QSP algorithm can simulate the overlap Hamiltonian with only a logarithmic overhead in the desired precision compared to the Wilson-Dirac Hamiltonian. Additionally, the study shows that while there is a slight increase in circuit complexity over domain-wall fermions, the overall qubit costs are reduced. The work also provides a deeper understanding of how the QSP algorithm constructs an additional dimension, similar to what happens in domain-wall fermions, reflecting the underlying physics of the overlap fermions.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the research is that the QSP algorithm offers a highly efficient way to simulate overlap fermions, which are essential for models requiring exact chiral symmetry. The algorithm not only reduces the qubit requirements but also provides insights into the intrinsic nature of overlap fermions by effectively generating an extra dimension, akin to the boundary conditions of domain-wall fermions.

Abstract: We give a quantum signal processing (QSP) algorithm for the overlap fermion Hamiltonian which preserves the Ginsparg-Wilson relation up to a controllable error $ε_e$. Quantum simulations of Dirac fermions with exact chiral symmetry are thus nearly free: applying the overlap Hamiltonian costs only a factor logarithmic in $ε_e$ more than the Wilson-Dirac Hamiltonian. Comparing to domain-wall fermions, a mild overhead is found in circuit complexity while reducing qubit costs. We show how QSP effectively constructs an extra dimension when simulating the overlap operator, illustrating that the scaling of quantum algorithms reflects the deeper physics of overlap fermions arising at the boundary of domain-wall fermions.
