### [1] [All-mode approach for general tensor renormalization group method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02394)
*Katsumasa Nakayama,Shinji Takeda*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces the application of the stochastic all-mode technique to tensor renormalization group (TRG) schemes, which eliminates systematic errors during the coarse-graining process, and showcases its use in higher-order TRG for 2D and 3D Ising models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address and eliminate potential systematic errors that can occur during the coarse-graining step of the TRG approach, while acknowledging that statistical errors from random noise will still be present. The authors aim to provide a more reliable method for TRG that can be applied to various TRG methods by employing the squeezer method.

Method: The method involves applying the stochastic all-mode technique, which uses the squeezer method as a key element, to general coarse-graining schemes within the TRG approach. The squeezer method allows the all-mode technique to be utilized, making it applicable to many TRG methods that can be described using squeezers. The new method is demonstrated with higher-order TRG on two- and three-dimensional Ising models.

Result: The results show that the stochastic all-mode technique, when applied to the TRG, is successful in removing systematic errors associated with the coarse-graining step. It is also shown to be applicable to higher-order TRG, indicating its versatility across different dimensions and orders of TRG, particularly in the context of the Ising model simulations.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the stochastic all-mode technique, with the use of the squeezer, provides an effective way to perform coarse-graining in TRG without introducing systematic errors, thereby improving the reliability of TRG calculations. This method has broad applicability to TRG schemes and can enhance the accuracy of numerical simulations in complex systems like the Ising model.

Abstract: We apply the stochastic all-mode technique to general coarse-graining schemes of the tensor renormalization group (TRG) approach. The technique possesses a notable characteristic of being entirely free from systematic errors which can potentially arise in the coarse-graining step, while it has statistical errors caused by random noise. The key element in our generalization is employing a method called squeezer, and the all-mode technique can be used when it is constructed. Since many TRG methods can be described in terms of the squeezers, our approach can be applicable in all such cases. In this work, we demonstrate the new method with higher-order TRG in the two- and three-dimensional Ising model.

### [2] [Towards Precision-Controlled Partonic Structures from First Principles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02430)
*Jinchen He*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This dissertation covers first-principles calculations of hadron's internal structure using lattice QCD and effective field theory, focusing on parton distributions with controlled uncertainties. It advances the understanding from collinear to 3D transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) distributions and includes novel lattice determinations of TMDs, essential for global QCD analyses and precision studies at facilities like the Electron-Ion Collider. Additionally, it explores the use of machine learning to improve the efficiency of lattice gauge simulations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the internal structure of hadrons through nonperturbative Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) by calculating partonic observables with controlled systematic uncertainties. The work aims to progress from one-dimensional collinear parton distributions to three-dimensional transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) distributions, which provide a more detailed picture of the hadron's partonic structure. Furthermore, there is an interest in enhancing the computational methods used in lattice QCD through machine-learning techniques to achieve more efficient simulations.

Method: The methods employed include lattice QCD and effective field theory for first-principles calculations of partonic observables. The large momentum effective theory (LaMET) framework is utilized for state-of-the-art calculations of pion distribution amplitudes and nucleon parton distributions, addressing renormalization, excited-state contamination, and other systematics. A Coulomb-gauge formulation is introduced to simplify ultraviolet structure, and neural field transformations are integrated into Hybrid Monte Carlo for potential acceleration of lattice simulations via machine learning.

Result: The results consist of advanced calculations of pion distribution amplitudes and nucleon parton distributions with improved control over systematic errors. There are also new lattice determinations of nucleon TMD parton distributions, the Collins-Soper kernel, and intrinsic soft functions, as well as pion TMD observables. The machine-learning approach shows promise in reducing autocorrelation and improving the performance of lattice simulations, particularly as the lattice spacing is refined.

Conclusion: The dissertation provides significant contributions to the field of hadron structure, offering precise lattice QCD calculations that are crucial for global QCD analyses and future experimental programs such as the Electron-Ion Collider. The research also opens up the possibility of using machine learning to enhance the computational efficiency of lattice QCD, potentially leading to more accurate and faster simulations of hadronic systems.

Abstract: The internal structure of hadrons is governed by nonperturbative Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). This dissertation presents first-principles calculations of partonic observables using lattice QCD and effective field theory, with controlled systematic uncertainties, advancing from collinear structure to transverse-momentum-dependent distributions (TMDs) that encode the three-dimensional partonic structure of hadrons. Within the large momentum effective theory (LaMET) framework, this work presents state-of-the-art calculations of pion distribution amplitudes and systematic studies of nucleon parton distributions, with control of renormalization, excited-state contamination, Fourier-transform systematics, and power corrections. A Coulomb-gauge formulation of quasi-distributions simplifies ultraviolet structure by avoiding Wilson-line related linear divergences, with Gribov-copy effects found to be negligible at current statistical precision. Building on these developments, this dissertation reports lattice determinations of nucleon TMD parton distributions, the Collins-Soper kernel, the intrinsic soft function, and pion TMD observables. These results provide nonperturbative inputs for global QCD analyses and the precision hadron-structure program, including the Electron-Ion Collider. In parallel, this work explores machine-learning acceleration of lattice gauge simulations through neural field transformations embedded in Hybrid Monte Carlo. In two-dimensional U(1) tests, the method reduces autocorrelation and improves performance toward finer lattice spacing, suggesting potential applications to more efficient lattice QCD simulations.

### [3] [Critical coupling and $Δ\langleφ^2\rangle$ in three-dimensional $φ^4$ theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03028)
*Stephan Durr,Tolga S. H. Kiel*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the φ^4 theory in 3D, determining the critical parameter for phase transition and extrapolating to find key values of f_c and Δ⟨φ^2⟩_c/λ in the continuum and infinite-volume limit.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the behavior of the φ^4 theory at the phase transition between the symmetric and broken phases, and to determine universal quantities in the continuum and infinite volume limits.

Method: Simulations are performed for various self-couplings (hatλ) to find the critical bare parameter (hatμ_{0mathrm{c}}^2(hatλ)). The system is then taken to an infinite volume and a universal scheme is applied. A continuum extrapolation is carried out to obtain the value of f_c, which is defined as the limit of hatλ^2/hatμ_{mathrm{c}}^2(hatλ) as hatλ approaches zero. Additionally, the change in the expectation value of φ^2 at the critical point, Δ⟨φ^2⟩_c/λ, is determined.

Result: The value of f_c is found to be 18.808(78)_{mathrm{tot}}, with statistical and systematic uncertainties. The quantity Δ⟨φ^2⟩_c/λ is -0.00303(17)_{mathrm{tot}} when both the continuum and infinite-volume limits are considered, also with combined uncertainties.

Conclusion: The study provides precise determinations of the critical parameter and associated universal quantities in the φ^4 theory, contributing to our understanding of the phase structure and critical phenomena in this model.

Abstract: We consider the $φ^4$ theory with $φ(x)\in\mathbb{R}$ in three Euclidean dimensions. For a variety of self-couplings $\hatλ$, the critical (bare) parameter $\hatμ_{0\mathrm{c}}^2(\hatλ)$ is determined where the lattice-regulated system changes from the symmetric phase to the broken phase. Next, we send the box volume to infinity and switch to a universal scheme; this yields $\hatμ_\mathrm{c}^2(\hatλ)$ for each simulated $\hatλ$. Finally, a continuum extrapolation gives $f_\mathrm{c}=\lim_{\hatλ\to0} \hatλ^2/\hatμ_\mathrm{c}^2(\hatλ)$, and we find $f_\mathrm{c} = 18.808(64)_{\mathrm{stat}}(45)_{\mathrm{sys}} = 18.808(78)_{\mathrm{tot}}$. In addition, $Δ\langleφ^2\rangle_\mathrm{c}/λ= -0.00303(12)_{\mathrm{stat}}(12)_{\mathrm{sys}} = -0.00303(17)_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is determined in the combined continuum and infinite-volume limit.
