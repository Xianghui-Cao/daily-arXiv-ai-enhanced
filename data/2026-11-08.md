### [1] [The 2d Ising Model on Hyperbolic Lattices and the Role of Boundary Conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07045)
*Pallabi Dey,Debasish Banerjee,Arnab Kundu,Ritam Sinha*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the impact of different boundary conditions on the critical behavior of the ferromagnetic Ising model in hyperbolic tessellations, using simulation methods. It finds that open boundaries lead to non-mean-field scaling exponents and large finite-size effects, while wired boundaries show mean-field behavior. The study underscores the significance of boundary conditions for understanding critical phenomena in non-amenable lattices.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand how boundary conditions affect the critical behavior of the ferromagnetic Ising model on hyperbolic tessellations, which are non-amenable lattices, and to clarify the distinction between interior and whole-system observables in such systems.

Method: The researchers use worm and Metropolis simulations to analyze the ferromagnetic Ising model under open and wired boundary conditions. They examine the finite-size scaling of susceptibility, Binder crossings, and peak analysis, as well as the response of central spins to magnetic fields, and layer-resolved responses to weak boundary fields.

Result: For open boundaries, non-mean-field scaling exponents with significant finite-size effects were observed. With wired boundaries, the system showed mean-field magnetization exponent behavior, and the central-spin response to a uniform field was consistent with a specific value. The induced magnetization increased towards the interior when a weak boundary field was applied.

Conclusion: The findings emphasize the role of boundary conditions in the characterization of critical phenomena on non-amenable lattices and suggest that there may be a common universality class for the models with open boundaries, while wired boundaries exhibit mean-field characteristics.

Abstract: We investigate the role of boundary conditions in the critical behaviour of the ferromagnetic Ising model on regular hyperbolic tessellations. Using worm and Metropolis simulations, we compare open boundaries with wired boundaries implemented through a common auxiliary spin. For open boundaries, the susceptibility exhibits volume-based finite-size scaling with non-mean-field scaling exponents that are approximately stable across four tessellations, supporting the possibility of a common universality class. Comparisons with Binder crossings and susceptibility peaks reveal substantial finite-size effects. For wired boundaries, the central-to-auxiliary spin correlator exhibits radial scaling consistent with a mean-field magnetization exponent. The central-spin response to a uniform magnetic field provides a further consistency test of $δ=3$. Layer-resolved responses to a weak boundary field also reveal an enhancement of the induced magnetization towards the interior. Our results highlight the importance of boundary conditions and the distinction between interior and whole-system observables in characterizing critical phenomena on non-amenable lattices.

### [2] [Exact and Near-Exact Inverse Renormalization Group with Constrained Monte Carlo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07641)
*Dimitrios Bachtis*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper demonstrates the exactness of inverse renormalization group using constrained Markov processes, which are Monte Carlo simulations under constraints. It shows that these processes exactly invert the renormalization group at a fixed point and nearly exactly in its vicinity. The method is optimized for high-dimensional systems, and it's applied to 3D φ⁴ quantum field theory, allowing for the extraction of critical exponents without critical slowing down.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation behind this research is to develop and validate a method for the exact inversion of the renormalization group, which is crucial for understanding the behavior of physical systems across different scales. By employing constrained Markov processes, the authors aim to create an effective and stable computational technique that can handle high-dimensional systems and avoid the common issue of critical slowing down near phase transitions.

Method: The method involves the use of constrained Markov processes, a type of Monte Carlo simulation, to sample the degrees of freedom on a fine lattice while maintaining consistency with the coarse lattice. The researchers then optimize the renormalization group transformations by directly applying them to inversely renormalized configurations, ensuring the action is effectively mapped onto itself and enhancing computational stability. This process is demonstrated with the 3D φ⁴ quantum field theory, starting from a small lattice volume and constructing uncorrelated configurations for a much larger volume.

Result: The results show that the constrained Markov processes provide an exact inversion of the renormalization group at the fixed point and a near-exact inversion in the vicinity of the fixed point. The application to the 3D φ⁴ quantum field theory successfully generates uncorrelated, near-equilibrium configurations for a significantly increased lattice volume. Additionally, the dynamic critical exponent z is measured to be 0.03(2), indicating no critical slowing down during the inversion process.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper is that the constrained Markomial processes offer a powerful and precise tool for the inverse renormalization group, which is particularly advantageous for studying high-dimensional systems. The absence of critical slowing down and the ability to generate large-scale configurations make this approach a valuable addition to the methods available for investigating complex physical phenomena.

Abstract: We establish conditions for exactness of the inverse renormalization group via constrained Markov processes, a class of Monte Carlo simulations which sample the degrees of freedom within a block on a fine lattice under the constraint that they remain consistent with the corresponding degree of freedom on the coarse lattice according to a forward renormalization group transformation. We show analytically, for an arbitrary transformation, that constrained Markov processes correspond to an exact inversion of the renormalization group at the fixed point action, and to a near-exact inversion in the vicinity of the fixed point. We then optimize renormalization group transformations directly on inversely renormalized configurations to effectively map the action onto itself and ensure computational stability in the generation of configurations for high-dimensional systems. Starting from a set of uncorrelated configurations for the three-dimensional $φ^{4}$ quantum field theory at lattice volume $V=16^{3}$, we invert the renormalization group with constrained Monte Carlo to construct a set of uncorrelated, near-equilibrium configurations for $V=128^{3}$ and extract two critical exponents of the system. The inversion exhibits no critical slowing down, with dynamic critical exponent $z=0.03(2)$.

### [3] [First Overlap Formulation and Perturbative Results for Supersymmetric QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07745)
*Marios Costa,Elias Antoniou,Herodotos Herodotou,Eleni Ioannou,Constantinos Kartoudes,Gregoris Spanoudes,Haralambos Panagopoulos*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces a lattice formulation for N=1 supersymmetric QCD (SQCD) that is consistent with the Ginsparg-Wilson relation, focusing on the construction of Yukawa terms that preserve a modified chiral symmetry. It also includes one-loop perturbative calculations to determine field and mass counterterms, setting a foundation for future fine-tuning studies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a discretization method for SQCD that respects the Ginsparg-Wilson relation and maintains a lattice-modified chiral symmetry in the presence of Yukawa interactions, which is important for preserving the supersymmetry on the lattice.

Method: The method involves using overlap fermions and Lüscher's strategy, introducing auxiliary Dirac and Majorana fermionic fields, and performing functional integration over these fields to create ultralocal interaction terms. One-loop perturbative calculations are then carried out to compute self-energies and determine the necessary counterterms.

Result: The results show a successful implementation of the lattice action with the desired symmetries and the computation of self-energies for quark, gluon, gluino, and squark fields. The study demonstrates a reduction in the number of required counterterms compared to Wilson-type fermions, as expected.

Conclusion: The work lays a robust foundation for further perturbative studies of fine-tunings in gauge, Yukawa, and quartic couplings, providing a promising approach for maintaining supersymmetry in lattice simulations of SQCD.

Abstract: We present a lattice formulation of N=1 supersymmetric QCD (SQCD) based on overlap fermions, providing a discretization consistent with the Ginsparg-Wilson relation. Particular emphasis is placed on the construction of Yukawa terms that preserve a lattice-modified chiral symmetry involving overlap quarks and gluinos. To realize this modified chiral symmetry in the Yukawa sector, we follow Lüscher's strategy and introduce auxiliary Dirac and Majorana fermionic fields for the quark and gluino fields, respectively. After functional integration over these auxiliary fields, ultralocal interaction terms arise as additional contributions to the lattice action. Within this framework, and using the Wilson gauge action for gluons, we also carry out one-loop perturbative calculations. In particular, we compute the self-energies of the quark, gluon, gluino, and squark fields in the presence of quark and squark masses, enabling an unambiguous determination of the corresponding field and mass counterterms on the lattice. This work provides a robust foundation for future perturbative determinations of the fine-tunings of the gauge, Yukawa, and quartic couplings, and already shows, as expected, a reduction in the number of required counterterms relative to Wilson-type fermions.

### [4] [Quark-mass dependence of two-nucleon scattering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07864)

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the quark-mass dependence of low-energy nucleon-nucleon scattering in specific channels, using lattice-QCD calculations and comparing results to phenomenology and a previous calculation. It uses chiral effective field theory for analysis, estimating pion-mass derivatives at the physical point, and suggests that the deuteron unbinds near a pion mass of approximately 220 MeV.

Motivation: To understand the dependency of the visible Universe's properties on the parameters of the Standard Model, this research focuses on investigating how quark masses influence low-energy nucleon-nucleon interactions, specifically in the context of deuteron and dineutron channels, with an emphasis on the role of pion masses.

Method: The method involves conducting lattice-QCD calculations at two different pion masses (approximately 200 and 280 MeV) and analyzing the data using quantization conditions that include long-distance effects from one-pion exchange. The analysis also employs chiral effective field theory with two types of power countings to examine the pion-mass dependence of scattering lengths and effective ranges.

Result: The results show the pion-mass dependence of the nucleon-nucleon scattering, with estimations made for the pion-mass derivatives of inverse scattering lengths and binding momentum at the physical point. The study concludes that the deuteron is likely to unbind near a pion mass of about 220 MeV.

Conclusion: The research provides insights into the quark-mass dependence of nucleon-nucleon scattering and suggests a critical pion mass at which the deuteron would become unbound, contributing to our understanding of the fundamental parameters that govern the structure of the visible Universe.

Abstract: Understanding how the properties of the visible Universe depend on the parameters of the Standard Model of particle physics requires \emph{ab initio} theoretical computations. To that end, we investigate the quark-mass dependence of low-energy nucleon-nucleon scattering in the deuteron and dineutron channels. We perform lattice-QCD calculations at pion masses $M_π\approx 200$ and $280$ MeV and compare their results to phenomenology at the physical point and to a previous calculation at $M_π\approx 710$ MeV. Scattering amplitudes, phase shifts, and threshold parameters are determined from finite-volume spectra using quantization conditions that explicitly incorporate long-distance effects from one-pion exchange. We analyze the pion-mass dependence of scattering lengths and effective ranges using chiral effective field theory with Weinberg and Kaplan-Savage-Wise power countings. From this, we estimate the pion-mass derivatives of the inverse scattering lengths and binding momentum, at the physical point. Both power countings indicate that the deuteron unbinds near $M_π\approx 220$ MeV.

### [5] [Primitive Quantum Gates for an $SU(3)$ Discrete Subgroup: $Σ(216\times3)$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08161)
*Erik J. Gustafson,Henry Lamm,Edison Murairi,Sebastian Osorio Perez*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper constructs a primitive gate set for simulating a 648-element discrete subgroup of SU(3) and provides cost estimates for two different quantum computing architectures.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a set of fundamental operations (gates) that can be used in the digital quantum simulation of a specific group, which is a 648-element discrete subgroup of SU(3). This is important for performing complex simulations on quantum computers, particularly for calculations such as shear viscosity.

Method: The authors define and provide decompositions for five algorithmic primitives: inversion, group multiplication, trace, group Fourier transform, and kinetic-phase gates. These are tailored for both qubit and qutrit-qubit quantum computing systems.

Result: For a fiducial shear-viscosity calculation with a given lattice size and time steps, the authors estimate the fault-tolerant costs in terms of the number of T gates for a qubit architecture and R gates for a qutrit-qubit architecture, providing a range of values for each.

Conclusion: The construction of these primitive gates and their associated cost analysis offers a foundation for more efficient and accurate quantum simulations of the specified group, with practical implications for the resource requirements of future quantum algorithms.

Abstract: We construct a primitive gate set for the digital quantum simulation of the $648$-element discrete subgroup of $SU(3)$, $\dsb$. The five algorithmic primitives are the inversion, group multiplication, trace, group Fourier transform, and kinetic-phase gates, for which we provide qubit and qutrit--qubit decompositions. For a fiducial shear-viscosity calculation on a $10^3$ spatial lattice with $50$ time steps and a total synthesis-error budget of $10^{-8}$, the estimated fault-tolerant costs are $2.3$--$6.8\times10^{10}$ $T$ gates in the qubit architecture and $1.3$--$5.9\times10^{12}$ $R$ gates in the qutrit--qubit architecture.

</details>
