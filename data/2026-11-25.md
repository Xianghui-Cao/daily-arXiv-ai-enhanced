### [1] [Fermionic 2+1D SU(2) quantum link models: an ED/DMRG reliability study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28590)
*Daming Li*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper benchmarks two Hilbert-space approaches, sparse exact diagonalization (ED) and matrix-product-state density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG), for a 2+1D SU(2) quantum link model with one staggered fermion flavor on small 2D lattices. The methods are validated against each other and an independent fPEPS, providing a reproducible reliability envelope for non-Abelian gauge-fermion systems.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: There is a need for controlled reliability studies of non-Abelian lattice gauge theory with dynamical fermions, especially for tensor-network and quantum-simulation methods. Such studies are crucial for the development and validation of computational techniques in this field, yet they are scarce for small systems.

Method: Two complementary Hilbert-space approaches are used: sparse exact diagonalization (ED) and matrix-product-state density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG). ED uses explicit cross-site Jordan-Wigner strings, while DMRG conserves total-fermion-number and constructs a matrix product operator (MPO) from local terms. The study also validates the link algebra, Gauss-law commutation, Hermiticity, pure-gauge limit, and MPO against ED component by component.

Result: The methods agree to a high degree of precision, with the $2\times2$ methods agreeing to $10^{-13}$ and the $2\times3$--$2\times5$ ladders controlled at or below the $10^{-5}$ level. The $3\times3$ geometry shows that color neutrality plays a role, with the even-filling $3\times2$ sector having an independent exact-Gauss fPEPS as a reference. The results provide a benchmark for non-Abelian gauge-fermion systems and indicate where larger-scale simulations may require adjustments.

Conclusion: The paper establishes a reliable benchmark for non-Abelian gauge-fermion systems using two Hilbert-space approaches. It demonstrates the importance of color neutrality and provides a framework for future simulations, suggesting that larger bond dimensions or different tensor-network geometries might be needed for more extensive simulations.

Abstract: Non-Abelian lattice gauge theory with dynamical fermions is a central target for tensor-network and quantum-simulation methods, but controlled reliability studies on small systems are scarce. We benchmark two complementary Hilbert-space approaches for a 2+1D SU(2) quantum link model with one staggered fermion flavour on small two-dimensional lattices: sparse exact diagonalization (ED) and matrix-product-state density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG). The link variables use the five-state $\{5\}$ truncation, and fermions are represented by two-colour four-state sites. We implement ED with explicit cross-site Jordan--Wigner strings and DMRG with total-fermion-number conservation and a matrix product operator (MPO) built from local terms. We validate the link algebra, Gauss-law commutation, Hermiticity, the pure-gauge limit, and the MPO against ED component by component. The $2\times2$ methods agree to $10^{-13}$ and the $2\times3$--$2\times5$ ladders are controlled at or below the $10^{-5}$ level. The $3\times3$ geometry illustrates the role of colour neutrality: at half filling the odd fermion number makes $G^2=3/4$ the exact lower bound, while one hole or one doublon restores neutrality and lowers the residual to $\sim2\times10^{-5}$ at $χ=2048$. An independent exact-Gauss fPEPS provides a reference for the even-filling $3\times2$ sector. The results define a reproducible reliability envelope for non-Abelian gauge-fermion benchmarks and show where larger-scale simulations require larger bond dimensions or different tensor-network geometries.

This is important for a better understanding of the phase diagram and thermodynamics of the system, especially due to the exact center-symmetry of the combined theory.

Method: The authors analyze the structure of the QCD+QED theory and discuss different strategies for simulating this theory on a lattice. They present new results from lattice QCD+QED simulations at finite temperature, with a focus on the role of the electromagnetic coupling in the thermodynamics of the system.

The study emphasizes the importance of developing new simulation techniques to fully explore the QCD+QED phase diagram.

Abstract: QED corrections to observables in QCD are by now well-understood at zero temperature, where QED effects are perturbative and required for precision physics observables, such as the neutron-proton mass difference or the muon anomalous magnetic moment, among many others. ### [3] [Hadron spectroscopy of QC2D at finite density](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29900)
*Kei Iida,Etsuko Itou,Kotaro Murakami,and Daiki Suenaga*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the chemical-potential dependence of hadron masses in two-color QCD at low temperatures, using lattice Monte Carlo simulations. It finds a meson mass hierarchy similar to three-color QCD in the hadronic phase, but this ordering changes after the superfluid transition. The study also examines diquark source dependence and discusses partial chiral-symmetry restoration.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how the chemical potential affects hadron masses in two-color QCD (QC2D) at low temperatures, and to explore the behavior of different meson and diquark channels through the superfluid transition, including the investigation of the Nambu-Goldstone mode and indications of partial chiral-symmetry restoration.

Method: Lattice Monte Carlo simulations were used to calculate all possible meson and diquark correlation functions, including disconnected diagrams, on a 32^4 lattice at T≈40 MeV. The researchers analyzed the meson mass hierarchy, the impact of the superfluid transition on the meson masses, and the diquark source dependence of the Nambu-Goldstone mode.

Result: In the hadronic phase, a meson mass hierarchy similar to that of three-color QCD was observed. Post superfluid transition, the σ and a1 channels became lighter, and the pion mass increased with density. The Nambu-Goldstone mode showed an almost vanishing mass in the superfluid phase after extrapolation to zero source, and there were indications of partial chiral-symmetry restoration based on the comparison of correlation functions for chiral partners.

Conclusion: The findings suggest that the chemical-potential dependence of hadron masses in QC2D exhibits distinct characteristics before and after the superfluid transition, with significant changes in the meson mass hierarchy and the appearance of a light Nambu-Goldstone boson, indicating U(1)_B symmetry breaking. The results also point towards partial chiral-symmetry restoration in the dense QC2D system.

Abstract: We investigate the chemical-potential dependence of hadron masses in two-color QCD (QC$_2$D) at low temperature using lattice Monte Carlo simulations. We calculate all possible meson and diquark correlation functions, including disconnected diagrams, on a $32^4$ lattice at $T\approx40\,\mathrm{MeV}$. In the hadronic phase, we obtain a meson mass hierarchy similar to that of three-color QCD. After the superfluid transition, the ordering changes: the $σ$ and $a_1$ channels become lighter, while the pion mass increases with density. We also examine the diquark source dependence of the Nambu--Goldstone mode of $U(1)_B$ symmetry breaking and find an almost vanishing mass in the superfluid phase after extrapolation to zero source. Finally, we compare correlation functions for chiral partners and discuss indications of partial chiral-symmetry restoration. This contribution is based on our recent study of hadron spectra in dense QC$_2$D~\cite{Iida:2026dmw}.

Abstract: We explore the finite temperature behaviour of the Sp(4) lattice gauge theory coupled to two dynamical Wilson fermions transforming in the fundamental representation. These findings motivate further studies of the continuum limit for this and other Sp(2N) gauge theories.

### [6] [Leading-order dynamical QED corrections to the thermodynamics of isospin-asymmetric QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31087)
*Gergely Endrődi,Ádám Fischer,Wei-Ping Huang,Gergely Markó*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents the first lattice calculation of the impact of dynamical photons on QCD thermodynamics at a nonzero isospin chemical potential, and it explores the transition from second-order to first-order due to the effect of dynamical photons. The researchers circumvent the sign problem by expanding the free energy density in terms of the electromagnetic coupling, and they find that the leading-order expansion coefficient changes sharply across the onset of charged-pion condensation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to study the influence of dynamical photons on the phase transition of QCD at a nonzero isospin chemical potential, specifically to determine whether the introduction of dynamical photons changes the nature of the phase transition from second-order to first-order, which is expected based on general grounds.

Method: The authors perform a lattice calculation, expanding the free energy density in the electromagnetic coupling around zero, where the sign problem does not occur. They measure the leading-order expansion coefficient using a newly proposed planar source on existing (2+1)-flavor pure-QCD ensembles with a pionic source and nonzero isospin chemical potential, and then extrapolate to vanishing pionic source.

Result: The results show that the leading-order expansion coefficient is independent of the isospin chemical potential below the onset of charged-pion condensation, but it rises sharply at the onset, indicating a change in the thermodynamic behavior of the system.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the research is that the presence of dynamical photons, as indicated by the sharp rise in the leading-order expansion coefficient, has a significant effect on the thermodynamics of QCD at the onset of charged-pion condensation, potentially driving the transition to become first order.

Abstract: We present a first lattice calculation of the leading-order effect of dynamical photons on the thermodynamics of QCD at nonzero isospin chemical potential $μ_I$, across the onset of charged-pion condensation. In QCD this onset is a second-order transition, which dynamical photons are expected, on general grounds, to drive first order. However, a direct simulation is not possible since unequal quark charges render the QCD+QED action complex at nonzero $μ_I$. We therefore expand the free energy density $f$ in the electromagnetic coupling $e$ around $e=0$, where the sign problem is absent. The leading-order expansion coefficient, $\partial^2 f/\partial e^2|_{e=0}$, is the convolution of the photon propagator with the electromagnetic current-current correlator, which we measure with a newly proposed planar source on existing (2+1)-flavor pure-QCD ensembles at nonzero $μ_I$ and pionic source $λ$. At physical quark masses and a single lattice spacing, after extrapolating to vanishing $λ$, we find that $\partial^2 f/\partial e^2|_{e=0}$ is, within errors, independent of $μ_I$ below the onset and rises sharply across it.

</details>
