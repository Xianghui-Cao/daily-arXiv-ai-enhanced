### [1] [Accessing the Wess-Zumino-Witten term using Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05006)
*Fernando Romero-López,Stephen R. Sharpe*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores the Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) interaction in chiral perturbation theory, which is related to chiral anomalies. The authors develop a finite-volume formalism for calculating amplitudes like $K \overline K \to π^+ π^0 π^-$ using lattice QCD, and provide threshold expansions and leading order predictions from chiral perturbation theory.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to provide a method for accessing the nonvanishing amplitude resulting from the WZW interaction, specifically the $K \overline K \to π^+ π^0 π^-$ amplitude, by developing a formalism that can utilize lattice QCD calculations in finite volumes. This is important for understanding the implications of chiral anomalies within the framework of chiral perturbation theory.

Method: The method involves deriving a finite-volume formalism applicable to the systems $K \overline K + 3π$ with $I=0$ and $πK + ππK$ with $I=1/2$ and $3/2$. The authors also determine the threshold expansions for the $2\leftrightarrow 3$ and $3\leftrightarrow 3$ K matrices and calculate the leading order predictions from chiral perturbation theory for these expansions.

Result: The results include the development of the finite-volume formalism, the determination of the threshold expansions for the relevant K matrices, and the calculation of the leading order predictions from chiral perturbation theory for the coefficients in these expansions. These outcomes enable the calculation of amplitudes using the spectra of two and three particles in finite volumes, as computed by lattice QCD.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper is that the finite-volume formalism developed allows for the calculation of amplitudes such as $K \overline K \to π^+ π^0 π^-$, and the provided leading order predictions serve as a basis for future work. The formalism and the calculated expansions are key steps toward a more comprehensive understanding of the WZW interaction and its consequences.

Abstract: The Wess-Zumino-Witten interaction in chiral perturbation theory is a manifestation of chiral anomalies. One of its consequences is the presence of a nonvanishing $K \overline K \to π^+ π^0 π^-$ amplitude. We derive the finite-volume formalism that allows one to access this, and related, amplitudes, using the spectra of two and three particles in finite volumes, spectra that can be calculated using lattice QCD. Specifically, we present the formalism for the systems $K \overline K + 3 π$ with $I=0$ and $πK + ππK$ with $I=1/2$ and $3/2$. In addition, we determine the threshold expansions for the $2\leftrightarrow 3$ and $3\leftrightarrow 3$ K matrices that enter the formalism, and calculate the leading order predictions from chiral perturbation theory for the coefficients that enter these expansions.

### [2] [Equation of state of dense magnetized QCD matter via a robust analytic continuation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05108)
*S. Borsányi,B. B. Brandt,G. Endrődi,J. N. Guenther,G. Markó,M. A. Petri,A. D. M. Valois*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study computes the equation of state for dense, magnetized, strongly interacting matter using lattice QCD with a non-zero lattice spacing, employing 2+1+1 flavors of quarks and overcoming the sign problem through a new T'-expansion scheme, providing results useful for heavy-ion phenomenology.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a more accurate description of the equation of state for dense and magnetized strongly interacting matter, which is relevant for understanding the conditions in heavy-ion collisions and astrophysical environments. The researchers aim to improve upon existing methods by reducing systematic errors and allowing for better-controlled extrapolations to real chemical potentials.

Method: The method involves using lattice Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) simulations with 2+1+1 dynamical flavors of rooted staggered quarks, at a non-zero value of the lattice spacing. They enforce strangeness neutrality and isospin asymmetry and use an innovative approach called the T'-expansion scheme to address the sign problem, enabling the analytic continuation from imaginary to real chemical potentials with smaller systematic errors.

Result: The research yields the pressure, entropy density, and interaction measure for dense, magnetized, strongly interacting matter, tabulated for direct application in the field of heavy-ion phenomenology. The T'-expansion scheme proves effective in minimizing systematic errors, leading to more reliable extrapolations to real chemical potentials.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the T'-expansion scheme provides a robust method for computing the equation of state of dense, magnetized matter with reduced systematic errors, thereby enhancing the precision of predictions in heavy-ion collision scenarios and other related fields.

Abstract: We compute the equation of state of dense and magnetized strongly interacting matter using lattice QCD at a non-zero value of the lattice spacing. Our simulations employ $2+1+1$ dynamical flavors of rooted staggered quarks with masses tuned to the physical point. To conform with experimental constraints, we impose strangeness neutrality and isospin asymmetry on our observables. We circumvent the sign problem by simulating imaginary values of the chemical potential and applying the $T'$-expansion scheme to carry out the analytic continuation of our observables to the real chemical potential axis. This novel scheme, adapted to the setup with nonzero magnetic fields, is demonstrated to produce significantly smaller systematic errors in the final observables, compared to the standard analytical continuation at fixed temperature, allowing for better-controlled extrapolations. Our results for the pressure, the entropy density and the interaction measure are tabulated for direct use in heavy-ion phenomenology applications.
