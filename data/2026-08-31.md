### [1] [A different kind of continuum limit for the three-dimensional U(1) gauge theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27976)
*Andreas Athenodorou,Claudio Bonati,Ivan Soler Calero*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper examines an extension to the Wilson action in 3D compact U(1) lattice gauge theory, which includes a parameter μ that couples to the number of lattice monopoles. By tuning β and μ, a line of constant physics is found where the ratio of the lightest glueball mass to the square root of the string tension remains constant, suggesting a regularization with a well-behaved continuum limit.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the pathological behavior of the continuum limit in three-dimensional compact U(1) lattice gauge theory, specifically the vanishing ratio of glueball masses to the square root of the string tension as the continuum limit is approached.

Method: The authors extend the Wilson action by introducing a new parameter μ that couples to the total number of lattice monopoles. They then tune both the coupling constant β and the new parameter μ to identify a line of constant physics, along which certain physical quantities remain unchanged.

Result: A line of constant physics is identified where the ratio of the lightest glueball mass to the square root of the string tension remains constant. This scaling is also observed for other low-lying glueball masses, and the string tension decreases with increasing β, indicating a potentially well-behaved continuum limit.

Conclusion: The modified lattice action, with the inclusion of the parameter μ, may provide a regularization of the three-dimensional compact U(1) gauge theory that leads to a physically acceptable continuum limit, solving the issue of the vanishing glueball mass-to-string tension ratio.

Abstract: In three-dimensional compact U(1) lattice gauge theory color confinement can be understood analytically through the dynamics of magnetic monopoles. However, its continuum limit is pathological, since the ratio of the glueball masses to the square root of the string tension vanishes as the continuum limit is approached. We investigate a simple extension of the Wilson action in which the total number of lattice monopoles is coupled to an additional parameter $μ$. By tuning $β$ and $μ$ simultaneously, we identify a line of constant physics along which the ratio of the lightest glueball mass to the square root of the string tension remains constant. We further show that the same scaling is satisfied, within numerical uncertainties, by the other low-lying glueball masses considered in this work. Along this trajectory, the string tension in lattice units decreases with increasing $β$, thus suggesting that the modified lattice action may provide a regularization of three-dimensional compact U(1) gauge theory with a physically well-behaved continuum limit.

### [2] [Renormalization-guided inverse blocking for lattice field generation: construction and validation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28580)
*Anna Hasenfratz,Ethan T. Neil,Letizia Parato,Noah Schwartz*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents an algorithm for generating lattice field configurations through the approximate inversion of a renormalization-group blocking transformation, with machine learning used to reconstruct short-distance degrees of freedom. The method is tested in 2D φ^4 theory and shows successful upscaling from 16^2 to 2048^2 lattices.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop an efficient method for generating lattice field configurations that can be applied recursively on larger lattices, potentially extending to higher-dimensional systems, including gauge and fermionic fields, by leveraging machine learning techniques for the reconstruction of short-distance details after decimation.

Method: The authors use a two-step process for the blocking transformation, consisting of smoothing followed by decimation, and employ a conditional normalizing flow (a form of machine learning) to reconstruct the removed short-distance degrees of freedom. A fine-action rethermalization is then performed to remove any remaining discrepancies. This process allows for the recursive application of the same blocking transformation and conditional flow, creating a cascade of configurations from a small-volume ensemble.

Result: The method was successfully tested in 2D φ^4 theory at criticality with λ=1, demonstrating stable cascade upscaling from 16^2 to 2048^2 lattices using local computational resources. It was also shown that short-distance mismatches relax quickly, while thermal direction mismatches take longer to resolve, indicating controlled behavior during rethermalization.

Conclusion: The proposed algorithm, which combines renormalization-group methods with machine learning, has proven effective for generating lattice field configurations and can be scaled to much larger lattices. The findings suggest potential applicability to more complex, higher-dimensional systems, as well as to gauge and fermionic fields, offering a promising approach for future research in the field.

Abstract: We propose an algorithm for generating lattice field configurations based on the approximate inversion of a renormalization-group blocking transformation. We optimize the blocking transformation using a ``perfect blocking'' condition so that the blocked lattice distribution is well approximated by a simple coarse action. The blocking is separated into an invertible smoothing transformation followed by decimation. Machine learning, in the form of a conditional normalizing flow, is used to reconstruct the short-distance degrees of freedom removed by the decimation. A short fine-action rethermalization then removes the residual mismatch. Because the coarse ensemble supplies the long-distance modes, the same blocking transformation and conditional flow can be reused recursively on larger lattices, producing a cascade of configurations from an initial small-volume ensemble. We test the method in two-dimensional $φ^4$ theory with $λ=1$ at criticality and demonstrate stable cascade upscaling from $16^2$ to $2048^2$ lattices on local computational resources. Controlled rethermalization tests show that short-distance mismatches relax rapidly, whereas a deliberately introduced mismatch in the relevant thermal direction relaxes much more slowly. The construction uses ingredients that admit natural extensions to higher-dimensional systems and, ultimately, to gauge and fermionic degrees of freedom.

### [3] [Renormalization-guided cascade upscaling for lattice field generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28581)
*Anna Hasenfratz,Ethan T. Neil,Letizia Parato,Noah Schwartz*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents an RG-guided machine-learning method for lattice field generation, combining perfect blocking, conditional normalizing flow, and rethermalization, to accurately generate long-distance physics in 2D φ⁴ theory up to L=2048.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a more efficient and accurate method for generating lattice field configurations that capture the correct long-distance physics, especially at criticality, by leveraging renormalization group (RG) concepts and machine learning techniques.

Method: The method involves using a 'perfect blocking' technique to provide equilibrated long-distance modes, a conditional normalizing flow to reconstruct short-distance details, and a brief rethermalization step to eliminate residual errors. The algorithm is trained on smaller lattices (L ≤ 32) and then applied recursively to larger lattices (up to L = 2048).

Result: The result shows that the trained flow can be reused recursively to generate lattice fields with the correct long-distance behavior, even when scaling up to much larger system sizes (L=2048) from the training set (L ≤ 32).

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the RG-guided machine-learning approach successfully generates lattice field configurations that exhibit the proper long-distance physics, demonstrating the potential of this method for simulating critical phenomena in lattice field theories.

Abstract: We introduce a renormalization-group (RG) guided machine-learning algorithm for lattice field generation based on approximate inversion of an RG transformation. A ``perfect blocking'' construction supplies equilibrated long-distance modes, while a conditional normalizing flow reconstructs short-distance details and brief rethermalization removes residual errors. In 2D $φ^4$ theory at criticality, a flow trained at $L\le32$ is reused recursively in cascades reaching $L=2048$ with correct long-distance physics.

</details>
