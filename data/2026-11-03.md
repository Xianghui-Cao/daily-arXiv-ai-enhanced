### [1] [Precision determination of $B_c$ and $B_c^*$ meson decay constants from lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01638)
*Navdeep Singh Dhindsa,Debsubhra Chakraborty,Nilmani Mathur*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study uses lattice QCD with the HISQ action for charm and bottom quarks to determine decay constants for heavy-heavy mesons, including preliminary results for specific meson systems.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to accurately determine the decay constants of heavy-heavy mesons, which are important for understanding the properties and behavior of these particles in the standard model of particle physics.

Method: The method involves using the Highly Improved Staggered Quark (HISQ) action on a gauge ensemble generated by the MILC Collaboration, with the quark masses tuned to their physical values to simulate the heavy-heavy meson systems and calculate their decay constants.

Result: Preliminary results are obtained for the decay constants of the $B_c$, $B_c^*$, $η_c$, $η_b$, and $Υ$ mesons, providing new data for the comparison with theoretical predictions and experimental measurements.

Conclusion: The research contributes to the body of knowledge regarding heavy-heavy meson decay constants, offering insights that can be used to refine theoretical models and inform future experiments in high-energy physics.

Abstract: We present a lattice QCD study of heavy-heavy meson decay constants using the HISQ gauge ensemble generated by the MILC Collaboration. Employing the HISQ action for both charm and bottom valence quarks tuned to their physical masses, we determine pseudoscalar, vector, and tensor decay constants for heavy quarkonium and $B_c$ systems. Preliminary results are presented for the decay constants of the $B_c$, $B_c^*$, $η_c$, $η_b$, and $Υ$ mesons.

### [2] [2+1 flavor light hadron spectrum with PACS10 configurations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02426)
*K. -I. Ishikawa,N. Ishizuka,Y. Kuramashi,Y. Nakamura,Y. Namekawa,K. Satoh,E. Shintani,Y. Taniguchi,N. Ukita,H. Watanabe,T. Yamazaki,T. Yoshié*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a study on the hadron spectrum, pseudoscalar meson decay constants, and quark mass ratio in 2+1 flavor QCD at the physical point using improved Wilson quark action and Iwasaki gauge. The authors investigate finite size effects and compare results with different conventions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide accurate calculations of the hadron spectrum, pseudoscalar meson decay constants, and quark mass ratio in the continuum limit while examining the impact of finite size effects and comparing two different conventions for defining pseudoexperimental values in isosymmetric QCD.

Method: Numerical simulations are performed at three lattice spacings with large space-time volumes. Additional smaller lattices are used to assess finite size effects. The expansion parameters for meson masses, decay constants, and baryon masses are determined using a reweighting technique, which helps fine-tune the physical point based on the $π$, $K$ mesons, and $Ξ$ baryon as inputs.

Result: There is a negligible finite size effect observed in the hadron effective masses, but discrepancies in the pseudoscalar meson decay constants. The octet baryon masses and pseudoscalar decay constants in the continuum limit are compared with pseudoexperimental values, and the quark mass ratio is compared against the recent FLAG summary. Differences between the two conventions are discussed.

Conclusion: The study provides a detailed analysis of the hadron spectrum and related quantities in 2+1 flavor QCD, highlighting the importance of considering finite volume effects and the choice of conventions when determining pseudoexperimental values.

Abstract: We present the results for the hadron spectrum, the pseudoscalar meson decay constants, and the quark mass ratio in the continuum limit using the ``PACS10'' gauge configurations generated with the $O(a)$-improved Wilson quark action and the Iwasaki gauge action. Numerical simulations are carried out at three lattice spacings keeping the space-time volumes over (10 fm$)^4$ in 2+1 flavor QCD at the physical point. We calculate physical quantities with two choices of conventions to define pseudoexperimental values expected in the isosymmetric QCD. In order to investigate the finite size effects on the physical quantities we have additionally generated the (5.4 fm$)^4$ lattice at the coarsest lattice spacing of $a=0.08487(14)$~fm, which is a hypercube with half the length in each direction compared to the PACS10 configurations. While the finite size effect is hardly observed in comparison of the hadron effective masses on the (10.9 fm$)^4$ and (5.4 fm$)^4$ lattices, the results for the pseudoscalar meson decay constants show discrepancies beyond the error bars. The (5.4 fm$)^4$ lattice also serves for determining the expansion parameters of the pseudoscalar meson masses and decay constants, and the octet baryon masses in terms of the quark masses around the physical point with the aid of the mass reweighting technique. The expansion parameters are used for the fine-tuning of the physical point employing the pseudoexperimental masses of the $π$ meson, $K$ meson, and the $Ξ$ baryon as physical inputs in each convention. We compare the results for the octet baryon masses and the pseudoscalar decay constants in the continuum limit with the corresponding pseudoexperimental values in both conventions. We also discuss the deviation between two results with different conventions. As for the quark mass ratio the result is compared with the recent FLAG summary.

### [3] [Generalizable Equivariant Diffusion Models for Non-Abelian Lattice Gauge Theory: Analytical Foundations and Numerical Scaling Tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03530)
*Gert Aarts,Diaa E. Habibi,Andreas Ipp,David I. Müller,Thomas R. Ranner,Lingxiao Wang,Wei Wang,Qianteng Zhu*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper rigorously derives the reverse-time SDE for gauge-equivariant diffusion models, discusses gauge invariance of the denoising loss, and benchmarks scaling behavior with lattice volume and inverse coupling. It also provides analytical expressions for Wilson loops and topological charge susceptibility.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a solid theoretical foundation and numerical validation for the use of gauge-equivariant diffusion models in generating field configurations in non-abelian lattice gauge theories, building upon previous work that used L-CNN-based diffusion models and MAALA.

Method: The authors present a rigorous derivation of the reverse-time stochastic differential equation (SDE) on the group manifold, analyze the gauge invariance of the denoising loss function, and perform extensive numerical benchmarks to study the scaling behavior of the model with respect to the lattice volume and the inverse coupling β. They also derive full analytical expressions for arbitrarily sized Wilson loops and the topological charge susceptibility during the forward process.

Result: The results include a mathematically sound framework for the reverse-time SDE, confirmation of the gauge invariance of the denoising loss, and detailed numerical insights into the scaling properties of the model. Additionally, the paper provides explicit formulas for Wilson loops and the topological charge susceptibility, which are important observables in lattice gauge theory.

Conclusion: The conclusion would likely emphasize the importance of the derived theoretical framework for understanding and applying gauge-equivariant diffusion models, as well as the practical implications of the numerical findings for simulations in lattice gauge theories, although the specific conclusion is not provided in the abstract.

Abstract: Gauge-equivariant diffusion models can be used to generate ensembles of field configurations in non-abelian lattice gauge theories. Previously, we demonstrated the feasibility of modeling these theories by combining L-CNN-based diffusion models with the Metropolis-adjusted annealed Langevin algorithm (MAALA). Here we focus on the underlying analytical framework. In particular, we present a rigorous derivation of the reverse-time stochastic differential equation on the group manifold, discuss the gauge invariance of the denoising loss function, and provide extensive numerical benchmarks regarding the scaling behavior with respect to the lattice volume and the inverse coupling $β$. Full analytical expressions for arbitrarily sized Wilson loops and the topological charge susceptibility during the forward process are given as well.
