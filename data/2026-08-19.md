### [1] [Computing the Critical Temperature of the Affine-Transformed $D=3$ Ising Model Using Masked Autoregressive Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17006)
*Kai Svenson,George T. Fleming,Richard C. Brower,Nobuyuki Matsumoto,Rohan Misra*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores the measurement of the critical temperature (β_c) in an affine-transformed Ising model on a face-centered cubic lattice, comparing traditional multiple histogram (MH) method and a machine learning-based masked autoregressive flow (MAF) for interpolation and extrapolation. It finds MAF to be a more computationally efficient alternative with additional benefits like system volume extrapolation.

Motivation: The motivation is to develop a conformal field theory (CFT) on an arbitrarily curved manifold by measuring the critical temperature (β_c) of the Ising model on a non-standard lattice, and to find a more efficient method for interpolating and extrapolating Monte Carlo observables.

Method: The authors compare two methods for interpolating and extrapolating Monte Carlo observables: the traditional multiple histogram (MH) method, which is designed for this specific purpose, and a newer machine learning approach using a masked autoregressive flow (MAF) that estimates the probability density function of observables.

Result: The results show that while both methods are applicable, the MAF method provides a viable and more computationally favorable alternative to the MH method, and it also offers additional advantages such as the ability to extrapolate in system volume.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the MAF method is not only a valid alternative to the MH method but also offers computational efficiency and scalability, making it a suitable choice for measuring the critical temperature and other related tasks in the development of CFT on curved manifolds.

Abstract: The simple Ising model provides a rich environment to build and study lattice field theories. As part of an ongoing project to construct a conformal field theory (CFT) on an arbitrarily curved manifold, in this work we develop methods to measure the critical temperature $β_c$ of the affine-transformed Ising model on the face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice. The main challenge in this endeavor is finding a computationally efficient and accurate method of interpolating and extrapolating Monte Carlo observables with respect to coupling coefficients and temperature. Herein, we compare two such methods. A traditional statistical approach uses the multiple histogram (MH) method, while a newer machine learning approach uses a masked autoregressive flow (MAF) to estimate the underlying probability density function of a set of observables. While the MH method is specifically designed to interpolate and extrapolate Monte Carlo observables, we find that MAF is a viable alternative for measuring $β_c$ with a computational cost that scales more favorably. Furthermore, we comment on additional advantages of MAF relevant to our work, such as extrapolating in system volume.
