### [1] [Complex Langevin kernels for real-time SU(N) lattice gauge theory in 3+1 dimensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37800)
*Kirill Boguslavski,Paul Hotzy,David I. Müller*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the use of complex Langevin dynamics in 3+1 dimensional real-time SU(N) gauge theories, introducing new kernel modifications for lattice gauge theory. It demonstrates stable simulations for pure SU(2) gauge theory with an extended reach in real-time extent and validates the approach with a generalized correctness criterion. The work paves the way for advanced real-time simulations of non-Abelian gauge theories.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to extend the applicability of complex Langevin (CL) dynamics to real-time simulations in Minkowski space, particularly for SU(N) gauge theories in 3+1 dimensions, and to develop a method that can achieve stable and correct convergence over unprecedented time scales.

Method: The authors employ complex Langevin dynamics with the introduction of new kernel modifications, such as a contour-dependent phase kernel and a local, field-dependent kernel based on traced plaquettes. They also generalize the CL correctness criterion to include these kernelled dynamics, which allows them to assess the validity of their simulation results.

Result: The study shows that with the anisotropic kernel, stable simulations are possible up to a real-time extent of $2β$ at a bare coupling $g=0.5$. The use of a contour-dependent phase kernel extends this to $3β$, while the local, field-dependent kernel achieves $3.5β$. These findings advance the capability to simulate non-Abelian gauge theories over longer time scales.

Conclusion: The paper concludes by establishing a framework for controlled studies of non-Abelian gauge theories using complex Langevin dynamics with tailored kernel modifications, which significantly enhances the ability to perform real-time simulations. This sets the stage for future work, including the computation of unequal-time correlation functions.

Abstract: We investigate complex Langevin (CL) dynamics for real-time SU($N$) gauge theories in 3+1 dimensions and introduce new kernel modifications tailored for lattice gauge theory (LGT). Using the anisotropic kernel as a baseline, we demonstrate that on a rectangular regularized Schwinger-Keldysh time contour, stable simulations are possible for pure SU(2) gauge theory up to a real-time extent of $2β$ at a bare coupling $g=0.5$, where $β$ denotes the inverse temperature. We show that a contour-dependent phase kernel extends this reach to $3β$, while a local, field-dependent kernel based on traced plaquettes achieves $3.5β$. These constructions represent the first application of such kernels to LGTs, especially in 3+1 dimensions. To validate the approach, we generalize the CL correctness criterion based on the drift magnitude to kernelled dynamics, providing a practical tool to assess correct convergence. Our results substantially advance the applicability of CL to real-time simulations in Minkowski space, establishing a framework for controlled studies of non-Abelian gauge theories at unprecedented time scales. A forthcoming paper applies these developments to the computation of unequal-time correlation functions.

### [2] [Agentic AI for QCD perturbation theory or: how I learned to stop worrying and love the bot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37960)
*Erik Lundstrum*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses the effectiveness of AI coding agents in performing perturbative QCD calculations, which are well-suited for these agents due to their modular and verifiable nature. The methods for instructing and verifying the work of AI in one- and two-loop calculations are presented, with an emphasis on avoiding common errors. Once an agent is proficient in one calculation, it can efficiently apply its knowledge to similar tasks with minimal user intervention.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to leverage the capabilities of AI coding agents to perform complex perturbatic QCD calculations, which are traditionally time-consuming and error-prone when done manually. The aim is to improve efficiency and reduce the likelihood of mistakes by using AI that can handle modular and verifiable steps of such calculations.

Method: The method involves developing and implementing instructions for AI coding agents to carry out perturbative QCD calculations at one- and two-loop levels. The process includes a verification system to ensure the accuracy of the results and to pre-empt common mistakes. The transferability of the learned instructions from one calculation to another is also explored.

Result: The result is that AI coding agents can successfully complete perturbative QCD calculations and, once they have mastered one calculation, they can effectively apply the same principles to other similar calculations with high efficiency and a low error rate, often within just a few hours and with little additional input from the user.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that AI coding agents are a powerful tool for perturbative QCD calculations, capable of significantly reducing the time and effort required while maintaining a high level of accuracy. The approach of providing clear instructions and a robust verification process enables the agents to perform reliably across different but related calculations.

Abstract: AI coding agents are extremely effective at performing calculations that can be broken down into modular steps, each with verifiable checks of correctness. Perturbative QCD calculations satisfy both of these properties and we find that they are well-suited to solution by agentic AI systems. We discuss our methods for instructing and verifying the work of agents in one- and two-loop perturbative calculations, with a focus on pre-empting common mistakes. We find that once an agent has successfully completed one perturbative calculation, its instructions transfer very well to other similar calculations which can then be completed and verified in a matter of hours with little effort from the user.

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first complex Langevin simulations at the physical pion mass to study the QCD equation of state, providing continuum-extrapolated results for energy, entropy, and enthalpy densities, as well as specific heat capacity, under conditions of high baryon chemical potentials and temperatures above the crossover.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to explore the QCD equation of state in a regime that is difficult to access with traditional lattice QCD methods, specifically at high baryon chemical potentials and temperatures above the crossover, using a novel simulation technique.

Method: The method employed is the use of complex Langevin dynamics for the first time at the physical pion mass, which allows the exploration of the QCD phase diagram at non-zero baryon density. The results are then extrapolated to the continuum limit to reduce lattice artifacts.

Result: The results include continuum-extrapolated values for the energy, entropy, and enthalpy densities, as well as the specific heat capacity, as functions of both the baryon chemical potential and temperature, offering new insights into the thermodynamics of QCD matter under these conditions.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper would likely emphasize the success of applying complex Langevin simulations to obtain reliable thermodynamic properties of QCD at finite baryon density and high temperatures, potentially opening up new avenues for studying the QCD phase diagram.

Abstract: We discuss the QCD equation of state at temperatures above the crossover and for high baryon chemical potentials, obtained via the first ever complex Langevin simulations at the physical pion mass. In particular, we present continuum-extrapolated results for the energy, entropy, and enthalpy densities, as well as for the specific heat capacity as a function of baryon chemical potential and temperature.

### [4] [Enhanced Signal and Excited-State Control in Nonlocal Lattice Correlators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39944)
*Shi-Yi Zhong,Jian-Hui Zhang,Dian-Jun Zhao*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A new fitting strategy is proposed for the extraction of nonlocal correlation functions in lattice calculations, which improves precision and stability while reducing sensitivity to excited states. This method, applicable to a wide range of correlators, simplifies the analysis of nonforward matrix elements and expands the accessible momentum range.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation stems from the need to overcome limitations in extracting nonlocal correlation functions, which are critical for lattice calculations in parton physics. The existing methods suffer from a poor signal-to-noise ratio at higher Euclidean separations and hadron momenta, as well as contamination from excited states.

Method: The method involves taking the ratio of nonlocal to local correlators, which helps in projecting out the ground-state dynamics with a cleaner signal that is less sensitive to excited states compared to traditional approaches. It has been demonstrated on quark and gluon PDFs, the quark GPD, and TMDPDF, showing improved precision and stability, and allowing for the analysis at larger hadron momenta.

Result: The results obtained using this new method are consistent with those from conventional three-point/two-point ratio fits but offer enhanced precision and stability. The method also simplifies the analysis of nonforward matrix elements like GPDs and can be applied to various Dirac and momentum structures, including connected and disconnected contributions.

Conclusion: This novel fitting strategy provides a more precise and stable way to extract nonlocal correlation functions, broadening the scope of lattice calculations for hadron structure and making it readily implementable in current lattice programs.

Abstract: Nonlocal correlation functions underpin lattice calculations of parton physics. However, their extraction is limited by a rapidly deteriorating signal-to-noise ratio with increasing Euclidean separation and hadron momentum, and by excited-state contamination. We propose a new fitting strategy that enhances control over both: by taking the ratio of nonlocal to local correlators, we can project out the dominant ground-state dynamics with a signal that is much cleaner and far less sensitive to excited states than the conventional approach. We demonstrate the method on the quark and gluon PDF, the quark GPD and TMDPDF. The results are consistent with those from the conventional three-point/two-point ratio fit but exhibit markedly improved precision and stability, and extend the accessible range to larger hadron momenta. For nonforward matrix elements such as GPDs, our strategy greatly simplifies the analysis procedure. Our method applies to general Dirac and momentum structures, for connected and disconnected contributions, and extends naturally to other nonlocal correlators, making it immediately deployable in existing lattice programs toward precision hadron structure.

### [6] [Complex potential and the thermal width of quarkonium in quark-gluon plasma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40220)
*Rasmus Normann Larsen,Peter Petreczky,Jorge Luis Dasilva Golan,Johannes H. Weber*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper investigates the complex heavy quark-antiquark potential in 2+1 flavor lattice QCD at non-zero temperatures, relating the imaginary part of the potential to the thermal quarkonium width. It then uses this relation to estimate and compare the thermal widths of charmonia and bottomonia with T-matrix analysis results, finding that for some excited states, the estimated thermal widths are smaller.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the behavior of quarkonium (bound states of a heavy quark and antiquark) in a hot QCD medium, which is relevant for understanding heavy-ion collision experiments. The study aims to fill the gap where direct lattice QCD calculations of quarkonium correlation functions are not feasible at high temperatures.

Method: The method involves studying the complex heavy quark-antiquark potential in lattice QCD with 2+1 flavors at non-zero temperature. The researchers establish a connection between the imaginary part of the potential and the thermal width of quarkonium states. They use this connection to infer the thermal width of charmonia and bottomonia at high temperatures.

Result: The results show that the estimated thermal widths for some excited quarkonium states are smaller than those obtained from the T-matrix analysis, which is another theoretical approach to describe the interaction of quarkonia with the medium. This suggests that the quarkonium states may be more stable at high temperatures than previously thought, based on the T-matrix analysis.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the thermal widths of charmonia and bottomonia can be estimated using the relationship between the imaginary part of the heavy quark-antiquark potential and the thermal quarkonium width. These estimates provide a different perspective on the stability of quarkonium states in a hot QCD medium, particularly for excited states, and they serve as a complementary approach to the T-matrix analysis.

Abstract: We study the complex heavy quark anti-quark potential in lattice 2+1 flavor QCD at non-zero temperature. We point out a close relation between the imaginary part of the potential and the thermal quarkonium width. Using this relation, we estimate the thermal width of charmonia and bottomonia at high temperatures, which are not yet accessible with direct lattice QCD calculations of the quarkonium correlation functions and compare these to the results of the T-matrix analysis. For some of the excited quarkonia states, our estimates of the thermal width are smaller than the ones obtained in the T-matrix analysis.
