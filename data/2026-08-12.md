### [1] [Vacuum polarization in QED with an electromagnetic background from the lattice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10457)
*En-Hung Chao*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A new framework for calculating vacuum polarization functions in QED under an electromagnetic background field, using lattice reweighting and worldline formalism, with potential applications in QCD at finite chemical potential.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a method for accurately computing the effects of an electromagnetic background field on vacuum polarization in QED, which has been challenging due to the complexity of including such fields in lattice simulations. The proposed technique aims to overcome these challenges by employing a reweighting approach that leverages existing Monte Carlo data and the worldline formalism.

Method: The method involves reweighting pre-existing lattice Monte Carlo simulation data, which were generated without an electromagnetic background field, by using the ratio of fermion determinants. This ratio is computed in continuous spacetime using the worldline formalism, allowing for the inclusion of the background field's effect on the vacuum polarization function.

Result: The result is a novel computational framework that can determine vacuum polarization functions in the presence of an electromagnetic background field, specifically in the spacelike region on the lattice. It also opens up possibilities for applying similar techniques to other areas, such as QCD with a finite chemical potential.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that this reweighting technique, combined with the worldline formalism, provides a viable approach for incorporating the effects of an external electromagnetic field into lattice QED calculations, potentially leading to more accurate predictions and enabling further research in related fields like QCD.

Abstract: We propose a framework to determine the vacuum polarization functions in QED in the presence of an electromagnetic background field in the spacelike region on the lattice. This method consists in reweighting lattice Monte Carlo data generated without the background with the fermion determinant ratio evaluated in the continuous spacetime using worldline formalism. This proposal can be further extended to other applications in lattice gauge theory, such as QCD in finite chemical potential.

### [2] [Approximating Grassmann-valued Path Integrals with Radial Basis Function Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10458)
*Gabor Balassa*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A radial basis function (RBF) type neural network is used to approximate fermionic path integrals in quantum field theories, achieving high accuracy even for large systems, and tested with staggered fermions in 1D and 2D.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The challenge of numerically handling noncommuting Grassmann fields in quantum field theories, especially in large systems and higher dimensions, necessitates a more efficient method for approximating fermionic path integrals.

Method: A radial basis function type neural network construction is utilized to isolate the interaction terms from the purely fermionic components, allowing for an approximation of the path integral with high accuracy.

Result: The RBF method can approximate the path integral with a few percent accuracy even for very large lattice sizes, as demonstrated through calculations of partition functions and expectation values with staggered fermions in 1 and 2 dimensions.

Conclusion: The proposed RBF neural network approach offers a promising and numerically efficient technique for approximating fermionic path integrals, which could be beneficial for solving complex problems in quantum field theories.

Abstract: Solving path integrals in quantum field theories often involves the numerical handling of noncommuting Grassmann fields, which is in many cases a highly nontrivial and numerically inefficient task, especially in large systems and at higher dimensions. In this paper a radial basis function type neural network construction is used to approximate fermionic path integrals that include local couplings in their hopping terms. By isolating the interaction terms from the purely fermionic components using a radial basis function expansion, the path integral can be approximated by a few percent accuracy even for very large lattice sizes. The method has been developed and tested using staggered fermions in 1 and 2 dimensions, through calculating the partition functions, and expectation values.

</details>
