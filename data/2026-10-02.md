### [1] [Thermal axion production in QCD from the lattice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01925)
*Luis Neubauer,Guy D. Moore*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper introduces a method to compute thermal axion production in the early Universe using Euclidean lattice QCD, which allows for the extraction of weighted k-integrals of the production rate, potentially enabling more reliable analysis at temperatures around and above the crossover temperature.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a new approach for calculating thermal axion production, which is important for understanding the early Universe, by leveraging Euclidean lattice QCD. This method aims to be effective in temperature ranges where other methods are unreliable.

Method: The authors use Euclidean lattice QCD to calculate weighted integrals of the QCD topological-charge-density spectral function, without requiring analytic continuation. They test this method in pure-glue QCD at T=1.5 Tc to demonstrate its feasibility.

Result: The results show that it is feasible to extract one or possibly two finite weighted k-integrals of the momentum-dependent production rate, indicating that the method could be used for full QCD at temperatures near and somewhat above the crossover temperature.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the proposed method using Euclidean lattice QCD can provide a viable way to analyze thermal axion production, especially at temperatures where previous methods have limitations, thus opening up new possibilities for research in this area.

Abstract: Thermal axion production in the early Universe is controlled by the QCD topological-charge-density spectral function evaluated on the light-cone as a function of momentum k. We show how weighted integrals of this quantity can be computed using Euclidean lattice QCD without any analytic continuation, similar to what Harvey Meyer and collaborators have recently done for the photon production rate. We then test the approach in pure-glue QCD at T=1.5 Tc, finding that it is feasible to extract at least one and possibly two finite weighted k-integrals of the k-dependent production rate. This opens the possibility for extractions in full QCD at temperatures around and somewhat above the crossover temperature, where other methods are not reliable.
