### [1] [Reconstruction of pion polarizabilities in lattice QCD with machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13117)
*Hanxing Jiang,Frank X. Lee*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper uses a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) to reconstruct electric and magnetic polarizabilities of charged and neutral pions from sparse lattice QCD data, without prescribing a single analytic fitting function, and provides mass extrapolations to the physical pion mass.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to apply advanced machine learning techniques for a model-independent reconstruction of pion polarizabilities, which are fundamental properties that can be used to understand the internal structure of pions and their interactions. The use of DDPM allows for a flexible approach that does not rely on a specific analytical form, thus providing a more general solution to the problem.

Method: The method involves using a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) to process the sparse four-point-function data in lattice QCD. The researchers have considered both charged and neutral pions at various masses, and they reconstructed the polarizabilities by training on synthetic functions, avoiding the imposition of a single analytic form for the fit. They also provide an analysis for the low-momentum transfer limit and perform a mass extrapolation to the physical pion mass.

Result: The results include the successful reconstruction of the electric and magnetic polarizabilities for both charged and neutral pions. For charged pions, both elastic and inelastic contributions were analyzed, while for neutral pions, only the inelastic channels were reconstructed. Additionally, the study presents reconstructions at the zero momentum transfer limit and mass extrapolations to 138 MeV, the physical pion mass.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper would likely highlight the effectiveness of the DDPM in reconstructing the polarizabilities without relying on a specific analytic form, as well as the consistency of the obtained values with known physics. It may also discuss the implications of the findings for our understanding of pion structure and suggest future directions for research, such as extending the method to other particles or improving the precision of the reconstructions.

Abstract: We apply the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) from generative machine learning to a model-independent reconstruction of electric and magnetic polarizabilities of both charged and neutral pions. The inputs are the sparse momentum-space four-point-function data in lattice QCD for the connected quark-line contributions obtained in previous simulations at four pion masses between approximately 370 and 1100 MeV on quenched Wilson lattices. For charged pions, the elastic contribution is obtained from the pion form-factor slope, and the complete electric and magnetic inelastic terms are reconstructed separately. The neutral-pion electric and magnetic channels are reconstructed without an elastic charge-radius contribution. Four distinct families of synthetic training functions are employed, so that no single analytic fitting function is prescribed for the lattice data. We present low-momentum reconstructions to the limit of zero momentum transfer where the polarizabilities are defined, and mass extrapolations to the physical pion mass of 138 MeV.

### [2] [Assessing the expressive power of a gauge-invariant PEPS ansatz across a topological phase transition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13852)
*Etsuko Itou,Tsuyoshi Okubo*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the effectiveness of the GGFPEPS ansatz, a gauge-invariant tensor network for 2+1 dimensional lattice gauge theories. By using a Grassmann tensor network and HOTRG, the authors achieve accurate calculations in large volumes and confirm that the ansatz with few parameters can accurately describe the pure Z2 gauge theory, including its topological phase transition.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to examine the expressive power of the GGFPEPS ansatz, which was not possible with VMC methods due to statistical errors. The authors aim to provide a more precise evaluation of the variational parameters and the overall performance of the ansatz in describing the pure Z2 gauge theory.

Method: The method involves reformulating the computation as a Grassmann tensor network, allowing for deterministic contraction, and employing higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG) for calculations in large volumes. Additionally, the corner transfer matrix renormalization group (CTMRG) is used to evaluate the infinite volume system.

Result: The results show that the GGFPEPS ansatz, even with only eight complex variational parameters, can accurately describe the pure Z2 gauge theory, including the topological phase transition. The critical coupling obtained from the peaks of susceptibilities agrees with predictions from continuous-time quantum Monte Carlo simulations. Near the transition point, the ansatz represents a state outside the plaquette product family, indicating its ability to capture more complex states than assumed in its construction.

Conclusion: The GGFPEPS ansatz is shown to be a powerful tool for describing 2+1 dimensional lattice gauge theories, particularly the pure Z2 gauge theory, with high accuracy across different regimes, including the topological phase transition. This suggests that the ansatz has a strong potential for simulating and understanding such systems.

Abstract: We investigate the expressive power of the gauged Gaussian fermionic projected entangled pair state (GGFPEPS) ansatz, which is a manifestly gauge-invariant tensor network ansatz for $(2+1)$-dimensional lattice gauge theories. In the GGFPEPS ansatz, the gauge field on each link is represented by coupling its configuration to the fermion number of virtual fermions placed at both ends of the link. This ansatz has been studied by variational Monte Carlo (VMC) methods in previous work~\cite{Emonts:2022yom}. However, the statistical error of VMC makes a precise determination of the variational parameters difficult, so that the expressive power of the ansatz could not be examined accurately. In this work, we reformulate the computation as a Grassmann tensor network (TN), in which the whole network is contracted deterministically, and introduce the higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG), which allows us to perform calculations in large volumes up to $L=34$. We also evaluate the infinite volume system using the corner transfer matrix renormalization group (CTMRG). We find that the ansatz with only eight complex variational parameters accurately describes the pure $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory including the region of the topological phase transition, not only in the energy but also in the topological entanglement entropy. The critical coupling obtained from the peaks of their susceptibilities agrees quantitatively with the one predicted by continuous-time quantum Monte Carlo simulations~\cite{wu2012phase}. Furthermore, near the transition point, we show that the ansatz represents a state outside the plaquette product family, which is a one-parameter family of states assumed in the construction of the ansatz as a simple interpolation between the strong and weak coupling limits.

Method: The researchers formulated a gauge-fixed lattice Hamiltonian for chiral gauge theories, where the bulk gauge field is determined by the boundary field and an integer label representing the topological charge. Abstract: We present a lattice Hamiltonian for chiral gauge theories on the boundary of a slab. The formulation is gauge-fixed, so the gauge variables are the physical modes of the boundary field, and Gauss's law holds exactly. The bulk gauge field is determined by the boundary field and an integer label, which is the topological charge that the boundary generates and the bulk stores as a localized instanton. The bulk enters as a dielectric, and both gauge anomaly conditions become the question of whether the boundary satisfies Gauss's law on its own. For background gauge fields, we show that sectors that differ by a unit of bulk charge are degenerate after Pauli-Villars subtraction and the mirror fermions decouple. The boundary theory has no vacuum angle. Every term in the Hamiltonian is defined in terms of the boundary gauge variables, the integer label and the fermion modes, a starting point for quantum circuit construction.

Method: The researchers use lattice QCD simulations with highly improved staggered quarks at four different lattice spacings to extrapolate to the continuum limit. They analyze connected correlators for pions, kaons, and a fictitious $s\bar s$ pseudoscalar. Additionally, they compare results from including the magnetic field in only the valence quark propagators or only in the sea quark determinant to disentangle the contributions of each sector.

This deviation, along with the dominance of the valence quark effect, provides new insight into the interplay between QCD and external electromagnetic fields.

Abstract: We study neutral and charged pseudoscalar meson masses in background magnetic fields in $(2+1)$-flavor QCD at zero temperature and physical quark masses. Using the highly improved staggered quark action at four lattice spacings, we obtain continuum-extrapolated masses from connected correlators for pions, kaons, and the fictitious $s\bar s$ pseudoscalar $η^0_{s\bar s}$. Neutral pseudoscalar masses decrease with increasing magnetic field, with the largest relative reduction for the neutral pion. In contrast, charged pion and kaon masses initially rise but tend to level off or decrease at stronger fields, departing from the point-particle lowest-Landau-level expectation. To investigate the origin of this field dependence, we compare neutral and charged pion masses at finite lattice spacings with the field included only in valence propagators or only in the sea-quark determinant. Valence-only masses approximately reproduce the full results, while sea-only masses remain close to their corresponding zero-field values.

Method: The method involves standard lattice discretizations of the action and the energy-momentum tensor, leading to a split of the irreducible representation of O(2) into two representations of D4. Non-perturbative renormalization constants are defined by imposing discretized Ward identities under shifted boundary conditions, and these constants are determined through Monte Carlo simulations.

Result: The result is a set of renormalization constants for the two different representations, obtained with an accuracy better than a permille over a specified range of the dimensionless bare coupling constant squared (0 ≤ g0^2 ≤ 0.6).

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the constructed traceless components of the energy-momentum tensor, along with the corresponding renormalization constants, provide a consistent framework for studying the 2D O(3) non-linear sigma model on the lattice, with high precision and proper symmetry properties.

Abstract: We construct the traceless components of the energy-momentum tensor on the lattice for the two-dimensional $O(3)$ non-linear $σ$-model, such that their correlation functions satisfy the appropriate Ward identities in the continuum limit. The discretization of space-time entails that the traceless two-index symmetric irreducible representation of $O(2)$ splits into two irreducible representations of the dihedral group $D_4$, each requiring an independent renormalization constant. These constants are defined non-perturbatively by imposing discretized versions of continuum Ward identities for one-point functions in the presence of shifted boundary conditions.
