### [1] [Efficient Quantum Simulations of Yang-Mills theory with Maximal-tree Gauge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27267)
*Tianyin Li,Ying-Ying Li,Xiaoyang Wang,Hongxi Xing*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: Researchers have created a quantum algorithm that can efficiently simulate Yang-Mills theories, including the SU(3) gauge theory in QCD, by using a specific gauge (maximal-tree gauge) to remove local gauge redundancies. The simulation's efficiency is supported by quantum singular value transformation (QSVT), and it scales polynomially with key parameters, indicating non-Abelian Yang-Mills theories can be effectively simulated on quantum computers.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation for this paper is to develop an efficient quantum algorithmic framework for simulating complex physical theories such as Yang-Mills theories, particularly the SU(3) gauge theory in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). This is important because these theories are fundamental to understanding the strong force between quarks and gluons, which is not fully accessible through classical computational methods, especially in the non-perturbative regime.

Method: The method involves the use of the maximal-tree gauge to eliminate local gauge redundancies, leading to a more efficient formulation. The researchers then apply a digitization process in the field-amplitude basis and utilize quantum singular value transformation (QSVT) to implement Hamiltonian time evolution. They also provide a theoretical analysis to derive upper bounds on the number of qubits and gate complexity, showing that the scaling is polynomial with respect to relevant parameters.

Result: The result of this work is a quantum algorithm that can simulate non-Abelian Yang-Mills theories, like the SU(3) gauge theory, with a complexity that scales polynomially with the inverse simulation precision, lattice volume, gauge coupling, and target energy scale. This implies that the simulation is efficient and feasible on a quantum computer, even for large-scale and high-precision simulations.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper is that the developed quantum algorithm provides a rigorous demonstration of the ability to efficiently simulate non-Abelian Yang-Mills theories on a quantum computer. This breakthrough opens the door to first-principles quantum simulations of non-perturbative QCD dynamics, which could lead to new insights into the behavior of quarks and gluons under the strong interaction.

Abstract: We develop a quantum algorithmic framework for the efficient simulation of Yang--Mills theories, including the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gauge theory in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). The framework uses maximal-tree gauge in terms of gauge field variables that removes all local gauge redundancies. In the resulting gauge-fixed formulation and digitization in the field-amplitude basis, we show that Hamiltonian time evolution admits an efficient implementation based on quantum singular value transformation (QSVT). We derive upper bounds on the total number of qubits and gate complexity, finding polynomial scaling with the inverse simulation precision $1/\varepsilon_s$, lattice volume $\mathcal{V}$, gauge coupling $g$, and target energy scale $E$. Our results provide a rigorous complexity-theoretic demonstration that non-Abelian Yang--Mills theories can be simulated efficiently on quantum computers, paving the way toward first-principles quantum simulations of non-perturbative QCD dynamics.
