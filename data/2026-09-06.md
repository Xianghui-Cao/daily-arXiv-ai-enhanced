### [1] [Real-Time String Dynamics in $3+1$D Lattice Quantum Electrodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04114)
*Helen Zwölfer,Giovanni Cataldi,Umberto Borla,Jad C. Halimeh*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first real-time simulations of string breaking in 3+1D lattice quantum electrodynamics, using tree tensor networks to simulate quench dynamics. It reveals that at strong coupling, strings break resonantly, converting electric energy into matter-antimatter pairs, and provides a detailed analysis of all competing channels away from resonance, setting benchmarks for future quantum simulators.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand real-time string dynamics in three spatial dimensions, which is critical for linking quantum simulations of lattice gauge theories (LGTs) to the physical dimensionality of QED and QCD, where transverse fluctuations and local processes are significant.

Method: The method involves using tree tensor networks to perform real-time simulations of the quench dynamics of electric flux strings in a 3+1D U(1) LGT with dynamical matter. The study analyzes the behavior of the system at strong coupling as well as off-resonance, identifying and classifying all competing channels such as pair production, string deformations, and flux loops.

Result: The results show that at strong coupling, the string breaks resonantly under specific conditions, leading to the conversion of electric energy into matter-antimatter pairs. Off-resonance, various competing channels, including pair production and flux loops, lead to depletion of the string sector. A channel-resolved perturbation theory accurately reproduces the observed dynamics and their Fourier spectrum.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from this work establishes diagnostics and benchmarks for future quantum simulators dealing with higher-dimensional LGTs, providing a deeper understanding of the real-time dynamics of string breaking and the interplay between different competing processes in 3+1D systems.

Abstract: Understanding real-time string dynamics in three spatial dimensions is essential for connecting quantum simulations of lattice gauge theories (LGTs) to the physical dimensionality of QED and QCD, where transverse fluctuations and competing local processes proliferate. We present the first real-time simulations of string breaking in $3+1$D lattice quantum electrodynamics. Using tree tensor networks, we simulate the quench dynamics of electric flux strings in a $3\!+\!1$D U(1) LGT with dynamical matter. At strong coupling, the string breaks resonantly at a sharp resonance condition of mass and gauge couplings, converting electric energy into matter--antimatter pairs that screen the static charges. Off resonance, we classify all competing channels---pair production, string deformations and extensions, and flux loops---whose multiplicity, extensive for pair production and flux loops, depletes the string sector even far from resonance. A channel-resolved perturbation theory quantitatively reproduces these dynamics and their Fourier spectrum. Our results establish diagnostics and benchmarks for upcoming quantum simulators of higher-dimensional LGTs.

### [2] [Non-Abelian string melting and thermalization in an open lattice gauge theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04117)
*Erick Parra Verde,Giovanni Cataldi,Stefan Kühn,Giuseppe Magnifico,Jad C. Halimeh*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study investigates the real-time dynamics of a 1+1D SU(2) Yang-Mills Lattice Gauge Theory (LGT) with matter, coupled to a thermal environment, using tensor networks. It reveals that the thermal medium can melt the chromoelectric flux string by delocalizing color charges and screening the flux, and that the thermalization time exhibits non-monotonic behavior in relation to the environment coupling. The research also shows a mesonic bias in the steady state and a decrease in thermalization time with temperature, which is contrary to trends in Abelian models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation for this paper is to explore how genuinely non-Abelian gauge structures affect dissipative real-time dynamics in open-system lattice gauge theories, an area that has been less studied compared to Abelian settings. The authors aim to understand the impact of a thermal medium on the dynamics of quark-antiquark pairs and the chromoelectric flux string, as well as to examine the thermalization process in the context of non-Abelian LGT, which could provide insights distinct from those in Abelian models.

Method: The method employed in this paper involves the use of a 1+1 dimensional SU(2) Yang-Mills Lattice Gauge Theory with dynamical matter, which is coupled to a thermal scalar environment through a gauge-preserving Lindblad evolution. The authors solve the resulting dynamics using tensor network techniques. They start with a configuration of a quark-antiquark pair connected by a chromoelectric flux string and then analyze the effects of the thermal environment on the system, including the melting of the string and the competition between coherent and dissipative processes. Additionally, they perform a Schrieffer-Wolff expansion to map the dynamics to a classical exclusion process under strong dephasing conditions.

Result: The results show that the thermal medium leads to the melting of the chromoelectric flux string by delocalizing the color charges and screening the flux. The thermalization time is found to be non-monotonic with respect to the environment coupling, first decreasing at weak dissipation due to environment-assisted transport, and then increasing in a quantum-Zeno regime. In the limit of strong dephasing, the dynamics are mapped to a classical exclusion process, allowing for an analytical calculation of the Liouvillian thermalization time. Furthermore, the non-Abelian structure of the matter produces a mesonic bias in the steady state, and the thermalization time decreases with temperature, which is in contrast to the trends observed in the Abelian Schwinger model but in qualitative agreement with pNRQCD studies of the quark-gluon plasma.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper is that the developed framework provides a gauge-preserving approach to studying thermalization and string dynamics in open non-Abelian lattice gauge theories. The findings suggest that the non-Abelian nature of the theory introduces unique phenomena such as a mesonic bias in the steady state and a counterintuitive relationship between thermalization time and temperature, which differ from the predictions of Abelian models. This work sets the stage for further exploration of non-Abelian gauge theories in open systems and their implications for the quark-gluon plasma and other high-energy physics scenarios.

Abstract: Open-system lattice gauge theory (LGT) has so far been developed predominantly in Abelian settings, leaving open how genuinely non-Abelian gauge structure reshapes dissipative real-time dynamics. Here, we study a $1+1$D SU(2) Yang--Mills LGT with dynamical matter coupled to a thermal scalar environment through a gauge-preserving Lindblad evolution, which we solve using tensor networks. Starting from a quark--antiquark pair connected by a chromoelectric flux string, we find that the thermal medium melts the string by delocalizing the color charges and screening the flux; on resonance, this dissipative melting competes with and delays coherent string breaking. The thermalization time is non-monotonic in the environment coupling, decreasing through environment-assisted transport at weak dissipation before increasing in a quantum-Zeno regime. In the strong-dephasing limit, a Schrieffer--Wolff expansion maps the dynamics to a classical exclusion process and yields the Liouvillian thermalization time analytically. Beyond these generic open-system effects, the non-Abelian matter structure produces a systematic mesonic bias in the steady state, while the thermalization time decreases with temperature, in contrast to the Abelian Schwinger model trend and in qualitative agreement with pNRQCD studies of the quark--gluon plasma. These results establish a gauge-preserving framework for thermalization and string dynamics in open non-Abelian lattice gauge theories.

### [3] [Lattice QCD calculation of the pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $Θ$-term](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04129)
*Fangcheng He,Tanmoy Bhattacharya,Rajan Gupta,Emanuele Mereghetti*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents lattice QCD results for the CP-violating pion-nucleon coupling, with two methods used to extract the coupling. The more precise result is obtained using low energy effective field theory methods or the axial Ward identity (AWI).


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to study the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling induced by the QCD Θ term, which is important for understanding fundamental symmetries in strong interactions.

Method: Lattice QCD simulations are performed on three 2+1+1-flavor ensembles with HISQ fermions at a specific lattice spacing and pion masses. The coupling is extracted from the matrix element of the correlation between the pseudoscalar current and the topological charge, and also through low energy effective field theory methods or the AWI, with corrections for Nπ contamination using chiral perturbation theory and the AWI.

Result: Two results for the coupling are presented: a noisy one after removing the Nπ contamination and extrapolating to the physical pion mass, and a more precise one using low energy effective field theory methods or the AWI, with the latter being 17.4(1.9)×10^-3 Θ̅/(2F_π).

Conclusion: The study provides a method to control the contamination from Nπ excited states in the calculation of nucleon matrix elements, and offers a more accurate determination of the CP-violating pion-nucleon coupling using the axial Ward identity or equivalent techniques.

Abstract: We present lattice QCD results for the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $\overlineΘ$ term from the analysis of three 2+1+1-flavor ensembles generated with highly improved staggered quarks (HISQ) by the MILC collaboration. These ensembles are at lattice spacing $a\approx 0.09~\text{fm}$ and pion masses of 313, 226 and 138 MeV, respectively. The coupling $\bar{g}_0$ is extracted in two ways. First, from the matrix element of the correlation between the pesudoscalar current and the topological charge evaluated between the nucleon ground state. The data for correlation functions with both the pseudoscalar and axial vector exhibit large contamination from the $Nπ$ excited state. We show that these can be controlled at the leading order using chiral perturbation theory ($χ$PT) and the axial Ward identity (AWI, also called the partially conserved axial current (PCAC) relation). The result after removing the $Nπ$ contamination and extrapolating to the physical pion mass is, however, noisy: $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=-7(63)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$. The more precise result $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=17.4(1.9)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$ is obtained using low energy effective field theory methods or equivalently the AWI. Since contamination from the $Nπ$ excited states arises in the calculation of many nucleon matrix elements, we give an extended discussion on them and the use of the AWI for controlling them in the calculation of $\bar g_0$.

### [4] [First Two-Hadron Form Factor from QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04142)
*Felipe G. Ortega-Gama,Raúl A. Briceño,Ivan M. Burbano,Robert G. Edwards*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first QCD calculation of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state, specifically for the $\pi^+\pi^+ + \gamma\to\pi^+\pi^+$ process at a pion mass of about 400 MeV. It uses lattice QCD and a finite-volume formalism to determine the amplitude, which is described by a real-valued, energy-dependent form factor. The findings validate the finite-volume approach and pave the way for future studies on the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of resonances and multi-hadron systems.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide the first QCD determination of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state, using the example of the forward electromagnetic $\pi^+\pi^+ + \gamma\to\pi^+\pi^+$ amplitude, in order to enable studies of the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of hadronic states that are not accessible through traditional methods due to the limitations of lattice calculations in finite Euclidean spacetime.

Method: The method involves calculating three-point correlation functions coupling $\pi^+\pi^+$ finite-volume states with lattice QCD, extracting electromagnetic matrix elements, and combining these with previously determined spectra. A finite-volume formalism is used to nonperturbatively constrain the infinite-volume $\pi^+\pi^+ + \gamma\to\pi^+\pi^+$ amplitude. The amplitude is then described by a single, real-valued, energy-dependent two-hadron form factor, taking into account Lorentz symmetry, unitarity, and analyticity constraints.

Result: The result is the successful determination of the infinite-volume $\pi^+\pi^+ + \gamma\to\pi^+\pi^+$ amplitude and its corresponding form factor, which satisfies the Ward-Takahashi identity across all energies and moving frames considered. This provides the first validation of the finite-volume approach within QCD, enabling further research on the electromagnetic and electroweak properties of resonances and multi-hadron bound states.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the finite-volume formalism, when applied to lattice QCD, can be used to calculate the electromagnetic form factors of two-hadron systems, providing a novel and validated method for studying the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of such states. This work establishes a foundation for future investigations into more complex hadronic systems.

Abstract: We present the first QCD determination of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state. In particular, we calculate the QCD contribution to the forward electromagnetic $π^+π^+ + γ\toπ^+π^+$ amplitude at $m_π\approx 400$~MeV using lattice QCD. Because lattice calculations are performed in a finite Euclidean spacetime, where asymptotic scattering states are absent, this amplitude cannot be accessed directly from correlation functions. Instead, we can constrain this and related amplitudes nonperturbatively using a finite-volume formalism that requires two ingredients: the discrete finite-volume spectrum and finite-volume matrix elements of the electromagnetic current. We calculate three-point correlation functions coupling $π^+π^+$ finite-volume states and extract the corresponding electromagnetic matrix elements. Combining these results with the previously determined spectrum, we constrain the infinite-volume $π^+π^+ + γ\toπ^+π^+$ amplitude in the forward limit. Using constraints from Lorentz symmetry, unitarity, and analyticity, we describe this amplitude in terms of a single real-valued energy-dependent two-hadron form factor. The resulting amplitude and form factor agree with the Ward-Takahashi identity across all energies and moving frames considered, providing the first QCD validation of this finite-volume approach and a pathway toward first-principles studies of the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of resonances and multi-hadron bound states.
