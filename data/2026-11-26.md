### [1] [Light-cone distributions of vector and scalar bound states in the Schwinger model from tensor networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31745)
*Juan Hormaza,Wenyang Qian*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a method to compute parton distribution functions (PDFs) and light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs) of vector and scalar mesons in the massive Schwinger model using tensor networks, revealing different light-cone structures for each meson type. The results show that tensor networks can be used to study light-cone distributions in low-dimensional gauge theories, even for excited hadrons.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to explore the partonic structure of hadrons through light-cone distributions, which are not directly accessible with Euclidean lattice methods, by using an alternative approach with tensor networks to perform real-time evolution in Minkowski space.

Method: The authors use the Kogut-Susskind lattice Hamiltonian, prepare the vacuum and meson states as matrix product states, and identify mesons based on their charge conjugation and momentum. They then evaluate gauge-invariant light-cone correlators in real-time to obtain PDFs and LCDAs of the mesons.

Result: The vector meson exhibits a single-peaked PDF and a symmetric LCDA, while the scalar meson has a double-peaked PDF and an antisymmetric LCDA. Decay constants from local matrix elements and moments of the LCDAs agree and align with nonrelativistic model predictions as fermion mass increases. For large masses, PDFs closely resemble squared LCDAs, indicating dominance of valence quarks.

Conclusion: Tensor networks provide a practical means to calculate light-cone distributions for both ground-state and excited hadrons in low-dimensional gauge theories, offering insights into the partonic structure of hadrons.

Abstract: Light-cone distributions encode the partonic structure of hadrons, but they involve real-time correlations that Euclidean lattice methods cannot access directly. We compute the parton distribution functions (PDFs) and light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs) of the vector and scalar meson bound states of the massive Schwinger model with tensor networks. Using the Kogut-Susskind lattice Hamiltonian with the gauge field eliminated through Gauss's law, we prepare the vacuum and the mesons as matrix product states and identify the vector and scalar mesons through their charge conjugation and momentum. The gauge-invariant light-cone correlators that define both distributions are evaluated directly in Minkowski space by real-time evolution. The two mesons exhibit qualitatively different light-cone structure, dictated by charge conjugation. The vector meson has a single-peaked PDF and a symmetric LCDA, while the scalar meson has a double-peaked PDF and an antisymmetric LCDA, whose vanishing zeroth moment requires a normalization through its first moment. The decay constants obtained from local matrix elements and from the moments of the LCDAs agree, and approach predictions from nonrelativistic model as the fermion mass increases. For the relatively large masses considered in this work, the PDFs closely follow the squared LCDAs, showing that the mesons are dominated by their valence component. Our results demonstrate tensor networks as a practical route to light-cone distributions of ground-state as well as excited hadrons in low-dimensional gauge theories.

### [2] [Parton Physics in the Schwinger Model from Matrix Product States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31749)
*Mari Carmen Bañuls,Krzysztof Cichy,C. -J. David Lin,Manuel Schneider*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents a method for directly computing parton distribution functions (PDFs) and light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs) in Minkowski space using the Hamiltonian framework, specifically applied to the Schwinger model. The approach utilizes tensor networks to represent states and incorporates a light-front Wilson line through a stepwise lattice evolution, demonstrating feasibility for more complex systems while verifying fundamental properties of the distributions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to overcome the difficulty of accessing PDFs and LCDAs, which are essential for understanding the non-perturbative structure of hadrons, in Euclidean lattice calculations by developing a technique that works in Minkowski space.

Method: The authors use a Hamiltonian framework in Minkowski space with tensor networks to efficiently represent Hilbert space states. They implement the light-front Wilson line as an alternating sequence of spatial shifts and Trotterized time-evolution steps on a matrix product state, allowing for the computation of fermion and antifermion PDFs, LCDAs, and decay constants of the vector meson in the Schwinger model.

Result: The study successfully computes the desired parton functions and confirms they meet expected normalizations, momentum sum rules, and symmetry properties. It also demonstrates that moderate bond dimensions in tensor networks are sufficient due to the slow growth of entanglement entropy, indicating the potential for this method to be used in more general scenarios.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the proposed method is effective for calculating PDFs and LCDAs, and it provides a promising approach for future studies of more complex systems, as it can be systematically controlled and all uncertainties are quantified and managed.

Abstract: Parton distribution functions (PDFs) and light-cone distribution amplitudes (LCDAs) encode the non-perturbative structure of hadrons in terms of their partonic constituents. They are defined through matrix elements containing parton operators that are connected by a Wilson line along a light-front direction, which makes them difficult to access in Euclidean lattice calculations. We demonstrate how parton functions can be directly obtained in Minkowski space using the Hamiltonian framework. We compute fermion and antifermion PDFs, LCDAs, and decay constants of the vector meson in the Schwinger model for several fermion masses. States in Hilbert space are efficiently represented by tensor networks, with an expressivity that is systematically controlled by a bond dimension. The light-front Wilson line is implemented as a stepwise evolution on the lattice that alternates spatial shifts of an external charge with Trotterized time-evolution steps acting on a matrix product state. All systematic uncertainties are quantified by varying and extrapolating in the lattice spacing, volume, bond dimension, gauge-field truncation, and Trotter step width. The slow growth of the entanglement entropy makes moderate bond dimensions sufficient, which supports the feasibility of using tensor networks for computing light-front matrix elements in more general settings. We confirm that the computed distributions fulfill the expected normalizations, momentum sum rules, and symmetry properties.

### [3] [Intrinsic Soft Function in the Continuum and Physical Mass Limit from Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31810)
*Zhi-Chao Gong,Min-Huan Chu,Jun Hua,Xiangdong Ji,Andreas Schäfer,Jin-Xin Tan,Han-Zhang Wang,Wei Wang,Jian-Hui Zhang,Mu-Hua Zhang,Qi-An Zhang*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first lattice determination of the intrinsic soft function, a key component in TMD factorization, using large-momentum effective theory. It incorporates the continuum, physical-pion-mass, and infinite-momentum limits, with results extending to transverse distances of about 1.1 fm, and is consistent with perturbative predictions at short distances. The study uses improved axial-vector interpolators for better signal and enables direct calculation at the physical pion mass, providing important input for precision TMD studies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation behind this research is to achieve a comprehensive understanding of the intrinsic soft function, an essential nonperturbative element in TMD factorization, by overcoming the limitations of previous studies that did not simultaneously control the continuum, physical-pion-mass, and infinite-momentum limits. This work aims to fill this gap and provide a precise determination of the intrinsic soft function, which is crucial for precision TMD studies.

Method: The method employed involves the use of large-momentum effective theory for the lattice QCD determination of the intrinsic soft function. The researchers utilize power-counting-guided axial-vector interpolators, which significantly enhance the signal quality and enable calculations at the physical pion mass. Data from six ensembles across four different lattice spacings are combined to ensure a precise and robust determination of the intrinsic soft function, covering a wide range of transverse distances.

Result: The result is a precise determination of the intrinsic soft function that extends to transverse distances of approximately 1.1 fm. The findings are in agreement with perturbative predictions at short distances. The transverse-distance dependence shows an exponential decay, characterized by an effective scale Λ ≈ 0.7 GeV at μ = 2 GeV, which provides a benchmark for nonperturbative Wilson-line dynamics and is valuable for precision TMD studies.

Conclusion: In conclusion, the paper achieves the first lattice determination of the intrinsic soft function with full control over the continuum, physical-pion-mass, and infinite-momentum limits. This determination, enabled by the use of advanced axial-vector interpolators, contributes a critical piece of information for the field, offering a quantitative measure of nonperturbative effects and serving as a significant input for future precision TMD studies.

Abstract: The intrinsic soft function is a universal nonperturbative ingredient of transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) factorization. Despite substantial progress in lattice QCD studies, a determination with simultaneous control of the continuum, physical-pion-mass, and infinite-momentum limits has remained unavailable. In this work we present the first lattice determination incorporating all these limits within large-momentum effective theory, extracting the intrinsic soft function from a light-meson form factor and quasi-TMD wave functions. A key advance is the use of power-counting-guided axial-vector interpolators, which substantially improve the signal and enable a direct calculation at the physical pion mass. Combined with results from six ensembles spanning four lattice spacings, this improvement allows a precise determination extending to $b_\perp\simeq1.1~\mathrm{fm}$, while remaining consistent with perturbative predictions at short distances. The resulting transverse-distance dependence exhibits an approximately exponential decay, with the decay rate being characterized by an effective scale $Λ\simeq0.7~\mathrm{GeV}$ at $μ=2~\mathrm{GeV}$, providing a quantitative benchmark for nonperturbative Wilson-line dynamics and essential input for precision TMD studies.

TL;DR: The paper presents a set of algorithms for preparing composite states in strongly coupled theories, such as non-Abelian gauge theories, using weakly coupled auxiliary fields, without the need for quantum phase estimation. It demonstrates the method's application to J/ψ production in quantum chromodynamics.

This approach is designed to work in scenarios where the system is strongly coupled and avoids the use of quantum phase estimation, which can be resource-intensive.

Abstract: Many quantum simulation tasks, including scattering in quantum field theories and effective non-relativistic theories, require the preparation of asymptotic states. Such a state, which may be a bound state, is typically the lowest-lying excited state in some sector of Hilbert space. At weak coupling these states can often be prepared adiabatically by beginning with the free theory, but this is not generally feasible in strongly coupled theories. We detail a family of algorithms for preparing composite states in strongly coupled theories, including non-Abelian gauge theories, by using weakly coupled auxiliary fields. These methods do not require quantum phase estimation. We demonstrate the method numerically, via classical simulation, on nonrelativistic theories in one spatial dimension. We estimate the yield of this method applied to $J/ψ$ production in quantum chromodynamics.

### [5] [Prospects and limitations of adaptive variational quantum algorithms for lattice field theories: a case study in 2+1 dimensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33138)
*Nico Dichter,Andrea Bulgarelli,Lena Funcke,Stefan Kühn*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper examines the application of the ADAPT-VQE algorithm to higher dimensional lattice field theories, identifies two limitations of the method, and proposes solutions to overcome these issues, providing practical guidance for its use in dimensions beyond 1+1D.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to investigate whether the ADAPT-VQE algorithm, which has been used to prepare the vacuum state of 1+1 dimensional lattice gauge theories, can be extended to higher dimensions, and to address the challenges that arise with such an extension.

Method: The study involves identifying and analyzing the limitations of the ADAPT-VQE algorithm when applied to higher-dimensional systems. The authors also apply the ADAPT-VQE to a 2+1D free Wilson fermion model and propose modifications to the operator pool and initial states to improve the algorithm's performance.

Result: The results show that the ADAPT-VQE algorithm faces two main limitations: real-valued pool operators have vanishing gradients when the qubit Hamiltonian and initial state are real, and the algorithm may converge to excited states instead of the ground state. By including charge conjugation symmetry in the operator pool and using suitable initial states, the researchers were able to recover the ground and first excited states over the full mass range.

Conclusion: The conclusions drawn from this work provide practical guidelines for applying the ADAPT-VQE algorithm to lattice field theories in dimensions greater than 1+1D, suggesting the importance of a carefully selected operator pool and appropriate initial states to avoid convergence to undesired states.

Abstract: The variational quantum algorithm ADAPT-VQE has previously been used to prepare the vacuum of lattice gauge theories in $1+1D$. In this paper, we study whether it extends to higher dimensions. We identify two general limitations of ADAPT-VQE. First, if the qubit Hamiltonian and the initial state are real, i.e., invariant under qubit-level time-reversal, all real-valued pool operators have vanishing gradients and are never selected. Second, the algorithm can converge to excited eigenstates. We illustrate both limitations, also providing examples from literature, and present a first application of ADAPT-VQE to a lattice field theory in $2+1D$, namely free Wilson fermions, where both obstacles occur. Adding charge conjugation symmetry to the operator pool improves the results, and combining different pools with suitable initial states recovers the ground and first excited states over the full mass range. These findings give practical guidelines for applying ADAPT-VQE to lattice field theories beyond $1+1D$.
