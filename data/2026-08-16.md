### [1] [Blind Spots of the Zwanziger Horizon Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12413)
*Daniel G. Tedesco*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the relationship between the first Gribov horizon and Zwanziger's horizon function, focusing on SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in 3D and 4D Euclidean spaces. It investigates if the spectral directions leading to the Gribov horizon are accessible by the sources of the horizon function, using variational estimates and finite-volume calculations.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the connection between the first Gribov horizon, where the Faddeev-Popov operator loses positivity, and Zwanziger's horizon function, which tests the inverse operator via background-dependent sources. The study aims to clarify if the critical spectral directions at the Gribov horizon are detectable by the sources within the horizon function, even when the critical subspace is degenerate.

Method: The method involves a configurationwise analysis of radial SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in three and four-dimensional Euclidean spaces. Variational estimates and finite-volume calculations are utilized to assess the threshold structure for smooth radial profiles. A special case is made for the regular-gauge BPST background due to specific domain issues.

Result: Results include a characterization of the threshold structure for smooth radial profiles in the context of the Gribov and Zwanziger horizons. The analysis provides insights into the accessibility of the critical spectral directions to the sources of the horizon function, but it does not address the statistical significance of these configurations in the Yang-Mills functional integral.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper would summarize the findings regarding the relationship between the Gribov horizon and the horizon function, the conditions under which the critical spectral directions can be accessed, and the implications for understanding the behavior of gauge fields in the context of the Faddeev-Popov operator and its inverse.

Abstract: We examine the configurationwise relation between the first Gribov horizon, defined by loss of positivity of the Faddeev-Popov operator, and Zwanziger's horizon function, which probes the inverse operator through background-dependent sources. The analysis focuses on whether the spectral directions associated with the onset of the Gribov horizon are necessarily accessible to the sources entering the horizon function, including situations in which the critical subspace is degenerate. This question is studied for radial SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in three and four Euclidean dimensions, where angular symmetry constrains the source sector while the radial profile controls the ordering of Faddeev-Popov thresholds. Variational estimates and finite-volume calculations are used to characterize the threshold structure for smooth radial profiles. The regular-gauge BPST background is treated separately because of domain issues associated with zero-energy behavior and the horizon source in the full-space setting. The discussion is restricted to configurationwise spectral properties and does not address the statistical weighting of these backgrounds in the Yang-Mills functional integral.

### [2] [Topological properties around the Roberge-Weiss transition in $N_f = 2 + 1 + 1$ QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12883)
*Massimo D'Elia,Fabio Siliberto,Kevin Zambello*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper examines the topological properties of QCD with $N_f = 2+1+1$ quark flavors at the Roberge-Weiss transition, using stout improved staggered fermions and different lattice spacings. It finds that the topological susceptibility is nearly constant below the transition temperature and rapidly decreases above it, with a sharp transition in the fourth-order cumulant of the topological charge distribution, similar to pure gauge theories.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the topological properties of QCD at the Roberge-Weiss (RW) transition, which occurs at specific values of imaginary baryon chemical potential, and to compare the findings with those from $N_f = 2 + 1$ QCD and pure gauge theories.

Method: The study uses $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD with physical quark masses, discretized by stout improved staggered fermions, and considers two different compactified dimensions, $N_t = 8$ and $N_t = 10$. The researchers analyze the topological susceptibility and the fourth-order cumulant of the topological charge distribution across the RW transition.

Result: Results show that the topological susceptibility remains almost constant for temperatures below the RW transition and then decays quickly for higher temperatures. The fourth-order cumulant, $b_2$, exhibits a sharp transition just after the RW transition, resembling the behavior seen in pure gauge theories rather than the slower transition observed in full QCD along the standard thermal line.

Conclusion: The paper concludes that the topological properties across the Roberge-Weiss transition in $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD are consistent with the $N_f = 2 + 1$ case, with a sharp transition in the topological charge distribution's fourth-order cumulant, suggesting similarities to pure gauge theories.

Abstract: We investigate the topological properties of QCD across the finite temperature Roberge-Weiss transition, which is found for particular values of the imaginary baryon chemical potential. Our study is conducted for $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD with physical quark masses, discretized via stout improved staggered fermions and considering mostly two different values of the compactifed dimension, $N_t = 8$ and $N_t = 10$. Results for $T_{RW}$ and for the associated universality class are consistent with those found in the $N_f = 2 + 1$ case with a slightly different discretization. The topological susceptibility appears to be practically constant for $T \lesssim T_{RW}$, then rapidly decaying for higher temperatures. The analysis of the fourth order cumulant of the topological charge distribution, $b_2$, reveals that it is compatible with the prediction of the Dilute Instanton Gas Approximation right after $T_{RW}$, showing thus a sharp transition, which is more similar to what observed in pure gauge theories rather than to full QCD along the standard thermal line, where instead a slower transition was observed in previous studies.
