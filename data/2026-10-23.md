### [1] [Higgs Fluctuation Solver in Four Dimensions: Part I-A Matrix-Free Global-Scalar Bounce and Determinant-Renormalization Audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30341)
*Merritt s. Cook*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A numerical method for calculating semiclassical false-vacuum decay in a global-O(4) scalar theory, using a matrix-free approach. The study presents the calculation of the bounce action, fluctuation spectra, and determinant factor, and discusses the necessity for additional counterterms to address one-loop divergence.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a robust, matrix-free numerical pipeline for calculating the false-vacuum decay rates in a four-dimensional global-O(4) scalar field theory, which is an important aspect of quantum field theories, especially in understanding vacuum stability and phase transitions.

Method: The authors use a combination of continuum bounce shooting, action relaxation, channel-resolved fluctuation spectra, deflated stochastic Lanczos quadrature (SLQ), and a continuum partial-wave determinant with Gel'fand-Yaglom radial ratios and large-angular-momentum subtraction to compute the decay rate. They also evaluate the trace-log and determine the determinant factor while discussing the need for additional counterterms to account for one-loop divergences.

Result: The key results include the precise computation of the bounce action (B = 145.203947), the RMS EOM residual, the negative radial mode, lattice translations, near-zero internal modes, and the SLQ trace-log. The determinant factor is found to be 1.2931 x 10^-9, and the translation-normalized scalar expression is 5.9974 x 10^-70. The paper also concludes that local rho^10 and rho^12 counterterms are necessary for addressing the one-loop divergence.

Conclusion: The research demonstrates a successful implementation of a matrix-free numerical technique for computing false-vacuum decay, but it also reveals that the leading potential is not closed under its scalar one-loop divergence, requiring further work on finite full-gauge matching and controlled limits.

Abstract: We report a matrix-free numerical pipeline for semiclassical false-vacuum decay in a four-component global-O(4) scalar theory in four Euclidean dimensions. The calculation combines continuum bounce shooting, action relaxation, channel-resolved fluctuation spectra, deflated stochastic Lanczos quadrature (SLQ), and a continuum partial-wave determinant with Gel'fand-Yaglom radial ratios and large-angular-momentum subtraction. For the benchmark potential, the bounce action is B = 145.203947 and the tail residual is 0.281955%. On an L = 26, h = 0.8 lattice, the relaxed field achieves an RMS EOM residual of 4.43 x 10^-12; its low spectrum contains one negative radial mode, four lattice translations, and three near-zero internal modes. The 16-probe bare SLQ trace-log is -2079.9042 +/- 6.4504. Under the subtraction prescription, the scalar partial-wave calculation returns a primed trace-log of 40.9324 at mu = mF = sqrt(0.4), giving a determinant factor of 1.2931 x 10^-9. We show the leading rho^8 potential is not closed under its scalar one-loop divergence: local rho^10 and rho^12 counterterms are required, leaving finite coefficients unmatched; hence this is a prescription-level result, not a complete EFT prediction. The translation-normalized scalar expression is 5.9974 x 10^-70 in lattice energy units to the fourth power, omitting the internal-orientation Jacobian. We summarize tested SU(2) operator and pole-algebra infrastructure, identifying finite full-gauge matching and controlled limits as open work.

### [3] [Learning Low Modes of the Wilson Dirac Operator with Gauge-Equivariant Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30982)
*Minjae Choi,Hiroshi Ohno*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the use of gauge-equivariant parallel-transport layers in neural networks to generate test vectors for adaptive multigrid methods, which reduces setup time without significantly affecting solve times, and shows potential to also reduce solve times near the critical hopping parameter.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to decrease the computational cost associated with generating test vectors for adaptive multigrid methods, which can be so high that it exceeds the time required for a single solution. The aim is to find a more efficient way to produce these vectors, which are essential for reducing the solve time of systems involving the Wilson Dirac operator.

Method: The method involves training neural networks with gauge-equivariant parallel-transport layers on a quenched ensemble using a Rayleigh-Ritz loss function specific to the $D^\dagger D$ operator. The trained networks are then tested on different configurations and at other hopping parameters to assess their performance and the quality of the test vectors they produce, while omitting the $V$-cycle refinement step to save on setup time.

Result: The results show that the trained networks are capable of producing effective test vectors, leading to a significant reduction in setup time. In most cases, the solve time remains largely unaffected, but there is a potential for reduced solve time as well, particularly when the system is near the critical hopping parameter $\kappa_c$.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the study is that by employing neural networks with gauge-equivariant properties, one can achieve a faster setup phase in the adaptive multigrid process, with a non-negative impact on the solve time, especially close to the critical point, thus offering a more efficient alternative to traditional test vector generation.

Abstract: In adaptive multigrid, test vectors that better approximate the low-mode subspace of the Wilson Dirac operator $D$ can reduce the solve time, but their construction can be costly. In some cases, the setup takes longer than a single solve. To reduce the cost of generating test vectors, we train networks built from gauge-equivariant parallel-transport layers once on a quenched ensemble, using a Rayleigh-Ritz loss for $D^\dagger D$. We then test whether the trained networks maintain their performance at other hopping parameters and on configurations not used in training. In these tests, the network outputs are used as test vectors, and the costly $V$-cycle refinement in the adaptive setup is omitted to reduce the setup time. This substantially reduces the setup time while leaving the solve time largely unchanged in most cases. Near the critical hopping parameter $κ_c$, the solve time is also reduced in some cases.

### [4] [Dyon Condensation at $θ=2π$ from Wilson-'t Hooft Loops in Yang-Mills Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31281)
*Hiromasa Watanabe,Issaku Kanamori,Okuto Morikawa,Yuki Nagai,Yuya Tanizaki,Akio Tomiya*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the nontrivial realization of periodicity in Yang-Mills theories at θ and θ+2π, with a focus on SU(2) lattice Yang-Mills theory. It provides numerical evidence for dyonic oblique-confinement at θ=2π by measuring 't Hooft and dyonic Wilson-'t Hooft loops, using a 1-form-covariant DBW2 flow to manage the reweighting factor.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to test the expectation that confining vacua at θ=0 and 2π have different symmetry-protected topological labels due to the nontrivial realization of periodicity when a background field for the 1-form center symmetry is present.

Method: The method involves the use of SU(2) lattice Yang-Mills theory to measure 't Hooft and dyonic Wilson-'t Hooft loops at θ=2π. A 1-form-covariant DBW2 flow is applied to ensure that the global reweighting factor is numerically manageable while keeping the physical non-integer topological charge in a defect ensemble.

Result: The results show a clear perimeter-law plateau for the dyonic loop on two lattice volumes. The direct 't Hooft-loop signal, although limited by noise, exhibits the expected magnitude and distance dependence for an area law, which together with the Wilson-'t Hooft classification, supports the dyonic oblique-confinement pattern at θ=2π.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that there is numerical evidence supporting the existence of a dyonic oblique-confinement phase at θ=2π, consistent with the theoretical expectations regarding the distinct topological properties of the confining vacua at θ=0 and 2π.

Abstract: Although Yang--Mills theories at $θ$ and $θ+2π$ are physically equivalent, their periodicity is realized nontrivially in the presence of a background field for the $1$-form center symmetry. The confining vacua at $θ=0$ and $2π$ are therefore expected to have distinct symmetry-protected topological labels. We test this expectation in $SU(2)$ lattice Yang--Mills theory by measuring 't~Hooft and dyonic Wilson--'t~Hooft loops at $θ=2π$. A $1$-form-covariant DBW2 flow makes the global reweighting factor numerically benign while retaining the physical non-integer topological charge in a defect ensemble. The dyonic loop exhibits a clear perimeter-law plateau on two lattice volumes. The direct 't~Hooft-loop signal is noise-limited but has the magnitude and distance dependence expected for an area law. Taken together with the Wilson--'t~Hooft classification, these results provide numerical evidence for the dyonic oblique-confinement pattern at $θ=2π$. This talk is based on our recent paper (arXiv:2606.13428).

### [5] [Towards Systematic Control of the Nucleon Gluon PDF with the Pseudo-PDF Method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31597)
*Alex NieMiera,Kai Ebira,William Good,Huey-Wen Lin*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study presents updated lattice QCD results for the unpolarized nucleon gluon PDF using a finer lattice spacing, advanced methods to control systematic errors, and shows consistency with some phenomenological PDF extractions, indicating progress in providing controlled constraints for future global PDF analyses.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to improve the precision and control over systematic uncertainties in the extraction of the gluon parton distribution function (PDF) from lattice QCD, which is important for understanding the internal structure of the nucleon and for global PDF analyses.

Method: The method involves using the pseudo-PDF approach on a fine lattice with HISQ fermions, employing the Lanczos method to handle excited-state contamination, and fitting renormalized short-distance matrix elements to account for higher-twist and discretization effects.

Result: Results show no significant dependence on valence pion mass, a suppressed gluon distribution at large x, and are consistent with certain phenomenological PDFs. The calculation reaches higher nucleon boost momenta than previous studies, contributing to more accurate PDFs.

Conclusion: The research concludes that the advancements in methodology lead to systematically controlled, high-precision gluon PDFs, which can serve as valuable inputs for future global PDF analyses and help refine our understanding of nucleon structure.

Abstract: We present updated lattice quantum chromodynamics (QCD) results for the unpolarized nucleon gluon parton distribution function (PDF) using the pseudo-PDF methodology. The calculation is performed at valence pion masses $M_π\approx 310$ and $690$ MeV on an $a \approx 0.06$ fm ensemble with $2+1+1$ flavors of highly improved staggered quarks (HISQ) generated by the MILC Collaboration. This lattice spacing is finer than those used in previous gluon pseudo-PDF studies, providing access to multiple short-distance separations and nucleon boost momenta as high as $4.4$ GeV. To control excited-state contamination and reduce model dependence, we employ the recently developed Lanczos method, finding good agreement with our previous two-state fit approach. Higher-twist contributions and discretization effects are accounted for through explicit parameterizations in fits to the renormalized short-distance matrix elements. Together, these advances represent important steps toward systematically controlled, high-precision extractions of the gluon PDF. The resulting PDFs exhibit no statistically significant dependence on the valence pion mass and indicate a strongly suppressed gluon distribution at large $x$, consistent with a subset of phenomenological PDF extractions. These results demonstrate progress toward providing systematically controlled lattice-QCD constraints for future global PDF analyses.
