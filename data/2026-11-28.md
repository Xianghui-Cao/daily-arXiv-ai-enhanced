### [2] [Unfreezing topology using out-of-equilibrium replica exchanges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34367)
*C. Bonanno,A. Bulgarelli,E. Cellini,R. Dionisio,A. Nada,D. Panfalone,D. Vadacchino,L. Verzichelli*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces a generalized version of Parallel Tempering on Boundary Conditions (PTBC) that uses a gradual exchange protocol between replicas, leading to improved swap rates and reduced computational cost. It validates the approach in the 2d CP^(N-1) model, showing a significant decrease in computational cost and the number of required replicas.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to improve upon the existing PTBC method for mitigating topological freezing by proposing a more efficient protocol for exchanging configurations between replicas, which could reduce the overall computational cost and increase the swap rate between different boundary conditions.

Method: The authors generalize the PTBC by implementing a short out-of-equilibrium protocol where the defect couplings are exchanged gradually while updating the fields around the defect. They use Crooks' and Jarzynski's fluctuation relations to derive the dependence of the swap rate on the average work done during the protocol. Theoretical predictions for the optimal protocol length and swap rate are made based on the scaling of work with respect to the number of replicas, protocol length, and defect size.

Result: The theoretical predictions were tested using the 2d CP^(N-1) model with N=21, and it was found that the total computational cost at a fixed statistical precision decreased by approximately 40% compared to standard PTBC across all explored lattice spacings. Additionally, the number of replicas needed for a fixed acceptance rate was reduced by half.

Conclusion: The generalization of PTBC with a gradual exchange protocol leads to an optimized setup that significantly reduces the computational cost and the number of required replicas, making it a more efficient method for addressing topological freezing in simulations.

Abstract: Parallel Tempering on Boundary Conditions (PTBC) mitigates topological freezing by exchanging configurations between replicas that interpolate between periodic and open boundaries on a small defect. We generalize PTBC by replacing the instantaneous swap with a short out-of-equilibrium protocol, in which the defect couplings of two replicas are exchanged gradually while the fields around the defect are updated. We show that the swap rate depends only on the average work spent along the out-of-equilibrium trajectory by virtue of Crooks' and Jarzynski's fluctuation relations. From the theoretical derivation of the scaling of the work as a function of the number of replicas, protocol length, and defect size, we predict the swap rate and the optimal protocol length. These predictions are tested in the $2d$ $\mathrm{CP}^{N-1}$ model with $N=21$. The optimal setup agrees with the measured one, the total computational cost at fixed statistical precision decreases by $\gtrsim40\%$ with respect to standard PTBC for all explored lattice spacings, and the required number of replicas at fixed acceptance is halved.

### [3] [Lattice gauge theory computation of $1/m_Q$ and $1/m_Q^2$ corrections to the quarkonium potential](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34836)
*Michael Eichberg,Marc Wagner*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper calculates the static quark-antiquark potential and its corrections up to 1/m_Q^2 in SU(3) lattice gauge theory, with parameterizations for certain quark-antiquark separations. It uses gradient flow for renormalization, extrapolates results to zero flow time and continuum, and matches spin-dependent potentials to the MSbar scheme at NLO.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide accurate calculations of the quark-antiquark potential and its corrections, which are essential for understanding the behavior of heavy quark systems, such as quarkonium, and for use in effective field theories like the Born-Oppenheimer approximation.

Method: The method involves using SU(3) lattice gauge theory to compute the static quark-antiquark potential, including 1/m_Q and 1/m_Q^2 corrections. Gradient flow is used for renormalizing the field strength insertions, and the potentials are extrapolated to zero flow time and the continuum limit using multiple lattice spacings and flow times. The spin-dependent potentials are matched to the MSbar scheme at next-to-leading order (NLO).

Result: The result is a set of parameterizations for the quark-antiquark potentials, including their 1/m_Q and 1/m_Q^2 corrections, over a range of quark-antiquark separations. These parameterizations have been extrapolated to the continuum and can be used to study quarkonium systems or other heavy quark applications by converting to specific heavy quark masses.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the provided parameterizations of the quark-antiquark potentials, with corrections up to 1/m_Q^2, offer an improved description of the interactions within heavy quark systems, particularly for quarkonium, and are suitable for use in theoretical studies and predictions in particle physics.

Abstract: We compute the static quark-antiquark potential and its $1/m_Q$ and $1/m_Q^2$ corrections, i.e.\ the $1/m_Q$ potential and all $1/m_Q^2$ potentials, which can be expressed in terms of correlators of two field strength insertions, in SU(3) lattice gauge theory and provide parameterizations for quark-antiquark separations $0.2 \, \text{fm} \lesssim r \lesssim 0.7 \, \text{fm}$. The field strength insertions in generalized Wilson loops are renormalized using gradient flow, which also leads to small statistical uncertainties. All potentials are extrapolated to zero flow time and, in contrast to previous lattice studies, to the continuum, using three lattice spacings and four flow times. The spin dependent potentials are matched to the $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme at NLO and provided at $μ= 3 \, \text{GeV}$. They can easily be converted to specific heavy quark masses, e.g.\ for Born-Oppenheimer effective field theory studies of quarkonium.

</details>
