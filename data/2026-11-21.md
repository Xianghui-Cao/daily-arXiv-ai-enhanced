### [1] [Testing the D234 Dirac operator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25241)
*Jorge Baeza-Ballesteros,Andreas Risch,Mattis Roost,Stefan Schaefer,Rainer Sommer*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper examines a D234 fermion action, which includes non-perturbative and tree-level improvements, stout-smeared gauge links, and an exponentiated clover term. In a quenched approximation, the study shows that this action notably reduces discretization effects, violation of rotational invariance, and the presence of small eigenvalues in the Dirac operator spectrum, when compared to the clover-improved Wilson action.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to improve the accuracy of lattice QCD simulations by reducing discretization errors and violations of symmetries such as rotational invariance, as well as to minimize the occurrence of exceptionally small eigenvalues in the Dirac operator spectrum, which can affect the stability and reliability of the simulations.

Method: The method involves the use of a modified D234 fermion action that has been improved to order a (non-perturbatively) and to order a^2 (at the tree level), with additional features such as stout smearing for gauge links and an exponentiated clover term. The authors perform a comparative analysis against the standard clover-improved Wilson action within the quenched approximation.

Result: The results indicate that the proposed D234 fermion action significantly reduces discretization effects and improves the adherence to rotational invariance. Additionally, it decreases the appearance of very small eigenvalues in the Dirac operator, suggesting better behavior of the fermions on the lattice.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the enhanced D234 fermion action provides a more accurate description of fermionic physics on the lattice, leading to reduced systematic errors and potentially more reliable simulation outcomes, particularly in the context of quenched QCD.

Abstract: We study a version of the so-called D234 fermion action. On top of non-perturbative improvement to $\mathcal{O}(a)$, this action is also tree-level improved at $\mathcal{O}(a^2)$. The version we consider also has gauge links smoothed with one level of stout smearing and the clover term in its exponentiated form. Working in the quenched approximation, we compare this action to the clover-improved Wilson action. The proposed action reduces the size of discretization effects and the violation of rotational invariance to a remarkable degree. The presence of exceptionally small eigenvalues in the spectrum of the Dirac operator is also reduced.

### [2] [Equation of state of hot and dense QCD at the physical point via the complex Langevin equation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25943)
*Michael Mandl,Dénes Sexty,Daniel Unterhuber*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This study uses the complex Langevin equation to calculate thermodynamic quantities for 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, including baryon density, pressure, speed of sound, and adiabatic compressibility, under specific conditions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation behind this paper is to explore the equation of state for 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, particularly in a region of parameter space (large baryon chemical potentials) that has not been extensively studied before, to better understand the behavior of QCD matter under these conditions.

Method: The authors use the complex Langevin equation to simulate the system, performing calculations for thermodynamic properties like baryon density, pressure, the speed of sound, and the adiabatic compressibility. The simulations are conducted above the crossover temperature, with isospin symmetry, and in a setting where strangeness and charm are neutral.

Result: The study yields the first results for the continuum-extrapolated thermodynamic quantities of 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, specifically at large baryon chemical potentials, which provides new insights into the phase structure and thermodynamics of QCD matter.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper would likely emphasize the significance of these first results obtained at the physical point and at large baryon chemical potentials, possibly highlighting the importance of the findings for the understanding of QCD and the potential impact on related fields such as astrophysics and heavy-ion collision experiments.

Abstract: This study presents the first results for the equation of state of 2+1+1-flavor QCD at the physical point, obtained using the complex Langevin equation. We calculate continuum-extrapolated thermodynamic quantities, such as the baryon density, pressure, the speed of sound and the adiabatic compressibility. The simulations are performed in the isospin-symmetric, strangeness- and charm-neutral setting above the crossover temperature and at unprecedentedly large baryon chemical potentials.

### [3] [Half-strings across the deconfinement transition of a $U(1)$ quantum link model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26415)
*Indrajit Sau,Arnab Sen,Debasish Banerjee*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores the confinement phenomenon in quantum link models (QLMs) with and without annealed charges, focusing on the finite temperature phase transition. The critical temperature and exponents are extracted, and the (effective) string tension is compared at zero and finite temperatures, revealing a new universal value for the theory without charges.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to deepen the understanding of the confinement phenomenon in QLMs, which are generalizations of Wilsonian lattice gauge theories. Specifically, it aims to investigate how the addition of annealed charges affects the confinement-deconfinement transition and to compare the (effective) string tension between theories with and without these charges, as well as to find new universal values related to the critical temperature and string tension ratio.

Method: The authors employ a theoretical approach, analyzing the finite temperature phase transition in the spin-1/2 U(1) QLM both with and without annealed charges. They extract the critical temperature and associated exponents, and they also compare the (effective) string tension at different temperatures to assess the impact of annealed charges on the model's behavior.

Result: The study reveals that the quantity T_c/√σ for the QLM with annealed charges resembles that of the usual Z_2 gauge theory, whereas the theory without charges provides a distinct, new universal value. This finding suggests that the presence or absence of annealed charges significantly influences the characteristics of the confinement-deconfinement transition and the associated physical parameters.

Conclusion: The research concludes that the addition of annealed charges qualitatively changes the nature of the confinement-deconfinement transition in the spin-1/2 U(1) QLM, leading to a similar T_c/√σ value to that of the Z_2 gauge theory. In contrast, the model without annealed charges exhibits a unique universal value, indicating a novel phase transition behavior within the context of QLMs.

Abstract: The phenomenon of confinement is arguably the key feature of gauge theories regularly studied in high-energy and condensed matter physics. This causes hadrons to exist as stable particles in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) as bound states of quarks (and gluons), and strings in pure gauge theories. The promise of realizing such strings in quantum simulator setups have fueled extensive studies of a plethora of simpler lattice gauge theories (LGTs). Quantum link models (QLMs) generalize Wilsonian LGTs and yield richer scenarios of confinement. For example, the string joining two static charges in the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ $U(1)$ QLM fractionalize, giving rise to confining phases that break the point group and internal symmetries spontaneously. It was further shown in PRL 130, 071901 (2023) that adding annealed $Q = \pm 2$ charges to this model qualitatively changes the confinement-deconfinement transition. In this paper, we examine the finite temperature phase transition both in the presence and the absence of annealed charges through detailed extraction of critical temperature, $T_c$ , and exponents. The (effective) string tension $σ$ at zero and finite- temperatures are compared between the theory with and without annealed charges. The quantity $T_c/\sqrtσ$ for the QLM with annealed charges is similar to the usual $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory, while the theory without the charges provides a new universal value.

### [4] [Review of Pion and Nucleon Gluon Parton Distribution Functions from Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26443)
*William Good,Huey-Wen Lin*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This review paper discusses the recent advances in calculating gluon parton distribution functions (PDFs) of hadrons using lattice QCD, with a focus on the LaMET and pseudo-PDF methods. It also addresses the current challenges and future opportunities in this field.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand how the dynamics of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) lead to the structure of hadrons, particularly focusing on the role of gluons. The experimental difficulty in directly measuring the gluon structure has led to the development of new theoretical methods like LaMET and pseudo-PDFs for calculating PDFs from lattice QCD, which is an area of significant progress and interest.

Method: The method involves the use of lattice QCD, specifically the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET) and pseudo-PDF frameworks, to calculate the Bjorken-$x$ dependence of parton distribution functions, including those of gluons within nucleons and pseudoscalar mesons.

Result: Recent developments have enabled first-principles studies of $x$-dependent hadron structures, leading to a better understanding of gluon PDFs. These calculations have provided new insights into the internal structure of hadrons and are contributing to the resolution of one of the key challenges in nuclear and particle physics.

Conclusion: The paper concludes that while there has been substantial progress in the calculation of gluon PDFs through lattice QCD, there are still challenges to overcome. The ongoing work in this area holds great potential for further discoveries regarding the structure of hadrons and the underlying QCD dynamics.

Abstract: Gluons play an essential role in the strong interaction that binds quarks into hadrons, as described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD). Understanding how the fundamental dynamics of QCD give rise to hadron structure remains one of the outstanding challenges in nuclear and particle physics. Parton distribution functions (PDFs) provide a successful framework for describing high-energy scattering processes involving hadrons. However, our knowledge of gluon PDFs remains comparatively limited because of the experimental challenges associated with directly constraining the gluon structure of hadrons. In recent years, a major breakthrough has enabled the calculation of the Bjorken-$x$ dependence of PDFs directly from lattice QCD. Among the available approaches, the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET) and pseudo-PDF frameworks have emerged as the most widely used methods and have produced the majority of lattice determinations of parton distributions. These developments have sparked rapid progress in first-principles studies of the $x$-dependent structure of hadrons, including gluon PDFs of the nucleon and pseudoscalar mesons. In this review, we summarize recent advances in lattice QCD calculations of gluon PDFs {using the LaMET and pseudo-PDF methods}, discuss the current status and impacts of these developments, and highlight the remaining challenges and future opportunities.
