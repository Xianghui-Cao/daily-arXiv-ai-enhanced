### [1] [Symplectic lattice gauge theories in the Grid framework: domain wall fermions and continuum extrapolations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19930)
*Ed Bennett,Peter A. Boyle,Luigi Del Debbio,Niccolò Forzano,Ryan C. Hill,Deog Ki Hong,Jong-Wan Lee,C. -J. David Lin,Biagio Lucini,Maurizio Piai,Gianmarco Simonetti,Davide Vadacchino,Alexis Verney-Provatas*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first numerical lattice study using domain-wall fermions in Sp(4) gauge theory, which is significant for models extending the Standard Model. The research demonstrates that the Möbius domain-wall fermions (MDWF) formulation can significantly improve the approach to the continuum limit with modest computational resources.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to explore a theory that is crucial for extensions of the Standard Model, including composite Higgs models and dark matter models, by providing a more accurate short-distance completion through the use of domain-wall fermions. This could lead to better predictions for physical observables and improved understanding of strong dynamics.

Method: The method involves the implementation of the Möbius formulation of domain-wall fermions within the Grid software environment, extensive testing and optimization of algorithmic parameters, and the measurement of meson masses and decay constants. The study compares results from MDWF to those obtained using Wilson fermions, focusing on the approach to the continuum limit.

Result: The results show that the MDWF formulation can provide order-of-magnitude gains in the approach to the continuum limit compared to Wilson fermions, with only moderate computational resources. This is achieved while measuring physical observables such as meson masses and decay constants in ensembles with various fermion masses and lattice couplings.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the MDWF formulation offers a superior approach to the continuum limit, which is beneficial for the phenomenological applications of the Sp(4) gauge theory with two flavors of fundamental fermions, leading to potentially more precise predictions in composite Higgs and dark matter models.

Abstract: We report the results of the first numerical lattice study using domain-wall fermions in the Sp(4) gauge theory coupled to two flavours of (Dirac) fermions, transforming in the fundamental representation of the gauge group. This theory plays a prominent role in the literature on extensions of the Standard Model with composite dynamics. It provides a short-distance completion for a class of composite Higgs models, or, alternatively, of dark matter models based on the strongly interacting massive particle paradigm. We adopt the Möbius formulation of domain-wall fermions (MDWF), implemented within the Grid software environment. We report the results of extensive tests of the algorithm implementation, and of the optimisation of the choices of algorithmic parameters appearing in the MDWF action. We then measure masses and decay constants of the lightest flavoured mesons in ensembles with moderately large fermion masses and several choices of lattice coupling, and perform an extrapolation to the continuum. We compare our results for the physical observables to published measurements obtained in the same field theory, but derived on the lattice by employing Wilson fermions. We demonstrate that, with the deployment of moderate computational resources, the MDWF formulation can yield order-of-magnitude gains in the approach to the continuum limit, in regions of physical parameter space relevant to phenomenological applications of this theory.

### [2] [Numerical Investigations of Phase Transitions in Lattice Field Theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20536)
*Vamika Longia*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This thesis presents two numerical methods for studying phase transitions in lattice systems. The first method uses GPU-accelerated HOTRG to study the 2D generalized XY model, and the second develops a configurational temperature estimator for compact U(1) lattice gauge theories, which can validate thermal sampling and diagnose issues.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: There is a strong demand for high-precision numerical tools that can resolve subtle critical phenomena in physical systems at criticality, despite the progress made over decades.

Method: The research employs two approaches: (1) A GPU-accelerated higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG) technique to characterize phases and map phase boundaries of the 2D generalized XY model; (2) Development and benchmarking of a configurational temperature estimator using gradients and Hessians of the Euclidean lattice action for compact U(1) lattice gauge theories.

Result: The tensor network methods are capable of capturing rich phase structures with controlled truncation and finite-bond effects, while the configurational temperature estimator provides an independent means to validate thermal sampling and can be used as a diagnostic tool to identify sampling pathologies.

Conclusion: The presented numerical investigations contribute to the field by providing new, precise methods to analyze phase transitions in lattice systems, offering both detailed phase structure analysis and a reliable way to ensure the accuracy of thermal sampling.

Abstract: The study of phase transitions plays an important role in understanding qualitative changes in the behaviour of physical systems at criticality. Despite decades of progress, there is still a strong demand for high-precision numerical tools capable of resolving subtle critical phenomena. Motivated by this need, in this thesis, we present two complementary numerical investigations of phase transitions in lattice systems. The first uses GPU-accelerated higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG) techniques to study the two-dimensional generalized XY model, characterizing its ferromagnetic, nematic, and paramagnetic phases and mapping their phase boundaries using thermodynamic observables in the thermodynamic limit. The second develops and benchmarks a configurational temperature estimator, constructed from gradients and Hessians of the Euclidean lattice action, in compact U(1) lattice gauge theories. On one hand, tensor network methods capture rich phase structures when truncation and finite-bond effects are adequately controlled. On the other hand, the configurational temperature estimator provides an independent, low-overhead means of validating thermal sampling across different algorithms and models, and can also be used as a runtime diagnostic to identify sampling pathologies before large-scale production runs.

### [3] [Computability of GPDs near $x=\pmξ$ in Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20545)
*Yushan Su,Xiangdong Ji,Yizhuang Liu,Rui Zhang*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper demonstrates that the condition for large momentum expansion in GPDs computations can be relaxed, allowing for a broader region of computability. It also provides the first observation of the expected partonic threshold behavior on the lattice and predicts GPDs in the distribution-amplitude-like region.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to expand the computational reach of generalized parton distributions (GPDs) within lattice QCD by relaxing the requirement for all hard scales to be much larger than Λ_QCD, specifically for 2|x±ξ|P^z at large ξ, and to observe the partonic threshold behavior which was not previously seen in lattice calculations.

Method: The method involves revisiting previous lattice QCD results with complete one-loop matching to relax the condition for the large momentum expansion and to examine the GPDs' behavior at the partonic threshold, where x is approximately ±ξ.

Result: The result is the successful observation of the expected partonic threshold behavior in GPDs, characterized by continuous GPDs but with discontinuous derivatives at x = ±ξ, and the ability to predict GPDs in the distribution-amplitude-like region, bridging quark and antiquark PDF-like behaviors.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that by relaxing the conditions for the large momentum expansion, the authors have expanded the range of GPDs that can be computed on the lattice, and they have provided the first lattice observation of the partonic threshold behavior, leading to important predictions for GPDs in regions connecting quark and antiquark PDF-like behaviors.

Abstract: In lattice QCD computations of generalized parton distributions (GPDs), the large momentum expansion generally requires all hard scales, $2|x\pmξ|P^z$ and $2|1\pm x|P^z$, to be much larger than $Λ_{\rm QCD}$. We show that this condition can be relaxed for $2|x\pmξ|P^z$ at large $ξ$, making the important $x\sim\pmξ$ regions accessible to lattice calculations and considerably expanding the region of computability. Revisiting previous lattice results with complete one-loop matching, we obtain the expected partonic threshold behavior---GPDs continuous at $x=\pmξ$ but with discontinuous derivatives---which has not previously been observed on the lattice. We thus obtain, for the first time, important prediction for GPDs in the distribution-amplitude-like region, which smoothly connects the quark and antiquark PDF-like behaviors.
