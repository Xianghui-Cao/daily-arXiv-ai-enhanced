### [1] [Machine Learning Unveils Finite-volume Energy Shifts in Three-body System](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28700)
*Wei-Jie Zhang,Zhenyu Zhang,Jifeng Hu,Bing-Nan Lu,Jin-Yi Pang,Qian Wang*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper uses symbolic regression (PySR) to develop universal three-body finite-volume extrapolation (FVE) formulae for both short- and long-range potentials, demonstrating the power of combining machine learning with physical constraints in hadron physics.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the challenge of performing finite-volume extrapolation (FVE) for lattice calculations involving long-range interactions, which are difficult to handle with conventional methods when the force ranges are comparable to the lattice size.

Method: The method involves applying symbolic regression (PySR) to discover new FVE formulae that can be used for both short- and long-range potentials in three-body systems. The approach builds upon a previous data-driven scheme for two-body systems.

Result: For short-range potentials, the known limiting cases are reproduced. For long-range potentials, a dedicated analytic expression is derived, and after including short-range contributions, a unified formula is found that works well for intermediate force ranges around 1 fm.

Conclusion: The study concludes that the combination of machine learning techniques, such as symbolic regression, with physical constraints can lead to novel analytical results, particularly in the field of hadron physics, where traditional theoretical tools have limitations.

Abstract: Finite-volume extrapolation (FVE) is essential for extracting physical observables in the lattice calculation. While rigorous FVE formulations are well established for short-range potentials in both two- and three-body systems, long-range interactions with force ranges comparable to the lattice size $L$ remain challenging. Extending a previous data-driven scheme for two-body systems, we apply symbolic regression (PySR) to uncover universal three-body FVE formulae. For short-range potentials, we reproduce the two limiting cases, i.e. $κ_3\ggκ_2$ and $κ_3\simκ_2$. For pure long-range potentials, we obtain a dedicated analytic expression, and after incorporating short-range contributions, we uncover a unified formula consistent with the original PySR solution, which performs excellently in the intermediate force range around 1 fm. This work demonstrates that combining machine learning with physical constraints can yield novel analytical results inaccessible to conventional theoretical tools, advancing data-driven methodologies in hadron physics.

Abstract: Quantum computers offer the potential to directly probe the dynamics of strongly coupled quantum field theories. Adjoint strings connected to dynamical gluons are constructed in this truncated theory, and the resonances dominating the long-term dynamics at a large value of the gauge coupling are determined. Local operators are constructed to identify string oscillations and breakings. This is used to perform a quantum simulation of adjoint string breaking on an $8\times8$ and $16\times8$-site lattice with ibm_boston using all 156 qubits. Quantitative agreement with tensor network simulations is obtained for circuits with 7,634 CZ gates with a two-qubit gate depth of 218. In this simulation, the rates of oscillations and glueball production are identified with a distinctly non-Abelian signature of the underlying gauge group.

### [3] [Detecting Multiple Phase Transitions in Lattice Systems with Intrinsic Dimensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29090)
*Jie Mei,Tetsuo Hatsuda,Mei Huang,Lingxiao Wang*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces the use of intrinsic dimension, estimated by the two-nearest-neighbors method, as a geometric diagnostic tool to resolve distinct transition scales and identify the degrees of freedom primarily associated with each transition in lattice systems. It demonstrates its effectiveness in the 2D q-state clock model and the 4D U(1) Higgs model, suggesting its potential for disentangling deconfinement and chiral crossover scales in full QCD.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the challenges of distinguishing between different phase transitions and identifying the primary degrees of freedom related to each transition in complex lattice systems, especially when these transitions are closely spaced or not clearly separated by conventional observables such as energy and magnetization.

Method: The authors employ the concept of intrinsic dimension, specifically using the two-nearest-neighbors method, to analyze Monte Carlo configuration ensembles. They apply this method to the 2D q-state clock model and introduce channel-decomposed intrinsic dimensions for the 4D U(1) Higgs model to track transitions associated with specific degrees of freedom. The importance of removing gauge redundancy before interpreting the geometry of the configuration space is also highlighted.

Result: In the 2D q-state clock model, the intrinsic dimension successfully distinguishes between ordered, quasi-critical, and disordered regimes, even in cases where traditional methods fail to resolve the transitions. For the 4D U(1) Higgs model, the channel-decomposed intrinsic dimensions show that each channel's dominant response tracks the transitions of the corresponding degrees of freedom, while the combined channel reflects characteristics of both. Additionally, it is shown that evaluating intrinsic dimensions on gauge-variant fields without removing gauge redundancy leads to results dominated by gauge-orbit directions, underscoring the necessity of addressing gauge redundancy.

Conclusion: Channel-decomposed intrinsic dimension is established as an effective geometric probe for analyzing lattice systems with multiple transitions, providing a means to distinguish between different phases and the degrees of freedom associated with each transition. This approach has the potential to be applied to more complex systems, such as full QCD, to separate deconfinement and chiral crossover scales.

Abstract: Lattice systems with multiple nearby transitions pose two related challenges: resolving distinct transition scales and identifying the degrees of freedom primarily associated with each transition. We show that the intrinsic dimension of Monte Carlo configuration ensembles, estimated by the two-nearest-neighbors method, provides a geometric diagnostic for both problems. In the two-dimensional $q$-state clock model, the intrinsic dimension distinguishes the ordered, quasi-critical, and disordered regimes for both well-separated ($q=9$) and closely spaced ($q=5$) Berezinskii--Kosterlitz--Thouless transitions. In the $q=5$ case, the intermediate phase appears as a broad low-dimensional valley even when energy and magnetization do not separately resolve the two transitions. In the four-dimensional $U(1)$ Higgs model, we introduce channel-decomposed intrinsic dimensions based on gauge-invariant plaquette and Higgs variables. The dominant response of each channel tracks transitions associated with the corresponding degrees of freedom, while the combined channel retains features of both. We further show that intrinsic dimensions evaluated directly on gauge-variant fields are dominated by gauge-orbit directions, demonstrating the importance of removing gauge redundancy before interpreting configuration-space geometry. These results establish channel-decomposed intrinsic dimension as a geometric probe of lattice systems with multiple transitions and motivate its application to disentangling deconfinement and chiral crossover scales in full QCD.

Method: The authors performed a simulation on a 2D Euclidean lattice, computing the path-integral of the Fidkowski-Kitaev Majorana chain with two boundaries. They evaluated the eigenvalue spectrum of the lattice Dirac operator and used the hybrid Monte Carlo algorithm with reweighting of the Pfaffian's complex phase.

Abstract: We report the first numerical examination of symmetric mass generation (SMG) for domain-wall fermions. Our simulation on a two-dimensional Euclidean lattice computes the path-integral of the Fidkowski-Kitaev Majorana chain with two boundaries, corresponding to the two (physical and mirror) walls. We demonstrate by evaluating the lattice Dirac operator eigenvalue spectrum that the local four-Fermi interaction selectively gaps the edge fermions on the mirror wall while leaving the physical wall intact. We further identify a characteristic signature of SMG in the path-integral: the gap of the Dirac operator opens along the imaginary axis of the complex spectrum, in contrast to the standard mass shift in the real direction. The conventional hybrid Monte Carlo algorithm remains practical with reweighting the complex phase of the Pfaffian, which is found to be remarkably small throughout the simulation. These results provide a practical nonperturbative framework for interaction-induced mirror-fermion decoupling of lattice domain-wall fermions.

### [7] [First-principles determination of anomaly-induced pion decay beyond the chiral limit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31024)
*Tian Lin,Xu Feng,Lu-Chang Jin,Chuan Liu,Qi-Yuan Luo*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses a novel method for calculating few-percent corrections to the two-photon decay of the neutral pion, induced by non-zero quark masses. The authors use an anomalous PCAC relation in lattice QCD, achieving 1% statistical precision and confirming π0-η-η' mixing enhancement.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to determine the few-percent corrections to the two-photon decay of the neutral pion, which are necessary for precision tests of QCD beyond the chiral limit, where quark masses are not negligible. This work aims to overcome the limitations of conventional approaches, such as dealing with four-point functions and the cancellation of chiral logarithms, by using a different approach based on the anomalous PCAC relation.

Method: The researchers employ lattice QCD and utilize the anomalous PCAC relation to compute the mass-induced corrections as deviations from the anomaly condition at q^2=0. This method avoids the need for four-point functions and the challenging cancellation of chiral logarithms. They perform calculations on two domain-wall ensembles that have nearly physical quark masses, enabling high-precision results due to the suppression of uncertainties related to light-quark masses.

Result: The study achieves approximately 1% statistical precision for the decay width, resulting in Γ(π^0→γγ) = 8.09(22) eV after continuum extrapolation. Additionally, they find a 2.3(1.4)% positive mass correction to the decay amplitude, which is primarily influenced by isospin-breaking effects, providing the first ab initio confirmation of the π0-η-η' mixing enhancement.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper is that the new method, based on the anomalous PCAC relation within lattice QCD, successfully computes the few-percent corrections to the neutral pion's two-photon decay with high precision. It confirms the theoretical expectation of the mass correction to the decay amplitude and provides the first direct evidence of the enhancement due to π0-η-η' mixing, supporting the validity of the approach for precision tests of QCD.

Abstract: The two-photon decay of the neutral pion is fixed in the chiral limit by the Adler-Bell-Jackiw anomaly, while nonzero quark masses induce few-percent corrections that must be determined for precision tests of QCD beyond the chiral limit. Using the anomalous PCAC relation, we compute them in lattice QCD as deviations from the exact anomaly condition at $q^2=0$, thereby avoiding both four-point functions and the cancellation of chiral logarithms that limits conventional approaches. Because the correction is proportional to the light-quark masses, both statistical and systematic uncertainties are correspondingly suppressed, making a precision calculation feasible. On two nearly-physical domain-wall ensembles we achieve $\sim1\%$ statistical precision each for the decay width, obtaining $Γ(π^0\toγγ)=8.09(22) eV$ after continuum extrapolation. The $2.3(1.4)\%$ mass correction to the decay amplitude, positive and isospin-breaking dominated, provides the first ab initio confirmation of the $π^0$-$η$-$η'$ mixing enhancement.
