### [1] [Real-Time String Dynamics in $3+1$D Lattice Quantum Electrodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04114)
*Helen Zwölfer,Giovanni Cataldi,Umberto Borla,Jad C. Halimeh*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first real-time simulations of string breaking in 3+1D lattice quantum electrodynamics using tree tensor networks, showing that at strong coupling, strings break resonantly and convert electric energy into matter-antimatter pairs. It also classifies competing channels away from resonance and validates these dynamics through a channel-resolved perturbation theory, providing diagnostics for future quantum simulators.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand real-time string dynamics in three spatial dimensions, which is key to linking quantum simulations of lattice gauge theories (LGTs) with the physical dimensionality of QED and QCD, where transverse fluctuations and local processes are significant.

Method: The method involves using tree tensor networks to simulate the quench dynamics of electric flux strings in a 3+1D U(1) LGT with dynamical matter. The study analyzes the behavior at strong coupling as well as the competing processes away from the resonance condition, including pair production, string deformations, and flux loops.

Result: At strong coupling, the research shows that strings break resonantly under a specific resonance condition, converting electric energy into matter-antimatter pairs. Off resonance, various competing channels, such as pair production, string deformations, and flux loops, are identified and their multiplicity impacts the string sector. A channel-resolved perturbation theory is developed to explain these dynamics and their Fourier spectrum quantitatively.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the results provide a new understanding of string breaking in 3+1D LGTs and set up diagnostics and benchmarks for future higher-dimensional LGT quantum simulators.

Abstract: Understanding real-time string dynamics in three spatial dimensions is essential for connecting quantum simulations of lattice gauge theories (LGTs) to the physical dimensionality of QED and QCD, where transverse fluctuations and competing local processes proliferate. We present the first real-time simulations of string breaking in $3+1$D lattice quantum electrodynamics. Using tree tensor networks, we simulate the quench dynamics of electric flux strings in a $3\!+\!1$D U(1) LGT with dynamical matter. At strong coupling, the string breaks resonantly at a sharp resonance condition of mass and gauge couplings, converting electric energy into matter--antimatter pairs that screen the static charges. Off resonance, we classify all competing channels---pair production, string deformations and extensions, and flux loops---whose multiplicity, extensive for pair production and flux loops, depletes the string sector even far from resonance. A channel-resolved perturbation theory quantitatively reproduces these dynamics and their Fourier spectrum. Our results establish diagnostics and benchmarks for upcoming quantum simulators of higher-dimensional LGTs.

### [2] [Non-Abelian string melting and thermalization in an open lattice gauge theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04117)
*Erick Parra Verde,Giovanni Cataldi,Stefan Kühn,Giuseppe Magnifico,Jad C. Halimeh*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores the real-time dynamics of a 1+1D SU(2) Yang-Mills lattice gauge theory with dynamical matter, coupled to a thermal environment. The study reveals how a non-Abelian gauge structure affects the thermalization and melting of chromoelectric flux strings, and it finds that the thermalization time is non-monotonic with respect to the environment coupling, while the steady state exhibits mesonic bias due to the non-Abelian matter structure.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand how genuinely non-Abelian gauge structure influences dissipative real-time dynamics in open-system lattice gauge theories, which has not been thoroughly explored beyond Abelian settings.

Method: A 1+1 dimensional SU(2) Yang--Mills Lattice Gauge Theory (LGT) with dynamical matter is coupled to a thermal scalar environment through a gauge-preserving Lindblad evolution. The researchers use tensor networks to solve the evolution, starting from an initial condition of a quark-antiquark pair connected by a chromoelectric flux string, and they analyze the system's response to the thermal medium as well as the impact of varying the environment coupling on the thermalization process.

Result: Results show that the thermal medium melts the chromoelectric flux string by delocalizing color charges and screening the flux. Coherent string breaking is delayed when the system is on resonance. The thermalization time displays a non-monotonic behavior with the environment coupling, first decreasing then increasing, and can be analytically derived in the strong-dephasing limit. A mesonic bias appears in the steady state, and the thermalization time decreases with temperature, contrary to the trend seen in the Abelian Schwinger model.

Conclusion: The research establishes a framework for studying thermalization and string dynamics in open non-Abelian LGTs, showing that non-Abelian gauge structures have distinct effects on these processes, including a unique temperature dependence and a mesonic bias in the steady state.

Abstract: Open-system lattice gauge theory (LGT) has so far been developed predominantly in Abelian settings, leaving open how genuinely non-Abelian gauge structure reshapes dissipative real-time dynamics. Here, we study a $1+1$D SU(2) Yang--Mills LGT with dynamical matter coupled to a thermal scalar environment through a gauge-preserving Lindblad evolution, which we solve using tensor networks. Starting from a quark--antiquark pair connected by a chromoelectric flux string, we find that the thermal medium melts the string by delocalizing the color charges and screening the flux; on resonance, this dissipative melting competes with and delays coherent string breaking. The thermalization time is non-monotonic in the environment coupling, decreasing through environment-assisted transport at weak dissipation before increasing in a quantum-Zeno regime. In the strong-dephasing limit, a Schrieffer--Wolff expansion maps the dynamics to a classical exclusion process and yields the Liouvillian thermalization time analytically. Beyond these generic open-system effects, the non-Abelian matter structure produces a systematic mesonic bias in the steady state, while the thermalization time decreases with temperature, in contrast to the Abelian Schwinger model trend and in qualitative agreement with pNRQCD studies of the quark--gluon plasma. These results establish a gauge-preserving framework for thermalization and string dynamics in open non-Abelian lattice gauge theories.

### [3] [Lattice QCD calculation of the pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $Θ$-term](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04129)
*Fangcheng He,Tanmoy Bhattacharya,Rajan Gupta,Emanuele Mereghetti*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents lattice QCD results for the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ caused by the QCD $\overlineΘ$ term, using 2+1+1-flavor ensembles with HISQ quarks. The analysis involves two methods to extract $\bar{g}_0$, and it discusses the control of $Nπ$ excited state contamination using chiral perturbation theory and the axial Ward identity (AWI).


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to study the CP-violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $\overlineΘ$ term, which is a significant aspect of understanding fundamental symmetries in particle physics, especially the strong CP problem.

Method: The method involves using three 2+1+1-flavor ensembles generated with highly improved staggered quarks (HISQ) at different pion masses. The researchers extract the coupling $\bar{g}_0$ from the matrix element of the correlation between the pseudoscalar current and the topological charge, and also use low energy effective field theory or the AWI to obtain a more precise result. They address the issue of large contamination from the $Nπ$ excited state by applying chiral perturbation theory and the AWI to control this contamination.

Result: The first extraction of the coupling yields a noisy result: $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=-7(63)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$, after removing the $Nπ$ contamination and extrapolating to the physical pion mass. A more precise result is obtained with the second method: $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=17.4(1.9)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the use of the axial Ward identity (AWI) provides a more reliable and less noisy method for determining the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $\overlineΘ$ term. The paper also emphasizes the importance of controlling $Nπ$ excited state contamination for accurate calculations.

Abstract: We present lattice QCD results for the CP violating pion-nucleon coupling $\bar{g}_0$ induced by the QCD $\overlineΘ$ term from the analysis of three 2+1+1-flavor ensembles generated with highly improved staggered quarks (HISQ) by the MILC collaboration. These ensembles are at lattice spacing $a\approx 0.09~\text{fm}$ and pion masses of 313, 226 and 138 MeV, respectively. The coupling $\bar{g}_0$ is extracted in two ways. First, from the matrix element of the correlation between the pesudoscalar current and the topological charge evaluated between the nucleon ground state. The data for correlation functions with both the pseudoscalar and axial vector exhibit large contamination from the $Nπ$ excited state. We show that these can be controlled at the leading order using chiral perturbation theory ($χ$PT) and the axial Ward identity (AWI, also called the partially conserved axial current (PCAC) relation). The result after removing the $Nπ$ contamination and extrapolating to the physical pion mass is, however, noisy: $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=-7(63)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$. The more precise result $\bar{g}_0/(2F_π)=17.4(1.9)\times 10^{-3} \,{\overlineΘ}$ is obtained using low energy effective field theory methods or equivalently the AWI. Since contamination from the $Nπ$ excited states arises in the calculation of many nucleon matrix elements, we give an extended discussion on them and the use of the AWI for controlling them in the calculation of $\bar g_0$.

### [4] [First Two-Hadron Form Factor from QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04142)
*Felipe G. Ortega-Gama,Raúl A. Briceño,Ivan M. Burbano,Robert G. Edwards*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first QCD calculation of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state, specifically the π+π+ + γ → π+π+ amplitude, using lattice QCD and a finite-volume formalism. The results validate the approach and open up possibilities for studying electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of resonances and multi-hadron states.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide the first QCD determination of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state, which has not been directly accessible in lattice calculations due to the absence of asymptotic scattering states. The work aims to overcome this limitation by using a finite-volume formalism, thus opening pathways for future studies on the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of complex hadronic systems.

Method: The method involves calculating three-point correlation functions that couple π+π+ finite-volume states to extract electromagnetic matrix elements. By combining these with the previously determined spectrum, the researchers constrain the infinite-volume π+π+ + γ → π+π+ amplitude in the forward limit. The amplitude is described using a single real-valued, energy-dependent two-hadron form factor, which is consistent with the Ward-Takahashi identity.

Result: The result is the successful determination of the QCD contribution to the forward electromagnetic π+π+ + γ → π+π+ amplitude at m_π ≈ 400 MeV. The derived amplitude and form factor satisfy the Ward-Takahashi identity across all energies and moving frames considered, providing validation for the finite-volume approach used in the study.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the finite-volume formalism, as applied in this study, is validated as a viable method for determining energy-dependent form factors for two-hadron scattering states. This opens the door for future first-principles studies of the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of resonances and multi-hadron bound states within the framework of QCD.

Abstract: We present the first QCD determination of an energy-dependent form factor for a two-hadron scattering state. In particular, we calculate the QCD contribution to the forward electromagnetic $π^+π^+ + γ\toπ^+π^+$ amplitude at $m_π\approx 400$~MeV using lattice QCD. Because lattice calculations are performed in a finite Euclidean spacetime, where asymptotic scattering states are absent, this amplitude cannot be accessed directly from correlation functions. Instead, we can constrain this and related amplitudes nonperturbatively using a finite-volume formalism that requires two ingredients: the discrete finite-volume spectrum and finite-volume matrix elements of the electromagnetic current. We calculate three-point correlation functions coupling $π^+π^+$ finite-volume states and extract the corresponding electromagnetic matrix elements. Combining these results with the previously determined spectrum, we constrain the infinite-volume $π^+π^+ + γ\toπ^+π^+$ amplitude in the forward limit. Using constraints from Lorentz symmetry, unitarity, and analyticity, we describe this amplitude in terms of a single real-valued energy-dependent two-hadron form factor. The resulting amplitude and form factor agree with the Ward-Takahashi identity across all energies and moving frames considered, providing the first QCD validation of this finite-volume approach and a pathway toward first-principles studies of the electromagnetic structure and electroweak responses of resonances and multi-hadron bound states.

</details>
