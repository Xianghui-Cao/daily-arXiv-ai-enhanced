*Hanxing Jiang,Frank X. Lee*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper applies the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) to reconstruct electric and magnetic polarizabilities of charged and neutral pions using sparse lattice QCD data, without relying on a single analytic fitting function. It provides reconstructions at low momentum and extrapolations to the physical pion mass.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to use advanced machine learning techniques, specifically DDPM, for a more accurate and model-independent reconstruction of pion polarizabilities from limited and sparse data obtained through lattice QCD simulations, which can be challenging with traditional methods.

Method: The method involves applying the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) to the sparse four-point-function data in lattice QCD, using four distinct families of synthetic training functions to avoid prescribing a single analytic form. The analysis covers both charged and neutral pions across a range of masses, with separate treatments for elastic and inelastic contributions.

Result: The results include the reconstructed electric and magnetic polarizabilities for both charged and neutral pions, with reconstructions extended to low-momentum transfers and mass extrapolations to the physical pion mass. This approach allows for a detailed examination of pion properties without being constrained by specific functional forms.

Conclusion: The application of DDPM to lattice QCD data demonstrates its capability to accurately reconstruct pion polarizabilities, offering a flexible and powerful alternative to conventional analytic fitting methods, especially when dealing with sparse data sets.

Abstract: We apply the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) from generative machine learning to a model-independent reconstruction of electric and magnetic polarizabilities of both charged and neutral pions. The inputs are the sparse momentum-space four-point-function data in lattice QCD for the connected quark-line contributions obtained in previous simulations at four pion masses between approximately 370 and 1100 MeV on quenched Wilson lattices. For charged pions, the elastic contribution is obtained from the pion form-factor slope, and the complete electric and magnetic inelastic terms are reconstructed separately. The neutral-pion electric and magnetic channels are reconstructed without an elastic charge-radius contribution. Four distinct families of synthetic training functions are employed, so that no single analytic fitting function is prescribed for the lattice data. We present low-momentum reconstructions to the limit of zero momentum transfer where the polarizabilities are defined, and mass extrapolations to the physical pion mass of 138 MeV.

### [2] [Assessing the expressive power of a gauge-invariant PEPS ansatz across a topological phase transition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13852)
*Etsuko Itou,Tsuyoshi Okubo*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper analyzes the gauged Gaussian fermionic projected entangled pair state (GGFPEPS) ansatz, a gauge-invariant tensor network for 2+1 dimensional lattice gauge theories. By using Grassmann tensor networks and higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG), the study demonstrates that the GGFPEPS with just eight complex variational parameters can accurately describe the pure Z_2 gauge theory, including its topological phase transition, in both energy and topological entanglement entropy. The results are consistent with continuous-time quantum Monte Carlo simulations, and the ansatz is shown to represent states beyond the simple interpolation assumed in its construction.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of the paper is to examine the expressive power of the GGFPEPS ansatz, which has been previously studied using variational Monte Carlo (VMC) methods. However, the statistical errors associated with VMC made it difficult to precisely determine the variational parameters. This work aims to provide a more accurate assessment by reformulating the computation as a Grassmann tensor network and applying HOTRG, allowing for deterministic contraction and calculations in large volumes, as well as an evaluation of the infinite volume system using CTMRG.

Method: The method employed in this paper includes the reformulation of the GGFPEPS ansatz into a Grassmann tensor network, which enables deterministic contraction of the network. The authors also utilize the higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG) for calculations on large volumes up to L=34, and the corner transfer matrix renormalization group (CTMRG) for the analysis of the infinite volume system. These techniques are applied to assess the accuracy of the ansatz in describing the pure Z_2 gauge theory, including its topological phase transition, through the evaluation of energy and topological entanglement entropy.

Result: The result of the paper shows that the GGFPEPS ansatz, with only eight complex variational parameters, can accurately describe the pure Z_2 gauge theory, including the region of the topological phase transition. The critical coupling derived from the peaks of susceptibilities matches the one predicted by continuous-time quantum Monte Carlo simulations. Additionally, near the transition point, the ansatz is found to represent a state outside the plaquette product family, indicating that it captures physics beyond the simple interpolation used in the construction of the ansatz.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper is that the GGFPEPS ansatz, despite its relatively small number of variational parameters, is capable of accurately capturing the physics of the pure Z_2 gauge theory, including the topological phase transition, and even represents states that go beyond the simple interpolation between strong and weak coupling limits. The use of Grassmann tensor networks and HOTRG has proven effective in providing a precise evaluation of the ansatz's expressive power.

Abstract: We investigate the expressive power of the gauged Gaussian fermionic projected entangled pair state (GGFPEPS) ansatz, which is a manifestly gauge-invariant tensor network ansatz for $(2+1)$-dimensional lattice gauge theories. In the GGFPEPS ansatz, the gauge field on each link is represented by coupling its configuration to the fermion number of virtual fermions placed at both ends of the link. This ansatz has been studied by variational Monte Carlo (VMC) methods in previous work~\cite{Emonts:2022yom}. However, the statistical error of VMC makes a precise determination of the variational parameters difficult, so that the expressive power of the ansatz could not be examined accurately. The critical coupling obtained from the peaks of their susceptibilities agrees quantitatively with the one predicted by continuous-time quantum Monte Carlo simulations~\cite{wu2012phase}. Furthermore, near the transition point, we show that the ansatz represents a state outside the plaquette product family, which is a one-parameter family of states assumed in the construction of the ansatz as a simple interpolation between the strong and weak coupling limits.

The researchers calculate the meson masses from connected correlators for pions, kaons, and a hypothetical $s\bar s$ meson. Abstract: We study neutral and charged pseudoscalar meson masses in background magnetic fields in $(2+1)$-flavor QCD at zero temperature and physical quark masses. To investigate the origin of this field dependence, we compare neutral and charged pion masses at finite lattice spacings with the field included only in valence propagators or only in the sea-quark determinant.
