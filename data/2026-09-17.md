### [1] [Testing strong isospin breaking effects in QCD thermodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17725)
*D. A. Clarke,B. B. Brandt*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the impact of strong isospin breaking (SIB) on QCD thermodynamics, particularly in the context of non-degenerate quark masses, using dynamical improved staggered fermions. It examines the difference between u- and d-quark condensates, pseudocritical temperature, equation of state, and conserved-charge fluctuations, comparing these to standard simulations with two degenerate and one heavier quark flavor.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation stems from the recent observation of a large ratio between the abundances of charged and neutral kaons in heavy-ion collision experiments, suggesting that the effect of SIB, even though $\Delta m_{ud}=m_d-m_u$ is small, might be significant for some thermodynamic observables, which has not been thoroughly studied before, especially with physical quark masses.

Method: The method involves performing lattice QCD calculations with three non-degenerate flavors ($N_f=1+1+1$), employing dynamical improved staggered fermions, to simulate QCD thermodynamics more accurately by incorporating the effects of SIB. This approach allows for the computation of various observables such as quark condensate differences, pseudocritical temperature, equation of state, and conserved-charge fluctuations, under conditions closer to the physical reality than previous studies.

Result: The study reveals the specific impacts of SIB on QCD thermodynamics, including how it affects the u- and d-quark condensates, the shift in the pseudocritical temperature, variations in the equation of state, and changes in conserved-charge fluctuations, when compared to the commonly used $N_f=2+1$ setup. These findings highlight the importance of considering SIB for a more precise understanding of QCD at finite temperatures and densities.

Conclusion: The research concludes that strong isospin breaking, despite the small mass difference between up and down quarks, can have noticeable effects on certain thermodynamic observables, indicating the necessity of including this factor in future lattice QCD studies for a more accurate description of QCD phenomena, especially those relevant to heavy-ion collisions and other high-energy physics scenarios.

Abstract: Most modern lattice QCD calculations take the masses of the light quarks to be degenerate, setting $m_u=m_d$, a choice that, while not physical, increases computational efficiency and lowers the complexity of simulations and analysis. For many observables, the systematic error associated with this simplification is expected to be negligible, as $Δm_{ud}=m_d-m_u$ is significantly below the QCD scale. So far lattice studies have examined the role of strong isospin breaking (SIB) in hadron mass splittings, but SIB effects in thermodynamic observables have only been studied in terms of the crossover temperature with unphysically heavy quarks. Motivated by the recent observation of an unexpectedly large ratio between the abundances of charged versus neutral kaons in heavy-ion collision experiments, we investigate the effects of SIB in QCD thermodynamics by computing observables for three non-degenerate flavors ($N_f=1+1+1$) with physical quark masses using dynamical improved staggered fermions. In particular, we investigate the difference between the $u$- and $d$-quark condensates and extract the pseudocritical temperature, the equation of state, and conserved-charge fluctuations, comparing results to standard $N_f=2+1$ simulations.

### [2] [The soft-gluon limit of the Landau gauge ghost-gluon vertex: results for pure Yang-Mills SU(3) theory from lattice simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18830)
*Nuno Brito,Orlando Oliveira,Paulo J. Silva*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses the computation of the ghost-gluon vertex for the pure Yang-Mills SU(3) gauge group using lattice simulations, revealing a form factor that peaks at ~1 GeV, is suppressed in the infrared, and becomes constant at high momenta, consistent with SU(2) results.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the behavior of the ghost-gluon vertex in the context of the pure Yang-Mills SU(3) theory, which is important for the non-perturbative regime of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD).

Method: The method involves lattice simulations in the Landau gauge, using large statistical ensembles of gauge configurations, with checks for finite size effects through different lattice spacings and volumes. The study also highlights the importance of lattice perturbation theory over continuum perturbation theory to account for the breaking of rotational symmetry on the lattice.

Result: The result shows that the bare lattice form factors are consistent across all ensembles within errors. The form factor exhibits a peak at around 1 GeV, it is suppressed in the infrared, and approaches a constant value at high momenta. These findings are in agreement with previous lattice estimations for the SU(2) gauge group.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the computed form factor for the ghost-gluon vertex in the SU(3) gauge group aligns well with expectations and previous studies on the SU(2) gauge group, suggesting a universal behavior of the vertex in these theories.

Abstract: This work reports on the computation of the ghost-gluon vertex for the pure Yang-Mills SU(3) gauge group, using lattice simulations and the Landau gauge. The simulations access only one of the form factors that describes the vertex. The form factor is estimated using large statistical ensembles of gauge configurations, with two different lattice spacings and two different volumes to check for finite size effects. Moreover, the calculation shows the importance of using lattice perturbation theory, instead of its continuum version, to correct for the breaking of rotational symmetry. The measured bare lattice form factors are compatible, within errors, for all the ensembles. The form factor has a maximum at momentum $\sim 1$ GeV, is suppressed in the infrared and is compatible with a constant behaviour at high momenta, in good agreement with the corresponding lattice estimations for the SU(2) gauge group.
