### [1] [Accessing the Wess-Zumino-Witten term using Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05006)
*Fernando Romero-López,Stephen R. Sharpe*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses the Wess-Zumino-Witten interaction in chiral perturbation theory, which leads to a nonvanishing $K \overline K \to π^+ π^0 π^-$ amplitude. It develops a finite-volume formalism for calculating amplitudes using lattice QCD and presents threshold expansions for K matrices, along with leading order predictions from chiral perturbation theory.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a method for calculating specific particle amplitudes, such as $K \overline K \to π^+ π^0 π^-$, which are a result of chiral anomalies, using lattice QCD through a finite-volume formalism.

Method: The authors derive a finite-volume formalism that uses the spectra of two and three particles in finite volumes to access the amplitudes related to the Wess-Zumino-Witten interaction. They also present the formalism for systems $K \overline K + 3 π$ with $I=0$ and $πK + ππK$ with $I=1/2$ and $3/2$, and determine threshold expansions for the K matrices involved.

Result: The results include the derivation of the finite-volume formalism, the determination of threshold expansions for the K matrices, and the calculation of the leading order predictions from chiral perturbation theory for the coefficients in these expansions.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the finite-volume formalism can be used to calculate amplitudes related to the Wess-Zumino-Witten interaction, and the leading order predictions from chiral perturbation theory have been determined, providing a basis for further lattice QCD calculations.

Abstract: The Wess-Zumino-Witten interaction in chiral perturbation theory is a manifestation of chiral anomalies. One of its consequences is the presence of a nonvanishing $K \overline K \to π^+ π^0 π^-$ amplitude. We derive the finite-volume formalism that allows one to access this, and related, amplitudes, using the spectra of two and three particles in finite volumes, spectra that can be calculated using lattice QCD. Specifically, we present the formalism for the systems $K \overline K + 3 π$ with $I=0$ and $πK + ππK$ with $I=1/2$ and $3/2$. In addition, we determine the threshold expansions for the $2\leftrightarrow 3$ and $3\leftrightarrow 3$ K matrices that enter the formalism, and calculate the leading order predictions from chiral perturbation theory for the coefficients that enter these expansions.

### [2] [Equation of state of dense magnetized QCD matter via a robust analytic continuation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05108)
*S. Borsányi,B. B. Brandt,G. Endrődi,J. N. Guenther,G. Markó,M. A. Petri,A. D. M. Valois*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a computation of the equation of state for dense, magnetized, strongly interacting matter using lattice QCD with a non-zero lattice spacing. It employs 2+1+1 flavors of quarks, and uses a T'-expansion scheme to handle the sign problem, which reduces systematic errors. The results include pressure, entropy density, and interaction measure, suitable for heavy-ion phenomenology.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to accurately determine the equation of state for dense and magnetized strongly interacting matter, which is important for understanding conditions in heavy-ion collisions and astrophysical environments, while overcoming computational challenges such as the sign problem.

Method: The method involves lattice QCD simulations with 2+1+1 dynamical flavors of rooted staggered quarks at physical masses, under conditions of strangeness neutrality and isospin asymmetry. To address the sign problem, the authors use imaginary chemical potentials and apply a T'-expansion scheme for analytic continuation to real chemical potentials, which they claim reduces systematic errors compared to traditional methods.

Result: The key results are the computed values of the pressure, entropy density, and interaction measure, which have been tabulated and are directly applicable to heavy-ion phenomenology. The use of the T'-expansion scheme has led to smaller systematic errors, allowing for more reliable extrapolations to the real chemical potential axis.

Conclusion: The study concludes that by employing the T'-expansion scheme, they were able to achieve a more accurate and controlled computation of the equation of state, with reduced systematic uncertainties, providing valuable input for further theoretical and experimental work in heavy-ion physics.

Abstract: We compute the equation of state of dense and magnetized strongly interacting matter using lattice QCD at a non-zero value of the lattice spacing. Our simulations employ $2+1+1$ dynamical flavors of rooted staggered quarks with masses tuned to the physical point. To conform with experimental constraints, we impose strangeness neutrality and isospin asymmetry on our observables. We circumvent the sign problem by simulating imaginary values of the chemical potential and applying the $T'$-expansion scheme to carry out the analytic continuation of our observables to the real chemical potential axis. This novel scheme, adapted to the setup with nonzero magnetic fields, is demonstrated to produce significantly smaller systematic errors in the final observables, compared to the standard analytical continuation at fixed temperature, allowing for better-controlled extrapolations. Our results for the pressure, the entropy density and the interaction measure are tabulated for direct use in heavy-ion phenomenology applications.
