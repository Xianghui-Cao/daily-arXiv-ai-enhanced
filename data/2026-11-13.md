### [1] [Critical behavior of the bosonized Schwinger model with uniform matrix product states](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16092)
*Takuya Okuda*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper uses a Hamiltonian lattice discretization and the VUMPS algorithm to compute the critical mass of the Schwinger model at θ=π, with results agreeing with recent staggered-fermion determinations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide an accurate determination of the critical mass for the continuum Schwinger model using a dual formulation and advanced computational methods, which can be compared against other approaches such as the staggered-fermion method.

Method: A non-compact boson field's Hamiltonian lattice discretization is used alongside the variational uniform matrix product state (VUMPS) algorithm to numerically obtain the ground state on an infinite chain. The oscillator basis that diagonalizes the one-site reduced density matrix of the free limit is employed, and the Symanzik effective theory is used to constrain fitting functions for the continuum extrapolation.

Result: The result is a precise value for the critical mass of the Schwinger model at θ=π, given by (m/g)_c = 0.333 555(5), which is in agreement with recent staggered-fermion determinations at similar precision levels.

Conclusion: The study concludes that their approach using a bosonized model and VUMPS algorithm provides a robust and accurate method for determining the critical mass of the Schwinger model, corroborated by the consistency with previous findings from different methods.

Abstract: Quantum electrodynamics in two spacetime dimensions, also known as the Schwinger model, admits a dual formulation in terms of a non-compact boson field. Using a Hamiltonian lattice discretization of the bosonized model, we compute the critical mass of the continuum Schwinger model at $θ=π$. We numerically obtain the ground state on an infinite chain by the variational uniform matrix product state (VUMPS) algorithm, preserving charge conjugation exactly. As a truncated basis for the local Hilbert space, we employ the oscillator basis that diagonalizes the one-site reduced density matrix of the free limit. We constrain the fitting functions for the continuum extrapolation using the Symanzik effective theory for lattice corrections. Our result for the critical mass, $(m/g)_c = 0.333\,555(5)$, agrees with recent staggered-fermion determinations at comparable precision.

### [2] [Automatic O(a) Improvement for Wilson Twisted Mass Fermions in the Hamiltonian Formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16658)
*Carsten Urbach*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper applies the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to Wilson twisted mass fermions, proving automatic $O(a)$ improvement at maximal twist and deriving necessary counterterms for clover on-shell improvement. It also shows that the clover term in Euclidean space-time can be split into two terms with independent coefficients.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to apply the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to Wilson twisted mass fermions and to prove the automatic $O(a)$ improvement at maximal twist, as well as to derive the structure of $O(a)$ counterterms required for clover on-shell improvement, thereby providing a better understanding of the lattice quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

Method: The method involves constructing an effective Hamiltonian based on the symmetries of the lattice Hamiltonian without using the Symanzik effective action in Euclidean space-time. The authors use this framework to demonstrate $O(a)$ improvement properties and to derive the necessary counterterms. They also employ canonical transformations to remove on-shell redundant contributions.

Result: The results include the proof of automatic $O(a)$ improvement at maximal twist and the derivation of the $O(a)$ counterterms needed for clover on-shell improvement. Additionally, it is shown that the clover term splits into two distinct terms with separate coefficients when transitioning from isotropic Euclidean space-time to the Hamiltonian formulation.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework successfully demonstrates the automatic $O(a)$ improvement of Wilson twisted mass fermions and provides a systematic approach to understand and implement the clover on-shell improvement. The splitting of the clover term into two parts suggests a more nuanced treatment of lattice artifacts.

Abstract: We apply the recently developed Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to Wilson twisted mass fermions. The effective Hamiltonian is constructed solely based on the symmetries of the lattice Hamiltonian without recourse to the Symanzik effective action in Euclidean space-time. We use it to prove automatic $O(a)$ improvement at maximal twist, as well as $O(a)$ improvement via so-called mass and Wilson average. Moreover, we derive the structure of $O(a)$ counterterms pre-requisite for clover on-shell improvement of Wilson fermions. We show how to remove on-shell redundant contributions using canonical transformations, and find that the well known clover term in isotropic Euclidean space time splits into two terms with independent coefficients.

### [3] [Numerical tests of chiral fermion weights with $η$-invariant phases in the 3450 model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16876)
*Tatsuhiro Misumi*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper numerically tests a method for coupling continuum chiral fermions to interpolated lattice gauge fields using a Pauli-Villars (PV) regularized magnitude and an Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) $\eta$-invariant phase. It validates the method's accuracy against known exact results in a 2D $U(1)$ theory, showing significant improvements over methods that omit the APS phase, and examines the impact of the $\eta$-invariant on the phase of a twelve-point correlator at unit flux.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to validate a new prescription for coupling chiral fermions to lattice gauge fields by comparing numerical results with known exact solutions, particularly focusing on the role of the APS $\eta$-invariant phase in improving the accuracy of the fermion weight.

Method: The authors use a 2D $U(1)$ theory with specific left-handed and right-handed charges, where the torus determinant is exactly known. They evaluate the Polyakov loops on a $16 \times 16$ grid, calculate the induced mass from vacuum-polarization response, and compute a twelve-point correlator on a cylinder with the generalized APS boundary condition, both with and without the inclusion of the $\eta$-invariant phase.

Result: At zero magnetic flux, the inclusion of the APS phase brings the Polyakov loop values within 0.04% of the exact result, while omitting it reduces the accuracy significantly. The induced mass calculation is also in close agreement with the theoretical value. At unit flux, the magnitude of the twelve-point correlator agrees with the exact result within 0.8%, and the phase deviation is much smaller when the $\eta$-invariant is included, compared to when it is omitted. The phase change due to different extensions into the third dimension cancels out in the anomaly-free combination.

Conclusion: The study concludes that the inclusion of the APS $\eta$-invariant phase in the fermion weight is crucial for accurately reproducing the known exact results, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method for coupling chiral fermions to lattice gauge fields.

Abstract: We test numerically a recently proposed prescription for coupling continuum chiral fermions to interpolated lattice gauge fields, in which the fermion weight has a Pauli-Villars (PV) regularized magnitude and a phase given by an Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) $η$-invariant in one higher dimension. We use the anomaly-free two-dimensional $U(1)$ theory with left-handed charges $3,4$ and right-handed charges $5,0$, whose torus determinant is known exactly. At zero magnetic flux, with gauge fields interpolated from lattice links, the Polyakov loops evaluated on a $16\times16$ grid of flat connections agree with the exact values within $0.04\%$, whereas omitting the APS phase substantially reduces them to about $3\%$ of the exact result. The vacuum-polarization response yields an induced mass $m/e=2.82089$, close to $5/\sqrtπ=2.82095$. At unit flux, where zero modes make the determinant vanish, we calculate a twelve-point correlator on a cylinder with the generalized APS boundary condition. For three smooth backgrounds its magnitude agrees with the exact result within $0.8\%$. Without the $η$-invariant its phase deviates by $1.8$-$3.0$ mrad, and with it by $0.2$-$0.5$ mrad. The phase of each charged fermion changes by $15$-$46$ mrad when the gauge field is extended differently into the third dimension, but the change cancels to below $0.2$ mrad in the anomaly-free combination.
