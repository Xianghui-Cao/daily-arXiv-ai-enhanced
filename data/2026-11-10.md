### [1] [Chiral Response of QCD Topology across the Thermal Crossover](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09150)
*Heng-Tong Ding,Jin-Biao Gu,José Javier Hernández Hernández,Sheng-Tai Li,Jia Ni*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a new relation in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) that connects the light-quark-mass derivative of the topological susceptibility to the correlation between the squared topological charge and the integrated light-quark scalar density. This allows for the determination of the chiral response of QCD topology at fixed quark masses without needing additional simulations. The study, conducted over a range of temperatures, finds that the chiral response increases through the crossover, indicating a shift from linear to quadratic dependence of the topological susceptibility on the light-quark mass. An effective amplitude, derived from the topological susceptibility, peaks near the crossover temperature, suggesting a complex reorganization of QCD topology with temperature.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a method for directly assessing the chiral response of QCD topology without requiring simulations at various quark masses, thereby simplifying the analysis of how the topological susceptibility changes with light-quark mass. Additionally, the authors aim to understand the thermal reorganization of QCD topology beyond the simple suppression of topological fluctuations.

Method: The researchers introduce a novel theoretical relation in QCD and apply it to (2+1)-flavor lattice QCD simulations at a specific pion mass. They analyze the data across a wide temperature range, both below and above the chiral crossover, to determine the local logarithmic light-quark-mass derivative of the topological susceptibility, which they term the 'chiral response'.

Result: The results show that the chiral response increases rapidly through the chiral crossover, changing the topological susceptibility's dependence on the light-quark mass from nearly linear to nearly quadratic. The effective amplitude, inferred from the topological susceptibility, has a peak near the crossover temperature, which reflects the interplay between the decreasing topological susceptibility and the increasing chiral response.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that there is a significant thermal reorganization of the quark-mass dependence of QCD topology, as evidenced by the evolution of the chiral response and the behavior of the effective amplitude. This finding suggests that the relationship between the topological susceptibility and the light-quark mass is more intricate than previously thought, particularly around the chiral crossover.

Abstract: We introduce a novel relation connecting the light-quark-mass derivative of the topological susceptibility in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) to the connected correlation between the squared topological charge and the integrated light-quark scalar density. This relation gives direct access to the chiral response of QCD topology---the local logarithmic light-quark-mass derivative of the susceptibility---from a single ensemble at fixed quark masses for each temperature, without additional simulations at shifted masses. Applying this relation to $(2+1)$-flavor lattice QCD at $m_π\simeq220\,\mathrm{MeV}$ over temperatures ranging from well below to well above the chiral crossover, we find that the response rises rapidly through the crossover from approximately unity toward two. This evolution corresponds to a change from nearly linear to nearly quadratic dependence of the topological susceptibility on the light-quark mass. Using the chiral response as the exponent of a local power law in the light-quark mass, we determine its effective amplitude from the topological susceptibility. This amplitude exhibits a maximum near the crossover temperature, reflecting the competition between the decreasing topological susceptibility and the increasing chiral response. These results reveal a thermal reorganization of the quark-mass dependence of QCD topology beyond the suppression of topological fluctuations alone.

### [3] [Emergence and polymer physics of the center-vortex network: Confronting SU(4) lattice Yang-Mills theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09865)
*Luis E. Oxman,Jackson A. Mickley,Derek B. Leinweber*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study explores the reorganization of elementary Cartan fluxes into center-charge sectors in a percolating phase, which follow a Casimir scaling law. It then compares this with observed doubly charged vortex chains in SU(4) lattice simulations, using new polymer-inspired techniques to analyze the growth and bending of center-vortex chains, showing good agreement for the SU(4) case.

Motivation: The motivation is to understand the infrared dynamics underlying confinement in Yang-Mills theory through the geometric organization and statistical properties of center-vortex networks, and to validate a previously proposed confinement mechanism based on physically equivalent elementary Cartan fluxes.

Method: The method involves the analysis of how elementary Cartan fluxes reorganize in the percolating phase and the application of polymer-inspired techniques to characterize the growth and bending of center-vortex chains in SU(3) and SU(4).

Result: The result is that the dominant vortex tensions of the reorganized center-charge sectors follow a Casimir scaling law, and there is good quantitative agreement for the SU(4) doubly charged sector when compared with lattice simulation data.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the results is that the proposed confinement mechanism, based on the reorganization of elementary Cartan fluxes, is further supported by the observed behavior of center-vortex chains in SU(4), particularly in the doubly charged sector.

Abstract: The geometric organization and statistical properties of center-vortex networks provide a window into the infrared dynamics underlying confinement in Yang-Mills theory. Motivated by this connection, we investigate new consequences of a previously proposed confinement mechanism based on physically equivalent elementary Cartan fluxes. We show that, in the percolating phase, these fluxes dynamically reorganize into center-charge sectors whose dominant vortex tensions satisfy a Casimir scaling law, similar to that observed for confining string tensions. We then confront this result with the first nontrivial Yang-Mills case: the doubly charged vortex chains recently observed in SU(4) lattice simulations. To extract the parameters needed to connect the lattice ensemble with the effective description, we characterize how center-vortex chains grow and bend in SU(3) and SU(4) using polymer-inspired techniques not previously applied to center vortices. We find good quantitative agreement for the SU(4) doubly charged sector, providing further evidence for the proposed mechanism.
