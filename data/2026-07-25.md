### [1] [Spin and momentum fraction carried by partons in the nucleon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21230)
*Constantia Alexandrou,Simone Bacchio,Jacob Finkenrath,Christos Iona,Giannis Koutsou,Christian Kummer,Yan Li,Bhavna Prasad,Gregoris Spanoudes*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study uses lattice QCD with physical quark masses to determine the momentum and angular momentum of quarks and gluons in a proton, finding that both the momentum and spin sum rules are satisfied, with results comparable to phenomenological analyses.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to accurately determine the contributions of quarks and gluons to the proton's momentum and angular momentum, including their fractions and total angular momentum, as well as the intrinsic quark spin and orbital angular momentum, using lattice QCD simulations at the physical pion mass point.

Method: The method involves the use of four ensembles in lattice QCD, each with up, down, strange, and charm quarks at their physical masses, allowing for the extraction of quark and gluon properties. The calculations are performed across different lattice spacings to enable the continuum limit to be taken directly at the physical pion mass.

Result: The results show a total momentum fraction <x_N> = 0.995(60)(29) and a total spin J_N = 0.507(43)(65), which satisfy the momentum and spin sum rules, and these findings are consistent with those from phenomenological analyses.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the momentum and angular momentum carried by quarks and gluons within the proton have been successfully quantified, and the calculated values agree with theoretical expectations, thereby providing a solid foundation for understanding the internal structure of the proton.

Abstract: We determine the momentum fraction and angular momentum carried by quarks and gluons in the proton in lattice QCD. We use four ensembles simulated with up, down, strange and charm quarks with their masses tuned to their physical values. These ensembles have similar physical volume and different lattice spacings allowing us to take the continuum limit directly at the physical pion mass point. We extract the quark and gluon momentum fractions and total angular momentum in the continuum limit as well as the intrinsic quark spin and orbital angular momentum contributions to the proton spin. We find the total momentum fraction $\langle x_N \rangle= 0.995(60)(29)$ and the total spin $J_N = 0.507(43)(65)$, showing that both the momentum and spin sum rules are satisfied. We compare our results to those extracted from phenomenological analyses.

### [2] [Perturbative quantum electrodynamics with generalized domain wall fermions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21346)
*Matteo Di Carlo,Antonin Portelli*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper expands on the generalized domain-wall fermion Dirac operator, including electromagnetic corrections up to the second order, and derives new local operator insertions for gauge covariant calculations in QCD+QED.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a more accurate framework for lattice computations of radiative corrections to hadronic processes involving chiral fermions by accounting for electromagnetic effects within the domain-wall fermion formalism.

Method: The authors use a background-field approach to re-derive the first-order correction and obtain new local operator insertions (seagull vertices) at the second order, which are necessary for gauge covariant calculations in the context of the generalized domain-wall fermion formulation of QCD+QED.

Result: The results include the expansion of the Dirac operator with electromagnetic corrections up to O(e^2), and the derivation of new seagull vertex terms that are essential for maintaining gauge covariance in the presence of electromagnetic interactions.

Conclusion: The paper concludes with a set of new terms that must be included in the lattice action for the correct treatment of electromagnetic corrections in simulations of hadronic processes, thereby improving the precision of such calculations.

Abstract: In this paper we derive the expansion of the generalized domain-wall fermion Dirac operator including electromagnetic corrections up to $\mathcal{O}(e^2)$, which are relevant for lattice computations of radiative corrections to hadronic processes with chiral fermions. In the generalized formulation of the domain-wall fermionic QCD+QED action, physical quark fields are related to the corresponding five-dimensional fields in a way which depends on the (QCD+QED) gauge links, generating extra contact terms when expanding correlation functions with respect to the electric charge. We re-derive the known first-order correction using a background-field approach and, at second order, obtain new local operator insertions (seagull vertices) required for gauge covariant calculations.

### [3] [Stochastic Quantization as Optimal Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21436)
*Lingxiao Wang*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents a method to formulate stochastic quantization as a finite-time stochastic optimal control problem, using a reference process and a neural network to learn the residual control. This approach, termed Optimal Stochastic Quantization (OSQ), allows for the recovery of observables in a 2D lattice scalar φ^4 theory, without introducing model bias even with imperfect training, and suggests a practical diffusion-horizon window set by noise amplitude.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a new perspective on stochastic quantization by framing it as an optimal control problem, which can be solved using a neural network. This approach aims to overcome limitations of traditional methods that rely on reaching equilibrium, particularly in dealing with multimodal potentials and ensuring unbiased estimations even when the training is not perfect.

Method: The method involves formulating the quantization as a stochastic optimal control problem, where a tractable reference process, such as the free theory, guides the dynamics. The interaction part is treated as a terminal cost, and a neural network is used to learn the residual control necessary to steer the system towards the desired target distribution at a given time. The Doob transform is applied to reweight the paths, and the variance of the estimators is managed through the path weights.

Result: The results show that all modes of multimodal potentials can be recovered within a finite time, and a practical diffusion-horizon window is identified based on the noise amplitude. In the case of two-dimensional lattice scalar φ^4 theory, the method successfully recovers observables close to the critical point, comparable to those obtained from hybrid Monte Carlo simulations.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that stochastic quantization can effectively be formulated as a control problem, offering a novel way to achieve quantization without relying on equilibration. This opens up possibilities for more efficient and accurate quantum field theory calculations, especially in complex systems with multimodal distributions.

Abstract: Stochastic quantization defines a Euclidean quantum field theory as the equilibrium of a fictitious-time Langevin dynamics, which reaches the Gibbs measure asymptotically. We show that this quantization can be formulated as a finite-time stochastic optimal control problem. A tractable reference process, naturally supplied by the free theory when available, provides an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck dynamics, while the full interaction enters as a reference-corrected terminal cost. The optimal control is a Doob-transform force that steers the path-reweighted terminal ensemble to the target at a prescribed time and for a given noise amplitude. A neural network learns the residual control, realizing this optimal stochastic quantization (OSQ). Because the path weights are exact, imperfect training increases the variance of estimators but does not introduce model bias. On multimodal potentials we recover all modes at finite time and find that the noise amplitude sets a practical diffusion-horizon window. In two-dimensional lattice scalar $φ^4$ theory we recover observables from hybrid Monte Carlo simulations near the critical point. Quantization is thereby formulated as control rather than equilibration.

### [4] [Parallel Tempered Metadynamics for full QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21575)
*Timo Eichhorn,Gianluca Fuwa,Christian Hoelbling,Lukas Varnhorst*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: An algorithm combining parallel tempering and enhanced sampling methods is introduced to solve topological freezing in lattice QCD simulations, successfully unfreezing a system with 2 staggered fermions that was previously frozen for about 40,000 MDU using the RHMC algorithm.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to address the issue of topological freezing in lattice Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) simulations, which can hinder the efficiency and effectiveness of such simulations, particularly in challenging systems like those involving 2 flavors of staggered fermions.

Method: The authors developed an algorithm that integrates parallel tempering with collective-variable-based enhanced sampling techniques. This novel approach is then applied specifically to a system featuring 2 flavors of staggered fermions, known for its difficulty in terms of topological freezing.

Result: The results show that the proposed algorithm effectively overcomes the topological freezing problem, allowing the system to be 'unfrozen' - a state where it was previously stuck for around 40,000 Molecular Dynamics Units (MDU) when using the Rational Hybrid Monte Carlo (RHMC) algorithm.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper suggests that the new algorithm, which combines parallel tempering and enhanced sampling, is a significant advancement in dealing with topological freezing within lattice QCD, especially for complex systems, as it demonstrates the ability to restore dynamical processes in otherwise frozen simulations.

Abstract: We present an algorithm that addresses topological freezing in lattice QCD simulations by combining parallel tempering with collective-variable-based enhanced sampling methods, and apply it to a particularly challenging system with $N_f = 2$ staggered fermions. We find that the algorithm unfreezes the system, which is otherwise completely frozen for approximately 40000 Molecular Dynamics Units with the Rational Hybrid Monte Carlo algorithm.

</details>
