### [1] [Higgs Fluctuation Solver in Four Dimensions: Part I-A Matrix-Free Global-Scalar Bounce and Determinant-Renormalization Audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30341)
*Merritt s. Cook*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a numerical method for studying false-vacuum decay in a specific scalar theory, providing precise calculations of the bounce action and other related quantities, but also indicating that certain counterterms are required for a complete effective field theory (EFT) prediction.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a matrix-free numerical approach for analyzing semiclassical false-vacuum decay, which allows for more accurate and efficient calculations in a four-component global-O(4) scalar theory. This work aims to provide a benchmark for future studies and to identify the necessary elements for a complete EFT prediction.

Method: The authors use a combination of continuum bounce shooting, action relaxation, channel-resolved fluctuation spectra, deflated stochastic Lanczos quadrature (SLQ), and a continuum partial-wave determinant with Gel'fand-Yaglom radial ratios and large-angular-momentum subtraction to calculate the relevant quantities such as the bounce action, trace-log, and determinant factor. They also test the SU(2) operator and pole-algebra infrastructure.

Result: The calculation yields a precise value for the bounce action (B = 145.203947) and demonstrates the presence of one negative radial mode, four lattice translations, and three near-zero internal modes in the low spectrum. The SLQ trace-log is -2079.9042 +/- 6.4504, and after applying the subtraction prescription, the scalar partial-wave calculation provides a primed trace-log of 40.9324, resulting in a determinant factor of 1.2931 x 10^-9. It is found that additional local rho^10 and rho^12 counterterms are required for a complete EFT prediction.

Conclusion: The study establishes a new numerical pipeline for investigating false-vacuum decay, achieving high precision in the calculated quantities. However, it also reveals that the leading potential is not closed under its scalar one-loop divergence, necessitating further terms for a full EFT description. The work sets the stage for future research, including finite full-gauge matching and controlled limits.

Abstract: We report a matrix-free numerical pipeline for semiclassical false-vacuum decay in a four-component global-O(4) scalar theory in four Euclidean dimensions. The calculation combines continuum bounce shooting, action relaxation, channel-resolved fluctuation spectra, deflated stochastic Lanczos quadrature (SLQ), and a continuum partial-wave determinant with Gel'fand-Yaglom radial ratios and large-angular-momentum subtraction. For the benchmark potential, the bounce action is B = 145.203947 and the tail residual is 0.281955%. On an L = 26, h = 0.8 lattice, the relaxed field achieves an RMS EOM residual of 4.43 x 10^-12; its low spectrum contains one negative radial mode, four lattice translations, and three near-zero internal modes. The 16-probe bare SLQ trace-log is -2079.9042 +/- 6.4504. Under the subtraction prescription, the scalar partial-wave calculation returns a primed trace-log of 40.9324 at mu = mF = sqrt(0.4), giving a determinant factor of 1.2931 x 10^-9. We show the leading rho^8 potential is not closed under its scalar one-loop divergence: local rho^10 and rho^12 counterterms are required, leaving finite coefficients unmatched; hence this is a prescription-level result, not a complete EFT prediction. The translation-normalized scalar expression is 5.9974 x 10^-70 in lattice energy units to the fourth power, omitting the internal-orientation Jacobian. We summarize tested SU(2) operator and pole-algebra infrastructure, identifying finite full-gauge matching and controlled limits as open work.

### [2] [Spontaneous chiral-symmetry breaking in four-dimensional QCD with $A_x=A_y=0$ projection in dimensional reduction gauge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30850)
*Kei Tohme,Hideo Suganuma*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores spontaneous chiral-symmetry breaking in 4D QCD using the dimensional reduction (DR) gauge, and finds that $A_{t}(s)$ and $A_{z}(s)$ are key to this process, while $A_{x}(s)$ and $A_{y}(s)$ are not, as evidenced by the chiral condensate's behavior under different projections.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the role of different components of the gauge field in the mechanism of spontaneous chiral-symmetry breaking within the context of the dimensional reduction (DR) gauge in 4D QCD.

Method: The authors use a specific definition of the DR gauge that minimizes a certain integral. They then apply two types of projections, 'tz-projection' and 'xy-projection', on the gauge configurations. The chiral condensate is estimated from the low-lying Dirac-eigenvalue density using the Banks-Casher relation, to observe its behavior under these projections.

Result: The study reveals that the chiral condensate is largely preserved with the 'tz-projection', but it vanishes with the 'xy-projection'. This suggests that the temporal and longitudinal ($A_{t}(s)$ and $A_{z}(s)$) components of the gauge fields are essential for spontaneous chiral-symmetry breaking, whereas the transverse components ($A_{x}(s)$ and $A_{y}(s)$) do not contribute to this effect.

Conclusion: The research concludes that in the DR gauge, the spontaneous chiral-symmetry breaking is driven by the $A_{t}(s)$ and $A_{z}(s)$ components, indicating their dominant role, while the $A_{x}(s)$ and $A_{y}(s)$ components are not involved in this symmetry-breaking process.

Abstract: We investigate spontaneous chiral-symmetry breaking in four-dimensional (4D) QCD in the dimensional reduction (DR) gauge, defined so as to minimize $R_{\mathrm{DR}}\equiv~\int d^{4}s ~ \mathrm{Tr} \left[ A_{x}^{2}(s) + A_{y}^{2}(s) \right]$. We consider ``$tz$-projection" of $A_{x,y}(s) \to 0$ and ``$xy$-projection" of $A_{t,z}(s) \to 0$, respectively, for the gauge configuration generated in the DR gauge, in a similar sense to Abelian, monopole and photon projections in the maximally Abelian gauge. The chiral condensate is estimated from the low-lying Dirac-eigenvalue density $ρ(λ)$ using the Banks-Casher relation. By the $tz$-projection in the DR gauge, the chiral condensate is largely reserved. In contrast, the chiral condensate becomes zero by the $xy$-projection in the DR gauge. This indicates that $A_{t}(s)$ and $A_{z}(s)$ play a dominant role, while $A_{x}(s)$ and $A_{y}(s)$ play no role, in spontaneous chiral-symmetry breaking in the DR gauge.

### [3] [Learning Low Modes of the Wilson Dirac Operator with Gauge-Equivariant Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30982)
*Minjae Choi,Hiroshi Ohno*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores the use of gauge-equivariant parallel-transport layers in neural networks to generate test vectors for the Wilson Dirac operator, which can reduce setup times for adaptive multigrid methods without significantly affecting solve times. The approach is tested across different hopping parameters and configurations, showing promising results, especially near the critical hopping parameter.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to reduce the computational cost associated with generating test vectors for the adaptive multigrid method, as this process can sometimes be more time-consuming than a single solve operation. By using neural networks, the authors aim to produce effective test vectors more efficiently, potentially speeding up the overall computation time for simulations involving the Wilson Dirac operator.

Method: The method involves training neural networks that are built from gauge-equivariant parallel-transport layers on a quenched ensemble, using a Rayleigh-Ritz loss function for $D^\dagger D$. The trained networks are then tested for their ability to generate effective test vectors at various hopping parameters and on unseen configurations, bypassing the traditional costly refinement steps in the adaptive setup process.

Result: The results indicate that the use of trained neural networks can substantially decrease the setup time for generating test vectors, while generally maintaining the solve time. There is also a reduction in solve time in some cases, particularly when the hopping parameter is close to its critical value, $\kappa_c$.

Conclusion: The conclusion suggests that employing neural networks with gauge-equivariant properties is an effective strategy to lower the setup costs in adaptive multigrid methods. This technique could lead to more efficient computations, especially in scenarios where the hopping parameter is near $\kappa_c$, and it opens up possibilities for further research into optimizing the use of machine learning in lattice field theory calculations.

Abstract: In adaptive multigrid, test vectors that better approximate the low-mode subspace of the Wilson Dirac operator $D$ can reduce the solve time, but their construction can be costly. In some cases, the setup takes longer than a single solve. To reduce the cost of generating test vectors, we train networks built from gauge-equivariant parallel-transport layers once on a quenched ensemble, using a Rayleigh-Ritz loss for $D^\dagger D$. We then test whether the trained networks maintain their performance at other hopping parameters and on configurations not used in training. In these tests, the network outputs are used as test vectors, and the costly $V$-cycle refinement in the adaptive setup is omitted to reduce the setup time. This substantially reduces the setup time while leaving the solve time largely unchanged in most cases. Near the critical hopping parameter $κ_c$, the solve time is also reduced in some cases.

### [4] [Dyon Condensation at $θ=2π$ from Wilson-'t Hooft Loops in Yang-Mills Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31281)
*Hiromasa Watanabe,Issaku Kanamori,Okuto Morikawa,Yuki Nagai,Yuya Tanizaki,Akio Tomiya*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study explores the nontrivial realization of periodicity in Yang-Mills theories at θ and θ+2π under a 1-form center symmetry, using SU(2) lattice Yang-Mills theory. It finds numerical evidence for dyonic oblique-confinement at θ=2π through measurements of 't Hooft and dyonic Wilson-'t Hooft loops, with a 1-form-covariant DBW2 flow improving the reweighting factor.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate the distinct topological labels of confining vacua at θ=0 and 2π, which are expected due to the nontrivial realization of periodicity in the presence of a 1-form center symmetry background field, and to provide numerical evidence for the dyonic oblique-confinement pattern at θ=2π.

Method: SU(2) lattice Yang-Mills theory is utilized, along with the measurement of 't Hooft and dyonic Wilson-'t Hooft loops at θ=2π. A 1-form-covariant DBW2 flow is applied to make the global reweighting factor numerically manageable while preserving the non-integer topological charge in a defect ensemble.

Result: The dyonic loop shows a clear perimeter-law plateau on two different lattice volumes, and although the direct 't Hooft-loop signal is limited by noise, its magnitude and distance dependence are consistent with an area law, supporting the dyonic oblique-confinement pattern at θ=2π.

Conclusion: The findings support the expectation that there are distinct symmetry-protected topological labels for the confining vacua at θ=0 and 2π, and they provide numerical evidence for the dyonic oblique-confinement pattern at θ=2π, aligning with the Wilson-'t Hooft classification.

Abstract: Although Yang--Mills theories at $θ$ and $θ+2π$ are physically equivalent, their periodicity is realized nontrivially in the presence of a background field for the $1$-form center symmetry. The confining vacua at $θ=0$ and $2π$ are therefore expected to have distinct symmetry-protected topological labels. We test this expectation in $SU(2)$ lattice Yang--Mills theory by measuring 't~Hooft and dyonic Wilson--'t~Hooft loops at $θ=2π$. A $1$-form-covariant DBW2 flow makes the global reweighting factor numerically benign while retaining the physical non-integer topological charge in a defect ensemble. The dyonic loop exhibits a clear perimeter-law plateau on two lattice volumes. The direct 't~Hooft-loop signal is noise-limited but has the magnitude and distance dependence expected for an area law. Taken together with the Wilson--'t~Hooft classification, these results provide numerical evidence for the dyonic oblique-confinement pattern at $θ=2π$. This talk is based on our recent paper (arXiv:2606.13428).

### [5] [Towards Systematic Control of the Nucleon Gluon PDF with the Pseudo-PDF Method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31597)
*Alex NieMiera,Kai Ebira,William Good,Huey-Wen Lin*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents an advanced lattice QCD calculation of the unpolarized nucleon gluon PDF using pseudo-PDF methodology, with improved accuracy and control over systematic errors. The findings show a suppressed gluon distribution at large x, consistent with some phenomenological models, and contribute to future global PDF analyses.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to improve the precision and reduce systematic uncertainties in the determination of the gluon parton distribution function (PDF) within the nucleon, which is essential for high-energy physics experiments and for testing the standard model predictions.

Method: The method involves using lattice QCD simulations with the pseudo-PDF approach on fine lattices, employing HISQ quarks, and utilizing the Lanczos method to address excited-state contamination. The analysis also includes parameterizations to account for higher-twist and discretization effects.

Result: The results provide a gluon PDF that shows no significant dependence on the valence pion mass and suggests a strongly suppressed gluon distribution at large momentum fractions x, in agreement with certain phenomenological PDFs. This work advances the field by offering more controlled and precise lattice-QCD inputs for global PDF fits.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the study has made significant progress in extracting the gluon PDF with better control over systematic errors, providing valuable input for future global PDF analyses and helping to refine our understanding of nucleon structure.

Abstract: We present updated lattice quantum chromodynamics (QCD) results for the unpolarized nucleon gluon parton distribution function (PDF) using the pseudo-PDF methodology. The calculation is performed at valence pion masses $M_π\approx 310$ and $690$ MeV on an $a \approx 0.06$ fm ensemble with $2+1+1$ flavors of highly improved staggered quarks (HISQ) generated by the MILC Collaboration. This lattice spacing is finer than those used in previous gluon pseudo-PDF studies, providing access to multiple short-distance separations and nucleon boost momenta as high as $4.4$ GeV. To control excited-state contamination and reduce model dependence, we employ the recently developed Lanczos method, finding good agreement with our previous two-state fit approach. Higher-twist contributions and discretization effects are accounted for through explicit parameterizations in fits to the renormalized short-distance matrix elements. Together, these advances represent important steps toward systematically controlled, high-precision extractions of the gluon PDF. The resulting PDFs exhibit no statistically significant dependence on the valence pion mass and indicate a strongly suppressed gluon distribution at large $x$, consistent with a subset of phenomenological PDF extractions. These results demonstrate progress toward providing systematically controlled lattice-QCD constraints for future global PDF analyses.
