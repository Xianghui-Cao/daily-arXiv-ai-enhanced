### [1] [Monopole crystal and effective theory for a monopole-enhanced 3D U(1) gauge theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15922)
*Alessandro Mariani*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores a U(1) lattice gauge theory with a monopole enhancement term, showing that for large enhancements, monopoles form an antiferromagnetic ground state. The study uses analytical and numerical methods to predict and confirm the presence of phase transitions within the model, including first-order and Ising-like transitions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to deepen the understanding of the U(1) lattice gauge theory when a monopole enhancement term is included, particularly how the system behaves under different conditions and the nature of phase transitions in such a theory.

Method: A combination of analytical techniques, such as a variant of Polyakov's argument, and numerical simulations to study the phase diagram and verify the predictions about the phase transitions in the theory.

Result: The results show that at high monopole enhancement, there is an antiferromagnetic Néel-ordered ground state. First-order transitions are predicted and observed numerically as the monopole chemical potential changes, and an Ising universality class transition is found as the gauge coupling varies.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the inclusion of a monopole enhancement term leads to a rich phase structure in the U(1) lattice gauge theory, with distinct types of phase transitions and ordering phenomena, which can be effectively described using an adapted version of the sine-Gordon model.

Abstract: Recently, a version of U(1) lattice gauge theory on the cubic lattice with a monopole enhancement term was considered in the literature. In this work, we further our understanding of this theory with a mixture of analytical and numerical methods. We show that for sufficiently large monopole enhancement, the monopoles are no longer dilute and they arrange themselves into an antiferromagnetic, Néel-ordered ground state. Nonetheless, a variant of Polyakov's argument for the dilute excitations over the dense ground state leads to an effective sine-Gordon model which is qualitatively similar to that of the standard theory. We also predict analytically the presence of first-order transitions as the monopole chemical potential is varied at fixed gauge coupling, corresponding to a change in the dominant monopole charge. Similarly, we predict a transition in the Ising universality class as the gauge coupling is varied. We then use numerical methods to study the phase diagram of the theory and check some of our predictions.

### [2] [Critical behavior of the bosonized Schwinger model with uniform matrix product states](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16092)
*Takuya Okuda*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study uses a Hamiltonian lattice discretization and the VUMPS algorithm to compute the critical mass of the Schwinger model at θ=π, finding (m/g)_c = 0.333555(5) which aligns with recent staggered-fermion studies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To accurately determine the critical mass of the continuum Schwinger model at θ=π using a non-compact boson field dual formulation, with the aim of corroborating or refining existing estimates from other methods such as staggered-fermion calculations.

Method: A Hamiltonian lattice discretization is applied to the bosonized model, and the variational uniform matrix product state (VUMPS) algorithm is used to numerically obtain the ground state on an infinite chain while preserving charge conjugation. The oscillator basis is used for the local Hilbert space, and Symanzik effective theory is utilized to constrain fitting functions for the continuum extrapolation.

Result: The calculated critical mass for the Schwinger model at θ=π is (m/g)_c = 0.333555(5), which is in agreement with the results obtained from recent staggered-fermion determinations at a similar level of precision.

Conclusion: The method of combining Hamiltonian lattice discretization with the VUMPS algorithm and Symanzik effective theory for lattice corrections provides a precise determination of the critical mass in the Schwinger model, confirming the consistency with previous studies and offering a robust alternative approach.

Abstract: Quantum electrodynamics in two spacetime dimensions, also known as the Schwinger model, admits a dual formulation in terms of a non-compact boson field. Using a Hamiltonian lattice discretization of the bosonized model, we compute the critical mass of the continuum Schwinger model at $θ=π$. We numerically obtain the ground state on an infinite chain by the variational uniform matrix product state (VUMPS) algorithm, preserving charge conjugation exactly. As a truncated basis for the local Hilbert space, we employ the oscillator basis that diagonalizes the one-site reduced density matrix of the free limit. We constrain the fitting functions for the continuum extrapolation using the Symanzik effective theory for lattice corrections. Our result for the critical mass, $(m/g)_c = 0.333\,555(5)$, agrees with recent staggered-fermion determinations at comparable precision.

*Carsten Urbach*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper uses the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to analyze Wilson twisted mass fermions, proving automatic O(a) improvement at maximal twist and deriving the structure of O(a) counterterms for clover on-shell improvement. It also shows that the clover term in Euclidean space-time can be split into two terms with separate coefficients.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to apply the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to Wilson twisted mass fermions to understand the $O(a)$ improvement and the structure of necessary counterterms for clover on-shell improvement, while demonstrating the splitting of the clover term into two independent components.

Method: The method involves constructing the effective Hamiltonian based on lattice symmetries and using it to prove automatic $O(a)$ improvement, as well as to derive the structure of $O(a)$ counterterms required for clover on-shell improvement. Canonical transformations are used to remove on-shell redundant contributions, leading to the identification of two independent terms from the clover term.

Result: The results include the proof of automatic $O(a)$ improvement at maximal twist, the derivation of the $O(a)$ counterterms for clover on-shell improvement, and the finding that the clover term splits into two terms with independent coefficients in isotropic Euclidean space-time.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the application of the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian to Wilson twisted mass fermions provides a clear understanding of $O(a)$ improvement, the necessary counterterms for clover on-shell improvement, and reveals the distinct nature of the clover term in the context of lattice QCD.

Abstract: We apply the recently developed Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to Wilson twisted mass fermions. The effective Hamiltonian is constructed solely based on the symmetries of the lattice Hamiltonian without recourse to the Symanzik effective action in Euclidean space-time. We use it to prove automatic $O(a)$ improvement at maximal twist, as well as $O(a)$ improvement via so-called mass and Wilson average. Moreover, we derive the structure of $O(a)$ counterterms pre-requisite for clover on-shell improvement of Wilson fermions. We show how to remove on-shell redundant contributions using canonical transformations, and find that the well known clover term in isotropic Euclidean space time splits into two terms with independent coefficients.

### [4] [Numerical tests of chiral fermion weights with $η$-invariant phases in the 3450 model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16876)
*Tatsuhiro Misumi*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper evaluates a method for coupling chiral fermions to lattice gauge fields using a Pauli-Villars regularized magnitude and an Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) η-invariant phase. The method is tested in a 2D U(1) theory, showing high accuracy in Polyakov loops and vacuum-polarization response, and the APS boundary condition leads to improved results for a twelve-point correlator at unit flux.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to test the effectiveness of a new prescription for coupling continuum chiral fermions to interpolated lattice gauge fields, which involves a PV-regularized weight and an APS η-invariant, by comparing numerical results with known exact values in a 2D U(1) theory.

Method: The researchers use a 2D anomaly-free U(1) theory and perform numerical tests on a 16x16 grid, comparing Polyakov loop calculations and the induced mass from the vacuum-polarization response with and without the APS phase. They also evaluate a twelve-point correlator at unit flux with generalized APS boundary conditions for various gauge field configurations.

Result: At zero magnetic flux, the Polyakov loops agree with exact values within 0.04% when the APS phase is included, while omitting it reduces the result to about 3% of the exact value. The induced mass is found to be close to the theoretical expectation. At unit flux, the magnitude of the twelve-point correlator agrees with the exact result within 0.8%, and the inclusion of the η-invariant improves the phase agreement to 0.2-0.5 mrad, as opposed to 1.8-3.0 mrad without it. The phase change due to different extensions into the third dimension is negligible in the anomaly-free combination.

Conclusion: The prescription that uses a PV-regularized fermion weight and an APS η-invariant phase provides accurate results for both the Polyakov loops and the twelve-point correlator in the 2D U(1) theory, demonstrating its potential for correctly handling chiral fermions on the lattice.

Abstract: We test numerically a recently proposed prescription for coupling continuum chiral fermions to interpolated lattice gauge fields, in which the fermion weight has a Pauli-Villars (PV) regularized magnitude and a phase given by an Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) $η$-invariant in one higher dimension. We use the anomaly-free two-dimensional $U(1)$ theory with left-handed charges $3,4$ and right-handed charges $5,0$, whose torus determinant is known exactly. At zero magnetic flux, with gauge fields interpolated from lattice links, the Polyakov loops evaluated on a $16\times16$ grid of flat connections agree with the exact values within $0.04\%$, whereas omitting the APS phase substantially reduces them to about $3\%$ of the exact result. The vacuum-polarization response yields an induced mass $m/e=2.82089$, close to $5/\sqrtπ=2.82095$. At unit flux, where zero modes make the determinant vanish, we calculate a twelve-point correlator on a cylinder with the generalized APS boundary condition. For three smooth backgrounds its magnitude agrees with the exact result within $0.8\%$. Without the $η$-invariant its phase deviates by $1.8$-$3.0$ mrad, and with it by $0.2$-$0.5$ mrad. The phase of each charged fermion changes by $15$-$46$ mrad when the gauge field is extended differently into the third dimension, but the change cancels to below $0.2$ mrad in the anomaly-free combination.
