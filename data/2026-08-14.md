### [1] [Blind Spots of the Zwanziger Horizon Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12413)
*Daniel G. Tedesco*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the relationship between the first Gribov horizon and Zwanziger's horizon function, particularly focusing on SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in three and four Euclidean dimensions. It uses variational estimates and finite-volume calculations to characterize the threshold structure for smooth radial profiles, with a separate treatment of the regular-gauge BPST background.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the configurationwise relation between the first Gribov horizon, which is defined by the loss of positivity of the Faddeev-Popov operator, and Zwanziger's horizon function, which probes the inverse operator through background-dependent sources. The aim is to determine if the spectral directions associated with the onset of the Gribov horizon are accessible to the sources entering the horizon function, even in cases where the critical subspace is degenerate.

Method: The method involves analyzing SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in 3D and 4D Euclidean spaces, using angular symmetry to constrain the source sector and radial profile to control the ordering of Faddeev-Popov thresholds. Variational estimates and finite-volume calculations are employed to assess the threshold structure for smooth radial profiles. A special analysis is conducted for the regular-gauge BPST background due to domain issues related to zero-energy behavior and the horizon source in the full-space setting.

Result: The results provide insights into the accessibility of the spectral directions at the Gribov horizon to the sources used in the horizon function, with detailed characterizations of the threshold structure for the given backgrounds. The study also highlights the specific challenges and considerations for the regular-gauge BPST background.

Conclusion: The conclusion would summarize the findings regarding the configurationwise relation between the Gribov horizon and Zwanziger's horizon function, emphasizing the implications for the understanding of the Faddeev-Popov operator and the role of the horizon function in these particular gauge field configurations.

Abstract: We examine the configurationwise relation between the first Gribov horizon, defined by loss of positivity of the Faddeev-Popov operator, and Zwanziger's horizon function, which probes the inverse operator through background-dependent sources. The analysis focuses on whether the spectral directions associated with the onset of the Gribov horizon are necessarily accessible to the sources entering the horizon function, including situations in which the critical subspace is degenerate. This question is studied for radial SU(2) hedgehog backgrounds in three and four Euclidean dimensions, where angular symmetry constrains the source sector while the radial profile controls the ordering of Faddeev-Popov thresholds. Variational estimates and finite-volume calculations are used to characterize the threshold structure for smooth radial profiles. The regular-gauge BPST background is treated separately because of domain issues associated with zero-energy behavior and the horizon source in the full-space setting. The discussion is restricted to configurationwise spectral properties and does not address the statistical weighting of these backgrounds in the Yang-Mills functional integral.

### [2] [Topological properties around the Roberge-Weiss transition in $N_f = 2 + 1 + 1$ QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12883)
*Massimo D'Elia,Fabio Siliberto,Kevin Zambello*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study explores the topological characteristics of QCD at the Roberge-Weiss transition with an imaginary baryon chemical potential, using $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD and stout improved staggered fermions. It finds that the topological susceptibility remains constant below the transition temperature, then decreases rapidly, and the behavior of the fourth-order cumulant of the topological charge distribution is consistent with a sharp transition, similar to pure gauge theories rather than full QCD along the standard thermal line.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate the topological properties of QCD during the finite temperature Roberge-Weiss phase transition, which occurs at specific values of the imaginary baryon chemical potential, and to understand how these properties compare to those found in $N_f = 2 + 1$ QCD with a different discretization method.

Method: The researchers used $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD with physical quark masses, applied stout improved staggered fermions for discretization, and considered two lattice sizes, $N_t = 8$ and $N_t = 10$, to study the topological susceptibility and the fourth-order cumulant of the topological charge distribution across the Roberge-Weiss transition.

Result: The topological susceptibility was observed to be nearly constant for temperatures lower than the Roberge-Weiss transition temperature and then showed a rapid decay above it. The analysis of the fourth-order cumulant, $b_2$, indicated a sharp transition, resembling the behavior seen in pure gauge theories rather than the more gradual change observed in full QCD under standard thermal conditions.

Conclusion: The results are consistent with previous findings in $N_f = 2 + 1$ QCD, indicating a similar universality class. The topological properties suggest a sharp transition after the Roberge-Weiss temperature, aligning with the predictions of the Dilute Instanton Gas Approximation and the behavior of pure gauge theories.

Abstract: We investigate the topological properties of QCD across the finite temperature Roberge-Weiss transition, which is found for particular values of the imaginary baryon chemical potential. Our study is conducted for $N_f = 2+1+1$ QCD with physical quark masses, discretized via stout improved staggered fermions and considering mostly two different values of the compactifed dimension, $N_t = 8$ and $N_t = 10$. Results for $T_{RW}$ and for the associated universality class are consistent with those found in the $N_f = 2 + 1$ case with a slightly different discretization. The topological susceptibility appears to be practically constant for $T \lesssim T_{RW}$, then rapidly decaying for higher temperatures. The analysis of the fourth order cumulant of the topological charge distribution, $b_2$, reveals that it is compatible with the prediction of the Dilute Instanton Gas Approximation right after $T_{RW}$, showing thus a sharp transition, which is more similar to what observed in pure gauge theories rather than to full QCD along the standard thermal line, where instead a slower transition was observed in previous studies.
