### [1] [Lattice study of spin interactions between heavy quarks in the quark-gluon plasma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21729)
*Dibyendu Bala,Olaf Kaczmarek,Sayantan Sharma,Swagatam Tah*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study calculates the spin-dependent potential, a correction term to the thermal potential between a static quark-antiquark pair. It shows that this potential has an imaginary part, which is different for pseudoscalar and vector quarkonium states. The research uses lattice techniques to non-perturbatively extract the imaginary part in the deconfined phase of quenched QCD at a temperature of around 470 MeV, finding that the spin-dependent potential in the quark-gluon plasma (QGP) phase is complex with a significant imaginary contribution. The implications for quarkonium spectral functions are also explored.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the behavior of the spin-dependent potential between a quark-antiquark pair in the context of non-relativistic QCD, particularly its complex nature and the impact on quarkonium states within the quark-gluon plasma (QGP).

Method: The method involves calculating the spin-dependent potential as an order 1/M^2 correction to the thermal potential. The researchers use hard thermal loop perturbation theory at leading order and apply lattice techniques to extract the imaginary part of the potential non-perturbatively in the deconfined phase of quenched QCD, with a continuum estimation and renormalization.

Result: The results show that the spin-dependent potential has an imaginary part that varies in magnitude between pseudoscalar and vector quarkonium states. In the QGP phase, the spin-dependent potential is found to be complex, with a substantial imaginary component, which notably affects the charmonium states' thermal static potential.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the study is that the thermal spin-dependent potential, with its significant imaginary part, has important consequences for the quarkonium spectral functions, especially in the quark-gluon plasma phase.

Abstract: We calculate the spin-dependent potential, which is the $\mathcal{O}(1/M^2)$ correction term to the thermal potential between a static quark-antiquark pair within non-relativistic QCD. At leading order in hard thermal loop perturbation theory, we show that this spin-dependent potential has an imaginary part which is different in magnitude for pseudoscalar and vector quarkonium states. For the first time, we extract the imaginary part non-perturbatively using lattice techniques, in the deconfined phase of quenched QCD at $T\sim 470$ MeV, after performing a continuum estimation and subsequent renormalization. We have found that the spin-dependent potential in the quark-gluon plasma phase is complex, and its imaginary part has a remarkably significant contribution over the thermal static potential for charmonium states. Consequences of this thermal spin-dependent potential on the quarkonium spectral functions are also discussed.

### [2] [Resonances at finite temperature from the lattice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22346)
*Jakob Hoffmann,Peter Lowdon,Owe Philipsen*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper introduces the concept of thermoparticles to define two-particle scattering at finite temperature and establishes a unitarity relation for thermal scattering amplitudes. It derives a quantisation condition for finite-temperature, which helps in understanding resonance properties. The approach also allows for the study of finite-temporal size effects in vacuum analyses.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to better understand the properties of hadronic resonances in a thermal QCD medium, which can provide insights into the behavior of particles under high-temperature conditions such as those found in heavy-ion collisions or the early universe. The work aims to establish a framework for describing particle interactions at finite temperature, with an emphasis on the impact of the thermal medium on resonance properties.

Method: The method involves the introduction of thermoparticles, which are stable particle-like states modified by thermal effects. A unitarity relation for the thermal scattering amplitude is established, and a finite-temperature generalization of the two-particle quantisation condition is derived using a skeleton expansion of the finite-volume correlation function. This theoretical framework is then used to constrain the form of the thermal scattering amplitude and the properties of resonances.

Result: The result is a new formalism that provides a way to describe two-particle scattering at finite temperature, including a generalized quantisation condition that accounts for the broadened spectral peaks of thermoparticles due to their interaction with the thermal medium. Additionally, the formalism can be applied to analyze finite-temporal size effects in lattice simulations, which is relevant for both thermal and vacuum conditions.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the concept of thermoparticles and the derived formalism offer a novel approach to studying the properties of hadronic resonances in a thermal medium, providing a basis for further research into the dynamics of QCD at finite temperatures and the interpretation of lattice QCD results.

Abstract: The properties of hadronic resonances at finite temperature constitute an important probe of the thermal QCD medium. In this work we use the concept of thermoparticles, which characterise thermally-modified but stable particle-like states, to define the notion of two-particle scattering at finite temperature, and establish a unitarity relation for the thermal scattering amplitude. We derive a finite-temperature generalisation of the vacuum two-particle quantisation condition via a skeleton expansion of the finite-volume correlation function. Solving this condition at the finite-volume energy levels of the system constrains the form of the thermal scattering amplitude, and hence the properties of resonances. In contrast to the vacuum case, the kinematic function containing the leading finite-volume corrections is finite at all energies, reflecting the fact that thermoparticles have broadened spectral peaks due to their interactions with the thermal medium. Since all lattice simulations involve a finite temporal extent, our approach can also be used to study finite-temporal size effects in vacuum analyses.

### [3] [First Lattice QCD Determination of Lepton-Flavor-Universality Ratios in Light-Meson Leptonic Decays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22358)
*Peter Boyle,Norman H. Christ,Xu Feng,Taku Izubuchi,Luchang Jin,Christopher T. Sachrajda,Xin-Yu Tuo*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents the first lattice QCD+QED calculation of the structure-dependent (SD) radiative correction for the ratio of electronic to muonic leptonic decay widths, R_{e/μ}, for π and K mesons, reducing uncertainties and providing the most precise Standard-Model predictions to date.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To provide a clean test of lepton flavor universality (LFU) and to probe physics beyond the Standard Model, with a focus on improving the precision of the SD radiative correction which is critical for high-precision experiments like PIONEER and NA62.

Method: Lattice QCD+QED calculations using the infinite-volume reconstruction (IVR) method with Coulomb-gauge photons to reduce statistical errors and finite-volume effects, performed at the physical pion mass and in the continuum limit.

Result: Obtained the Standard-Model predictions of R_{e/μ}=1.23501(10)×10^{-4} for π and R_{e/μ}=2.47653(34)×10^{-5} for K, significantly reducing the hadronic uncertainty in R_{e/μ}.

Conclusion: The results establish new benchmarks for future high-precision tests of LFU and improve the precision of the Standard-Model predictions for R_{e/μ} of π and K mesons.

Abstract: The ratio of electronic to muonic leptonic decay widths, $R_{e/μ}$, for the light mesons $π$ and $K$, provides a clean test of lepton flavor universality (LFU) and a sensitive probe of physics beyond the Standard Model. Its Standard-Model prediction is exceptionally precise, with the leading uncertainty associated with the structure-dependent (SD) radiative correction of $O(0.1\%)$. As experiments such as PIONEER and NA62 aim for unprecedented precision, this SD correction has become an essential ingredient in precision experiment--theory comparisons. We present the first lattice QCD$+$QED calculation of this SD correction at the physical pion mass and in the continuum limit. We employ the infinite-volume reconstruction (IVR) method with Coulomb-gauge photons, significantly reducing both statistical errors and finite-volume effects. We obtain the Standard-Model predictions, $R_{e/μ}=1.23501(10)\times10^{-4}$ for $π$ and $R_{e/μ}=2.47653(34)\times10^{-5}$ for $K$. Our results reduce the hadronic uncertainty in $R_{e/μ}$, provide the most precise Standard-Model predictions to date, and establish first-principles benchmarks for future high-precision tests of LFU.

</details>
