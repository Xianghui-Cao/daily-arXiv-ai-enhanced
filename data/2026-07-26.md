### [1] [Spin and momentum fraction carried by partons in the nucleon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21230)
*Constantia Alexandrou,Simone Bacchio,Jacob Finkenrath,Christos Iona,Giannis Koutsou,Christian Kummer,Yan Li,Bhavna Prasad,Gregoris Spanoudes*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper uses lattice QCD to determine the momentum and angular momentum of quarks and gluons in a proton, with physical mass values for up, down, strange, and charm quarks. It finds that both the momentum and spin sum rules are satisfied, with the total momentum fraction and total spin being 0.995(60)(29) and 0.507(43)(65), respectively.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to understand the internal structure of the proton by determining the distribution of momentum and angular momentum among its constituent quarks and gluons using lattice Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD).

Method: The method involves simulating four ensembles with physical masses for up, down, strange, and charm quarks, and taking the continuum limit at the physical pion mass point. The simulations are used to extract quark and gluon momentum fractions, as well as their contributions to the proton's total angular momentum, including intrinsic quark spin and orbital angular momentum.

Result: The result shows that the total momentum fraction is 0.995(60)(29) and the total spin is 0.507(43)(65), which satisfies the momentum and spin sum rules. These findings are compared with those from phenomenological analyses.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the study successfully determines the momentum and angular momentum carried by quarks and gluons within the proton, and the results are consistent with the expected sum rules, providing a detailed insight into the proton's internal dynamics.

Abstract: We determine the momentum fraction and angular momentum carried by quarks and gluons in the proton in lattice QCD. We use four ensembles simulated with up, down, strange and charm quarks with their masses tuned to their physical values. These ensembles have similar physical volume and different lattice spacings allowing us to take the continuum limit directly at the physical pion mass point. We extract the quark and gluon momentum fractions and total angular momentum in the continuum limit as well as the intrinsic quark spin and orbital angular momentum contributions to the proton spin. We find the total momentum fraction $\langle x_N \rangle= 0.995(60)(29)$ and the total spin $J_N = 0.507(43)(65)$, showing that both the momentum and spin sum rules are satisfied. We compare our results to those extracted from phenomenological analyses.

### [3] [Stochastic Quantization as Optimal Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21436)
*Lingxiao Wang*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents a novel method for stochastic quantization by formulating it as a finite-time stochastic optimal control problem, using a neural network to learn the necessary controls. This approach, termed Optimal Stochastic Quantization (OSQ), is shown to effectively recover observables in 2D lattice scalar φ^4 theory and multimodal potentials, with the benefit of not introducing model bias despite potential training imperfections.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation behind this work is to introduce a new perspective on stochastic quantization, moving away from the traditional equilibrium-based Langevin dynamics towards a control-oriented framework. This shift aims to provide a more efficient and accurate method for reaching the target quantum field state, especially in complex systems like those with multimodal potentials or near critical points in theories such as the 2D lattice scalar φ^4 theory.

Method: The authors propose to formulate stochastic quantization as a finite-time stochastic optimal control problem. A reference process, ideally derived from the free theory, sets an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck dynamics baseline. The interaction part of the system is incorporated through a terminal cost that corrects for deviations from the reference. A neural network is then employed to learn the residual control needed to steer the system's path-reweighted terminal ensemble to the desired target, given specific noise amplitude and time constraints. This method, called Optimal Stochastic Quantization (OSQ), leverages the Doob-transform force for the control mechanism.

Result: The application of OSQ successfully recovers all modes in multimodal potentials within a finite time, indicating its effectiveness in handling complex systems. Additionally, in the context of two-dimensional lattice scalar φ^4 theory, the method accurately reproduces observables close to the critical point, demonstrating its capability to match results obtained from hybrid Monte Carlo simulations. The research also highlights that while imperfect training increases estimator variance, it does not lead to model bias, a significant advantage of the proposed approach.

Conclusion: By framing stochastic quantization as a control problem rather than an equilibration process, the study opens up new avenues for efficiently simulating and understanding quantum field theories. The success of the OSQ method, particularly in dealing with multimodal potentials and 2D lattice scalar φ^4 theory, underscores its potential as a powerful tool in theoretical physics. Furthermore, the absence of model bias due to training imperfections adds to the robustness of the technique, making it a promising direction for future research.

Abstract: Stochastic quantization defines a Euclidean quantum field theory as the equilibrium of a fictitious-time Langevin dynamics, which reaches the Gibbs measure asymptotically. We show that this quantization can be formulated as a finite-time stochastic optimal control problem. A tractable reference process, naturally supplied by the free theory when available, provides an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck dynamics, while the full interaction enters as a reference-corrected terminal cost. The optimal control is a Doob-transform force that steers the path-reweighted terminal ensemble to the target at a prescribed time and for a given noise amplitude. A neural network learns the residual control, realizing this optimal stochastic quantization (OSQ). Because the path weights are exact, imperfect training increases the variance of estimators but does not introduce model bias. On multimodal potentials we recover all modes at finite time and find that the noise amplitude sets a practical diffusion-horizon window. In two-dimensional lattice scalar $φ^4$ theory we recover observables from hybrid Monte Carlo simulations near the critical point. Quantization is thereby formulated as control rather than equilibration.

### [4] [Parallel Tempered Metadynamics for full QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21575)
*Timo Eichhorn,Gianluca Fuwa,Christian Hoelbling,Lukas Varnhorst*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: An algorithm combining parallel tempering and enhanced sampling methods is introduced to tackle topological freezing in lattice QCD simulations, effectively unfreezing a system with 2 staggered fermions that was previously frozen for about 40,000 MDUs using the RHMC algorithm.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the issue of topological freezing in lattice QCD simulations, which hinders the efficiency and accuracy of such simulations, particularly in systems with 2 staggered fermions.

Method: The method involves the development of an algorithm that integrates parallel tempering with collective-variable-based enhanced sampling techniques. This approach is then applied to a challenging system characterized by 2 staggered fermions to evaluate its effectiveness in overcoming topological freezing.

Result: The result shows that the new algorithm successfully unfreezes the system, which would otherwise remain frozen for approximately 40,000 Molecular Dynamics Units (MDUs) when the Rational Hybrid Monte Carlo (RHMC) algorithm is used.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the proposed algorithm is effective in resolving topological freezing, thereby improving the dynamics and potentially the reliability of lattice QCD simulations involving 2 staggered fermions.

Abstract: We present an algorithm that addresses topological freezing in lattice QCD simulations by combining parallel tempering with collective-variable-based enhanced sampling methods, and apply it to a particularly challenging system with $N_f = 2$ staggered fermions. We find that the algorithm unfreezes the system, which is otherwise completely frozen for approximately 40000 Molecular Dynamics Units with the Rational Hybrid Monte Carlo algorithm.
