### [1] [Confinement and String Breaking in the Compact Abelian Higgs Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27550)
*Blake Senseman,Zane Ozzello,Yannick Meurice,Stephen Mrenna*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents a simplified model, based on a spin-1 truncation of the Compact Abelian Higgs Model, for simulating confinement and string breaking in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) using current technology. It explores the spectrum of string-like excitations and demonstrates how a local chemical potential can be used to measure physical features such as string tension and effective meson mass, allowing for a new way to characterize string stability.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to create a simplified model that allows for the simulation and study of QCD phenomena, specifically confinement and string breaking, which are typically not feasible with real-time simulations due to technological limitations. The authors aim to use this model to better understand these phenomena and to enable the measurement of important physical properties like string tension and meson mass through the use of a local chemical potential.

Method: The method involves developing a spin-1 truncation of the Compact Abelian Higgs Model, simulated on qutrit sites, and integrating out heavy modes in 1+1D scalar electrodynamics to produce an effective Hamiltonian. The researchers then use DMRG methods to study the spectrum of string-like excitations across approximately 100 sites. They also introduce a local chemical potential to simulate external charges, which enables them to measure physical features such as string tension and effective meson mass, and to assess the stability of strings by varying the chemical potential.

Result: The results show that it is possible to simulate confinement and string breaking within the developed model, and that the addition of a local chemical potential allows for the parameter-dependent measurement of string tension and effective meson mass. By varying the chemical potential, the researchers were able to characterize the stability of the strings, which was not done in previous studies of confining lattice models.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the proposed simple model provides a viable approach to simulating elements of QCD, including confinement and string breaking, and that the introduction of a local chemical potential offers a novel means to measure key physical parameters. This work opens up new possibilities for characterizing the stability of strings and contributes to the understanding of QCD phenomenology.

Abstract: While real-time simulation of Quantum Chromodynamics remains technologically out of reach, simplified models for studying elements of QCD phenomenology abound. This work presents a simple model, a spin-1 truncation of the Compact Abelian Higgs Model simulated on qutrit sites, in which confinement and string breaking is accessible to current simulation methods. In the low-energy regime of 1+1D scalar electrodynamics, the heavy modes are integrated out, producing a spin chain effective Hamiltonian in which Gauss' law is implicitly satisfied. We study the spectrum of string-like excitations using DMRG methods on the order of 100 sites. We demonstrate that an added, local chemical potential, playing a role analogous to external charges, permits parameter-dependent measurements of physical features of interest like the string tension and effective meson mass. Varying the chemical potential also permits a characterization of string stability not assessed in prior studies of confining lattice models.

### [2] [Pion Transition Form Factor in Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27868)
*Shihao Su,Liuming Liu,Peng Sun*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper investigates the neutral pion transition form factor in lattice QCD, using a blending method for all-to-all propagators, and finds that disconnected contributions, although small, have the same sign as connected ones, leading to constructive interference.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to investigate the neutral pion transition form factor within the lattice Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) framework, with a particular interest in understanding the relative contribution and sign of the connected and disconnected quark diagrams, which are essential for the accurate description of the process.

Method: The authors use a recently proposed blending method to obtain unbiased and cost-effective estimators for all-to-all quark propagators. They apply the distillation framework for handling external pion states and evaluate electromagnetic currents in the full blending space, performing numerical tests on an ensemble with two light and one strange quark flavors ($N_f=2+1$).

Result: The results show that the disconnected part of the form factor contributes about 1% of the value of the connected part, and both parts have the same sign, which leads to a constructive interference of probability amplitudes.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the study is that the blending method successfully provides a viable approach to calculate the neutral pion transition form factor, confirming the positive sign of the disconnected contribution and its role in the constructive interference with the connected part.

Abstract: We investigate the neutral pion transition form factor $F_{π^0γ^\astγ^\ast}(q_1^2,q_2^2)$ in lattice QCD and confirm that the connected and disconnected contributions have the same sign. We employ the recently proposed blending method, which supplies an unbiased and cheap estimators for the required all-to-all propagators. The external pion states are treated within the distillation framework, while the electromagnetic currents are evaluated in the full blending space. Numerical tests are performed on an $N_f=2+1$ lattice ensemble. Our result shows that the contribution of the disconnected part is approximately $1\%$ of that of the connected part and enables constructive interference of probability amplitudes.

### [4] [Exact chiral symmetry with quantum signal processing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28524)
*Henry Lamm,Alessandro Roggero,Hersh Singh,Luca Spagnoli*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A QSP algorithm for the overlap fermion Hamiltonian is presented, which almost preserves chiral symmetry with a controllable error. It has a slight increase in circuit complexity compared to domain-wall fermions but reduces qubit costs, and demonstrates the physics of overlap fermions as if creating an extra dimension.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to develop a quantum signal processing (QSP) algorithm that can simulate Dirac fermions while preserving the Ginsparg-Wilson relation, thus maintaining chiral symmetry, with only a small, controllable error, and to do so more efficiently in terms of qubit usage compared to existing methods such as domain-wall fermions.

Method: The method involves creating a QSP algorithm tailored for the overlap fermion Hamiltonian. The algorithm simulates the application of the overlap Hamiltonian with an overhead that scales logarithmically with the desired precision $ε_e$, and it effectively constructs an additional dimension similar to what is seen in the physics of domain-wall fermions.

Result: The result is a QSP algorithm that allows for the simulation of Dirac fermions with near-exact chiral symmetry, introducing only a mild overhead in circuit complexity relative to domain-wall fermions while also reducing the number of required qubits. The algorithm's efficiency reflects the deeper physical relationship between overlap fermions and the boundary of domain-wall fermions.

Conclusion: In conclusion, the QSP algorithm for the overlap fermion Hamiltonian provides a way to simulate Dirac fermions with controlled chiral symmetry preservation and improved qubit efficiency, offering a new approach to lattice quantum chromodynamics simulations on a quantum computer.

Abstract: We give a quantum signal processing (QSP) algorithm for the overlap fermion Hamiltonian which preserves the Ginsparg-Wilson relation up to a controllable error $ε_e$. Quantum simulations of Dirac fermions with exact chiral symmetry are thus nearly free: applying the overlap Hamiltonian costs only a factor logarithmic in $ε_e$ more than the Wilson-Dirac Hamiltonian. Comparing to domain-wall fermions, a mild overhead is found in circuit complexity while reducing qubit costs. We show how QSP effectively constructs an extra dimension when simulating the overlap operator, illustrating that the scaling of quantum algorithms reflects the deeper physics of overlap fermions arising at the boundary of domain-wall fermions.
