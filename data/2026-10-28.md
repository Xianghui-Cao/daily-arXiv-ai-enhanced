### [1] [Spin Hamiltonian for Quantum Simulation of Minimally Truncated $3+1$D SU(2) Lattice Gauge Theory and Testing Eigenstate Thermalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36608)
*Ziyuan Li,Xiaojun Yao*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper constructs a spatial-gauge-singlet Hamiltonian for pure SU(2) lattice gauge theory in 3+1 dimensions on qubits, and tests the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH). It finds that the 3+1D truncated SU(2) lattice gauge theory thermalizes in line with ETH, providing a benchmark for future quantum simulations of non-equilibrium dynamics.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to test the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) in the context of a 3+1 dimensional pure SU(2) lattice gauge theory. The authors aim to provide a benchmark for real-time quantum simulations of non-equilibrium dynamics by constructing a minimal representation of the Hamiltonian on qubits and analyzing its properties.

Method: The method involves constructing a spatial-gauge-singlet Hamiltonian for the SU(2) lattice gauge theory under a minimal truncation, enforcing Gauss's law, and finding a minimal set of independent loop variables that can be mapped onto qubits. The researchers then exactly diagonalize the Hamiltonian on a 2x3x3 lattice, analyze level statistics, and compute local energy density operators' matrix elements and entanglement entropies of all eigenstates.

Result: The results show that at an ergodic coupling value of g=0.3, the matrix elements of local energy density operators and the entanglement entropies of all eigenstates are consistent with the predictions of the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH). This indicates that the 3+1D truncated SU(2) lattice gauge theory exhibits thermalization behavior as expected from ETH.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn is that the 3+1D truncated SU(2) lattice gauge theory does indeed thermalize in accordance with the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis, which provides a foundation for future studies involving real-time quantum simulations of non-equilibrium dynamics.

Abstract: We construct the spatial-gauge-singlet Hamiltonian of pure SU(2) lattice gauge theory in $3+1$ dimensions ($3+1$D) on qubits under the minimal truncation $j_{\rm max}=1/2$, building on previous work on the hyperhoneycomb lattice, and test the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH). By enforcing Gauss's law and exploiting the redundancy among gauge-invariant loop operators, we find the minimal set of independent loop variables that can be bijectively mapped onto qubits, and write the Hamiltonian as a sum of Pauli strings. An $n\times n\times n$ lattice with fixed boundary conditions requires $2(n-1)^3+3(n-1)^2$ qubits. We exactly diagonalize the Hamiltonian on a $2\times 3\times 3$ lattice and identify the coupling range of quantum ergodicity from the level statistics. At the ergodic coupling $g=0.3$, we calculate the matrix elements of local energy density operators and the entanglement entropies of all eigenstates and find them consistent with the ETH. These results show that the $3+1$D truncated SU(2) lattice gauge theory thermalizes in accordance with the ETH and provide a benchmark for future real-time quantum simulations of non-equilibrium dynamics.

### [2] [Easier parameter scans with hmcdj](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37120)
*Alexis Verney-Provatas,Ed Bennett,Gaurav Ray*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: hmcdj is a new, open-source tool that simplifies the generation of large gauge ensembles for lattice gauge theories. It features easy C++ theory specification, runtime parameter control, and various job management capabilities, including automatic tuning and HPC scheduler integration.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation behind this paper is to introduce a more efficient and user-friendly solution for generating large numbers of gauge ensembles in lattice gauge theories, which are fundamental in theoretical physics research. The existing processes can be cumbersome and require significant manual intervention, thus the need for a streamlined tool like hmcdj.

Method: The method involves developing hmcdj as a tool that builds upon the Grid library. This development allows for the quick specification of theories in C++, with parameters controlled at runtime via a YAML input file. The tool also incorporates several features for managing large job sets, such as automatic molecular dynamics step size adjustment, standardized data output, and dynamic adaptation to HPC scheduler constraints.

Result: The result is a versatile and powerful tool, hmcdj, capable of significantly easing the process of generating gauge ensembles. It provides researchers with a flexible framework for specifying and running simulations, while also ensuring good performance and compatibility with high-performance computing environments.

Conclusion: In conclusion, hmcdj represents an important advancement in the field of lattice gauge theory simulation, offering a robust, open-source platform for ensemble generation. Its design emphasizes ease of use, adaptability, and efficiency, making it a valuable resource for the physics community. The tool is available under the GPLv2 license, encouraging contributions and further development.

Abstract: We present hmcdj, a new tool developed to ease the process of generating large numbers of gauge ensembles for a given theory. Building on top of the Grid library, hmcdj allows quick specification of lattice gauge theories in C++ with minimal boilerplate, and precise specification of per-ensemble parameters to be controlled at run time from a YAML input file. Additionally, it supports many quality-of-life features for managing large numbers of running jobs, including automatic tuning of the molecular dynamics step size to control the Metropolis acceptance rate; output of ILDG-compliant data; and integration with HPC schedulers to dynamically adapt the target trajectory count of a job to match its time limits. hmcdj is openly available under the GPLv2 license, and welcomes new contributions.
