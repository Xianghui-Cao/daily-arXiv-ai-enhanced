### [2] [Optimized solver algorithms for computing $\mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1})$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26854)
*Benjamin Luke,Sudip Shiwakoti,Shayan Nadeem,Ronald Morgan,Walter Wilcox*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A new class of Krylov solvers, called Twin methods, is introduced to estimate the trace of the inverse of a large quark matrix in lattice QCD. These methods reduce computation time by reusing the Krylov polynomial for multiple right-hand sides. Additionally, a more economical estimation using a polynomial approximation and singular value deflation significantly speeds up the trace computation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to improve the efficiency of estimating the trace of the inverse of a large quark matrix, which is a critical and challenging problem in lattice QCD. The standard method, Hutchinson's, is computationally expensive, and there is a need for a faster alternative.

Method: The authors introduce Twin methods, a new type of Krylov solvers that reuse the Krylov polynomial for all noise vectors, thus reducing the number of basic linear algebra operations. They also present an economical estimation technique that uses a polynomial approximation of the inverse matrix and singular value deflation, allowing the solvers to be applied to fewer noise vectors.

Result: The tests on various lattice QCD matrices show that the combination of the polynomial approximation and the deflation technique provides a 17-fold speedup in the time required to compute the trace compared to the standard Hutchinson's method.

Conclusion: The introduction of Twin methods and the use of polynomial approximation with singular value deflation offer significant improvements in the computational efficiency for estimating the trace of the inverse of large quark matrices, outperforming the conventional Hutchinson's method.

Abstract: Estimating the trace of the inverse of a large quark matrix is a longstanding problem in lattice QCD. The standard approach is Hutchinson's method, which uses random noise vectors to estimate the trace to a desired statistical tolerance. Each noise vector $b$ requires solving a system of linear equations $Ax = b$. In this work, we introduce a new class of Krylov solvers for this task, called Twin methods. Twin methods generate the Krylov polynomial once then reuse it on all other right-hand sides. This reduces the number of basic linear algebra operations in the algorithm and therefore lowers the trace computation time. We apply the Twin approach to BiCG and BiCGStab and test them on lattice QCD matrices of varying size and in a range of computing contexts. We also present a more economical estimation of $\mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1})$ which implements a polynomial approximation of $A^{-1}$ and a singular value deflation. In this scheme, the solvers are applied to only a few noise vectors, and the remainder are treated by the polynomial and the deflation. In our tests, the polynomial approximation combined with the deflation yield a 17-fold speedup in the time required to compute the trace over standard Hutchinson's method.

Main category: hep-lat

Method: The authors calculate the 4D phase using 3D Dirac spectra and compare it with phases obtained from two types of 5D bulk extensions. Conclusion: The 5D APS calculation effectively reproduces the 4D chiral phase for various gauge field configurations, supporting its use for studying chiral anomalies in higher-dimensional theories, even after anomaly cancellation.

Abstract: We calculate the phase of a 4D chiral fermion determinant from the 5D Atiyah--Patodi--Singer (APS) $η$-invariant. We study the anomaly-free $SU(5)$ representation $\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf 5}$ on a four-torus, using gauge fields independent of one coordinate. For these backgrounds, the 4D phase can be calculated independently from a 3D Dirac spectrum. For an Abelian background embedded in $SU(5)$, two different 5D bulk extensions reproduce the 4D phase within $0.03\%$ in the tested heat-kernel extrapolations. For a background generating the full $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ algebra, the 5D phase with a fixed spatial field agrees with the independent 4D phase within $0.5\%$ over the six heat-kernel parameters examined. Main category: hep-lat

Method: The method involves Bayesian reconstruction of the R-ratio from Euclidean lattice correlators, direct integration of the ρ peak to determine f_ρ, and comparison of lattice results with experimental data across a range of energies, while also assessing the impact of Gaussian smearing and spatial extents on the reconstructed spectrum.

Abstract: We investigate resonance properties and inclusive spectral behavior through Bayesian reconstruction of the $R$-ratio from Euclidean lattice correlators near the physical pion mass. ### [5] [Solving fermionic sign problems with physics-informed kernels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27768)
*Kenji Fukushima,Friederike Ihssen,Renzo Kapust,Jan M. Pawlowski*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper introduces an extension of the physics-informed kernels (PIKs) method to address sign problems in complex systems, such as fermionic ones, by transforming the sampling task into a Gaussian one, and demonstrates its application using the one-site Hubbard model and its $\phi^4$-deformed versions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to resolve sign problems encountered in generic weighted sums of complex Gaussian distributions for a broad class of physics systems, including those with structures similar to fermionic systems, making simulations more efficient and accurate.

Method: The method involves two steps. First, the PIKfan maps a real normal distribution to a weighted sum of complex Gaussian distributions, maintaining the sampling cost of a real normal distribution. Second, this sum is evolved into the target theory's distribution using another PIK, reducing the problem to a Gaussian sampling task while the main computational effort is shifted towards computing the PIKs.

Result: The approach successfully reduces the complexity of sampling from a general class of complex systems to that of sampling from a Gaussian distribution, which is computationally less demanding. It is illustrated through applications to the one-site Hubbard model at various chemical potentials and its $\phi^4$-deformed extensions, demonstrating the method's versatility and effectiveness.

Conclusion: The extended PIKs method, specifically the PIKfan procedure, offers a promising solution to the sign problem in complex system simulations, significantly simplifying the sampling process and potentially enabling more efficient and accurate numerical studies across a wide range of physical theories.

Abstract: We extend the generative sampling approach with physics-informed kernels (PIKs) [1,2] to a general class of physics systems with complex distributions and a structure also encountered in fermionic systems. The resolution of sign problems of generic weighted sums of complex Gaußian distributions with the PIK approach is central to this application. In a first step, PIKs map a real normal distribution to such a weighted sum, and this procedure is called the PIKfan. The PIKfan has the sampling cost of a real normal distribution. In a second step, this weighted sum is evolved with a second PIK into the distribution of the theory at hand. In combination, we reduce the sampling task for a general class of complex systems to a Gaußian one, and the main computational task is that of computing the PIK. We illustrate the approach with the one-site Hubbard model at arbitrary chemical potential and with $φ^4$-deformed extensions of it.

### [6] [Sampling non-abelian lattice gauge theories with diffusion models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27821)

TL;DR: The paper introduces gauge-equivariant diffusion models, using L-CNNs and MAALA, to sample lattice gauge theory field configurations. It demonstrates the model's effectiveness in 2D U(2) and 4D SU(3) scenarios, with good extrapolation for larger inverse couplings, validated by comparison with known values and hybrid Monte Carlo results.

Motivation: The motivation is to develop a new method for accurately sampling lattice gauge theory (LGT) field configurations, which are important for understanding the behavior of quantum fields on a lattice. The goal is to improve upon existing methods, particularly in terms of computational efficiency and accuracy, especially for higher-dimensional theories like four-dimensional SU(3).

Method: The method involves the use of gauge-equivariant diffusion models that incorporate lattice gauge-equivariant convolutional neural networks (L-CNNs) and the Metropolis-adjusted annealed Langevin algorithm (MAALA). These tools are used to generate samples of two-dimensional U(2) and four-dimensional SU(3) field configurations.

Result: The results show that the models can effectively sample two-dimensional U(2) field configurations, and preliminary results for four-dimensional SU(3) are promising. The two-dimensional models also exhibit strong extrapolation capabilities for larger inverse coupling constants, as confirmed by comparing observables to analytical solutions and hybrid Monte Carlo simulations.

Conclusion: The conclusion suggests that gauge-equivariant diffusion models, combined with L-CNNs and MAALA, offer a powerful approach for sampling LGT field configurations, with potential applications in both 2D and 4D settings. The ability to extrapolate beyond the training range indicates the robustness and generalization capacity of the proposed method.

Abstract: We present gauge-equivariant diffusion models as samplers for lattice gauge theories (LGTs). Using lattice gauge-equivariant convolutional neural networks (L-CNNs) and the Metropolis-adjusted annealed Langevin algorithm (MAALA), we are able to accurately sample two-dimensional U(2) field configurations and show first promising results for four-dimensional SU(3). Our two-dimensional models extrapolate remarkably well to larger inverse couplings than originally trained on. We confirm this by comparing observables to analytically known values and to hybrid Monte Carlo results.
