### [2] [Unfreezing topology using out-of-equilibrium replica exchanges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34367)
*C. Bonanno,A. Bulgarelli,E. Cellini,R. Dionisio,A. Nada,D. Panfalone,D. Vadacchino,L. Verzichelli*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces a generalized PTBC method that uses a gradual exchange of defect couplings between replicas, rather than an instantaneous swap, to improve the swap rate and reduce computational cost. This method is tested in the 2d CP^(N-1) model with N=21, showing a decrease of about 40% in total computational cost and a reduction in the required number of replicas by half.

Motivation: The motivation is to improve upon the Parallel Tempering on Boundary Conditions (PTBC) method, which alleviates topological freezing, by optimizing the swapping process between replica configurations. The goal is to increase the swap rate and reduce the overall computational cost while maintaining or improving the acceptance rate.

Method: A generalization of the PTBC method is proposed, where instead of an instantaneous swap, a short out-of-equilibrium protocol is used. In this protocol, the defect couplings of two replicas are gradually exchanged while updating the fields around the defect. The theoretical analysis predicts the swap rate and the optimal protocol length based on the average work along the trajectory, using fluctuation relations from Crooks and Jarzynski.

Result: The predictions for the swap rate and optimal protocol length are tested in the 2d CP^(N-1) model with N=21. Abstract: Parallel Tempering on Boundary Conditions (PTBC) mitigates topological freezing by exchanging configurations between replicas that interpolate between periodic and open boundaries on a small defect. We generalize PTBC by replacing the instantaneous swap with a short out-of-equilibrium protocol, in which the defect couplings of two replicas are exchanged gradually while the fields around the defect are updated. We show that the swap rate depends only on the average work spent along the out-of-equilibrium trajectory by virtue of Crooks' and Jarzynski's fluctuation relations. From the theoretical derivation of the scaling of the work as a function of the number of replicas, protocol length, and defect size, we predict the swap rate and the optimal protocol length. These predictions are tested in the $2d$ $\mathrm{CP}^{N-1}$ model with $N=21$. The optimal setup agrees with the measured one, the total computational cost at fixed statistical precision decreases by $\gtrsim40\%$ with respect to standard PTBC for all explored lattice spacings, and the required number of replicas at fixed acceptance is halved.

### [3] [Lattice gauge theory computation of $1/m_Q$ and $1/m_Q^2$ corrections to the quarkonium potential](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34836)
*Michael Eichberg,Marc Wagner*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a computation of the static quark-antiquark potential, including its 1/m_Q and 1/m_Q^2 corrections, using SU(3) lattice gauge theory. It provides parameterizations for specific quark-antiquark separations, renormalizes field strength insertions with gradient flow, and extrapolates to zero flow time and the continuum. The potentials are matched to the MS-bar scheme at NLO for use in heavy quark mass studies, such as quarkonium in Born-Oppenheimer effective field theory.

Motivation: The motivation is to provide accurate calculations of the quark-antiquark potential and its corrections, which are essential for understanding the behavior of heavy quark systems, such as quarkonium. These calculations are important for theoretical predictions in particle physics and for testing the non-perturbative aspects of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD).

Method: The method involves computing the static quark-antiquark potential and its 1/m_Q and 1/m_Q^2 corrections within the framework of SU(3) lattice gauge theory. The computations are performed for quark-antiquark separations between 0.2 fm and 0.7 fm, with renormalization of the field strength insertions through gradient flow. Extrapolation to the continuum is achieved by using three different lattice spacings and four flow times, and matching the spin-dependent potentials to the MS-bar scheme at next-to-leading order (NLO).

Result: The results include parameterized expressions for the static quark-antiquark potential and its 1/m_Q and 1/m_Q^2 corrections over a range of quark-antiquark separations. The potentials have been extrapolated to the continuum limit and are provided in the MS-bar scheme at a scale of 3 GeV. The study also achieves small statistical uncertainties due to the use of gradient flow for renormalization.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper would likely emphasize the importance of these new parameterizations and the improved accuracy of the quark-antiquark potential, especially when considering 1/m_Q and 1/m_Q^2 corrections. It would highlight the successful extrapolation to the continuum and the relevance of the results for further theoretical work, such as in the context of quarkonium physics and other heavy quark systems.

Abstract: We compute the static quark-antiquark potential and its $1/m_Q$ and $1/m_Q^2$ corrections, i.e.\ the $1/m_Q$ potential and all $1/m_Q^2$ potentials, which can be expressed in terms of correlators of two field strength insertions, in SU(3) lattice gauge theory and provide parameterizations for quark-antiquark separations $0.2 \, \text{fm} \lesssim r \lesssim 0.7 \, \text{fm}$. The field strength insertions in generalized Wilson loops are renormalized using gradient flow, which also leads to small statistical uncertainties. All potentials are extrapolated to zero flow time and, in contrast to previous lattice studies, to the continuum, using three lattice spacings and four flow times. The spin dependent potentials are matched to the $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme at NLO and provided at $μ= 3 \, \text{GeV}$. They can easily be converted to specific heavy quark masses, e.g.\ for Born-Oppenheimer effective field theory studies of quarkonium.
