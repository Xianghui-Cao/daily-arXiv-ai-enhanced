### [1] [Monopole crystal and effective theory for a monopole-enhanced 3D U(1) gauge theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15922)
*Alessandro Mariani*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores a U(1) lattice gauge theory with a monopole enhancement term, revealing an antiferromagnetic ground state for large enhancements. It predicts first-order transitions by varying the monopole chemical potential and a transition in the Ising universality class by altering the gauge coupling, supported by numerical methods to study the phase diagram.

Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to deepen the understanding of a U(1) lattice gauge theory that includes a monopole enhancement term, which has been previously considered in the literature. The authors aim to explore how the inclusion of this term affects the behavior of monopoles and the overall properties of the theory, particularly at different values of the monopole chemical potential and gauge coupling.

Method: The method employed in this paper combines analytical and numerical approaches. Analytically, the authors use a variant of Polyakov's argument to derive an effective sine-Gordon model and predict the presence of first-order transitions as well as a transition in the Ising universality class. Numerically, they investigate the phase diagram of the theory to verify their predictions.

Result: The results show that for large monopole enhancement, the monopoles form an antiferromagnetic, Néel-ordered ground state. The authors' predictions include the occurrence of first-order transitions when the monopole chemical potential changes, and a transition in the Ising universality class when the gauge coupling is varied. These predictions are tested and supported by numerical studies of the phase diagram.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from the research is that the introduction of a monopole enhancement term in the U(1) lattice gauge theory leads to a rich structure in the phase diagram, including antiferromagnetic ordering and distinct phase transitions. The combination of analytical arguments and numerical simulations provides a comprehensive view of the theory's behavior under different conditions.

Abstract: Recently, a version of U(1) lattice gauge theory on the cubic lattice with a monopole enhancement term was considered in the literature. In this work, we further our understanding of this theory with a mixture of analytical and numerical methods. We show that for sufficiently large monopole enhancement, the monopoles are no longer dilute and they arrange themselves into an antiferromagnetic, Néel-ordered ground state. Nonetheless, a variant of Polyakov's argument for the dilute excitations over the dense ground state leads to an effective sine-Gordon model which is qualitatively similar to that of the standard theory. We also predict analytically the presence of first-order transitions as the monopole chemical potential is varied at fixed gauge coupling, corresponding to a change in the dominant monopole charge. Similarly, we predict a transition in the Ising universality class as the gauge coupling is varied. We then use numerical methods to study the phase diagram of the theory and check some of our predictions.

### [2] [Critical behavior of the bosonized Schwinger model with uniform matrix product states](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16092)
*Takuya Okuda*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper uses a Hamiltonian lattice approach to study the critical mass of the continuum Schwinger model at θ=π, employing VUMPS algorithm and Symanzik effective theory for corrections, resulting in a critical mass value that aligns with recent studies.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to compute the critical mass of the continuum Schwinger model at a specific parameter (θ=π) using a non-compact boson field formulation and advanced numerical methods, while ensuring charge conjugation symmetry is preserved.

Method: The authors use a Hamiltonian lattice discretization of the bosonized model and the variational uniform matrix product state (VUMPS) algorithm to numerically obtain the ground state. They also apply an oscillator basis for the local Hilbert space and utilize the Symanzik effective theory to constrain fitting functions for lattice corrections.

Result: The result of the study is a precise computation of the critical mass, (m/g)_c = 0.333 555(5), which is consistent with other recent determinations using staggered-fermion methods.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the critical mass of the continuum Schwinger model at θ=π has been accurately determined using a combination of lattice techniques and theoretical constraints, providing a value that corroborates with previous findings.

Abstract: Quantum electrodynamics in two spacetime dimensions, also known as the Schwinger model, admits a dual formulation in terms of a non-compact boson field. Using a Hamiltonian lattice discretization of the bosonized model, we compute the critical mass of the continuum Schwinger model at $θ=π$. We numerically obtain the ground state on an infinite chain by the variational uniform matrix product state (VUMPS) algorithm, preserving charge conjugation exactly. As a truncated basis for the local Hilbert space, we employ the oscillator basis that diagonalizes the one-site reduced density matrix of the free limit. We constrain the fitting functions for the continuum extrapolation using the Symanzik effective theory for lattice corrections. Our result for the critical mass, $(m/g)_c = 0.333\,555(5)$, agrees with recent staggered-fermion determinations at comparable precision.

*Carsten Urbach*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper uses the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to analyze Wilson twisted mass fermions, proving automatic $O(a)$ improvement and deriving necessary counterterms for clover on-shell improvement. It also shows how to eliminate on-shell redundant contributions, revealing that the clover term splits into two independent terms in isotropic Euclidean space-time.

Motivation: The motivation is to apply the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian approach to the study of Wilson twisted mass fermions, aiming to achieve a better understanding of the automatic $O(a)$ improvement and the structure of the required counterterms for on-shell improvement.

Method: The method involves constructing an effective Hamiltonian based on the symmetries of the lattice Hamiltonian, which is then used to prove automatic $O(a)$ improvement and derive the $O(a)$ counterterms. Canonical transformations are utilized to remove on-shell redundant contributions, leading to the identification of two separate terms with distinct coefficients from the clover term.

Result: The results include the proof of automatic $O(a)$ improvement at maximal twist, the derivation of the $O(a)$ counterterms needed for clover on-shell improvement, and the discovery that the clover term can be decomposed into two terms with individual coefficients in isotropic Euclidean space-time.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the application of the Symanzik effective Hamiltonian to Wilson twisted mass fermions successfully demonstrates automatic $O(a)$ improvement, provides the structure of the necessary counterterms, and reveals new insights into the decomposition of the clover term, contributing to the theoretical foundation for lattice QCD simulations.

Abstract: We apply the recently developed Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to Wilson twisted mass fermions. Moreover, we derive the structure of $O(a)$ counterterms pre-requisite for clover on-shell improvement of Wilson fermions. We show how to remove on-shell redundant contributions using canonical transformations, and find that the well known clover term in isotropic Euclidean space time splits into two terms with independent coefficients.

### [4] [Numerical tests of chiral fermion weights with $η$-invariant phases in the 3450 model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16876)
*Tatsuhiro Misumi*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper tests a new method for coupling chiral fermions to lattice gauge fields, using a 2D U(1) theory. The results show that including the APS phase in the calculation significantly improves the accuracy of Polyakov loops and vacuum-polarization response. At unit flux, the twelve-point correlator's magnitude agrees well with exact results, and the inclusion of the η-invariant further refines the phase, ensuring anomaly cancellation in the combined result.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to validate a recently proposed prescription for coupling continuum chiral fermions to interpolated lattice gauge fields by comparing numerical results with known exact solutions, specifically in an anomaly-free 2D U(1) theory. The goal is to assess the impact of incorporating the Pauli-Villars regularized magnitude and the APS η-invariant on the accuracy of the calculated physical quantities, such as Polyakov loops and vacuum polarization.

Method: The authors employ a 2D U(1) theory with specific left- and right-handed charges, where the torus determinant is exactly known. They numerically evaluate Polyakov loops and the vacuum-polarization response at zero magnetic flux, and a twelve-point correlator at unit flux. The calculations are performed with and without the inclusion of the APS phase (η-invariant) to compare the outcomes and their agreement with the exact results.

Result: At zero magnetic flux, the inclusion of the APS phase leads to a high level of agreement (within 0.04%) between the calculated Polyakov loops and the exact values, whereas omitting the APS phase results in a significant reduction in accuracy. For the vacuum-polarization response, the induced mass is found to be very close to the theoretical value. At unit flux, the magnitude of the twelve-point correlator matches the exact result within 0.8%, and the phase is more accurate when the η-invariant is included, showing only minor deviations. The study also demonstrates that the anomaly cancellation is preserved in the combined result, despite individual phase changes for charged fermions.

Conclusion: The study concludes that the incorporation of the APS phase (η-invariant) in the fermion weight is crucial for achieving high precision in the numerical calculations of physical observables, such as Polyakov loops and vacuum-polarization response, in the context of the 2D U(1) theory. This supports the effectiveness of the proposed prescription for coupling chiral fermions to lattice gauge fields and highlights the importance of the η-invariant for maintaining anomaly cancellation.

Abstract: We test numerically a recently proposed prescription for coupling continuum chiral fermions to interpolated lattice gauge fields, in which the fermion weight has a Pauli-Villars (PV) regularized magnitude and a phase given by an Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) $η$-invariant in one higher dimension. We use the anomaly-free two-dimensional $U(1)$ theory with left-handed charges $3,4$ and right-handed charges $5,0$, whose torus determinant is known exactly. At zero magnetic flux, with gauge fields interpolated from lattice links, the Polyakov loops evaluated on a $16\times16$ grid of flat connections agree with the exact values within $0.04\%$, whereas omitting the APS phase substantially reduces them to about $3\%$ of the exact result. The vacuum-polarization response yields an induced mass $m/e=2.82089$, close to $5/\sqrtπ=2.82095$. At unit flux, where zero modes make the determinant vanish, we calculate a twelve-point correlator on a cylinder with the generalized APS boundary condition. For three smooth backgrounds its magnitude agrees with the exact result within $0.8\%$. Without the $η$-invariant its phase deviates by $1.8$-$3.0$ mrad, and with it by $0.2$-$0.5$ mrad. The phase of each charged fermion changes by $15$-$46$ mrad when the gauge field is extended differently into the third dimension, but the change cancels to below $0.2$ mrad in the anomaly-free combination.

### [5] [$NΩ$ Dibaryon near Unitarity from Physical-Point Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16989)
*Liang Zhang,Yan Lyu,Sinya Aoki,Takumi Doi,Tetsuo Hatsuda,Song Zhang,Yu-Gang Ma*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The study uses (2+1)-flavor lattice QCD and the HAL QCD method to determine the attractive $^{5}S_{2}$ $NΩ$ interaction, which supports a shallow state near unitarity. It provides binding energy values for the $nΩ$ and $pΩ$ systems, serving as a benchmark for future dibaryon research.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The $NΩ$ system is considered a promising dibaryon candidate because its constituents do not share valence quarks, thus avoiding Pauli repulsion. The aim is to provide a first-principles benchmark for future $NΩ$ femtoscopy and dibaryon searches by determining the $NΩ$ interaction and associated binding energies.

Method: The researchers used (2+1)-flavor lattice QCD at physical quark masses with the HAL QCD method to calculate the $^{5}S_{2}$ $NΩ$ interaction. They also performed a direct $T$-matrix pole analysis to extract the binding energies, taking into account finite-charge-size Coulomb attraction for the $pΩ$ system.

Result: The $^{5}S_{2}$ $NΩ$ interaction was found to be attractive, supporting a shallow bound state near unitarity. The binding energy of the $nΩ$ system was determined to be 0.61 MeV, with uncertainties, and for the $pΩ$ system, it was 1.25 MeV, also with uncertainties, when including the effect of Coulomb attraction.

Conclusion: The results from this study offer a significant benchmark for the $NΩ$ interaction and the corresponding binding energies, which will be valuable for guiding future experimental and theoretical work on dibaryons and related femtoscopy studies.

Abstract: The $NΩ$ system is a promising dibaryon candidate because its constituents share no valence quarks and therefore avoid valence-quark Pauli repulsion. We determine the $^{5}S_{2}$ $NΩ$ interaction in (2+1)-flavor lattice QCD at physical quark masses ($m_π\simeq137~\text{MeV}$) using the HAL QCD method. The interaction is attractive over the resolved range and supports a shallow state near unitarity. A direct $T$-matrix pole analysis gives $B_{nΩ} = 0.61\,(^{+0.41}_{-0.30})\,(^{+0.60}_{-0.18})~\text{MeV}$; including finite-charge-size Coulomb attraction yields $B_{pΩ} = 1.25\,(^{+0.46}_{-0.39})\,(^{+0.69}_{-0.25})~\text{MeV}$. These results provide a first-principles benchmark for future $NΩ$ femtoscopy and dibaryon searches.
