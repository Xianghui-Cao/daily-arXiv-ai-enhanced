### [1] [Spin Hamiltonian for Quantum Simulation of Minimally Truncated $3+1$D SU(2) Lattice Gauge Theory and Testing Eigenstate Thermalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36608)
*Ziyuan Li,Xiaojun Yao*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a construction of the spatial-gauge-singlet Hamiltonian for pure SU(2) lattice gauge theory in 3+1 dimensions on qubits with minimal truncation, and tests the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH). It finds a minimal set of independent loop variables, maps them to qubits, and diagonalizes the Hamiltonian. The results support that the 3+1D truncated SU(2) lattice gauge theory follows the ETH, providing a benchmark for future quantum simulations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to test the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) in the context of the 3+1 dimensional pure SU(2) lattice gauge theory, and to provide a benchmark for future real-time quantum simulations of non-equilibrium dynamics.

Method: The method involves constructing the Hamiltonian under a minimal truncation, enforcing Gauss's law, and using the redundancy among gauge-invariant loop operators to find a minimal set of independent loop variables. These are then mapped onto qubits, and the Hamiltonian is written as a sum of Pauli strings. The researchers also exactly diagonalize the Hamiltonian on a specific lattice size and analyze level statistics, local energy density operators, and entanglement entropies of all eigenstates.

Result: The results show that at an ergodic coupling value, the matrix elements of local energy density operators and the entanglement entropies of all eigenstates are consistent with the ETH. This indicates that the 3+1D truncated SU(2) lattice gauge theory does indeed thermalize in accordance with the ETH.

Conclusion: The conclusion drawn from this work is that the 3+1D truncated SU(2) lattice gauge theory thermalizes according to the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis, which provides a solid foundation and reference point for future research into the non-equilibrium dynamics of such systems through real-time quantum simulations.

Abstract: We construct the spatial-gauge-singlet Hamiltonian of pure SU(2) lattice gauge theory in $3+1$ dimensions ($3+1$D) on qubits under the minimal truncation $j_{\rm max}=1/2$, building on previous work on the hyperhoneycomb lattice, and test the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH). By enforcing Gauss's law and exploiting the redundancy among gauge-invariant loop operators, we find the minimal set of independent loop variables that can be bijectively mapped onto qubits, and write the Hamiltonian as a sum of Pauli strings. An $n\times n\times n$ lattice with fixed boundary conditions requires $2(n-1)^3+3(n-1)^2$ qubits. We exactly diagonalize the Hamiltonian on a $2\times 3\times 3$ lattice and identify the coupling range of quantum ergodicity from the level statistics. At the ergodic coupling $g=0.3$, we calculate the matrix elements of local energy density operators and the entanglement entropies of all eigenstates and find them consistent with the ETH. These results show that the $3+1$D truncated SU(2) lattice gauge theory thermalizes in accordance with the ETH and provide a benchmark for future real-time quantum simulations of non-equilibrium dynamics.

### [2] [Easier parameter scans with hmcdj](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37120)
*Alexis Verney-Provatas,Ed Bennett,Gaurav Ray*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: hmcdj is a new, open-source tool for generating large numbers of gauge ensembles with features to manage job execution and quality-of-life improvements, built on the Grid library.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation behind this paper is to introduce a user-friendly and efficient tool, hmcdj, which simplifies the process of creating a large number of gauge ensembles. It addresses the need for a streamlined method to specify lattice gauge theories, manage parameters, and handle large-scale job management, all while being adaptable to high-performance computing (HPC) environments.

Method: The method involves the development of the hmcdj tool, which is based on the Grid library. The tool enables users to quickly define lattice gauge theories in C++ with little boilerplate code and to fine-tune per-ensemble parameters through a YAML input file. It also includes features such as automatic adjustment of the molecular dynamics step size, output of data compliant with the ILDG standard, and dynamic adaptation to HPC scheduler constraints.

Result: The result is a versatile and powerful software solution that can generate a large number of gauge ensembles efficiently, with support for runtime parameter control, automatic tuning, and integration with HPC schedulers. This leads to improved productivity and ease of use for researchers working with lattice gauge theories.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that hmcdj provides a significant enhancement to the workflow for researchers by offering a flexible and efficient way to generate gauge ensembles. With its open-source nature under the GPLv2 license, it also encourages community contributions and further development.

Abstract: We present hmcdj, a new tool developed to ease the process of generating large numbers of gauge ensembles for a given theory. Building on top of the Grid library, hmcdj allows quick specification of lattice gauge theories in C++ with minimal boilerplate, and precise specification of per-ensemble parameters to be controlled at run time from a YAML input file. Additionally, it supports many quality-of-life features for managing large numbers of running jobs, including automatic tuning of the molecular dynamics step size to control the Metropolis acceptance rate; output of ILDG-compliant data; and integration with HPC schedulers to dynamically adapt the target trajectory count of a job to match its time limits. hmcdj is openly available under the GPLv2 license, and welcomes new contributions.
