### [1] [MORE Thermal Gauge Theories at Finite $θ$ and $μ$ from Real-Time Quantum Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38569)
*Henry Lamm*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses a method to reconstruct imaginary-time evolution from real-time quantum simulations, extends this method to thermal traces and correlators, and demonstrates its application in lattice Schwinger models, including noisy simulations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to enable the reconstruction of finite-temperature and chemical potential physics from real-time quantum simulations without needing to prepare thermal states, which can be challenging in quantum computing contexts.

Method: The authors use an exact integral transform to reconstruct imaginary-time evolution. They extend the previously known construction by Guo et al. to include thermal traces and correlators, allowing for post-processing to target specific inverse temperatures, Euclidean separations, and chemical potentials. The method is benchmarked using one- and two-flavor lattice Schwinger models, with and without depolarizing noise.

Result: The results show that the slow convergence of the original construction by Guo et al. is due to a redundant kernel component. The extension to thermal quantities enables the extraction of finite-temperature and -chemical-potential properties from a single real-time dataset. The method's robustness is demonstrated through circuit-level simulations, even under the influence of depolarizing noise.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that this approach provides a viable way to access finite-temperature and -chemical-potential regimes in quantum simulations, bypassing the need for direct thermal-state preparation, and it is resilient to certain types of noise, making it a promising tool for future studies in quantum field theory and condensed matter physics.

Abstract: Imaginary-time evolution can be reconstructed from real-time quantum simulations using exact integral transforms. We identify the construction of Guo, Shibu, Lin, and Zhao as a continuous linear combination of Hamiltonian simulations and show that its slow $1/t_{\rm cut}$ convergence arises from a redundant kernel component. We extend the construction from pure-state matrix elements to thermal traces and correlators. One real-time dataset then reconstructs targeted inverse temperatures, Euclidean separations, and chemical potentials through classical post-processing, bringing finite-$(T,θ,μ)$ physics within reach of real-time quantum simulation without thermal-state preparation. We benchmark the method on one- and two-flavor lattice Schwinger models, including circuit-level simulations with depolarizing noise.

### [2] [No-go theorems for dual Hamiltonians of Non-Abelian Lattice Gauge Theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39780)
*Marco Garofalo,Tobias Hartung,Timo Jakobs,Paul Ludwig,Johann Ostmeyer,Carsten Urbach*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses the challenges in simulating non-Abelian lattice gauge theories on quantum computers, particularly the inevitability of long-range interactions. It also explores an approach to mitigate this issue, which is successful for U(1) but proven inconsistent for non-Abelian cases.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the lack of an efficient formulation for simulating non-Abelian lattice gauge theories, which are important for understanding QCD, especially as quantum computing hardware advances and larger simulations become possible.

Method: The authors use a theoretical approach to prove that long-range interactions are unavoidable when reparametrizing the gauge configuration while maintaining the number of degrees of freedom and fundamental commutation relations. They then introduce additional gauge links and constraints to try and mitigate the problem, testing this method on both U(1) and non-Abelian cases.

Result: It is shown that long-range interactions are inevitable in non-Abelian lattice gauge theories under the given conditions. The proposed mitigation strategy works for U(1) but is found to be inconsistent for non-Abelian theories.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that while it is possible to reduce the impact of long-range interactions in Abelian (U(1)) lattice gauge theories, such a solution does not extend to the non-Abelian case, indicating a need for new approaches to efficiently simulate these theories on quantum computers.

Abstract: Hamiltonian simulations of lattice gauge theories promise new insights into the inner workings of QCD. The rapid development of quantum computing hardware suggests that large scale simulations may soon become feasible. However, no efficient formulation suitable for simulations near the continuum limit is currently known for the non-Abelian case, and existing candidates typically feature long range interactions in the electric part of the Hamiltonian. We prove that such non- localities are unavoidable when the gauge configuration is reparametrised while preserving both the number of degrees of freedom and the fundamental commutation relations. We then attempt to mitigate this by introducing additional gauge links alongside additional constraints. Although this approach is successful in U(1), we prove that the corresponding construction is inconsistent for non-Abelian lattice gauge theories.

### [3] [Information-preserving boundary reconstruction for center-flux energetics in a regulated SU(3) gauge ladder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40171)
*Hongyu Ma*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper explores the manipulation of gauge systems, particularly in an SU(3) ladder setup, by reconstructing boundaries and preserving interior physics. It finds that path probabilities alone are insufficient for maintaining magnetic observables, and demonstrates a method to lower output energy using coherent resources without needing purification. The research establishes vacuum and flux energy densities independently and shows that open and periodic line-energy densities are equal under certain conditions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the challenge of manipulating (opening, closing, or joining) gauge systems while keeping their internal physics intact, which is complicated by Gauss constraints. The work aims to understand how boundary reconstructions can be performed on a pure SU(3) ladder with specific cutoffs, and to investigate the role of coherent resources in controlling the energy of the system without resorting to traditional methods like purification or global ground-state preparation.

Method: The authors use boundary reconstructions with all allowed intertwiner multiplicities on an SU(3) ladder at six site-singlet cutoffs. They employ legal column paths and physical self-loops to obtain replacements of a given length, ensuring that the channels preserve unnormalized conditional interior matrices. Additionally, they apply lowest-cutoff dephasing controls and same-sector gluing to analyze the energy costs and stability of the system, as well as to compare the energy densities between different boundary conditions.

Result: The results show that path probabilities alone cannot maintain the matched magnetic observables, but the use of coherent resources can reduce the output energy. The study also demonstrates that the energy cost is bounded and independent of the longitudinal length due to the cancellation of matched interior Hamiltonian terms. Furthermore, it establishes the equality of open and periodic vacuum-subtracted line-energy densities through boundary comparisons using a qualified periodic minimizing domain.

Conclusion: The paper concludes that it is possible to manipulate gauge systems while preserving their internal physics by carefully constructing boundary conditions. It highlights the importance of coherent resources in lowering energy costs and provides evidence that the energy density properties of the system are preserved across different boundary configurations, subject to the fixed parameters of lattice spacing, transverse width, coupling, and chosen cutoff.

Abstract: Gauss constraints make it nontrivial to open, close or join a gauge system while retaining its interior physics. Boundary reconstructions are constructed on a pure SU(3) ladder at six site-singlet cutoffs, with all allowed intertwiner multiplicities. Replacements of prescribed length are obtained using legal column paths and physical self-loops. On their stated physical domains, the channels preserve unnormalized conditional interior matrices, including their weights and within-branch coherence. Lowest-cutoff dephasing controls show that path probabilities alone do not preserve the matched magnetic observables. In that example, coherent resources lower the output energy without purification or global ground-state preparation. Matched interior Hamiltonian terms cancel exactly, so the energy cost is bounded independently of longitudinal length. The vacuum and flux energy densities are then established separately by same-sector gluing and stability. The open and periodic vacuum-subtracted line-energy densities are shown to be equal by two same-length boundary comparisons using the qualified periodic minimizing domain. These limits hold at fixed lattice spacing, transverse width, coupling and chosen cutoff.

### [4] [Unpolarized quasi- and pseudo-distributions at one loop: gluon correlator decomposition, matching, and the region $|x|>1$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40205)
*Christopher Monahan,Tobias Neumann*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses the computation of one-loop unpolarized quark and gluon quasi- and pseudo-distributions in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme, with a focus on matching them to light-cone PDFs. It provides a detailed analysis of the renormalization mixing matrix for the gluon correlator, the structure of quasi-distributions outside the typical range, and differences in matching kernels used by different research groups, which affect the extracted gluon moments.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a more accurate and consistent method for computing and matching quark and gluon distributions, which are essential for understanding the partonic structure of hadrons. The work aims to resolve discrepancies between different approaches and to clarify the non-perturbative physics carried by the exterior of quasi-distributions.

Method: The authors use a combination of theoretical techniques including dimensional regularization, renormalization group analysis, and the Paley-Wiener theorem. They decompose the gluon correlator into covariant form factors, derive the renormalization mixing matrix, and analyze Fourier transforms in d dimensions to understand the structure of quasi-distributions.

Result: The results include a 6x6 renormalization mixing matrix for the gluon correlator, an analysis of the structure of quasi-distributions in the region |x|>1, and a comparison of the matching kernel with that of other researchers. The study also reveals that the difference in the matching kernel leads to a systematic underestimation of the second and third gluon moments by about 11% and 13%, respectively, at a given value of the strong coupling constant.

Conclusion: The conclusions drawn from the paper are that there is no independent non-perturbative information in the exterior region of quasi-distributions, and that the choice of matching kernel can significantly impact the extracted gluon moments. The paper suggests that the true $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ gluon distribution may be harder than previously reported.

Abstract: We compute one-loop unpolarized quark and gluon quasi- and pseudo-distributions in $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ and match them to light-cone PDFs. Decomposing the gluon correlator into six covariant form factors, we derive the $6\times6$ renormalization mixing matrix: its eigenvectors classify multiplicatively renormalizable combinations, and any lattice operator choice projects onto our results without a further loop calculation.
  Evaluating all Fourier transforms in $d$ dimensions, we resolve the structure of the exterior $|x|>1$ in quasi-distributions. The correlator carries a short-distance logarithm with a branch cut at $ν=0$. By the Paley-Wiener theorem, the analytic part transforms into $|x|\le1$, so the exterior is generated solely by this branch cut. At leading power, the exterior carries no independent non-perturbative physics: it is fixed by the light-cone PDF through the renormalization group. In LaMET, lattice data therefore cannot determine the exterior independently of the interior, and unconstrained exterior parameters in reconstructions risk absorbing genuine power corrections into the leading-twist PDF.
  In the gluon channel, the matching kernel of Balitsky, Morris and Radyushkin differs from our $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ result by a finite polynomial, identical across index choices. We trace this to contracting an internal loop index over two instead of $d-2$ transverse directions, omitting an evanescent operator on the collinear pole. Using our basis, we show that the independent kernel of Yao, Ji and Zhang agrees with ours, separated from Balitsky et al. by the same polynomial.
  While the ratio scheme protects the momentum fraction $\langle x\rangle_g$, this kernel difference suppresses the extracted normalized second and third gluon moments by $11\%$ and $13\%$ at $α_s=0.3$, so the true $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ gluon is systematically harder than reported.
