### [1] [Nucleon-sigma terms at $m_π=222 ~\rm MeV$ with a variational analysis from lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05989)
*Lorenzo Barca,Gunnar Bali,Sara Collins,Marcel Rodekamp*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper discusses the importance of nucleon sigma terms and presents preliminary results on reducing excited-state contamination in lattice QCD analyses at a lower pion mass, using a variational analysis with N and Nσ operators.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: There is a persistent discrepancy between lattice QCD and phenomenological determinations of nucleon sigma terms, possibly due to uncontrolled excited-state contamination. The study aims to address this issue, particularly at a lower pion mass where the σ particle becomes unstable, to see if the same method can suppress the expected Nππ contamination.

Method: The authors employ a variational analysis technique with N (nucleon) and Nσ (nucleon-sigma) operators to reduce the excited-state contamination, previously demonstrated to be effective at a higher pion mass (m_π=429 MeV). They now apply this method to a lower pion mass (m_π=222 MeV) where the σ particle decays into ππ, to investigate the suppression of Nππ contamination.

Result: Preliminary results are presented for the analysis at m_π=222 MeV, indicating that the variational basis used may also be effective in removing the Nππ contamination, although the detailed outcomes are not fully provided in the abstract.

Conclusion: The study suggests that the variational approach with N and Nσ operators could potentially mitigate the Nππ contamination in lattice QCD calculations at a lower pion mass, which is critical for improving the accuracy of nucleon sigma term determinations.

Abstract: Nucleon sigma terms are important for the decomposition of the nucleon mass and for searches for new physics beyond the Standard Model involving scalar interactions. A persistent tension between lattice QCD and phenomenological determinations may be due to uncontrolled excited-state contamination in lattice QCD analyses. In previous work at $m_π=429~\rm MeV$, we showed that this contamination is dominated by $Nσ$ states and can be strongly suppressed with a variational analysis using $N$ and $Nσ$ operators. In this talk, we present preliminary results at $m_π=222~\rm MeV$, where the $σ$ becomes unstable and decays into $ππ$. We investigate whether the same small variational basis can remove the expected $Nππ$ contamination through the overlap of the $Nσ$ operator with these states.

### [3] [Sector-Resolved Flow Sampling for Topologically Frozen Lattice Gauge Theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07781)
*Ankur Singha,Jacob Kauffmann,Karl Jansen,Jacob Finkenrath,Vipul Arora,Shinichi Nakajima*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: A new method, Mixture of Sector-Resolved Samplers (MSRS), is introduced to overcome topological barriers in lattice gauge theory, enabling more accurate and efficient sampling compared to existing methods, particularly in challenging regimes where other techniques struggle or fail.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation stems from the difficulty in sampling topological fluctuations in nonperturbative gauge theories as one approaches the continuum limit. Traditional Markov chains can get stuck in sectors with fixed topological charge, leading to inadequate sampling and inaccurate results. The goal is to develop a method that can efficiently and accurately sample across these disconnected sectors.

Method: The authors introduce a generative sampler, termed a Mixture of Sector-Resolved Samplers (MSRS), which resolves topological sectors explicitly. This approach leverages the strength of generative models to directly evaluate the partition function within specific domains, allowing for the determination of relative weights between different sectors. A key component involves training a generative model in a reference sector, combined with a deterministic bijective mapping (topological shift) to transfer samples to other sectors. The method's efficacy is demonstrated in 2D compact U(1) lattice gauge theory.

Result: The MSRS method successfully reproduces the distribution of topological charges and provides an unbiased estimate of susceptibility, even in regimes where hybrid Monte Carlo methods are ineffective and overrelaxation yields poor estimates. Additionally, it significantly outperforms current flow-based samplers, showing orders of magnitude improvement in performance. These outcomes highlight the potential of the MSRS approach for overcoming challenges associated with topological barriers in lattice gauge theories.

Conclusion: The study concludes by affirming that the explicit resolution of topological sectors through the MSRS method offers a promising avenue for addressing the long-standing issue of topological freezing in lattice gauge theory simulations. It suggests that this approach could be broadly applicable, enhancing our ability to explore and understand nonperturbative aspects of gauge theories.

Abstract: Topological fluctuations are essential to nonperturbative gauge theories but become increasingly difficult to sample toward the continuum limit, where Markov chains can freeze in sectors of fixed topological charge. We introduce a generative sampler, a mixture of sector-resolved samplers (MSRS) that explicitly resolves these sectors and exploits a key advantage of generative models, the ability to directly evaluate the domain-restricted partition function and thereby determine the relative weights of disconnected sectors. We train a generative model in a reference topological sector combined with a bijective topological shift that deterministically maps its samples to other sectors. We demonstrate the method in two-dimensional compact U (1) lattice gauge theory, where it reproduces the topological-charge distribution and yields an unbiased susceptibility in a regime where hybrid Monte Carlo is frozen and overrelaxation gives inaccurate estimates. Our approach also outperforms existing flow-based samplers by orders of magnitude. These results demonstrate that explicit sector resolution provides a promising route to overcoming topological barriers in lattice gauge theory.

### [4] [Gauge field digitization in the Hamiltonian limit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07886)
*Attila Pasztor,David Pesznyak*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the digitization of U(1) gauge theories using Z(N) subgroups on anisotropic Euclidean lattices, finding that the freezing transition is present in the Hamiltonian limit and finite-N theories can significantly differ from U(1), providing a benchmark for errors in quantum simulations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to reduce the required quantum resources by using a finite subgroup to represent continuous gauge fields, while controlling the introduced digitization errors as the Hamiltonian (or continuous-time) limit is approached. The study aims to understand the behavior of these discrete groups in 2+1 dimensions and their accuracy in approximating the continuous group.

Method: The authors derive trajectories for spatial and temporal gauge couplings for approaching the Hamiltonian limit with fixed coupling. They use classical lattice simulations and exact diagonalization to verify the reproduction of the corresponding Hamiltonian theories, and analyze the scaling of the temporal coupling and the presence of the freezing transition in the Hamiltonian limit.

Result: The results show that the temporal coupling scales logarithmically for finite Z(N) in contrast to the power-law scaling of the continuous U(1) theory. The freezing transition persists in the Hamiltonian limit, and even outside this regime, the finite-N theories can differ substantially from U(1).

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the digitization of gauge fields using discrete subgroups can lead to significant differences compared to the continuous U(1) theory, even in regimes where one would expect good approximation. These findings provide a classical benchmark for quantifying systematic errors due to digitization in quantum simulations.

Abstract: Quantum computers can circumvent the numerical sign problem in gauge theories at finite density or in real time. Quantum simulations of gauge theories require a finite-dimensional representation of continuous gauge fields. Replacing a continuous gauge group by a finite subgroup can substantially reduce the required quantum resources, but introduces digitization errors that must be controlled in the Hamiltonian, or continuous-time, limit. Previous studies, using the isotropic Euclidean lattices showed that the freezing transition of the discrete subgroup can make it a bad approximation for the continuous group at large Euclidean couplings. Here, we study the digitization of U(1) by its Z($N$) subgroups in 2+1 dimensions using anisotropic Euclidean lattices. We derive the trajectories of the spatial and temporal gauge couplings along which the Hamiltonian limit is approached at fixed Hamiltonian coupling. While the temporal coupling exhibits power-law scaling in the continuous U(1) theory, it grows only logarithmically for finite Z($N$). Using classical lattice simulations and exact diagonalization, we verify that these trajectories reproduce the corresponding Hamiltonian theories. We find that the freezing transition persists in the Hamiltonian limit of discrete gauge groups and that finite-$N$ theories can differ substantially from U(1) even outside the frozen regime, in contrast to the behavior on isotropic Euclidean lattices, where for small couplings, the discrete group provides a very accurate approximation of the continuous group. Our results provide a classical benchmark for quantifying the systematic errors due to gauge-field digitization in quantum simulations.

</details>
