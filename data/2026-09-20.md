### [1] [Symplectic lattice gauge theories in the Grid framework: domain wall fermions and continuum extrapolations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19930)
*Ed Bennett,Peter A. Boyle,Luigi Del Debbio,Niccolò Forzano,Ryan C. Hill,Deog Ki Hong,Jong-Wan Lee,C. -J. David Lin,Biagio Lucini,Maurizio Piai,Gianmarco Simonetti,Davide Vadacchino,Alexis Verney-Provatas*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper presents the first numerical lattice study using domain-wall fermions in Sp(4) gauge theory with two flavors of fermions. The research demonstrates that the Möbius formulation of domain-wall fermions (MDWF) can significantly improve the approach to the continuum limit, offering substantial gains for phenomenological applications.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a short-distance completion for composite Higgs models or dark matter models based on strongly interacting massive particles, by studying Sp(4) gauge theory coupled to fermions. This work aims to compare and demonstrate the advantages of using MDWF over Wilson fermions in lattice calculations.

Method: The method involves adopting the Möbius formulation of domain-wall fermions (MDWF) within the Grid software environment, performing extensive tests and optimizations, and measuring masses and decay constants of the lightest flavoured mesons across various lattice couplings and fermion masses. An extrapolation to the continuum is then performed, and results are compared with those obtained using Wilson fermions.

Result: The results show that the MDWF formulation, even with moderate computational resources, can lead to order-of-magnitude improvements in approaching the continuum limit, particularly in parameter regions important for phenomenological applications of this theory. The physical observables measured are also compared against published data from studies that used Wilson fermions.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the use of MDWF in the context of Sp(4) gauge theory with two flavors of fermions leads to significant benefits for achieving accurate and efficient simulations, which are crucial for the exploration of beyond-Standard Model physics, such as composite Higgs models and dark matter models.

Abstract: We report the results of the first numerical lattice study using domain-wall fermions in the Sp(4) gauge theory coupled to two flavours of (Dirac) fermions, transforming in the fundamental representation of the gauge group. This theory plays a prominent role in the literature on extensions of the Standard Model with composite dynamics. It provides a short-distance completion for a class of composite Higgs models, or, alternatively, of dark matter models based on the strongly interacting massive particle paradigm. We adopt the Möbius formulation of domain-wall fermions (MDWF), implemented within the Grid software environment. We report the results of extensive tests of the algorithm implementation, and of the optimisation of the choices of algorithmic parameters appearing in the MDWF action. We then measure masses and decay constants of the lightest flavoured mesons in ensembles with moderately large fermion masses and several choices of lattice coupling, and perform an extrapolation to the continuum. We compare our results for the physical observables to published measurements obtained in the same field theory, but derived on the lattice by employing Wilson fermions. We demonstrate that, with the deployment of moderate computational resources, the MDWF formulation can yield order-of-magnitude gains in the approach to the continuum limit, in regions of physical parameter space relevant to phenomenological applications of this theory.

### [2] [Numerical Investigations of Phase Transitions in Lattice Field Theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20536)
*Vamika Longia*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This thesis presents two numerical methods for studying phase transitions in lattice systems: the use of GPU-accelerated HOTRG to study the 2D generalized XY model, and a configurational temperature estimator for compact U(1) lattice gauge theories, which together offer precise analysis and validation of thermal sampling.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The need for high-precision numerical tools to resolve subtle critical phenomena in physical systems at phase transitions motivates this research. The aim is to develop methods that can provide detailed insights into the behavior of these systems, particularly at critical points where qualitative changes occur.

Method: Two complementary approaches are used: (1) A GPU-accelerated higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG) technique to analyze the 2D generalized XY model, identifying its different phases and their boundaries; (2) Development and benchmarking of a configurational temperature estimator, derived from the Euclidean lattice action's gradients and Hessians, for use in compact U(1) lattice gauge theories.

Result: The HOTRG method successfully characterizes the ferromagnetic, nematic, and paramagnetic phases of the 2D generalized XY model, and maps out their phase boundaries using thermodynamic observables. The configurational temperature estimator provides an independent way to validate thermal sampling, with low computational overhead, and can diagnose sampling issues before large-scale simulations.

Conclusion: The combination of advanced tensor network methods and the novel configurational temperature estimator offers enhanced precision and reliability in the study of phase transitions, providing both a rich description of phase structures and a robust validation tool for thermal sampling across various algorithms and models.

Abstract: The study of phase transitions plays an important role in understanding qualitative changes in the behaviour of physical systems at criticality. Despite decades of progress, there is still a strong demand for high-precision numerical tools capable of resolving subtle critical phenomena. Motivated by this need, in this thesis, we present two complementary numerical investigations of phase transitions in lattice systems. The first uses GPU-accelerated higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG) techniques to study the two-dimensional generalized XY model, characterizing its ferromagnetic, nematic, and paramagnetic phases and mapping their phase boundaries using thermodynamic observables in the thermodynamic limit. The second develops and benchmarks a configurational temperature estimator, constructed from gradients and Hessians of the Euclidean lattice action, in compact U(1) lattice gauge theories. On one hand, tensor network methods capture rich phase structures when truncation and finite-bond effects are adequately controlled. On the other hand, the configurational temperature estimator provides an independent, low-overhead means of validating thermal sampling across different algorithms and models, and can also be used as a runtime diagnostic to identify sampling pathologies before large-scale production runs.

### [3] [Computability of GPDs near $x=\pmξ$ in Lattice QCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20545)
*Yushan Su,Xiangdong Ji,Yizhuang Liu,Rui Zhang*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper demonstrates that the condition for large momentum expansion in GPDs computations can be relaxed, allowing for a broader region of computability and revealing partonic threshold behavior not previously observed, leading to new predictions for GPDs.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to expand the computable regions of generalized parton distributions (GPDs) in lattice QCD calculations, specifically by relaxing the requirement on one of the hard scales, thus making it possible to explore the important $x\sim\pm\xi$ regions.

Method: The authors revisit previous lattice results with complete one-loop matching to relax the condition for the large momentum expansion, enabling the exploration of GPDs at large $\xi$ values where $2|x\pm\xi|P^z$ does not need to be much larger than $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$.

Result: By relaxing the conditions, the study reveals the expected partonic threshold behavior of GPDs, showing continuity at $x=\pm\xi$ but with discontinuous derivatives, which had not been seen before in lattice calculations. This leads to the first-time prediction for GPDs in the distribution-amplitude-like region, connecting quark and antiquark PDF-like behaviors.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that by relaxing the usual condition for large momentum expansion, the computable region for GPDs has been expanded, and significant new insights into the partonic structure of nucleons have been gained, including the observation of the threshold behavior and the connection between quark and antiquark PDF-like behaviors.

Abstract: In lattice QCD computations of generalized parton distributions (GPDs), the large momentum expansion generally requires all hard scales, $2|x\pmξ|P^z$ and $2|1\pm x|P^z$, to be much larger than $Λ_{\rm QCD}$. We show that this condition can be relaxed for $2|x\pmξ|P^z$ at large $ξ$, making the important $x\sim\pmξ$ regions accessible to lattice calculations and considerably expanding the region of computability. Revisiting previous lattice results with complete one-loop matching, we obtain the expected partonic threshold behavior---GPDs continuous at $x=\pmξ$ but with discontinuous derivatives---which has not previously been observed on the lattice. We thus obtain, for the first time, important prediction for GPDs in the distribution-amplitude-like region, which smoothly connects the quark and antiquark PDF-like behaviors.
