### [1] [A first look at Structured-Multiscale Algebraic Multigrid for Lattice Field Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03515)
*Pauline Schauerte,Jaime Fabián Nieto Castellanos,Arnold Krechel,Marc Alexander Schweitzer,Stefan Krieg*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper introduces and benchmarks a new multigrid approach, Structured-Multiscale Algebraic Multigrid (SM-AMG), in the context of Lattice QCD, comparing it with an existing method, DD$α$AMG. It shows that while both methods have similar computational costs on fine lattices, AM-AMG, which is based on SM-AMG, has more fine-grid iterations and struggles with low modes on coarse lattices.

Motivation: The motivation is to improve the solvers for the Dirac equation in Lattice QCD by reducing the complexity of parameter tuning, which is necessary for achieving optimal performance with current state-of-the-art adaptive multigrid methods.

Method: A new multigrid approach, called Structured-Multiscale Algebraic Multigrid (SM-AMG), is adapted from oil-reservoir simulations and applied to Lattice Field Theory. This method uses compact aggregates with overlapping borders and mainly requires tuning of the aggregate size. The efficiency of the algebraic version, Aggregative-Multiscale AMG (AM-AMG), is then compared to the established DD$α$AMG solver using the two-flavor Schwinger model with Wilson discretization.

Result: On fine lattices, the operation count for both AM-AMG and DD$α$AMG is similar, indicating comparable computational cost, but AM-AMG requires a larger number of fine-grid iterations. On coarse lattices, AM-AMG faces challenges in removing the low modes near the critical mass, where it underperforms compared to DD$α$AMG.

Conclusion: While the SM-AMG-based AM-AMG method simplifies the tuning process, its performance is mixed. It performs comparably to DD$α$AMG on fine lattices but encounters issues with low modes on coarser lattices, suggesting that further improvements are needed for it to be a viable alternative across all lattice spacings.

Abstract: State-of-the-art solvers for the Dirac equation in Lattice QCD are based on adaptive multigrid methods. These require fine-tuning of many algorithmic parameters to achieve optimal performance. We apply a new multigrid approach to Lattice Field Theory adapted from oil-reservoir simulations: Structured-Multiscale Algebraic Multigrid (SM-AMG). This method builds compact aggregates with overlapping borders to coarsen the grid and yields accurate interpolation. A key advantage is that aggregate size is the primary tunable parameter. For our results, we used SM-AMG in an algebraic approach, called Aggregative-Multiscale AMG (AM-AMG). We benchmark the efficiency of AM-AMG against that of DD$α$AMG, a successful adaptive multigrid solver which alleviates critical slowing down. The two solvers are compared within the framework of the two-flavor Schwinger model using the Wilson discretization. On fine lattices, the operation count of both methods is similar near the critical point and for large volumes, reflecting a comparable computational cost. However, the number of fine-grid iterations is larger for AM-AMG. On coarse lattices, AM-AMG encounters difficulties to remove the low modes close to the critical mass.
