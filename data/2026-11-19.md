### [1] [Generative diffusion models for lattice field theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23399)
*Gert Aarts,Diaa E. Habibi,Lingxiao Wang,Kai Zhou*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper explores the application of generative diffusion models in lattice field theory, especially for theories with a sign problem. It highlights the use of complex Langevin dynamics and energy-based diffusion models to learn distributions from complex data, which can then be utilized in Monte Carlo simulations. Demonstrated through a simple quartic model with a complex action.

Motivation: The motivation is to find a method to overcome the sign problem in certain lattice field theories where direct sampling is not feasible due to complex Boltzmann weights. The aim is to utilize generative diffusion models and complex Langevin dynamics to generate configurations that can be used for accurate simulations.

Method: The author employs generative diffusion models, particularly focusing on score-based and energy-based models, to learn the real and semi-positive distribution generated by complex Langevin dynamics. This learned distribution is then used as the weight in standard Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) simulations, allowing for the simulation of systems with a sign problem.

Result: The results show that energy-based diffusion models can successfully learn the distribution from complex Langevin data, and this learned distribution can be effectively used within MCMC simulations. The approach is demonstrated to work on a simple quartic model with a complex action.

Conclusion: The paper concludes that generative diffusion models, especially the energy-based ones, provide a promising method for dealing with the sign problem in lattice field theories. By learning the underlying distribution, it's possible to carry out simulations that would otherwise be hindered by complex Boltzmann weights, thus opening up new possibilities for studying these theories.

Abstract: Generative diffusion models offer a new route to ensemble generation in lattice field theory. The backward, or denoising, process underlying these models is a stochastic process with a learned, time-dependent drift, which leads to a clear connection with stochastic quantisation. After reviewing this correspondence, I turn to theories with a sign problem. There the complex Boltzmann weight obstructs direct sampling, but complex Langevin dynamics can sometimes evade the sign problem by generating configurations on a complexified manifold, distributed according to a real and semi-positive distribution. This distribution is not known in closed form. I discuss how this distribution can be learned from complex Langevin data using score-based and, in particular, energy-based diffusion models. The latter give direct access to the unnormalised distribution itself, which can subsequently be used as the weight in a standard Markov chain Monte Carlo simulation. Results are shown for a simple quartic model with a complex action.

### [2] [Positivity, Noise, and Cones](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23982)
*Ryan Abbott,Claire X. Davis,Sarah Fields,William I. Jay,Patrick Oare,Matteo Saccardi*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper introduces a projection procedure to restore positivity in Euclidean-time correlation functions, which often lose this property due to statistical fluctuations during Monte Carlo computations. The method utilizes the conic structure of the causal space and can be computed using primal-dual interior-point methods, leading to confidence intervals for smeared spectral densities.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to address the issue of statistical fluctuations in numerically computed quantum field theory correlation functions that violate the causality-induced positivity conditions, by developing a method to decompose these noisy correlators into causal and acausal components while leveraging the conic structure of the causal space.

Method: The authors propose a projection procedure that uses the convex conic geometry defined by the positivity conditions and a statistical covariance matrix to restore the positivity of the correlation functions. This approach employs primal-dual interior-point methods for computation and results in a unique decomposition of the correlators.

Result: The result is a method that successfully restores positivity in correlation functions, providing a clear separation between causal and acausal components. Additionally, it establishes frequentist confidence intervals for smeared spectral densities, supported by numerical examples.

Conclusion: The conclusion is that the new projection technique, based on the conic structure, offers a robust solution for restoring the positivity of correlation functions and allows for the estimation of confidence intervals, thus improving the reliability of Monte Carlo simulations in quantum field theory.

Abstract: As a consequence of causality, Euclidean-time correlation functions in quantum field theory are subject to constraints in the form of positivity conditions on certain Hankel matrices. Such conditions define convex conic structures. Correlation functions computed numerically using Monte Carlo techniques generally contain statistical fluctuations that violate the positivity conditions. This work describes a projection procedure, based on the convex conic geometry induced by the positivity conditions and a statistical covariance matrix, that can be used to restore positivity, providing a unique decomposition for noisy correlators into causal and acausal components. In contrast to previous literature, this work explicitly leverages the conic structure and not just the convex nature of the causal space. The projection can be computed using primal-dual interior-point methods. Constrained projection gives rise to observable-specific frequentist confidence intervals for smeared spectral densities in the causal bootstrap of [Phys. Rev. D 114, 074510 (2026)]. Analytic results are supported with numerical examples.

### [3] [Interpretable uncertainties for bounds on spectral functionals from Euclidean correlators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24374)
*Christoph Lehner*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: Error


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Error

Conclusion: Error

Abstract: Positivity of the spectral density, combined with a $χ^2$ measure of compatibility with the data, bounds every linear functional of the spectral density of a Euclidean correlator to an interval. We show how such an interval is turned into a central value with statistical and systematic errors that have a definite meaning. We first show that, at typical precision, the best positive fit of noisy data has $χ^2_{\min}>0$, with a probability bounded from below by the distance of the noise-free data from the boundary of the set of data of positive spectra. Numerically, it does not depend on the number of states. The threshold must therefore be set relative to $χ^2_{\min}$. With $Δχ^2=1$, the interval reproduces Gaussian error propagation when positivity does not act, and positivity can only narrow it. We define the statistical error by the jackknife and the systematic error as the remaining, non-Gaussian part of the interval, which becomes a systematic error that does not decrease with the statistics in the high-statistics limit. Coverage tests for correlators and for smeared spectral densities and step functions give nominal coverage for outputs determined by the data and mostly conservative coverage otherwise. The exceptions are effects of the statistical error on the error estimate that disappear as the statistics is increased. Additional bounds on the spectral density, for example from perturbation theory, further reduce the systematic error substantially without loss of coverage. For long-distance correlators the method uses the same information as the bounding method, and its error agrees with that of the bounding method up to the remaining gap between the bounds.

### [4] [Light fermions can trigger a Brout-Englert-Higgs effect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24658)
*Axel Maas,Sofie Martins,Georg Wieland*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents evidence from lattice simulations that the Brout-Englert-Higgs effect, which gives mass to Standard Model particles, could be caused by the light masses of fermions. This suggests a potential explanation within the Standard Model itself and points to a new mechanism in an SU(2) scalar-fermion-gauge system for generating a non-zero vacuum expectation value, with a transition to Higgs-like behavior when the fermion mass is below twice the W/Z mass.

Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to explore the cause of the Brout-Englert-Higgs effect, which is responsible for the masses of particles in the Standard Model but whose origin remains unknown. The authors seek to determine if the effect can be explained without invoking theories beyond the Standard Model, such as through the influence of light fermion masses on the Higgs potential.

Method: The method used in this paper involves conducting lattice simulations with vectorially coupled fermions and no Yukawa couplings to investigate the impact of fermion masses on the Brout-Englert-Higgs effect. The simulations are designed to examine the conditions under which the Higgs-like behavior emerges, specifically focusing on the fermion mass relative to the W and Z boson masses.

Result: The results indicate that the Brout-Englert-Higgs effect may be triggered by the light masses of fermions, suggesting a possible explanation within the Standard Model. The findings also reveal a novel mechanism in an SU(2) scalar-fermion-gauge system that can produce a non-zero vacuum expectation value, with the transition to Higgs-like behavior occurring at a specific threshold related to the W/Z mass.

Conclusion: The conclusion of the paper is that the light masses of fermions could potentially explain the Brout-Englert-Higgs effect within the framework of the Standard Model, without the need for additional theories. Furthermore, the discovery of a new mechanism for generating a non-zero vacuum expectation value and the identification of a critical fermion mass threshold contribute to our understanding of particle physics.

Abstract: The Brout-Englert-Higgs effect produces the masses of the Standard Model particles. However, the cause of this effect is unknown. Beyond-the-Standard-Model theories aim to find the source of this effect, for example using a composite Higgs and dynamically generating its vacuum expectation value. In this paper, we show evidence from lattice simulations with vectorially coupled fermions and without Yukawa couplings that the Brout-Englert-Higgs effect could be triggered by the light masses of fermions. This suggests that an explanation entirely within the Standard Model could be possible. Moreover, our results are consistent with weak couplings and fermions can only impact the Higgs potential at NNLO. This implies a so far unidentified mechanism in an SU(2) scalar-fermion-gauge system that produces a non-zero vacuum expectation value. In addition, we find that the transition from a QCD-like behavior to a Higgs-like behavior happens when the fermion mass falls below twice the W/Z mass: a threshold that is integer valued but not connected to other critical physical behavior.
