### [1] [Mellin Moments of Proton Unpolarized GPDs at Nonzero Skewness from Lattice QCD with a Neural-Network Parametrization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29726)
*Min-Huan Chu,Manuel Colaço,Krzysztof Cichy,Martha Constantinou,Arif Sarkar*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: This paper employs lattice QCD to calculate the generalized form factors (GFFs) for the first four Mellin moments of unpolarized isovector $u-d$ proton GPDs at nonzero skewness. The method uses a combination of polynomiality in skewness and short-distance expansion, with an artificial neural network to parameterize the GFFs. It determines 14 GFFs and the quark total angular momentum, as well as impact-parameter distributions, while addressing systematic uncertainties.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to provide a more accurate and comprehensive understanding of the generalized parton distribution functions (GPDs) which are key to describing the internal structure of protons, especially at nonzero skewness where traditional methods face challenges.

Method: The researchers use lattice Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) along with the polynomiality property of Mellin moments and short-distance expansion. They employ artificial neural networks (ANN) to parameterize GFFs as continuous functions of the invariant momentum transfer squared, fitting these to the lattice data to extract the GFFs.

Result: The study successfully extracts 14 GFFs that contribute to the first four Mellin moments, including those dependent on skewness. Additionally, it calculates the quark total angular momentum of the isovector combination, $J^{u-d}=0.296(11)(7)$, and determines the impact-parameter distributions for the first three Mellin moments, both for unpolarized and transversely polarized protons.

Conclusion: The results agree with previous lattice calculations but with reduced uncertainty. The analysis also identifies and discusses the main systematic uncertainties and the specific challenges associated with the nonzero-skewness regime, contributing to a better understanding of the proton's internal structure.

Abstract: We use lattice QCD to determine the generalized form factors (GFFs) entering the first four Mellin moments of the unpolarized isovector $u-d$ proton GPDs at nonzero skewness. The framework combines the polynomiality of the moments in the skewness $ξ$, with the short-distance expansion formalism, allowing the lattice-extracted reduced Ioffe-time distributions (ITDs) to be expressed in terms of GFFs. In order to extract the GFFs by fitting these relations to the lattice data, we parameterize each GFF with an artificial neural network (ANN) as a continuous function of the invariant momentum transfer squared $t$, which accommodates the kinematic coverage of the data in a flexible way. With this fit, we determine the fourteen GFFs that enter the first four Mellin moments, including the skewness-dependent GFFs. From the extracted GFFs, we obtain the quark total angular momentum of the isovector combination, $J^{u-d}=0.296(11)(7)$ in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme at $μ=2~\mathrm{GeV}$, in agreement with previous lattice determinations based on nonlocal operators and with a smaller total uncertainty. We also determine the impact-parameter distributions of the first three Mellin moments in an unpolarized and in a transversely polarized proton. We finally identify and discuss the principal systematic uncertainties which enter this extraction, and the challenges specific to the nonzero-skewness regime.

### [2] [Progress on meson-glueball mixing in lattice gauge theories coupled to fermions in multiple representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29861)
*Nuno Brito,Ed Bennett,Deog Ki Hong,Jong-Wan Lee,C. -J. David Lin,Biagio Lucini,Maurizio Piai,Davide Vadacchino,Fabian Zierler*

Main category: hep-lat

TL;DR: The paper presents a numerical lattice study of low-lying flavor-singlet bound states in the Sp(4) gauge theory, using Wilson gauge action and fermions. The GEVP method with smeared operators is used to analyze two high-statistics ensembles. Evidence for meson-glueball mixing is observed, and there's an improvement in the determination of energy eigenstates and mixing angles for pseudoscalar states.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation of this paper is to explore the low-lying flavor-singlet bound states within the Sp(4) gauge theory, which includes investigating potential meson-glueball mixing and improving the accuracy of energy eigenstate and mixing angle determinations in the pseudoscalar sector.

Method: The authors use a numerical lattice approach with the Wilson gauge action and Wilson fermions, and they employ the generalized eigenvalue problem (GEVP) method with a basis of smeared glueball and meson interpolating operators. They analyze two different high-statistics ensembles with the same lattice coupling but varying fermion masses, focusing on both scalar and pseudoscalar states.

Result: The study yields preliminary results that include the first evidence for meson-glueball mixing in the Sp(4) gauge theory. Additionally, the paper reports improved determinations of energy eigenstates and mixing angles in the flavor-singlet pseudoscalar sector compared to previous studies.

Conclusion: The research provides new insights into the dynamics of the Sp(4) gauge theory, particularly regarding the interaction between mesons and glueballs, as well as more precise measurements of the physical properties of pseudoscalar states.

Abstract: We present preliminary results from our numerical lattice investigation of the low-lying flavour-singlet bound states in the ${\rm Sp}(4)$ gauge theory coupled to fermions transforming in the fundamental and two-index antisymmetric representations. We use the Wilson gauge action and Wilson fermions. We employ the generalized eigenvalue problem (GEVP) method with a basis of smeared glueball and meson interpolating operators. We analyse two high-statistics ensembles with the same lattice coupling and different fermion mass. We study both the scalar and pseudoscalar states. We observe the first evidence for meson-glueball mixing in this theory. We improve upon previous determinations of the energy eigenstates and mixing angles in the flavour-singlet pseudoscalar sector.
